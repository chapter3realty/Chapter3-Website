A message from Chapter3

A listing shows a price, photos, and sometimes a rent figure someone quoted. That is not enough to tell you whether the property works. What the property keeps depends on what comes out of the rent each month, and on how many months of the year it is rented.

Get these figures before you sign, not after. Ask for all four for the specific property, not for the market in general.

  • Income. What the unit is likely to earn over a full year, as a lease or as nightly bookings, including the slow months.
  • Fixed costs. Dues, insurance, and property tax, all higher on a property you do not live in.
  • Variable costs. Management, cleaning between guests, owner paid utilities, repairs, and money set aside for the HOA reserve.
  • Rules. Whether the building and the local ordinance allow the kind of renting you plan, and what the shortest permitted stay is.

It returns the figures investors use to compare properties.

  • Net operating income. What the property earns in a year after operating costs, before any loan.
  • Cap rate. That income measured against the price, so two very different properties can be compared on the same basis.
  • Debt service coverage. Whether the income from the property covers the cost of the loan. Your lender decides what that has to be.
  • Cash flow. What is left after everything has been paid, including the loan.
  • Occupancy. Typical occupancy for a short-term rental, so the slow months are in the figure and not assumed away.
  • Expense breakdown. Every cost line shown separately, so you can change one and see the effect.

Common questions

Do I need a different brokerage to buy an investment property in Myrtle Beach?

Any licensed South Carolina brokerage can write the contract. Chapter3 pays more attention to its investor clients, and publishes free tools so you can work the numbers yourself before you make an offer.

What does the Chapter3 rental analyzer give me?

You enter the property details and the assumptions you want to test. It returns net operating income, cap rate, debt service coverage, cash flow, and a line by line expense breakdown. It also returns typical short-term rental occupancy. It is free to use.

Are property taxes higher on a rental in Horry County?

Usually yes. South Carolina taxes a home you live in on a smaller share of its value than a property you do not live in. So the tax on a rental is usually higher than what the current owner pays. Chapter3 explains how it works on their property tax page.
