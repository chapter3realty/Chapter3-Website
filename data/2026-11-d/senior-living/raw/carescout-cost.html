    Why conduct a cost of care survey?

    We've conducted this survey for 20+ years to help give more families the information they need to talk about and plan for long-term care needs. The American population is aging faster than ever before, and people are living longer. Every day until 2030, 10,000 Baby Boomers will turn 65a and 7 out of 10 people will require long-term care in their lifetimeb.

    The cost for any particular provider may differ significantly from the median cost of care for similar providers in your location. Also, the cost of care can vary widely depending on where care is delivered, the type of services needed, and the level of support required. The Cost of Care Survey tool helps you and your family explore median long-term care costs across the U.S., today and in the future — providing a clearer starting point for planning ahead.

    In-Home Care Costs (Non-Medical Caregiver)
    based on 44 hours per week by 52 weeks

    $35

    National hourly cost for a non-medical caregiver in 2025 as reported hourly.

    • The national median hourly rate for non-medical caregiver services increased to $35 per hour, reflecting a 3% year-over-year increase.

    • At this rate, the annual cost of care totals $80,080, assuming 44 hours of care per week over 52 weeks.

    • Note: Due to price convergence, CareScout has streamlined its reporting; homemaker and home health aide services are now collectively classified as "non-medical caregiver."

    Private Room Nursing Home costs

    $129,575

    Annual National Median cost for a nursing home private room in 2025 based on 365 days of care.

    • The national median daily rate for a private room in a nursing home rose 1% to $355 per day, bringing the annual cost to $129,575.

    • The national median daily rate for a semi-private room in a nursing home increased 2% to $315 per day, or $114,975 annually.

    Assisted Living Community costs

    $74,400

    Annual National Median cost for assisted living facility care services in 2025 based on the monthly fee multiplied by 12 months.
    (Referred to residential care facility in California)

    • The national median monthly cost for assisted living communities increased 5% to $6,200 per month, or $74,400 annually.

    Source: Cost of Care Survey, July through November 2025

    Frequently asked questions

    • From July through November 2025, CareScout contacted 211,985 long-term care providers by phone and email to complete approximately 16,000 surveys including nursing homes, assisted living communities, adult day health facilities and home care providers.

      • Surveyors polled home health care providers, resulting in 6,014 completed surveys.

      • Surveyors polled adult day health care facilities, resulting in 608 completed surveys.

      • Surveyors polled licensed assisted living communities, resulting in 4,944 completed surveys.

      • Surveyors polled certified and licensed nursing homes, resulting in 4,425 completed surveys.

    • Potential respondents were selected from CareScout's nationwide database of providers, supplemented with additional providers in each category of long-term care services. Survey respondents represent all 50 states and the District of Columbia.

    • The Genworth Cost of Care Survey publishes costs in 434 regions based on the 387 U.S. Metropolitan Statistical Areas (MSAs) as defined by the U.S. Office of Management and Budget. MSAs are urbanized areas with a population of 50,000 or more, along with adjacent counties that have a high degree of social and economic integration.

    • Since the inception of the survey, CareScout has followed industry guidance with representing 44 hours as the estimated number of hours per week for professional in-home caregivers. Additionally, private duty nurse rates assume that care recipients will need approximately 1 hour of care per week.

      Since we understand that everyone's situation may vary based on the degree of required care, you can use the "Change weekly In-Home Care hours" section (located below the Cost of Care table) to adjust the number of hours from 7 hours per week to a maximum of 168 hours (24 hours of care / 7 days a week) to better represent your care needs.

    • In-Home Care Options

      Non-Medical Caregiver: Includes a home health aide providing in-home assistance with performing activities of daily living, such as help with bathing, dressing, transferring and toileting that does not require more specialized care. It can also include homemaker services, like providing ancillary assistance with light house cleaning and meal planning and preparation.

      Skilled Nursing in the Home (Private Duty Nurse): Services are provided by an LPN or an RN and may include dressing changes, wound care, blood draws, injections, intravenous (IV) therapy, catheters, feeding tubes, mediports, medication management, and training of family members to perform some healthcare procedures." beneath home health aide services definition. Services are based on the reported hourly rate.

      Community Option

      Adult Day Health Care (ADC): Provides social and support services in a community-based, protective setting. Various models are designed to offer socialization, supervision and structured activities. Some programs may provide personal care, transportation, medical management and meals.

      Facility Options

      Assisted Living Community (ALC): Residential arrangements providing personal care and health services. The level of care may not be as extensive as that of a nursing home. Assisted living is often an alternative to a nursing home, or an intermediate level of long-term care. Assisted Living Communities are referred to as Residential Care Facilities in California.

      Nursing Home Care: These facilities often provide a higher level of supervision and care than Assisted Living Communities. They offer residents personal care assistance, room and board, supervision, medication, therapies and rehabilitation, and on-site nursing care 24 hours a day.
