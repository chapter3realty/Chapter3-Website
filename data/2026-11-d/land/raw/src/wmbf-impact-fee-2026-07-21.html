Impact fee schedule change now in effect in Horry County; concerns raised over homeownership

By Jenna Guzman
Published: Jul. 20, 2026 at 9:40 PM EDT
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

HORRY COUNTY, SC (WMBF) - Votes were cast in Horry County Council, resulting in changes to impact fees.

Impact fees are fees collected on new development and construction in the county. They have existed in the county since 2021, and the council unanimously voted on Ordinance 30-2026 last week.

These fees help support county improvements aimed at benefitting the community, such as in infrastructure, recreation, and public safety. The demand for this grows with the county’s population.

Local realtor Alysia Vulgaris said, “It’s a growing county, one of the fastest growing cities in the whole country, that comes with some growing pains.”

Based on the Impact Fee 2026 Fee Schedule, which went into effect Monday, changes to impact fees will relate to a development’s type and size in square footage.

For example, a house that is approximately 1,500 square feet will have a total fee of $1,615, and a retail space will have a fee of $1,776 per 1,000 square feet.

However, it’s a change some are concerned about when it comes to building and buying homes because of potentially higher costs.

“You need impact fees to pay for development, but you don’t want it to be too much that it overburdens the cost of housing,” said Stanley Haines with the Horry Georgetown Homebuilders Association. “And then you’re going to see a lot less houses being built when there’s a big need for that in our area.”

Haines, who spoke to council before the council voted on the ordinance, mentioned the area is in a housing affordability crisis.

“We want to make sure that our workforce has places to live,” Haines told WMBF. “Part of the American dream is homeownership, so we want to make sure we’re not adding extra costs to the house that are passed on to these working families that are trying to buy homes in Horry County.”

A Capital Improvement Plan and Impact Fee Study by TischlerBise has more details regarding the impact fees, and the developments and total fees that may be collected.
