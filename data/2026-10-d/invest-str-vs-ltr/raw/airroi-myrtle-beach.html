The short answer

Myrtle Beach Airbnb Market at a Glance

Rates and bookings both trail the South Carolina average. Listings averaged $257 a night and were booked on 36% of available nights, against $355 and 39% for the South Carolina average.

Timing matters: in July, usually the best month, the average listing earns about five times as much as in January, usually the slowest.

Revenue

How Much Do Airbnb Hosts Make in Myrtle Beach?

Gross bookings per Myrtle Beach listing averaged $25,807 over the 12 months to September 2026 (South Carolina average: $37,525), before cleaning, platform and management costs and taxes.

In a typical year, the average Myrtle Beach listing brings in $6,800 in July, its best month, and $1,340 in January, its slowest. The three strongest months bring in 47% of a full year's revenue, where an even calendar would give any three months 25%.

In a typical month, a top-quarter listing earns at least 3.4 times as much as a bottom-quarter one.

  • Best-in-class properties (top 10%) earn $6,770+ a month, about 2.7 times the typical property.
  • Strong performers (top 25%) earn $4,330 or more, roughly $1,800 a month above the median.
  • Typical properties (median) earn about $2,530 a month.
  • Entry-level properties (bottom 25%) earn $1,270 or less, about half of the median.

These are monthly figures for listings active in each month. The annual average above also counts listings that operated for only part of the year, so it runs below twelve times these figures.

Profit

Is an Airbnb in Myrtle Beach Profitable?

Yes, when it's run well. The average Myrtle Beach Airbnb took in $25,807 in the 12 months to September 2026, and run by its owner at everyday costs, about $1,330 a month of that is left after running costs, so a home that costs less than that to own or rent makes money. A listing in the top quarter leaves about $2,310 a month.

Cleaning it yourself keeps the $3,300 a year guests pay in cleaning fees, for about $1,610 a month. Handing the listing to a co-host at about 10% of nightly bookings still leaves about $1,140 a month.

These amounts come before housing costs. Where the mortgage or rent is less each month, the difference is profit; a home owned outright keeps it all after property tax, home insurance and any HOA or condo fees.

How Myrtle Beach's Top Earners Get There

  • More nights booked. The busiest quarter of Myrtle Beach listings are booked on 56% or more of their nights, against 34% for the median listing. Ten more booked nights a year at the median rate of $210 adds about $2,090 in bookings.
  • Prices that follow demand. Ranked by price, the top quarter charge $285 a night or more, against a median of $210. That is a separate ranking from the busiest quarter, so the two marks don't multiply into one listing's takings. For the average listing, a 10% higher rate on the nights it already books adds about $2,250 a year.
  • Open for the busy months. In a typical year, July brings the average listing about $6,800, against $1,340 in January, so a calendar that is open and full in the busy months carries the year.
  • Reviews that stand out. Myrtle Beach listings average 4.66 stars, and 23% carry Airbnb's Guest Favorite badge, which guests can filter for.

What a listing keeps comes down to the figures this page measures: nightly rate, nights booked and the months it's open. A listing at the top-quarter mark takes in about $3,690 a month, against $2,150 for the average one.

Occupancy

What Is the Airbnb Occupancy Rate in Myrtle Beach?

Myrtle Beach listings were booked on 36% of the nights they were open over the past year, 3 percentage points below the South Carolina average (39%) and 5 points below the U.S. average (41%). In a typical year, occupancy swings from 26% in January to 56% in July.

The busiest tenth of listings are booked about 4.2 times as often as the bottom quarter.

  • Best-in-class properties (top 10%) are booked 74%+ of open nights, 40 points above the median.
  • Strong performers (top 25%) fill 56% or more, roughly four nights a week.
  • Typical properties (median) fill about 34% of open nights.
  • Entry-level properties (bottom 25%) fill 18% or less, under two nights a week.

Nightly rates

How Much Does an Airbnb Cost per Night in Myrtle Beach?

The average nightly rate in Myrtle Beach, known as ADR (average daily rate), was $257 over the past year, about 28% below the South Carolina average of $355 and about 20% below the U.S. average of $320. In a typical year, rates swing with the seasons, from about $180 in November and December to about $320 in July.

The top 10% charge about 2.5 times the rate of the bottom quarter.

  • Best-in-class properties (top 10%) charge $400+ a night, about twice the median.
  • Strong performers (top 25%) charge $285 or more, about $75 above the median.
  • Typical properties (median) charge about $210 a night.
  • Entry-level properties (bottom 25%) charge $160 or less, about three-quarters of the median.

What Is the RevPAR for Airbnb in Myrtle Beach?

RevPAR, or revenue per available night, divides a listing's income by every night it was open to book, so it rises with both price and bookings. In Myrtle Beach it averaged $106 over the past year, from about $43 in a typical January to about $219 in a typical July.

Per open night, by tier:

  • Best-in-class properties (top 10%) earn $204+ per open night, about 2.4 times the median.
  • Strong performers (top 25%) earn $136 or more, about $50 a night above the median.
  • Typical properties (median) earn about $86 per open night.
  • Entry-level properties (bottom 25%) earn $49 or less, about 57% of the median.

Method

About This Data

Figures on this page cover 8,698 active Airbnb listings in Myrtle Beach from October 2025 to September 2026 (trailing twelve months). AirROI refreshes them monthly from listing-level booking and calendar data covering 20+ million properties worldwide. Money figures are in U.S. dollars. Annual revenue averages every listing active during the period, including those listed for only part of it; monthly figures average the listings active in each month; the tier lists show the spread.
