Get a Quote What Is HO-6 Insurance in South Carolina? Condo insurance is officially known as HO-6 insurance, which is a type of policy designed specifically for condo unit owners. HO-6 insurance in South Carolina covers the interior of your condo unit and your personal belongings. HO-6 insurance in South Carolina covers your unit from damage from covered perils such as theft, fire, water, and wind damage. All the coverages in your policy are laid out as follows: Personal Property Coverage Personal property coverage safeguards your belongings, such as furniture, clothes, electronics, and more, from covered perils. Loss of Use Coverage Loss of use coverage helps pay for lodging, gas, groceries, and more if damage from a covered peril temporarily forces you out of your home. Liability Coverage Liability coverage covers you if someone gets injured at your condo unit and pursues you for damages. Your liability coverage can cover medical expenses and legal fees. It can also cover you if you accidentally cause property damage to someone else. Medical Payments Coverage Medical payments coverage also helps cover injuries someone can suffer. It’s designed to cover medical expenses related to minor injuries someone can suffer without the need for legal liability. Loss Assessment Coverage Loss assessment coverage comes to your aid if your condo association suffers damage that exceeds their insurance plan and tries to assess you for the costs. Building Property (Dwelling) Coverage Dwelling coverage in condo insurance covers permanent fixtures and features in your unit that your association doesn’t cover, such as appliances, countertops, and lighting. How to Get the Best Condo Insurance in South Carolina You need to address your coverage needs to make sure you get the best condo insurance in South Carolina. Once you know what to cover and how much you need, compare quotes from multiple companies to find a policy within your budget. Your personal condo insurance should work in conjunction with your condo association’s insurance . Every condo homeowners insurance association (HOA) has an insurance policy that covers common areas and building structures. You help fund this insurance plan with your HOA dues. Work With Your HOA Insurance Some HOA policies extend coverage into your unit, covering permanent installations such as countertops, flooring, and cabinets – dwelling features that are part of the condo. This is called walls-in coverage. If you have walls-in coverage , you can likely drop dwelling coverage in your HO-6 insurance in South Carolina, saving you money on premiums. If your HOA has walls-out or bare walls coverage, your association won’t cover any surfaces in your unit. In this case, you may want to include dwelling coverage in your personal policy to protect the interior features of your unit. Also, note that some HOA plans with walls-in coverage only cover the original surfaces that were in the unit. So, suppose you installed some new features, such as upgrading your original countertops from laminate to granite. In that case, the walls-in HOA coverage may not cover the cost of the upgraded materials . Consult your master policy to know what’s covered. Actual Cash Value vs. Replacement Cost Personal condo insurance plans can cover your belongings in two ways: actual cash value and replacement cost coverage . Actual cash value coverage means your provider will replace your things during a claim after subtracting depreciation. This means you’ll never get the full value of your items back unless they are brand new. With replacement cost coverage, your insurer will reimburse you what you paid for a damaged item, regardless of its age. Replacement cost coverage has better payouts, but it’s the more costly choice. Consider Endorsements If your budget can afford it, considering extra coverages in your policy is not a bad idea if needed. Many carriers offer optional coverages not included by default. Common endorsements include water backup coverage, which helps cover damage caused by unexpected water backups from your pipes, or ordinance or law coverage, which helps offset building costs after damage when bringing outdated elements up to current code. You can also schedule especially valuable possessions you may have to make sure they’re fully covered. And, if you run a business out of your unit, you should consider a home business endorsement, officially called a permitted incidental occupancies endorsement . These optional coverages will cost extra, but you can shop around to see who has what you need at the cheapest rate. South Carolina Condo Insurance Cost Factors The cost of condo insurance in South Carolina depends on a few variables that affect the risk profile of your property. Some of the most critical elements that insurers look at to determine premiums are your condo’s location and building features. Condos near the Atlantic coast of South Carolina will likely have higher premiums, as those buildings are much more prone to wind and water damage from hurricanes and tropical storms. Condo building characteristics also play a vital role. Older buildings will have higher premiums when their dated elements or construction methods sacrifice the building’s ability to withstand severe weather. For instance, old pipes, siding, and roofs are more prone to damage from wear and tear or extreme weather and have an increased chance of causing extensive damage, which makes insuring the home more risky to insurance carriers. Thus, they’ll charge higher rates. Newer buildings can have safety features or building methods that lower rates. But, new, high-end condos with expensive or unique features will have higher replacement costs, so those buildings will have higher premiums than a more standard condo. South Carolina Condo Insurance Laws and Requirements The state of South Carolina doesn’t legally require condo insurance. However, mortgage lenders or condo associations can require unit owners to get personal condo insurance policies. Mortgage lenders require condo owners to carry insurance as a condition of the home loan. Lenders want to ensure that their investment is protected in case of damage to the unit. HOAs can require unit owners to have policies, also. Some associations will make condo insurance a requirement before moving in. It further protects the association and other residents from liability incidents and more. Even though you may not always be required to have it, condo insurance provides essential protection for yourself, your unit, and your family, and we wouldn’t recommend going without a policy.
