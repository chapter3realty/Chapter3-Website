The fees beyond the monthly percentage

The monthly percentage is the advertised number. Most management contracts add several more line items, and they apply in Myrtle Beach the same way they do everywhere else:

Tenant placement / leasing fee
50-100% of first month's rent (iPropertyManagement measured average: 70.6% of one month's rent when bundled with management; 81.8% for placement-only; flat-rate alternative averages $555.53 with management, $670.23 placement-only). Charged each time a new tenant is placed. Covers marketing, showings, screening, lease prep, move-in. This is the single biggest add-on and it recurs with every turnover, so high-turnover properties pay it repeatedly.
Lease renewal fee
$200-$500 flat (iPropertyManagement measured average: $211.92; Baselane cites $100-$300 or a % of one month's rent). Charged when an EXISTING tenant simply re-signs - i.e., a fee for paperwork on a tenant the owner already has. Charged annually on every renewal.
Maintenance markup / coordination fee
10-15% added to vendor invoices is the most-cited range (sources span 5-25%). A surcharge on top of the actual repair bill for coordinating the vendor. On $3,000/yr of repairs a 15% markup is $450/yr of pure fee. Some firms instead use in-house crews at above-market hourly rates.
Vacancy fee
$50-$150 per month while the unit sits empty; some contracts charge one month's rent equivalent or keep billing the full management fee ('rent due' contracts). Charged for watching an empty unit (inspections, lawn, marketing). Stings because it hits exactly when the owner has zero income. Verify whether the management fee is on 'rent collected' or 'rent due' - the latter means full fees during vacancy.
Setup / onboarding fee
$200-$500 one-time per property (most sources cluster at $300-$500). One-time charge to open the account: bookkeeping setup, initial inspection, registration help. Charged before the PM has collected a dollar of rent.
Eviction coordination fee
$200-$500 for the PM's time (DoorLoop cites $300-$1,000+), PLUS court filing fees and attorney costs billed to the owner. Covers serving notices, court appearances, coordinating turnover. The PM fee is on top of, not instead of, actual legal costs.
Early termination fee
Flat $50-$1,000, or roughly one month's management fee or more; some contracts demand all remaining management fees for the lease/contract term. Charged for firing the manager before the contract ends. Contracts typically also require 30-60 day notice, and some allow the PM to hold owner funds (including the reserve) for 30-60 days after termination to cover trailing expenses.
Inspection fees
$75-$300 per inspection (iPropertyManagement measured average: $106.72). Move-in/move-out inspections are often bundled, but periodic or extra inspections (drive-by, annual interior) are frequently billed separately per visit.

Industry blog analyses converge on a real all-in cost well above the advertised percentage: ClearLead Digital's 2026 fee analysis says the monthly fee is only one of 5-8 fees most managers charge and total FIRST-YEAR cost typically runs 18-20% of gross rent; the same and related guides advise budgeting 12-15% of gross rent even when the base rate is 8-10%, because lease-up fees, maintenance markups, and misc charges add roughly 3-5 points to the effective rate. Worked example from those sources: $2,000/mo rent at 10% = $2,400/yr base + $750/yr amortized lease-up (0.5 turnovers x $1,500) + $450/yr maintenance markup ($3,000 repairs x 15%) = $3,600/yr = effective 15% of gross rent. Turnover frequency is the biggest swing factor: a placement fee of 70-100% of one month's rent equals 6-8% of a full year's gross by itself in a turnover year. These are practitioner/blog estimates, not government statistics - present them as 'typically 12-20% all-in, first year highest.'

Where the rent sits each month

With a management company, rent lands in the manager's trust account. You get the remainder after fees on their statement schedule, usually mid-month or later, and repair costs come out before you see them, with any markup already applied. Most owner complaints about managers are some version of this paragraph: slow statements, unclear deductions, surprises at payout time.

Self-managing with software inverts that. Rent goes from the tenant's bank to yours through the payment processor, every charge and payment sits in a ledger you can open any hour, and repair invoices are numbers you approved rather than line items you discover. When hiring a Myrtle Beach management company still makes sense

This page argues for self-managing with software because the math usually favors it. Not always. The honest exceptions:

  • Out-of-state and remote owners: maintenance and emergency response is the single hardest thing to do from hundreds of miles away; a local manager provides around-the-clock physical response and an existing vendor network (Redfin, 'Managing a Rental Property Out of State'; Baselane, 'How to Manage Out-of-State Rental Property'). For short-term rentals managed from out of state, the guidance is that hiring a manager is almost always advisable despite 25-40% fees.
  • Scale: the Census/HUD 2021 Rental Housing Finance Survey shows professional management climbing from ~22% of 1-4 unit properties to 84% of 150+ unit properties - past a certain door count you are hiring management labor either way, whether it's a PM firm or your own staff.
  • Eviction-heavy or heavily regulated markets: an average eviction runs $3,500 and can reach $10,000+ (TransUnion SmartMove; Snappt), and procedural mistakes restart the clock and burn additional months of rent (~$2,540 average lost rent). A PM firm with an attorney pipeline and process discipline earns its fee if evictions are a recurring reality in your market.
  • Owners with no time or high-value time: Census-derived data (iPropertyManagement) shows roughly 1 in 6 landlord-managed properties consumes 20-79 hours/month. If your property is a high-touch building or your working hour is worth more than the fee, 8-12% is a rational trade.
  • Honest caveat that cuts both ways: UK survey data (Pegasus Insight, 2026) found landlords' reported time commitment was broadly similar whether or not they used an agent - hiring a PM buys execution, not zero involvement; the owner still oversees, approves, and audits.

Common questions

How much does property management cost in Myrtle Beach?

US management companies typically charge 8-12% of collected rent (the advertised national average is about 8.5%). At Myrtle Beach's median asking rent of about $1,726/mo (Zillow Observed Rent Index, 2026-05), that is roughly $1,657 to $2,485 per unit per year, before add-on fees like tenant placement (commonly 50-100% of one month's rent), lease renewals, and maintenance markups.

What is a fair management fee for a Myrtle Beach rental?

Within the 8-12% range, what matters more than the percentage is the contract fine print: whether the fee is charged on rent collected or rent due (rent due means you pay during vacancy), the size of the placement and renewal fees, and the maintenance markup. A cheap percentage with a 100% placement fee and 15% markup often costs more than a higher percentage with clean terms.

Can I manage my own rentals in Myrtle Beach instead?

Most small landlords already do: Census data shows only about 22% of 1-4 unit rental properties are professionally managed. Software covers the office half (rent collection, late-payment follow-up, accounting, owner statements, maintenance dispatch, screening). What does the 8-12% fee actually pay for?

Rent collection, tenant communication, bookkeeping and statements, maintenance coordination, and the physical work: showings, inspections, being on call. The office tasks are now automated well by software; the physical tasks are the part you genuinely cannot automate, and many owners handle them directly or pay per task rather than a monthly percentage.
