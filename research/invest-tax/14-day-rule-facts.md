# The 14-day rule and personal use — verified facts

Read at Publication 527, chapter on personal use of a dwelling unit, on
2026-09-04. Re-open before reuse.

## The test, quoted
A dwelling unit is used as a home when personal use exceeds "the greater of
14 days or 10% of the days rented at fair rental price" during the year.
**Greater of.** On a high-occupancy short-term rental the 10 percent figure is
the one that applies: a unit rented 200 nights allows 20 personal days, not 14.

## A day of personal use, quoted
- "You or a family member" occupying the unit.
- Anyone using it "at less than fair rental price".
- Use "under an arrangement to use another unit".
- Exception: days spent "mainly on repairs and maintenance" do not count.

## Rented fewer than 15 days, quoted
When a unit used as a home is "rented less than 15 days during the tax year",
the income "is not included in your income" and "expenses from this activity
are not considered rental expenses".

## Dividing expenses, quoted
Expenses are "divided between rental and personal use" on the ratio of rental
days to total days used. When used as a home, "rental expenses cannot exceed
rental income"; the excess carries forward. Ordering: mortgage interest and
taxes first, then utilities and maintenance, then depreciation.

## Local interactions, link only
- The property-tax side: renting your own legal residence past a set count of
  days a year costs the 4 percent assessment rate. The count is on
  `/buyers/property-taxes/`. **Do not restate the number** (A9).
- The accommodations-tax side: your own stays are not transient rentals, but
  the 90 and 30-day thresholds on `/invest/accommodations-tax/` are about
  guests, not you.
- The passive side: personal use days are not customer use; the seven-day
  average on `/invest/str-tax-treatment/` counts guests only.
