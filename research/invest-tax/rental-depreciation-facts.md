# Depreciating a rental from closing day — verified facts

Read at the sources on 2026-09-04. Re-open before reuse.

**Scope decision 2026-09-04.** The brief was "depreciation and recapture at
sale". By the time this page was reached, `/invest/cost-segregation/` carried
the 27.5-year schedule, the land rule and both recapture layers, and
`/sell/capital-gains/` already owned recapture at sale with a calculator. This
page is the everyday version nobody had: what goes into basis on closing day,
how the land split is made, the first year, repairs against improvements, and
the one sale-side fact the other pages do not state (allowed or allowable).

## Basis — Publication 527
Settlement fees and closing costs that go INTO basis, quoted list:
"Abstract fees. Charges for installing utility services. Legal fees. Recording
fees. Surveys. Transfer taxes. Title insurance."

NOT in basis, quoted: "Points (discount points, loan origination fees), Loan
assumption fees, Cost of a credit report, and Fees for an appraisal required by
a lender." (Loan costs are handled as loan costs, not as basis.)

## The land split — Publication 527
"The part of the cost that you allocate to each asset is the ratio of the FMV
of that asset to the FMV of the whole property at the time you buy it."
"If you aren't certain of the FMVs of the land and the buildings, you can
divide the cost between them based on their assessed values for real estate
tax purposes."

## Horry County's record carries the two numbers — Guide to Assessment
horrycountysc.gov/departments/assessor/guide-to-assessment/: licensed staff
appraisers "determine land values" separately, and "structural improvements to
the land are valued using a market sales modified Marshall & Swift cost
service." So the county record lists land and building values on separate
lines, which is exactly what the assessed-values method above needs.
**Do not state a typical ratio.** It varies by street and by year.

## First year — Publication 527
- Residential rental: 27.5 years, straight line, GDS.
- "A mid-month convention is used for all residential rental property and
  nonresidential real property."
- "You place property in service in a rental activity when it is ready and
  available for a specific use in that activity." Ready and available, not
  first rented. A unit listed and vacant is in service.

## Repairs against improvements — Publication 527
- "You must capitalize any expense you pay to improve your rental property."
- De minimis safe harbor, quoted: "If you elect this de minimis safe harbor
  for your rental activity for the tax year, you aren't required to capitalize
  the de minimis costs of acquiring or producing certain real and tangible
  personal property." The dollar ceiling lives in the regulations, not in the
  publication. **Do not state the ceiling** without reading the regulation.

## Allowed or allowable — Publication 946
Depreciation you were entitled to take reduces basis whether or not you took
it. Publication 946: if you do not claim depreciation you are entitled to
deduct, you must still reduce your basis by the full amount allowable. This is
the sale-side fact the other two pages do not say, and the one that hurts
owners who "skipped" depreciation to keep things simple.
