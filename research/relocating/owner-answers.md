## Pros and cons material (owner voice)

- **Not walkable.** "We are a golf cart community, not a walking community like
  a bigger city." Actionable: check whether the neighbourhood is golf-cart
  legal (35 mph roads, permit rules) before buying.
- **Not a party city.** Do NOT mention police complaint volume (owner asked us
  not to, legal).
- **Making friends is harder than expected** because many people around the
  city core are tourists or seasonal workers who leave. Owner's fix: buy
  outside the city of Myrtle Beach proper and be a regular at places tourists
  do not go (gyms, clubs, bars). Use, phrased as seasonal population, no visa
  categories.
- **Business owners** who relocate report they cannot charge as much for
  services as they did up north. Use: real con for self-employed movers.
- **Northerners arrive suspicious of everyone, or trust everyone instantly
  because they heard about southern hospitality; Ohio movers feel people are
  the same as home.** About people. Do not use as written. At most: "the pace
  is slower and people talk to strangers; some transplants find that takes
  adjusting."
- **Traffic.** Summer traffic "is not very bad." Most delay is waiting for
  geese to cross. Rush hour adds 5 to 10 minutes. Leaving for work 5 to 8 am
  has few stops. Workaround: take the interstate (SC-31 / SC-22) because US-17
  has many stop lights. Use.
- **Off season.** Nothing closes except tourist attractions and water parks.
  Boardwalk stays open, possibly reduced hours. Winter feels "more open"
  because fewer people. Use.
- **Mosquitoes** are more noticeable than in a big city because it is more
  rural. Use.
- **Heat and humidity.** First summer felt like a sauna; acclimatised within a
  short period and now likes being outside. Use (as one person's experience).
- **Brightness.** Never needed sunglasses before; needs them here in summer;
  got used to it. Use.
- **Roads** are better paved than New York, Maryland or Texas (co-worker's
  observation). Use as anecdote.
- **Housing types mix within short distances.** Owner phrased it as rich and
  poor living two blocks apart. Do NOT publish as written (about people). The
  property version is usable on a buying page: zoning here mixes housing types
  more than the Northeast does; look at what is within two blocks of any home
  you are considering.
- **Homeless observations.** Do not use anywhere (safety / who lives where).
- **Dollar stores are dense** compared to Maryland and Texas. Marginal; skip.
- **New hospital under construction; homes nearby "will probably appreciate."**
  Publish the fact (which hospital, where, when) only. Never publish the
  appreciation prediction (A19, no forecasts).

## 2026-08-26 — Timmy's Hugo account (relayed by Devin, this session)

Recorded near-verbatim for provenance; published on /buyers/relocating/hurricanes/
as "his recollection, as he told it to us," with similes ("like a war zone,"
"like toothpicks") paraphrased to literal detail per the no-metaphor style rule.

- Family evacuated to Augusta, GA, leaving ~3-4 p.m., about 5 hours ahead of the
  outer bands. Eye hit McClellanville ~midnight. Waited in a hotel; returned at
  checkout time.
- Socastee bridge: National Guard turning people back; father was EMS, let through.
- Home: power, water, telephone all out. Father had filled bathtubs + two
  50-gallon barrels before leaving. Power out ~2 weeks; daily water pickup at
  town hall about as long.
- Dirt bike ride up the beach 5 miles north to grandmother's oceanfront house,
  south end Myrtle Beach: houses and pier pieces strung down the beach, dead
  fish everywhere, campers piled in her front yard; house stood, flood water had
  filled and drained. National Guard sent him home.
- His figures: 18+ ft surge / 135 mph at McClellanville, "not much worse than
  100 if that" here. RECORD CHECK (weather.gov/ilm/hurricanehugo, 2026-08-26):
  landfall midnight Sept 22 near Sullivan's Island, 140 mph sustained; surge up
  to 20 ft (Bulls Bay, debris marks), 19.4 ft Awendaw, 16 ft McClellanville
  (6 ft of seawater in the school gym); Myrtle Beach 45 kt sustained / 66 kt
  gusts = about 52 / 76 mph. HIS WIND NUMBERS NOT PUBLISHED — the record
  paragraph on the page carries the NWS figures; his verbatim close ("We were
  lucky. One shift north of 50 miles and nothing would have been left here.")
  published as quoted judgment.
- OPEN: Timmy should read the published passage before deploy and confirm the
  details (which town hall; five-miles-north geography) are as he wants them.
