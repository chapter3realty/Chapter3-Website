## SOUTH CAROLINA INCOME TAX (tax year 2026)

### 1. "1.99 percent on your first $30,000 of taxable income and 5.21 percent above that" — VERIFIED

- Source: https://dor.sc.gov/news/information-about-h-4216 (SC Department of Revenue)
- Quote: "The tax rate for income less than $30,000 is 1.99%. The tax rate for income from $30,000 and above is 5.21%, minus $966."
- Quote: "H. 4216 is effective beginning with the 2026 tax year, with returns due April 15, 2027."
- As of: page posted Wednesday, Apr 15, 2026.
- Statute: H.4216 became Act 110 (R117), signed by the Governor 2026-03-30, amending
  SC Code 12-6-510.

Note on the wording: SCDOR states the rate as 5.21% of the whole amount **minus $966**.
That is arithmetically identical to the site's phrasing. 5.21% x $30,000 = $1,563;
1.99% x $30,000 = $597; the $966 difference is exactly the flat subtraction. The
published sentence is a correct simplification.

Caveat worth knowing (not a defect): Act 110 also decoupled SC from the federal
standard and itemized deductions and created the South Carolina Income Adjusted
Deduction (SCIAD) — $15,000 single / MFS, $22,500 head of household, $30,000 married
filing jointly. It also capped the state EITC at $200 per filer. "Taxable income"
in 2026 therefore means something different from 2025, so any worked example that
starts from a gross salary needs the SCIAD applied first.

### 2. "No city or county adds its own income tax" in SC — VERIFIED

- Source: SC Code 6-1-310, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t06c001.php
- Quote: "A local governing body may not impose a new tax after December 31, 1996,
  unless specifically authorized by the General Assembly."
- Supporting: 6-1-300(3) defines "local governing body" as the governing authority of a
  county, municipality, or special purpose district; 6-1-300(4) defines a "new tax" as
  any tax the body had not enacted before December 31, 1996.
- As of: HISTORY — 1997 Act No. 138, SECTION 7.

No South Carolina county or municipality levied a local income tax before that cutoff,
and the General Assembly has never authorized one since. Income tax in South Carolina
exists only at state level under Title 12, Chapter 6. The local taxes the General
Assembly has authorized are sales, accommodations, hospitality and property taxes —
the ST-575 chart at item 14 is the full menu of local sales taxes, and none of them is
an income tax. The published sentence is correct.

### 3. "Social Security is not taxed" in SC — VERIFIED

- Source: SC Code 12-6-1120(4), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c006.php
- Quote: "South Carolina gross income is determined without application of Internal
  Revenue Code Sections 78 (Gross-up of Dividends received from Certain Foreign
  Corporations), 86 (Social Security and Tier 1 Railroad Retirement Benefits), and 87
  (Alcohol Fuel Credit)."
- As of: HISTORY — 2024 Act No. 222 (H.4087), SECTION 5, eff July 2, 2024.

IRC Section 86 is the provision that pulls a portion of Social Security benefits into
federal taxable income. South Carolina switches it off, so no part of a Social Security
benefit enters South Carolina gross income, at any income level. Tier 1 Railroad
Retirement is excluded by the same sentence. Act 110 (H.4216) did not amend 12-6-1120,
so this is unchanged for tax year 2026 even though the starting point moved to federal
AGI.

### 4. "Military retirement pay is not taxed at any age" — VERIFIED

- Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c006.php, SECTION 12-6-1171
- Quote: "(A) An individual taxpayer may deduct all military retirement income that is
  included in South Carolina taxable income."
- As of: HISTORY line ends 2022 Act No. 228 (S.1087), SECTION 3.A, eff June 17, 2022.
  No age condition and no dollar cap appear anywhere in the section.
- Act 110 (H.4216) did not amend 12-6-1171, so this is unchanged for tax year 2026.

### 5. Retirement deduction "$3,000 before 65, $10,000 after" — VERIFIED

- Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c006.php, SECTION 12-6-1170(A)(1)
- Quote: "An individual taxpayer who is the original owner of a qualified retirement
  account is allowed an annual deduction from South Carolina taxable income of not
  more than three thousand dollars of retirement income received. Beginning in the
  year in which the taxpayer reaches age sixty-five, the taxpayer may deduct not more
  than ten thousand dollars of retirement income that is included in South Carolina
  taxable income."
- As of: HISTORY ends 2016 Act No. 272 (H.3147), SECTION 1.A, eff June 7, 2016.
  Not amended by Act 110.

### 6. "plus a separate deduction of up to $15,000 at 65 against any income" — WRONG (the word "separate"/"plus")

- Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c006.php, SECTION 12-6-1170(B)
- Quote: "Beginning for the taxable year during which a resident individual taxpayer
  attains the age of sixty-five years, the resident individual taxpayer is allowed a
  deduction from South Carolina taxable income received in an amount not to exceed
  fifteen thousand dollars **reduced by any amount the taxpayer deducts pursuant to
  subsection (A)** not including amounts deducted as a surviving spouse. If married
  taxpayers eligible for this deduction file a joint federal income tax return, then
  the maximum deduction allowed is fifteen thousand dollars in the case when only one
  spouse has attained the age of sixty-five years and thirty thousand dollars when
  both spouses have attained such age."
- As of: HISTORY ends 2016 Act No. 272, eff June 7, 2016. Not amended by Act 110.

Answers to the two questions asked:

- **Is it reduced by the retirement deduction claimed? YES.** The $15,000 is reduced
  dollar for dollar by whatever was deducted under subsection (A). A 66-year-old who
  takes the full $10,000 retirement deduction has only $5,000 of the $15,000 left.
  They are not additive. The only exception in the statute is an amount deducted as a
  surviving spouse, which does not reduce it.
- **Is it $30,000 for a couple where both are 65+? YES**, and $15,000 where only one
  spouse has reached 65.

The published sentence says "plus a separate deduction of up to $15,000." "Plus" and
"separate" both signal additivity, which is the opposite of what the statute says. The
$15,000 figure and the age trigger are right; the relationship between the two
deductions is wrong.

## SC PROPERTY TAX

Primary source used throughout this section: **"2025 South Carolina Property Tax
Rates by County," South Carolina Association of Counties**, PDF at
https://www.sccounties.org/sites/default/files/uploads/resource-files/property-tax-rates-by-county-2025.pdf
Tax year covered: **2025**. Publication: originally December 2025, revised February
2026. Quote from the revision statement: "This revised edition of the report,
published in February 2026, updates the original edition released in December 2025."
This is the most recent edition. Tax year 2026 bills go out in October 2026 and the
2026 report will not exist until December 2026 — but see the "still current for 2026"
note under item 9, both levying bodies have already voted no increase.

### 7. "A main home is taxed on 4 percent of its value and is exempt from the school operating tax" — VERIFIED

- Source: SC Association of Counties 2025 report, "Methodology of Property Tax
  Collection" section.
- Quote (assessment ratio): "Residential real estate (owner-occupied) - 4.0%"
- Quote (school operating exemption): "SCHOOL DISTRICT(S) MILLAGE RATE: Millage rates
  levied for each school district. Residential owner-occupied properties that qualify
  for the 4% assessment rate are exempt from school property taxes, with the exception
  of mils imposed for school bonded indebtedness."
- As of: tax year 2025 report, revised February 2026.
- Underlying law: the 4% ratio is SC Code 12-43-220(c); the school operating exemption
  is the Act 388 exemption at SC Code 12-37-220(B)(47).

The published sentence is correct. One precision point worth knowing: the exemption is
from school **operating** millage only. An owner-occupied home still pays school
**bonded debt** millage — in Horry that is the 10.0-mill Board of Education Debt
Service Fund, which our worked example already includes. The published sentence says
"school operating tax," which is the right word.

### 8. "A second home or a rental is taxed on 6 percent and pays that school tax too" — VERIFIED

- Source: same report.
- Quote: "Commercial and residential non-owner-occupied real property - 6.0%"
- Quote: "Primary residential owner-occupied properties are exempt from school property
  taxes; therefore, school district millages should be omitted from the total millage
  rate to calculate property tax for this property classification." (The corollary —
  every other class does pay it — is how the report's own worked examples run.)
- As of: tax year 2025 report, revised February 2026.

### 9. Horry millage components and the $1,300 / $4,200 worked examples — VERIFIED, every component correct

Our published figures were: unincorporated owner-occupied = county 52.1 + school debt
10.0 + fire 20.2 + fire apparatus 1.5 + solid waste 8.1 = 91.9 mills; second home adds
school operating 109.1 = 201.0 mills.

Checked line by line against the Horry County page of the 2025 report. Every one is
right:

| Our figure | Report line item | Report location column | Report value | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| County 52.1 | "Horry County Total" (County Base Millages) | Countywide | 0.0521 | correct |
| School debt 10.0 | "Board of Education Debt Service Fund" | — | 0.0100 | correct |
| Fire 20.2 | "Fire District" / Fire | "Unincorporated Areas except Murrells Inlet" | 0.0202 | correct |
| Fire apparatus 1.5 | "County Fire Apparatus Replacement" / Fire equipment | "Unincorporated Areas except Murrells Inlet" | 0.0015 | correct |
| Solid waste 8.1 | "Solid Waste Program" / Solid Waste | "Unincorporated Areas" | 0.0081 | correct |
| School operating 109.1 | "School Operation Fund" | — | 0.1091 | correct |

The county base of 0.0521 is itself the sum of six published sub-lines: County
Operations 0.04390, Capital Planning Fund 0.00370, County Recreation 0.00190, Higher
Education Commission 0.0006, Horry/Georgetown Tech 0.0016, Senior Citizens Fund
0.0004. They add to 0.0521 exactly.

Totals therefore check: 52.1 + 10.0 + 20.2 + 1.5 + 8.1 = **91.9 mills**, and
91.9 + 109.1 = **201.0 mills**.

**The dollar figures on a $350,000 house:**

- Main home: $350,000 x 4% = $14,000 assessed. $14,000 x 0.0919 = **$1,286.60**.
  Published as "roughly $1,300." Correct.
- Second home: $350,000 x 6% = $21,000 assessed. $21,000 x 0.2010 = **$4,221.00**.
  Published as "roughly $4,200." Correct.

**Still current for tax year 2026.** Both levying bodies have already set FY2027 (the
tax year 2026 bill) with no millage increase:

- County: https://www.myhorrynews.com/news/county-council-okays-second-reading-of-1-billion-budget/article_80b5575b-76c1-4d69-ba40-23115b833aab.html
  — "There will be no tax millage increase, keeping the level at 52.1 mills."
  Published May 6, 2026.
- Schools: Horry County Schools' 2026-27 budget received final board approval in June
  2026 with no millage increase
  (https://www.wmbfnews.com/2026/06/09/horry-county-schools-approves-new-budget-with-pay-raises/).

Two caveats that are not defects but are worth knowing:

1. **These are taxes only, not the whole bill.** Horry also charges a **$89.00
   stormwater fee** on single-family residential in unincorporated areas (2025 report,
   "Storwater Fees" [sic] / Stormwater / "Single Family Res, unincorp. Areas" /
   $89.00). A real unincorporated bill on that house is closer to $1,376. If the page
   says "property tax," $1,300 is right. If it says "what you pay the county," it is
   light by about $89.
2. **Horry does not levy the 1% Local Option Sales Tax**, so there is no local-option
   property tax credit factor to subtract. Horry is one of the counties with no "Tax
   Credit Factors" block in the report. That is why the arithmetic lands cleanly.

**Independent confirmation of the millage stack from Horry County itself.** The
county's own FAQ (https://www.horrycountysc.gov/tax-payer-services/faqs/) publishes a
worked example that uses exactly the structure our page uses. Verbatim:

```
$200,000   Fair market value of home
x 4%       Assessment ratio (4% if qualified legal resident)
$8,000     Assessed value
x .2162    County millage rate 1
$1,729.60  Taxes
- 944.8    School credit - ($8,000 * .1181) school operations
$784.80    Taxes due
```

with the footnote: "County millage rate: County - 56.2, School - 128.1, Fire - 23.2,
Waste Mngt - 8.7 (Tax Year 2023)".

Two things this proves. First, **fire and waste millage are part of the general stack**
for property in the unincorporated area — the county adds them in before computing.
Second, **the owner-occupied credit is school operations only** (.1181 in 2023),
leaving school debt of 10.0 mills in the bill (128.1 total - 118.1 operations = 10.0).
Our 2025 figures are the same structure with the current rates: 201.0 total, minus
109.1 school operations, equals 91.9. The county FAQ is stale (tax year 2023) but the
method is identical, and it independently confirms both our 10.0-mill school debt line
and the inclusion of fire and solid waste.

## SC VEHICLE TAX

### 10. "South Carolina taxes your car every year" + 6 percent ratio — VERIFIED

- Source (ratio, state level): SC Association of Counties, 2025 Property Tax Rates by
  County, assessment ratio list. Quote: "Motor vehicles - 6.0%"
- Source (ratio, county level): Horry County Auditor,
  https://www.horrycountysc.gov/departments/auditor/ Quote: "Vehicles, RV's,
  Watercraft 6%"
- Source (annual): Horry County Vehicle Tax page,
  https://www.horrycountysc.gov/tax-payer-services/vehicle-tax/ Quote: "In South
  Carolina, taxes on your vehicle are paid a year ahead."
- Underlying law: SC Code 12-37-2645 defines which vehicles fall under the assessment
  ratio in Article X, Section 1(8) of the SC Constitution, which sets a permanent
  6 percent ratio for personal motor vehicles. It borrows the definition of "private
  passenger motor vehicle" from SC Code 56-3-630.
- As of: 2025 tax year report (revised February 2026); the two county pages carry no date.

One precision point for the page, not a defect: the taxable value is the SCDOR
assessment guide value, not what you paid. Horry County states: "The Auditor's Office
is issued Assessment Guides by the South Carolina Department of Revenue. We must use
these guides to assess your property. This is SC state law."

### 11. "$30,000 car, about $360 outside the city, about $460 inside Myrtle Beach, plus a $50 road fee" — VERIFIED

**The $50 road fee is exactly right and is current.**

- Source: SC Association of Counties, 2025 report, Horry County page, "Fees" block.
- Quote (row): "Road Maintenance Fee | Road Maintenance | Per Vehicle | $50.00"
- As of: tax year 2025, report revised February 2026.

**The dollar estimates are sound.** $30,000 x 6% = $1,800 assessed.

- Unincorporated: the full 201.0-mill stack applies (county 52.1 + school operating
  109.1 + school debt 10.0 + fire 20.2 + fire apparatus 1.5 + solid waste 8.1). A
  vehicle is not owner-occupied residential, so it gets no Act 388 school-operating
  exemption. $1,800 x 0.2010 = **$361.80**. Published as "about $360." Correct.
- Inside Myrtle Beach: the fire, fire apparatus and solid waste districts are scoped in
  the report to "Unincorporated Areas," so they drop out, and the city's 83.4 mills
  come in. County 52.1 + school 119.1 + Myrtle Beach 83.4 = 254.6 mills.
  $1,800 x 0.2546 = **$458.28**. Published as "about $460." Correct.

Supporting evidence that fire and solid waste belong in the unincorporated stack: the
Horry County FAQ worked example quoted above adds Fire and Waste Mngt into the total
millage before computing the tax. Caveat for honesty: the Horry County Auditor "does
not give estimates over the phone because there are too many variables," so no official
per-vehicle worked example exists to quote. The arithmetic is ours, built from the
county's own published millage and its own published method.

### 12. "$250 once per car when you first register it here" — VERIFIED

- Source: SC Code 56-3-627(D)(1), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c003.php
- Quote: "If upon purchasing or leasing the item, the owner or lessee first titles or
  registers the item in another state, and subsequently registers the item in this
  State, then the fee equals two hundred fifty dollars."
- As of: HISTORY — 2017 Act No. 40 (H.3516), SECTION 5.A, eff July 1, 2017; 2021 Act
  No. 70 (H.3505), SECTION 1, eff May 17, 2021.
- Cross-check: SCDMV https://dmv.sc.gov/driver-services/moving-to-sc confirms the
  one-time $250 Infrastructure Maintenance Fee for a new resident.

For contrast, if you buy the car in South Carolina the IMF is different: "the fee
equals five percent, not to exceed five hundred dollars, of the gross proceeds of
sales, or sales price" (56-3-627(B)). The page's $250 is the correct figure for a car
driven in from another state, which is the relocator case the page is about.

### 14. "8 percent in the county and 9 percent inside Myrtle Beach" — VERIFIED

The two operative rows, quoted:

```
Unincorporated    Horry    ECI (5261), TT (1026)               8%    9%     0%
Myrtle Beach      Horry    ECI (5261), TT (1026), TD (2615)    9%   10%     0%
```

Unincorporated Horry is **8%**; Myrtle Beach is **9%**. Exactly as published.

The composition, per the form's own abbreviation key ("ECI - Education Capital
Improvement, TT - Transportation Tax, TD - Tourism Development"): 6% state + 1% ECI +
1% TT = 8% countywide; Myrtle Beach adds the 1% Tourism Development fee to reach 9%.
Horry does not levy the 1% Local Option Sales Tax, which is also why there is no
local-option property tax credit in item 9.

Note for anyone reading the chart later: the Myrtle Beach row shows 10% in the
*accommodations* column. Do not confuse that column with the general rate. Every other
Horry municipality (Atlantic Beach, Aynor, Briarcliffe Acres, Conway, Loris, North
Myrtle Beach, Surfside Beach) is 8%, the same as unincorporated.

### 15. "Food you cook at home is not taxed at all here" — VERIFIED, 0 percent including local taxes

- Quote: the "Total Tax Rate Unprepared Foods" figure is **0%** for both the Horry
  "Unincorporated" row and the "Myrtle Beach" row.
- As of: ST-575 Rev. 2/5/26.
- Why it is zero rather than 1 or 2 percent: unprepared food is exempt from the state
  6%, and Horry's two local taxes both exempt it as well. SCDOR,
  https://dor.sc.gov/sales-use-tax-index/local-sales-taxes, states for the Education
  Capital Improvement Tax: "Sales of unprepared food will be exempt from 1% Education
  Capital Improvement Tax. This exemption applies to everyone", and confirms the
  unprepared food exemption applies to the Transportation Tax in Horry and Jasper.
- Sanity check on the reading, per rule 4 (one case that should match, one that should
  not): counties that levy the Local Option Sales Tax show a non-zero figure in the
  same column — "Abbeville | Abbeville | LO (2005) | 7% | 8% | 1% LO" and
  "Arcadia Lakes | Richland | TT (1040), LO (2030) | 8% | 9% | 2% TT, LO". The column
  does report non-zero values where they exist, so Horry's 0% is a real zero and not a
  blank cell being misread.

The published sentence is correct. Groceries are genuinely untaxed in both
unincorporated Horry County and inside Myrtle Beach. Prepared food is taxed heavily,
but the page's sentence is explicitly about food you cook at home.

## STATES WE COMPARE AGAINST

### 16. NY, NJ, PA, OH do NOT levy an annual property/excise tax on personal vehicles — VERIFIED

Checked each state's own registration fee authority. In all four the annual charge is
flat or weight-based, with no component measured by the vehicle's value.

- **New York** — https://dmv.ny.gov/registration/registration-fees-use-taxes-and-supplemental-fees-passenger-vehicles
  Quote: "The Registration Fee Chart shows the 2 year fee for Passenger Vehicles by
  vehicle weight." By weight, not value. The only recurring local add-on is the flat
  MCTD supplemental fee, quoted as "$50 for two years ($25 per year)" in the 12-county
  Metropolitan Commuter Transportation District. Flat, not value-based.
- **New Jersey** — https://www.nj.gov/mvc/vehicles/regfees.htm
  Quote: "The fee is based upon the vehicle's passenger weight class and model year."
  No value-based annual tax.
- **Pennsylvania** — PennDOT Form MV-70S, Schedule of Fees (rev. 4-26),
  https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/penndot/documents/public/dvspubsforms/bmv/bmv-forms/mv-70s.pdf
  Annual passenger car registration is a flat $48.00. Counties may add a flat $5
  per-vehicle county use fee. Neither is value-based.
- **Ohio** — Ohio BMV, https://www.bmv.ohio.gov/doc-fees.aspx and the BMV permissive
  tax FAQ. The local charge is a flat "permissive tax" assessed "in $5.00 increments,"
  capped at "$30.00 per registration per registration year." Flat per registration,
  not measured by value.

One footnote worth having in the back pocket, since it is the only point that could be
argued: Ohio's local charge is statutorily named a "motor vehicle license tax." It is
still a flat dollar amount per registration and not an ad valorem tax, so the published
claim holds. Separately, Ohio's business tangible personal property tax was phased out
2006-2009 under Am. Sub. H.B. 66; that was a business tax and never applied to
personally-owned cars, so it is not relevant to the comparison.

Note on a source I checked and then rejected: the Tax Foundation's "Treatment of
Tangible Personal Property Taxes by State" table labels New York, Pennsylvania and Ohio
"Fully Taxed," which looks like it contradicts our claim. It does not. That table is
about business machinery and equipment, not household vehicles. Using it here would
have produced a false correction.

### 17. NC, VA and MA DO levy an annual value-based vehicle tax — VERIFIED

- **North Carolina** — NCDMV, "Vehicle Property Taxes,"
  https://www.ncdot.gov/dmv/title-registration/taxes/Pages/default.aspx, page date
  6/1/2026. Vehicles are "subject to property taxes" collected by the DMV on behalf of
  counties. NCDOR's Tag & Tax Together page confirms the design: registration renewal
  and vehicle property tax are billed together, and "you cannot renew your registration
  unless both are paid."
- **Virginia** — Va. Code 58.1-3503(A)(3),
  https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title58.1/chapter35/section58.1-3503/
  Quote: "Automobiles, except those described in subdivisions 7, 8, and 9 of this
  subsection and in subdivision A 8 of 58.1-3504, which shall be valued by means of a
  recognized pricing guide". Classified as tangible personal property, valued from a
  pricing guide, levied annually by localities. Last amended 2023 (chs. 148, 149);
  code page effective date shown as 8/20/2026.
- **Massachusetts** — M.G.L. c. 60A, s. 1,
  https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleIX/Chapter60A/Section1
  Quote: "there shall be assessed and levied in each calendar year on every motor
  vehicle and trailer registered under chapter ninety, for the privilege of such
  registration, an excise measured by the value thereof, as hereinafter defined and
  determined, at the rate of twenty-five dollars per thousand of valuation."
  Annual, value-based, $25 per $1,000.

Both halves of the comparison hold up.

Nothing here has to change. These came up while checking and the owner should have them.

1. **Gas price gaps drift.** The Ohio number was presumably right when written. Any
   sentence citing a cents-per-gallon gap needs a re-check on a schedule, or it should
   be written qualitatively instead ("South Carolina is consistently among the cheapest
   states for gas").
2. **The $1,300 figure is property tax only.** Horry also bills an $89.00 stormwater
   fee on single-family residential in unincorporated areas. If the surrounding copy
   implies "everything the county bills you," it is light by about $89.
3. **"Taxable income" changed meaning in 2026.** Act 110 decoupled South Carolina from
   the federal standard and itemized deductions and created the SCIAD ($15,000 single,
   $22,500 head of household, $30,000 married filing jointly). Any worked example that
   starts from a gross salary has to apply the SCIAD before applying the 1.99 / 5.21
   rates.
4. **Millage is confirmed stable for tax year 2026.** The county held at 52.1 mills for
   FY2027 and Horry County Schools approved its 2026-27 budget with no millage
   increase. The property tax figures do not need revisiting until the FY2028 budgets
   land in mid-2027.
5. **Vehicle tax is on the SCDOR guide value, not the purchase price.** "A $30,000 car"
   is shorthand; the county assesses from the state's assessment guide.
6. **The Horry County FAQ page is stale.** It still publishes tax year 2023 millage
   (County 56.2, School 128.1, Fire 23.2, Waste Mngt 8.7). Anyone re-checking these
   numbers later should use the SC Association of Counties annual report, not that FAQ.
