## A. SC Department of Motor Vehicles — new residents

### A1. Deadline to get an SC driver's license

- **Fact:** 45 days after moving to South Carolina. (Not 90. The 90-day figure that circulates on third-party DMV sites is stale; the statute was amended in 2023 to 45 days.)
- **Source URL:** https://dmv.sc.gov/driver-services/moving-to-sc
- **Quote (SCDMV):** "If you have a valid driver's license or identification card from another state and permanently move to South Carolina, you must apply for an SC license or ID within 45 days of moving to the state. You must turn in your out-of-state license and/or ID when you get an SC license or ID."
- **Statute:** SC Code § 56-1-20, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c001.php — "No person shall receive a motor vehicle driver's license unless and until he surrenders to the Department of Motor Vehicles all valid operators' licenses in his possession issued to him by any other state within forty-five days of becoming a resident of this State, unless specifically exempted by law." HISTORY line ends "2023 Act No. 51 (S.549), SECTION 37, eff May 18, 2023."
- **Also on the page:** temporary residents (military, students) are not required to get an SC license: "If you're moving to South Carolina temporarily, like a military member or a student, you are not required to get a driver's license for yourself or your dependents. However, you must have your valid out-of-state license to drive here."
- **Fee:** "An eight-year driver's license is $25." (same page; also on https://dmv.sc.gov/fees: "8-year License $25")
- **Tests:** "you must pass a vision test to get a driver's license and pay all applicable fees." "If your out-of-state license is expired by nine months or more, you must also pass the knowledge and road tests." "If you were issued an out-of-state license and do not have it to surrender, you must provide a driving record from that state as proof of driving."
- **Date:** fetched 2026-08-16; no page date shown. Statute effective 2023-05-18.
- **Confidence:** HIGH.
- **Contradiction flag:** Third-party sites (dmv.org, trafficschoolonline, dmvcheatsheets) still say 90 days. SCDMV and the statute say 45. Use 45.

### A2. Deadline to register / title a vehicle; what "residency" means

- **Fact:** SCDMV says 45 days to transfer vehicle registration. Statute says a resident's vehicle becomes liable "immediately"; a nonresident's vehicle must be registered on establishing domicile or after 150 accumulated days of use in SC. There is no statutory 45-day grace period for vehicles that I could find; the 45 days on the SCDMV page mirrors the licence rule and the 45-day temporary-plate period.
- **Source URL:** https://dmv.sc.gov/driver-services/moving-to-sc (section "Vehicle Registrations")
- **Quote (SCDMV):** "You have 45 days to transfer your vehicle registration to this state. If you own any vehicles, you must register them in this state."
- **Statute on residency, SC Code § 56-3-160,** https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c003.php: "Every foreign vehicle moved into this State the owner of which is a resident of this State immediately becomes liable for registration and license under the provisions of this chapter, and for the purpose of this section, the term 'resident of this State' shall include every person who moves temporarily or permanently into this State for the purpose of engaging in any business, profession or employment."
- **Statute on nonresidents, SC Code § 56-3-150(B):** "The vehicle of a nonresident must be registered and licensed pursuant to this chapter upon the earlier of a nonresident's: (1) subsequent establishment of domicile in this State; or (2) operation of the vehicle in this State for an accumulated period exceeding one hundred fifty days."
- **SCDMV's own definition of residency:** none published on the moving-to-SC pages. Its practical test is that the owner permanently moved here; the vehicle page also lists the "kept in SC six months or longer" route for people who do not hold an SC licence (Form TI-006).
- **Date:** fetched 2026-08-16.
- **Confidence:** HIGH for the SCDMV 45-day statement; HIGH for the statute text. Flag: the two do not say the same thing. Recommend the page say "SCDMV gives you 45 days" and not cite the statute.

### A3. Infrastructure Maintenance Fee (IMF) for a vehicle a new resident brings in

- **Fact:** $250 flat, once per vehicle, when a vehicle first titled/registered in another state is later registered in SC. On an in-state purchase the IMF is 5% of the price, capped at $500. IMF is paid instead of sales tax on the vehicle.
- **Source URL (SCDMV):** https://dmv.sc.gov/vehicle-owners/moving-to-sc
- **Quote:** "In most cases, if you have a regular passenger vehicle that will be titled and registered in SC, you will pay $305 per vehicle (vehicles with a higher gross vehicle weight may have a higher fee) to the SCDMV upon entering the state. This includes the $250 IMF, $15 title fee, and $40 registration/license plate fee but does not include the property tax you will pay to your county of residence." The page also states the IMF is paid once per vehicle and never again.
- **Source URL (fees):** https://dmv.sc.gov/fees — "Infrastructure Maintenance Fee (IMF) 5% of the purchase price, but no more than $500"
- **Statute:** SC Code § 56-3-627, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c003.php — (B) dealer purchase: "the fee equals five percent, not to exceed five hundred dollars"; (C)(1) private-party purchase: "the fee equals five percent, not to exceed five hundred dollars, of the fair market value"; (D)(1): "If upon purchasing or leasing the item, the owner or lessee first titles or registers the item in another state, and subsequently registers the item in this State, then the fee equals two hundred fifty dollars."
- **Date:** fetched 2026-08-16.
- **Confidence:** HIGH.

### A4. Title fee, registration/plate fee, registration term

- **Fact:** Title $15 ($35 expedited, in person only). Registration/plate for most passenger cars $40, biennial (every two years). $36 biennial for owners 65+ or handicapped (statute).
- **Source URL:** https://dmv.sc.gov/fees — "Title (or Expedited Title only available in person) $15 ($35)"; "Most passenger cars $40".
- **Source URL:** https://dmv.sc.gov/vehicle-owners/moving-to-sc — "$15 title fee, and $40 registration/license plate fee"; the page states the registration/plate fee recurs every two years.
- **Statute:** SC Code § 56-3-620 — "For persons under the age of sixty-five years the biennial registration fee for every private passenger motor vehicle, excluding trucks, is forty dollars." "For persons sixty-five years of age or older or persons who are handicapped, as defined in Section 56-3-1950, the biennial registration fee for every private passenger motor vehicle, excluding trucks, is thirty-six dollars."
- **Date:** fetched 2026-08-16.
- **Confidence:** HIGH.

### A5. Documents for a new-resident licence; REAL ID

- **Fact:** Visit a branch in person. Bring (1) proof of identity/US citizenship/date of birth (e.g., certified birth certificate or unexpired US passport), (2) Social Security number (verified electronically with SSA), (3) proof of current physical SC address dated within nine months — one proof for a standard licence, TWO proofs from different organisations for a REAL ID, (4) proof of legal name change if applicable, (5) the out-of-state licence, which is surrendered, (6) name of your SC-licensed auto liability insurer. REAL ID costs the same as a non-REAL ID.
- **Source URL:** https://dmv.sc.gov/driver-services/moving-to-sc — "You must visit an SCDMV branch and bring all of the following information and types of documents that prove your identity: Proof of identity, US citizenship, and date of birth (For example, a government-issued birth certificate or valid US Passport); Social Security number; Proof of current, physical S.C. address dated within nine months of its issue (two proofs for a REAL ID); Proof of legal name change, if applicable." "You must surrender all out-of-state-issued licenses and ID cards to the SCDMV."
- **Source URL (checklist):** SCDMV Form MV-93, United States Citizens' Checklist (Rev. 09/2022), https://dmv.sc.gov/sites/scdmv/files/media/Forms/MV-93.pdf — "Two proofs of address are required if you are interested in getting a REAL ID beginner's permit, driver's license, or identification card. You MUST provide two items from the categories listed below and each item should be from a different organization. Both proofs of address must show the same address." "Only one proof of address is required if you prefer to have a beginner's permit, driver's license, or ID that has 'NOT FOR FEDERAL IDENTIFICATION' printed across the front of it." "Regardless of what choice you make, the cost of a South Carolina REAL ID is the same as a beginner's permit, driver's license, or ID that is not a REAL ID." "You must provide the name of your automobile liability insurance company that is licensed to do business in SC (not required for a beginner's permit or ID)."
- **Accepted proofs of SC address (MV-93 list, useful for a mover):** SC vehicle registration; voter registration card; county tax bill or property tax receipt for vehicle or home; current lease; utility bill or approved application for service at the SC address; bank/credit card statement; mortgage statement or deed; homeowner's insurance policy or bill; letter of employment or pay stub; USPS change-of-address confirmation letter or postmarked mail with forwarding label.
- **Date:** page fetched 2026-08-16; MV-93 rev 09/2022.
- **Confidence:** HIGH.

## B. Golf carts

### B8. SC golf-cart law; 2025 change; local ordinances

- **Fact (state law, current):** The old golf-cart statute § 56-2-105 was REPEALED and replaced by § 56-2-90 by 2025 Act No. 64 (H.3292), effective 2025-05-22. Under § 56-2-90: SCDMV permit decal + registration certificate required to drive on a public road; $5 fee; permit must be replaced every five years or when the owner's address changes; proof of ownership and liability insurance required; driver must be 16+ with a valid driver's licence and carry the registration, proof of insurance and licence. Default rules where no local ordinance exists: daylight only; secondary roads posted 35 mph or less; within four miles of the address on the registration (or four miles of a gated community's entrance); may cross a road posted over 35 at an intersection. Cities/counties may by ordinance set hours, methods and locations (still 35 mph roads only), allow night driving if the cart has working headlights and taillights, and designate cart paths. Passengers under 12 must wear a fastened seat belt on public roads (new in the 2025 act; the older statute had no seat-belt rule).
- **Statute URL:** https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t56c002.php — § 56-2-105: "SECTION 56-2-105. Repealed." History: "Repealed by 2025 Act No. 64, SECTION 2, eff May 22, 2025." § 56-2-90(A): "To operate a vehicle commonly known as a golf cart on a public highway, the owner must obtain a permit decal and registration certificate from the Department of Motor Vehicles. Proof of ownership, proof of liability insurance, and payment of a five-dollar fee must be provided. The permit decal must be replaced every five years, or at the time the owner changes his address, whichever is sooner." (B): "A person operating a golf cart on a public highway must be at least sixteen years of age, hold a valid driver's license, and have in his possession: (1) the registration certificate; (2) proof of liability insurance in conformance with Section 38-77-140; and (3) his driver's license." (C): "A municipality or a county within its unincorporated portions, may: (1) by ordinance stipulate the hours, methods, and locations of golf cart operations, provided that golf carts may be operated only on a highway where the speed limit is thirty-five miles per hour or less; (2) by ordinance permit the operation of golf carts at night, provided that golf carts are equipped with working headlights and taillights, and provided that golf carts may be operated only on a highway where the speed limit is thirty-five miles per hour or less; and (3) on the shoulder of primary highways, secondary highways, streets and roads, designate separate golf cart paths..." (D): "In the absence of an ordinance enacted pursuant to subsection (C), a permitted golf cart may: (1) be operated only during daylight hours; (2) be operated only on a secondary highway where the speed limit is thirty-five miles per hour or less; (3) be operated only within four miles of the address on the registration certificate, or only within four miles of a point of ingress and egress of a gated community if the address is within a gated community; and (4) cross a highway at an intersection where the speed limit is more than thirty-five miles an hour." (E): "Each golf cart passenger that is under the age of twelve years old, when it is being operated on the public streets and highways of this State, must wear a fastened safety belt."
- **SCDMV form GC-2 (Rev. 05/2026)** https://dmv.sc.gov/sites/scdmv/files/2026-06/Fillable%20GC-2%20Golf%20Cart%20Permit,%20Decal,%20and%20Registration%20Application%20Form%205-2026_0.pdf — "First-time Decal and Registration $5 – New or Expired more than 5 years"; "Decal and Registration Renewal $5 – Prior to Expiration"; "Replace Permit Decal $5"; "Replace Permit Registration $1". ## C. Property tax and residency filings

### C9. Horry County 4% legal-residence special assessment

- **Fact:** Apply online through horrycountysc.gov (Online Services → Tax column → "Legal Residence Application"; the application is an OnBase web form). Documents: SC driver's licence or state ID for every owner-occupant AND spouse showing the current address; SC vehicle registration for all vehicles, current address; redacted federal and SC income tax returns (or SSA-1099); trust/LLC papers, divorce papers, and proof that any other home is not receiving an exemption, if applicable. Missing documents can go by mail, fax (843-915-6040), email (asktps@horrycountysc.gov) or in person. Deadline: Horry County's administrative deadline is May 31 so the fall tax bill prints at 4%; the statutory deadline is later — the application must be filed "before the first penalty date for the payment of taxes for the tax year for which the owner first claims eligibility" (i.e., by January 15 of the following year; a late-year filer gets a corrected bill/refund). ### F24. Combined sales tax rate in Horry County

- **Fact:** 8% in Horry County generally. 9% inside the City of Myrtle Beach. Unprepared food is taxed at 0% everywhere in the county, including inside Myrtle Beach.
- **Source URL:** SCDOR Form ST-575, "South Carolina Sales Tax Rate by Municipality," Rev. 2/5/26, https://dor.sc.gov/sites/dor/files/forms/ST575.pdf
- **Quote (rows, verbatim from the chart — columns are Municipality / County / Local Taxes and Codes / Total Tax Rate / Total Tax Rate Accommodations / Total Tax Rate Unprepared Foods):**
  - "Myrtle Beach | Horry | ECI (5261), TT (1026), TD (2615) | 9% | 10% | 0%"
  - "North Myrtle Beach | Horry | ECI (5261), TT (1026) | 8% | 9% | 0%"
  - "Surfside Beach | Horry | ECI (5261), TT (1026) | 8% | 9% | 0%"
  - "Conway | Horry | ECI (5261), TT (1026) | 8% | 9% | 0%"

- **Source URL (cross-check):** SCDOR Form ST-500, "South Carolina Local Tax Designation by County Effective May 1, 2026," Rev. 3/9/2026, https://dor.sc.gov/sites/dor/files/forms/ST500.pdf — county table lists "Horry 8%" and "Horry-Myrtle Beach 9%"; the map shows Horry as "TT, ECI" and Myrtle Beach as "ECI, TD, TT."
- **Composition:** 6% state, plus 1% Education Capital Improvement, plus 1% Transportation. Inside Myrtle Beach add the 1% Tourism Development tax.
- **Source URL (the two county taxes):** https://dor.sc.gov/tax/education-capital-improvement — "Once approved, this 1% tax is used for schools"; Horry listed effective March 1, 2024; "Sales of unprepared food will be exempt from 1% Education Capital Improvement Tax. And https://dor.sc.gov/tax/transportation — "The Transportation Tax can be up to 1% and is imposed specifically for transportation needs such as roads and bridges in the counties that impose the tax"; Horry and Jasper effective May 1, 2025; "Sales of unprepared food are exempt from Horry and Jasper Counties' 1% Transportation Tax."
- **Unprepared groceries, one line:** unprepared food is not taxed locally in Horry County. The state exempts it from the 6%, and both of Horry's local taxes exempt it too, so the rate on groceries is 0% countywide and 0% in Myrtle Beach.
