## 1. The 90-day thresholds

### State statute

Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c036.php, SECTION 12-36-920 "Tax on accommodations
for transients; reporting". Downloaded with curl, read 2026-09-06.

- The state rate in the statute is one figure, 7%. Quote: "A sales tax equal to seven percent
  is imposed on the gross proceeds derived from the rental or charges for any rooms, campground
  spaces, lodgings, or sleeping accommodations furnished to transients".
- The 90-day line. Quote: "The gross proceeds derived from the lease or rental of sleeping
  accommodations supplied to the same person for a period of ninety continuous days are not
  considered proceeds from transients."
- Owner-occupied small property exclusion. Quote: the tax does not apply "where the facilities
  consist of less than six sleeping rooms, contained on the same premises, which is used as the
  individual's place of abode".

### SC Department of Revenue

Source: https://dor.sc.gov/tax/accommodations, page title "Accommodations | SCDOR". No date on
the page. Read 2026-09-06.

- Quote: "Accommodations Tax applies to sleeping accommodations of any kind that are rented to
  guests for less than 90 consecutive days."
- Quote: "Accommodations Tax does not apply to: The lease or rental of accommodations supplied to
  the same person for a period of 90 continuous days".
- The 7% in the statute is shown as two lines. Quote: "Accommodations are subject to: 5% Sales
  Tax 2% Accommodations Tax Any local Sales & Use Tax that is collected on behalf of counties by
  the SCDOR."
- Quote: "If you are directly booking short-term rentals, you must have a Retail License to file
  and pay Accommodations Tax."
- The six-bedroom exclusion has a platform condition. Quote: "the owner or operator does not use
  a rental agency or online travel company to rent rooms."

### Horry County hospitality fee

Source: https://www.horrycountysc.gov/departments/treasurer/hospitality-fee/, page title
"Hospitality Fee - Horry County SC.Gov". Read 2026-09-06.

- Quote: "Long-term accommodation rentals 90 consecutive days at the same location to the same
  patron does not require collection and remittance of the Hospitality Fee."
- Rate outside city limits. Quote: "3% of gross proceeds from Rental of Transient
  Accommodations".
- Rate inside city limits. Quote: "1.5% of gross proceeds from the Sale of Food/Beverages,
  Rental of Transient Accommodation and paid Admissions".
- Quote: "All businesses must register with the S.C. Department of Revenue. In addition,
  businesses located inside the city limits must register with their local municipality."

Second county source: https://www.horrycountysc.gov/media/hwtb4agg/hospitality-letter-to-customer.pdf
(county letter to accommodation owners, PDF). Downloaded with curl, read 2026-09-06.

- Quote: "If your property or condo unit is being rented to the same patron, at the same unit
  location, for 90 consecutive days (or longer), then that specific patron (only) is exempt from
  the hospitality fee."
- Quote: "If all your renters are long term (90 consecutive days or longer), you are not subject
  to the collection of the county hospitality fee."

### City of Myrtle Beach

Source: https://www.cityofmyrtlebeach.com/careers/business/business_faq.php, page title
"Business License FAQs". Read 2026-09-06.

- Quote: "Any stay of ninety (90) or more consecutive days in the same place is not considered a
  short-term accommodation."
- The same answer defines short-term first. Quote: "Accommodations provided on a daily, weekly,
  or monthly basis as defined in S.C. Code 1976 & 12-36-920."
- A licence is needed for every rental, not only short-term. Quote: "The ordinance requires a
  business license for ALL rental properties which include short-term, long-term, residential and
  commercial."
- City local accommodations tax. Quote: "It is a one-half percent (.5%) tax on short-term or
  transient accommodations".

## 2. Zoning: what stay length makes a rental legal where

### City of Myrtle Beach

Source: Code of Ordinances, Appendix A Zoning, Section 203 "Definitions". Browser URL:

  with sleeping accommodations".
- Quote (same definition, the stay test): "which is exchanged, furnished to another, used,
  rented, leased or sub-leased for any time period less than ninety (90) continuous days".
- Quote: "A visitor accommodation shall be considered a commercial use."

Source: same code, Article 18, Section 1808 "Short-Term Rental Conversion (STRC) Overlay Zone",
history line "( Ord. No. 2024-69 , 12-10-24)". Browser URL:

- Quote: "No building or structure consisting of more than two (2) units, including each unit

  commonly known as short term rental, may be used for rental or lease for ninety (90) continuous
  days or more."
- Quote: "It shall be a violation of this prohibition to enter into successive leases or rental
  agreements, each for less than ninety (90) days, for the purpose of allowing an occupant to
  remain for more than ninety (90) continuous days."

  business license for long term rental at the time of adoption may continue to use the licensed

Source: https://www.cityofmyrtlebeach.com/news_detail_T6_R1468.php, city news item headed "Ever

- Quote: ""Short-term" means any rental of less than 90 days."
- Quote: "Any zoning district that begins with R (for residential) does not allow short-term
  rentals, with the exception of the RMV zone (Residential Multifamily Visitor)."
- Quote: "City-wide, fewer than 30 houses in traditional residential zones are grandfathered for

- Reading of the three sources together (mine, not a quote): inside an R district other than
  RMV, a furnished rental is a permitted residential use only when each stay is 90 continuous
  days or more; in the STRC overlay, a short-term building of more than two units may not do
  that at all.

## 3. When a furnished stay is a tenancy under the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act

Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c040.php, SECTION 27-40-120 "Exclusions from
application of chapter" and SECTION 27-40-210 "General definitions". Downloaded with curl, read
2026-09-06.

- The exclusion that matters. Quote: "(4) transient occupancy in a hotel, motel, or other
  accommodations subject to the sales tax on accommodations as provided by SECTION 12-36-920;"
- Employer housing exclusion. Quote: "(5) occupancy by an employee of a landlord whose right to
  occupancy is conditional upon employment in and about the premises;"
- Institutional exclusion. Quote: "(1) residence at an institution, public or private, if
  incidental to detention or the provision of medical, geriatric, educational, counseling,
  religious, or similar service;"
- Timeshare exclusion. Quote: "(8) occupancy under a rental agreement in a premises regulated by
  the provisions of Chapter 32 of Title 27 of the 1976 Code (Vacation Time Sharing Plan Act)."
- Dwelling unit. Quote: ""dwelling unit" means a structure or the part of a structure that is
  used as a home, residence, or sleeping place by one person who maintains a household or by two
  or more persons who maintain a common household".
- Reading of the two statutes together (mine, not a quote): exclusion (4) hangs on the
  accommodations tax, and 12-36-920 says 90 continuous days to the same person are not transient
  proceeds. A 90-day-plus stay by one person is therefore outside exclusion (4). Neither statute
  says whether a 30-to-89-day furnished stay is "transient occupancy". No SC page I opened
  answers that. Treat it as a question for a lawyer, not for the page.

## 9. Insurance: landlord policy versus homeowners policy

No primary page I opened uses the term "DP-3". The facts below are what the primary pages say.

Insurance, page title "Understanding the Types of Homeowner Insurance Policies for Your
Dwelling". Read 2026-09-06.

- HO-3. Quote: "It offers broad coverage for your dwelling and personal belongings against all
  perils unless specifically excluded in the policy."
- HO-4. Quote: "As a renter, you are not responsible for insuring the physical structure of the
  dwelling, which is the landlord's responsibility."
- HO-6. Quote: "Since the condominium association typically insures the building's structure,
  HO-6 policies focus on interior damage and personal liability within the unit."
- The page lists HO-1 to HO-6 only. It says nothing about landlord, dwelling fire or DP forms.
  The SC DOI homeowners FAQ (https://online.doi.sc.gov/Eng/Public/faqs/hofaq.aspx, read
