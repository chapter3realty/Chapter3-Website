## 1. Financing eligibility

### 1(a) The conventional rule (Fannie Mae Selling Guide)

**1.1** Fannie Mae buys loans to non-citizens only when the borrower is a
lawful permanent or non-permanent resident *of the United States*. The
section does not mention borrowers who live outside the country.
- Source: Selling Guide B2-2-02, Non-U.S. Citizen Borrower Eligibility Requirements (section date 07/28/2015), https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-2-02/non-us-citizen-borrower-eligibility-requirements
- Quote (complete section): "Fannie Mae purchases and securitizes mortgages made to non–U.S. citizens who are lawful permanent or non-permanent residents of the United States under the same terms that are available to U.S. citizens. Fannie Mae does not specify the precise documentation the lender must obtain to verify that a non–U.S. citizen borrower is legally present in the United States. The lender must make a determination of the non–U.S. citizen's status based on the circumstances of the individual case, using documentation it deems appropriate. By delivering the mortgage to Fannie Mae, the lender represents and warrants that the non–U.S. citizen borrower is legally present in this country."
- read 2026-09-07
- Researcher reading, not a quote: a Canadian who lives in Canada is not a "resident of the United States", so the sentence does not reach them. The guide never says "non-resident aliens are ineligible" in those words.

**1.2** Every conventional borrower needs an SSN or an ITIN.
- Source: Selling Guide B2-2-01, General Borrower Eligibility Requirements (section date 09/03/2025), https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-2-01/general-borrower-eligibility-requirements
- Quote: "Fannie Mae requires that each borrower have a valid Social Security number or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN)"
- read 2026-09-07

**1.3** The EAD point. Fannie Mae's own non-citizen fact sheet could not be
opened (403 on singlefamily.fanniemae.com). Two lender documents reproduce
it. SECONDARY sources; use them only to support "an EAD is one accepted
proof of status", never as a quote from Fannie.
- Source A: Union Home Mortgage Corp., "FNMA Non-Citizen Borrower Eligibility" (Date Published 05/05/2026), https://www.uhmgo.com/contentimages/706405964/FNMA_NonUS_Citizen_Borrower_Fact_Sheet_for_Wholesale.pdf
- Quote A: "Per the FNMA Selling Guide, Fannie Mae considers a borrower legally present in the United States if: They have a Social Security Number (SSN); and They have current, verified status, which may be documented by a valid employment authorization document (EAD), or other documentation showing immigration status is current (e.g., Green Card, work visa, etc.)."
- Source B: Essent Guaranty job aid EGI-8727.001 (11/23), https://www.essent.us/sites/default/files/2023-12/job-aid-non-us-citizen-borrower-eligibility-documentation.pdf
- Quote B: "A non-US citizen who is lawfully residing in the U.S. as a permanent or non-permanent resident is eligible for a mortgage on the same terms as a U.S. Citizen (Ref. FNMA B2-2-02/FHLMC 5103.2)"; the table for a non-permanent resident reads "SS# or ITIN AND any of the following: EAD, Work Visa, Passport with Entry Stamps, Passport with I-551 Stamps" with the note "*valid unexpired".
- read 2026-09-07 (both PDFs, extracted text)

**1.4** FHA is closed to non-permanent residents since 25 May 2025. An EAD
holder can still be a conventional borrower (1.1, 1.3) but not an FHA one.
- Source: HUD press release No. 25-048 (March 26, 2025), https://www.hud.gov/news/hud-no-25-048
- Quote: "Non-permanent residents will no longer be eligible for Federal Housing Administration (FHA) mortgages"
- Effective date on the page: May 25, 2025.
- read 2026-09-07

NOT VERIFIED: Freddie Mac Guide 5103.2 (page is script-only; not opened).
Fannie Mae's own fact sheet (403). The exact Fannie wording on EADs is known

## 2. The tax number: ITIN (Form W-7)

**2.1** What an ITIN is and who needs one.
- Source: IRS, Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (page last reviewed or updated October 28, 2025), https://www.irs.gov/individuals/individual-taxpayer-identification-number
- Quote: "An ITIN is a 9-digit number the IRS issues if you need a U.S. taxpayer identification number for federal tax purposes, but you aren't eligible for a Social Security number (SSN)."
- Quote (who may need one): "Nonresident alien claiming a tax treaty benefit"; "Nonresident alien filing a U.S. federal tax return."
- Quote (how): "Apply for an ITIN by mail or in person."; "Apply on or before your tax return due date. Include the tax return in your ITIN application package. Don't file it separately."
- Quote (time): "Allow 7 weeks for us to notify you about your ITIN application status. It can take 9-11 weeks if it's tax season (January 15 to April 30) or if you applied from overseas."
- read 2026-09-07

**2.2** Form W-7 instructions: who must apply, and the two exceptions that let a landlord or a seller apply without attaching a return.
- Source: Instructions for Form W-7 (Rev. December 2024), https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7
- Quote (who): "Nonresident alien individual not eligible for an SSN who is required to file a U.S. federal tax return or who is filing a U.S. federal tax return to claim a refund"
- Quote (Exception 1(d), rental income): applies to individuals receiving "distributions during the current tax year of income such as pensions, annuities, rental income, royalties, dividends, etc." who must give an ITIN to their withholding agent.
- Exception 4 (FIRPTA): applies to dispositions of U.S. real property by foreign persons; the required documents include "Form 8288-B, Application for Withholding Certificate for Dispositions by Foreign Persons of U.S. Real Property Interests" and a copy of the purchase contract.
- Quote (time): "Allow 7 weeks for the IRS to notify you of your ITIN application status (9 to 11 weeks if you submit the application during peak processing periods)."
- Quote (expiry): "If your ITIN wasn't included on at least one U.S. federal tax return for the last 3 consecutive tax years, it expires on December 31 of the third consecutive tax year."
- read 2026-09-07

**2.3** When a Canadian buyer needs it: to file Form 1040-NR on rental income (3.7), to give a tenant or manager a valid Form W-8ECI (3.6), and for a FIRPTA withholding certificate, which the IRS will not act on without TINs for every party (4.6). SC's I-290 also asks for the seller's SSN or ITIN (8.10).

## 3. Rental income tax

**3.1** Two categories of income for a nonresident alien; the second is taxed flat at 30% on gross.
- Source: IRS Publication 519 (2025), U.S. Tax Guide for Aliens, "For use in preparing 2025 Returns", chapter 4, https://www.irs.gov/publications/p519
- Quote: "Income that is not effectively connected is taxed at a flat 30% (or lower treaty) rate."
- Quote: "The 30% (or lower treaty) rate applies to the gross amount of U.S. source fixed, determinable, annual, or periodical (FDAP) gains, profits, or income."
- Quote: "Items specifically included as fixed or determinable income are interest (other than OID), dividends, dividend equivalent payments (defined in chapter 2), rents, premiums, annuities, salaries, wages, and other compensation."
- read 2026-09-07 (downloaded HTML, text extracted)

**3.2** The election to treat rent as effectively connected and be taxed on net income.
- Source: Publication 519 (2025), chapter 4, "Income From Real Property", same URL
- Quote: "If you have income from real property located in the United States that you own or have an interest in and hold for the production of income, you can choose to treat all income from that property as income effectively connected with a trade or business in the United States. The choice applies to all income from real property located in the United States and held for the production of income and to all income from any interest in such property. This includes income from rents, royalties from mines, oil or gas wells, or other natural resources."
- Quote: "If you make the choice, you can claim deductions attributable to the real property income and only your net income from real property is taxed."
- Quote (the IRS example): "You are a nonresident alien and are not engaged in a U.S. trade or business. You own a single-family house in the United States that you rent out. Your rental income for the year is $10,000. This is your only U.S. source income."
- read 2026-09-07

**3.3** IRS FDAP page: no deductions against gross-basis income; rents are on the list.
- Source: IRS, Fixed, Determinable, Annual, Periodical (FDAP) income (page last reviewed or updated 23-Jul-2026), https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/fixed-determinable-annual-periodical-fdap-income
- Quote: "Tax at a 30% (or lower treaty) rate applies to FDAP income or gains from U.S. sources"
- Quote: "Deductions and netting are not allowed against FDAP income."
- Quote (list item): "Real property income, such as rents, other than gains from the sale of real property"
- read 2026-09-07

**3.4** Who withholds the 30%: whoever pays or handles the rent, unless the owner hands them Form W-8ECI.
- Source: IRS Publication 515 (For use in 2026), "Real Property Income and Natural Resources Royalties (Income Code 14)", https://www.irs.gov/publications/p515
- Quote: "You must withhold tax under chapter 3 on income (such as rents and royalties) from real property located in the United States and held for the production of income, unless the foreign payee elects to treat this income as effectively connected with a U.S. trade or business. If the foreign payee chooses to treat this income as effectively connected, the payee must give you Form W-8ECI (discussed earlier)."
- Quote (who is a withholding agent): "You are a withholding agent if you are a U.S. or foreign person, in whatever capacity acting, that has control, receipt, custody, disposal, or payment of an amount subject to chapter 3 withholding."
- read 2026-09-07 (downloaded HTML, text extracted)
- Researcher note: a property manager collecting rent for a Canadian owner meets that definition. Pub 515 does not use the words "property manager". NOT VERIFIED as a quote.

**3.5** The withholding agent is personally liable.
- Source: IRS, Withholding agent (page last reviewed or updated 21-Mar-2026), https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/withholding-agent
- Quote: "You are a withholding agent if you are a U.S. or foreign person that has control, receipt, custody, disposal, or payment of any item of income of a foreign person that is subject to withholding."
- Quote: "As a withholding agent, you are personally liable for any tax required to be withheld. This liability is independent of the tax liability of the foreign person to whom the payment is made."
- read 2026-09-07

**3.6** Form W-8ECI: the paper that stops the 30% withholding; it needs a U.S. TIN.
- Source: Instructions for Form W-8ECI (Rev. October 2021), https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw8eci
- Quote: "Foreign persons are generally subject to U.S. tax at a 30% rate on income they receive from U.S. sources. However, no withholding under section 1441 or 1442 is required on income that is, or is deemed to be, effectively connected with the conduct of a trade or business in the United States."
- Quote: "However, if you made or will make an election under section 871(d) or 882(d), provide Form W-8ECI."
- Quote: "You are required to provide a TIN for this form to be valid."
- read 2026-09-07

**3.7** The return and its due date.
- Source: IRS, About Form 1040-NR (page last reviewed or updated 07-May-2026; current revision 2025), https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1040-nr
- Quote (who files): "Were a nonresident alien engaged in a trade or business in the United States."
- Source: Publication 519 (2025), chapter 7, same URL as 3.1
- Quote: "If you are not an employee who receives wages subject to U.S. income tax withholding, you must file by the 15th day of the 6th month after your tax year ends. For the 2025 calendar year, file your return by June 15, 2026."
- read 2026-09-07

**3.8** The treaty. The U.S.-Canada convention lets the country where the property sits tax the rent and the sale gain, and Canada credits the U.S. tax.
- Source: IRS, "Canada Income Tax Treaty and Protocols - 1980" (listed on https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/canada-tax-treaty-documents, page last reviewed or updated 08-Aug-2026), PDF https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/canada.pdf. File header: "Convention Signed at Washington, D.C. on September 26,1980; Protocol Signed at Ottawa June 14, 1983; Protocol Signed at Washington, D.C., March 28,1984; ... Entered into Force August 16, 1984." The same file carries the 1995 protocol.
- Quote, Article VI(1): "Income derived by a resident of a Contracting State from real property (including from agriculture or forestry) situated in the other Contracting State may be taxed in that other State."
- Quote, Article VI(3): "The provisions of paragraph 1 shall apply to income derived from the direct use, letting or use in any other form of real property and to income from the alienation of such property."
- Quote, Article XIII(1): "Gains derived by a resident of a Contracting State from the alienation of real property situated in the other Contracting State may be taxed in that other State."
- Quote, Article XXIV(2)(a): "income tax paid or accrued to the United States on profits, income or gains arising in the United States shall be deducted from any Canadian tax payable in respect of such profits, income or gains"
- read 2026-09-07 (PDF downloaded, text extracted)
- One-sentence page version: the treaty does not exempt a Canadian's Myrtle Beach rent from U.S. tax; it lets the U.S. tax it and makes Canada credit the U.S. tax paid.

## 4. Selling: FIRPTA withholding

**4.1** The rate and who withholds.
- Source: IRS, FIRPTA withholding (page last reviewed or updated 21-Jul-2026), https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/firpta-withholding
- Quote: "The rate of withholding generally is 15% (10% for dispositions before Feb. 17, 2016)."
- Quote: "In most cases, the buyer (transferee) is the withholding agent."
- Quote (residence exception, fragment): "the amount realized is $300,000 or less."
- Form named on the page: Form 8288-B, "Application for Withholding Certificate for Dispositions by Foreign Persons of U.S. Real Property Interests"; Forms 8288 and 8288-A report and remit the tax.
- read 2026-09-07

**4.2** The statute: 15% of the amount realized; the $300,000 residence exception; the 10% rate for residences up to $1,000,000.
- Source: 26 U.S.C. 1445 via Cornell LII, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1445
- Quote, 1445(a): "the transferee shall be required to deduct and withhold a tax equal to 15 percent of the amount realized on the disposition."
- Quote, 1445(b)(5): "(A) the property is acquired by the transferee for use by him as a residence, and (B) the amount realized for the property does not exceed $300,000."
- Quote, 1445(c)(4): "subsection (a) shall be applied by substituting '10 percent' for '15 percent'" where the property is acquired for use as a residence and the amount realized does not exceed $1,000,000 and the $300,000 exemption does not apply.
- read 2026-09-07
- Note: the IRS FIRPTA and exceptions pages opened today do not restate the 10% tier; cite the statute for it.

**4.3** The residence exception has an occupancy condition on the buyer.
- Source: IRS, Exceptions from FIRPTA withholding (page last reviewed or updated July 15, 2026), https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/exceptions-from-firpta-withholding
- Quote: "The buyer (transferee) acquires the property for use as a residence and the amount realized (sales price) is not more than $300,000."
- Quote: "The transferee or a member of the transferee's family must have definite plans to reside at the property for at least 50% of the number of days the property is used by any person during each of the first two 12-month periods following the date of transfer."
- Quote: "The transferee receives a withholding certificate from the Internal Revenue Service that excuses withholding."
- read 2026-09-07
- Researcher note: a buyer who will rent the unit out cannot give this, so a Canadian selling a rental to another investor should expect the full 15% on the price, not on the gain.

**4.4** to **4.6** The withholding certificate (Form 8288-B).
- Source: IRS, Withholding certificates (page last reviewed or updated 23-Jul-2026), https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/withholding-certificates
- Quote: "The amount that must be withheld from the disposition of a United States (U.S.) real property interest can be adjusted pursuant to a withholding certificate issued by the Internal Revenue Service (IRS)."
- Quote: "The IRS will generally act on these requests within 90 days after receipt of a complete application including the Taxpayer Identification Numbers (TIN's) of all the parties to the transaction."
- Quote (a ground): "The amount that must be withheld would be more than the transferor's maximum tax liability"
- read 2026-09-07
- NOT VERIFIED as a quote: that the buyer may hold the withheld amount in escrow while an application filed before closing is pending. The page opened today does not say it in the text returned.

**4.7** The closing attorney's place in it. Federal law names the buyer (4.1). SC law says the closing attorney is not liable for the state withholding but must remit what it actually withheld (8.8).

**4.8** Pub 515 confirms the 15% on the amount realized: "must deduct and withhold 15% of the amount realized on the disposition." (same URL as 3.4, read 2026-09-07)

NOT VERIFIED: a verbatim IRS sentence that FIRPTA withholding is credited against the tax shown on the 1040-NR. Not captured from the pages opened today.

## 5. Estate tax exposure

**5.1** The $60,000 threshold, the form, and real estate as a U.S. asset.
- Source: IRS, Some nonresidents with U.S. assets must file estate tax returns (page last reviewed or updated 27-Jun-2026), https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/some-nonresidents-with-us-assets-must-file-estate-tax-returns
- Quote (threshold, fragment): "if the fair market value at death of the decedent's U.S.-situated assets exceeds $60,000"
- Form: "Form 706-NA, United States Estate (and Generation-Skipping) Tax Return, Estate of a nonresident not a citizen of the United States"
- Quote (U.S.-situated assets): "Real estate located in the U.S., Tangible personal property (excluding some art), and Stock of corporations organized in or under U.S. law, even if the nonresident held the certificates abroad"
- Quote (treaties): "Estate tax treaties between the U.S. and other countries often provide more favorable tax treatment to nonresidents by limiting the type of asset considered situated in the U.S. and subject to U.S. estate taxation."
- read 2026-09-07

**5.2** The $13,000 unified credit and the treaty-country list.
- Source: Instructions for Form 706-NA (Rev. September 2025), https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i706na
- Quote: "The executor must file Form 706-NA if the date of death value of the decedent's U.S.-situated assets, together with the gift tax specific exemption and the amount of adjusted taxable gifts, exceeds the filing threshold of $60,000."
- Quote: "In general, the maximum unified credit is $13,000."
- The instructions name Canada among the treaty countries whose residents may get a different unified credit under section 2102(b)(3)(A).
- Quote: "Real estate and tangible personal property are located in the United States if they are physically located there."
- Quote: "File Form 706-NA within 9 months after the date of death unless an extension of time to file was granted."
- read 2026-09-07

**5.3** The treaty's pro-rated unified credit for a Canadian resident's estate.
- Source: 1995 Protocol, Article 19, adding Article XXIX B to the convention; in the IRS consolidated PDF https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/canada.pdf
- Quote, Article XXIX B(2): "In determining the estate tax imposed by the United States, the estate of an individual (other than a citizen of the United States) who was a resident of Canada at the time of the individual's death shall be allowed a unified credit equal to the greater of (a) The amount that bears the same ratio to the credit allowed under the law of the United States to the estate of a citizen of the United States as the value of the part of the individual's gross estate that at the time of the individual's death is situated in the United States bears to the value of the individual's entire gross estate wherever situated; and (b) The unified credit allowed to the estate of a nonresident not a citizen of the United States under the law of the United States."
- Quote, same paragraph: "A credit otherwise allowable under subparagraph (a) shall be allowed only if all information necessary for the verification and computation of the credit is provided."
- Treasury technical explanation in the same file: "First, the Protocol provides a limited U.S. estate tax waiver for small estates of Canadian resident decedents. Second, it provides a pro rata unified credit by the United States for estates of Canadian resident decedents."
- read 2026-09-07 (PDF downloaded, text extracted)

**5.4** The U.S. citizen exclusion the credit is pro-rated from.
- Source: IRS, What's new - Estate and gift tax (page last reviewed or updated 23-Jul-2026), https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/whats-new-estate-and-gift-tax
- Basic exclusion amount for 2026: "$15,000,000"; for 2025: "$13,990,000".
- read 2026-09-07
