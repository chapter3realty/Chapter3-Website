# Selling a South Carolina rental with a tenant or bookings in place: verified facts

Every fact below was read at the primary source on 2026-09-06, with WebFetch or by downloading
with curl and reading the text. SC Code chapters came from scstatehouse.gov; DOR and LLR PDFs were
converted with pypdf. Quotes are verbatim. Re-open every source before reuse (non-negotiable 8).
Facts only; no page copy. What could not be opened, or is not said in words, is under "Not
verified". Item 10 (commission) was skipped as instructed. No conclusions about a named building.

## 1. The lease survives the sale

Source: SC Code Title 27, Chapter 40 (Residential Landlord and Tenant Act),
https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c040.php. Downloaded with curl, read 2026-09-06.

- No section says in words "the lease survives the sale". The chapter assumes it: 27-40-450(a)
  treats a sold unit as still "subject to a rental agreement" and only moves liability.
- 27-40-450(a) "Limitation of liability". Quote: "a landlord who conveys the premises that include
  a dwelling unit subject to a rental agreement in a good faith sale to a bona fide purchaser is
  relieved of liability under the rental agreement and this chapter". The relief runs "as to
  events occurring after written notice to the tenant of the conveyance." The sentence opens
  "Unless otherwise agreed". HISTORY: "1986 Act No. 336, SECTION 1".
- 27-40-410(e). Quote: "Subject to the provisions of Section 27-40-450, the holder of the
  landlord's interest in the premises at the time of the termination of the tenancy is bound by
  this section." (27-40-410 is the security deposit section; see item 6.)
- 27-40-210(6), the buyer becomes the landlord by definition. Quote: "'landlord' means the owner,
  lessor, or sublessor of the premises, and it also means a manager of the premises who fails to
  disclose as required by SECTION 27-40-420".
- 27-40-420(b), the owner/agent disclosure carries to the buyer. Quote: "The information required
  to be furnished by this section must be kept current and this section extends to and is
  enforceable against any successor landlord, owner, or manager."
- 27-40-340 is the only section naming an assignment or conveyance. Quote: "A rental agreement,
  assignment, conveyance, trust deed, mortgage, or security instrument may not permit the receipt
  of rent absent the obligation to comply with SECTION 27-40-440(a)." (440(a): maintenance duty.)

## 2. Showing a tenant-occupied home

Source: SC Code 27-40-530 "Access", same URL as item 1. Read 2026-09-06. HISTORY: "1986 Act No.
336, SECTION 1; 1995 Act No. 112, SECTION 4".

- (a). Quote: "A tenant shall not unreasonably withhold consent to the landlord to enter into the
  dwelling unit" for listed purposes ending "or exhibit the dwelling unit to prospective or actual
  purchasers, mortgagees, tenants, workmen, or contractors."
- (c). Quote: "the landlord shall give the tenant at least twenty-four hours notice of his intent
  to enter and may enter only at reasonable times." Same subsection: "A landlord shall not abuse
  the right of access or use it to harass the tenant."
- (b) allows entry without consent only for an emergency, scheduled periodic services the lease
  provides for, and services the tenant asked for. Showings are not in (b), so (c) governs them.
- (e). Quote: "A tenant shall not change locks on the dwelling unit without the permission of the
  landlord." The statute does not say the (c) notice must be written.

## 3. Nonresident seller withholding

### Statute

Source: SC Code 12-8-580, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c008.php. Downloaded with curl,
read 2026-09-06.

- The rate is no longer 7%. "Effect of Amendment" note, quote: "2024 Act No. 215, SECTION 4, in
  (A)(1), in (a) and (b), substituted 'a percentage equal to the maximum individual tax rate' for
  'seven percent'." HISTORY: "2024 Act No. 215 (S.577), SECTION 4, eff July 2, 2024".
- (A)(1)(a), with a gain affidavit. Quote: "a percentage equal to the maximum individual tax rate
  of the gain recognized on the sale by a nonresident individual, partnership, trust, or estate
  and five percent for a nonresident corporation". Condition: "if the seller provides the buyer
  with an affidavit, described in subsection (E), stating the amount of gain".
- (A)(1)(b), without an affidavit. Quote: "a percentage equal to the maximum individual tax rate
  of the amount realized on the sale for a nonresident individual, partnership, trust, or estate".
- (A)(1)(c), the cap. Quote: "the entire net proceeds payable to the nonresident seller, if the
  amount required to be withheld in subitem (1) or (2) exceeds the net proceeds payable to the
  seller."
- (B)(2)(a). Quote: "A sale does not include tax exempt or tax deferred transactions, other than
  installment sales." (B)(2)(b), principal residence. Quote: "A sale does not include a
  transaction to the extent the gain on the sale of a principal residence is excluded in
  accordance with Internal Revenue Code Section 121." Next sentence: "Any gain in excess of this
  permitted exclusion is subject to the provisions of this section."
- (C)(1)(a), who is a nonresident. Quote: "an individual whose permanent home is outside of this
  State on the date of the sale".
- (D)(1). Quote: "The buyer shall remit the amount withheld to the department with the
  appropriate form on or before the fifteenth day of the month following the month in which the
  sale takes place." (D)(2). Quote: "The buyer is liable for the collection and payment of an
  amount due pursuant to this section." A real estate agent or closing attorney "is not liable".
- (E), reliance. Quote: "The buyer may rely on an affidavit provided by the seller if the buyer
  does not know the affidavit is false and the seller, under penalties of perjury, states the
  following:" name, address, tax number, date of sale, description of the property.

## 4. Federal: depreciation recapture and 1031 periods

Source: IRS Topic no. 409 "Capital gains and losses", https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409, "Page
Last Reviewed or Updated: 25-Feb-2026". Read 2026-09-06. Quote: "The portion of any unrecaptured
section 1250 gain from selling section 1250 real property is taxed at a maximum 25% rate."

Source: IRS Publication 544 (2025) "Sales and Other Dispositions of Assets", "For use in preparing
2025 Returns", https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544. Downloaded with curl, read 2026-09-06.

- Quote: "Unrecaptured section 1250 gain. Generally, this is the part of any long-term capital
  gain on section 1250 property (real property) that is due to depreciation."
- Quote: "For 2025, the maximum tax rates for individuals are 0%, 15%, 20%, 25%, and 28%."
- Identification, quote: "You must identify the property to be received within 45 days after the

  day after the date on which you transfer the property given up in the exchange." Second date:
  "The due date, including extensions, for your tax return for the tax year in which the

## 5. Residential Property Condition Disclosure Statement

Source: SC Code Title 27, Chapter 50, Article 1, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c050.php.
Downloaded with curl, read 2026-09-06.

- 27-50-20 scope. Quote: "This article applies to the following transfers of residential real
  property consisting of at least one but not more than four dwelling units". Listed: sale or
  exchange, installment land sales contract, lease with option to purchase.
- 27-50-30 has fifteen exemptions. None names rental, investment, tenant-occupied or
  non-owner-occupied property, so a rental owner is covered unless one applies. Quote: "(3) by a
  fiduciary in the course of the administration of a decedent's estate, guardianship,
  conservatorship, or trust;". Item (1) covers transfers "pursuant to court order including
  transfers in administration of an estate, pursuant to a writ of execution, by foreclosure sale,
  by a trustee in bankruptcy, by a receiver". Also "(13) between parties when both parties agree
  in writing not to complete a disclosure statement;".
- 27-50-40(A). Quote: "Except for transactions exempted under Section 27-50-30, the owner of the
  real property shall furnish to a purchaser a written disclosure statement." Item (7), quote:
  "existence of a rental, rental management, vacation rental, or other lease contract in place on
  the property at the time of closing" and, if known, "any outstanding charges owed by the tenant
  for gas, electric, water, sewerage, or garbage services provided to the property the tenant
  leases". Item (9) covers HOA governance. Last amended "2018 Act No. 245 (H.3886), SECTION 2,
  eff May 17, 2018".
- 27-50-40(B). Quote: "The disclosure statement must give the owner the option to indicate that
  the owner has actual knowledge of the specified characteristics or conditions, or that the
  owner is making no representations as to any characteristic or condition."
- 27-50-50(A), timing. Quote: the owner "shall deliver to the purchaser the disclosure form
  required by this article before a real estate contract is signed by the purchaser and owner, or
  as otherwise agreed in the real estate contract". (B). Quote: "Failure to provide the
  disclosure form required by this article to the purchaser does not: (1) void the agreement;
  (2) create a defect in title;" or "(3) present a valid reason to delay or otherwise interfere
  with the closing".
- 27-50-70(A). Quote: "A listing agent or any real estate licensee operating for any party in a
  residential real estate transaction must inform in writing each owner covered by the listing
  agreement of the owner's obligations prescribed in this article."

Source: SC Real Estate Commission form "STATE OF SOUTH CAROLINA RESIDENTIAL PROPERTY CONDITION
DISCLOSURE STATEMENT", "Effective 6/1/2023",
https://www.llr.sc.gov/re/recpdf/Property-Condition-Disclosure-Statement-06.01.2023.pdf.
Downloaded with curl, read 2026-09-06.

- Quote: "This disclosure statement is not required in connection with transactions listed and
  exempted by South Carolina Code § 27-50-30."
- Heading, quote: "VII. EXISTENCE OF A RENTAL, RENTAL MANAGEMENT, VACATION RENTAL, OR OTHER LEASE

## 6. Security deposit at the sale

Source: SC Code 27-40-450(a) and 27-40-410, URL as item 1. Read 2026-09-06.

- The statute does not say the seller must transfer the deposit. It says the seller stays liable
  unless he does. 27-40-450(a), quote: "he remains liable to the tenant for security recoverable
  by the tenant under SECTION 27-40-410, unless the security deposit is transferred from the
  seller to the purchaser" and "the tenant is notified in writing a reasonable time after the
  transaction in which case the purchaser is liable under SECTION 27-40-410."
- 27-40-410(a), what the holder owes at the end. Quote: "Any deduction from the security/rental
  deposit must be itemized by the landlord in a written notice to the tenant together with the
  amount due, if any, within thirty days after termination of the tenancy". (b), quote: "the
  tenant may recover the property and money in an amount equal to three times the amount
  wrongfully withheld and reasonable attorney's fees."
- No section sets the form of the transfer or who sends the tenant the written notice.

## 7. Short-term bookings that transfer with the sale

Source: SC Code Title 27, Chapter 50, Article 3, "South Carolina Vacation Rental Act", URL as
item 5. Downloaded with curl, read 2026-09-06. HISTORY on every section: "2002 Act No. 336,
SECTION 2". The chapter ends at 27-50-270.

- 27-50-230(3). Quote: "'Vacation rental' means the lease, sublease, or other rental of
  residential property for a period of fewer than ninety days". Carve-out: "except that it does
  not include rental of residential property on a weekly or monthly basis pursuant to Chapter 40
  of this title, the South Carolina Residential Landlord and Tenant Act."
- 27-50-220(A). Quote: "This article applies to any rental management company acting on behalf of
  an owner or to any other persons or entities otherwise engaged in the renting or managing of
  residential property for vacation rental".
- 27-50-250(A), the 90-day rule. Quote: the grantee "shall take title subject to the vacation
  rental agreement and the vacation rental management agreement for all vacation rental periods
  that begin no later than ninety days after the date the grantee's interest is recorded". Later
  bookings, quote: "If the vacation rental begins more than ninety days after the recording of the
  grantee's interest, then no party has the right to enforce the terms of the vacation rental
  agreement" and "the tenant is due a refund of any payments towards the agreement within
  forty-five days of the recording of the transfer of interest."
- 27-50-250(B), seller duties. Quote: "Before ratification of any contract of sale, the grantor
  shall disclose to the grantee in writing all future time periods that the property is subject to
  a vacation rental." Quote: "Not later than fourteen consecutive days after entering into a
  contract of sale or transfer of interest, whichever is earlier, the grantor shall disclose in
  writing to the rental management company the grantee's name and address." Quote: "Not later
  than fourteen consecutive days after the date of the transfer of interest, the grantor shall
  disclose in writing to the rental management company the grantee's name, address, and date the
  transfer of interest was recorded."
- Penalty, same subsection. Quote: "A grantor or grantee who knowingly violates or fails to
  perform any duty prescribed by any provision of this article is liable for actual damages
  proximately caused to the tenant and court costs."
- 27-50-270. Quote: "Failure by the owner to disclose the existence of a vacation rental agreement
  to the purchaser, closing attorney, lender, or title insurer does not: (1) void the sales
  agreement; (2) create an encumbrance or defect in title".
- The Act makes the buyer take title subject to "the vacation rental management agreement" for
  the same 90-day window. It does not say how deposits or advance rent held for post-closing
  stays move from seller to buyer.

Source: SC Code 40-57-135(J), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php. Downloaded with curl,
read 2026-09-06. Quote: "A real estate brokerage firm shall manage residential and commercial real
estate under a written management agreement". (J)(6), quote: "a management agreement may not
contain a provision binding the property under a future listing agreement if the property is to
be sold in the future, in which case a separate listing agreement is required."

Source: SC Real Estate Commission laws page, https://www.llr.sc.gov/re/laws.aspx, read 2026-09-06.
It links the disclosure act and the time-share act. No Commission guidance on bookings or management
agreements at closing was found there or by site search; form section VII (item 5) is all there is.

## 8. Capital gains on a rental: the Section 121 exclusion

Source: IRS Publication 523 (2025) "Selling Your Home", "For use in preparing 2025 Returns",
https://www.irs.gov/publications/p523. Downloaded with curl, read 2026-09-06.

- Residence test. Quote: "If you owned the home and used it as your residence for at least 24
  months of the previous 5 years, you meet the residence requirement." The publication does not
  say "never lived in" in words; a property the seller never used as a residence fails this test.
- Nonqualified use. Quote: "Gain from the sale or exchange of your main home isn't excludable from
  income if it is allocable to periods of nonqualified use." Quote: "Nonqualified use means any
  period after 2008 when neither you nor your spouse (or former spouse) used the property as your
  main home, with certain exceptions."
- The exception for a home rented after the owner moved out. Quote: nonqualified use does not
  include "Any portion of the 5-year period ending on the date of the sale or exchange after the
  last date you or your spouse (or former spouse) used the property as your main home".
- Depreciation. Quote: "you can't exclude the part of your gain equal to any depreciation allowed
  or allowable as a deduction for periods after May 6, 1997." Quote: "Gain from depreciation for
  periods after May 6, 1997, isn't taken into account to determine gain from nonqualified use."

Source: IRS Topic no. 701 "Sale of your home", https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc701, "Page Last
Reviewed or Updated: 08-Jun-2026". Read 2026-09-06. Quote: "If you and your spouse owned the home
and used it as a residence for at least 24 months (2 years) of the previous 5 years, you meet the
use test."

## 9. HOA statement of amounts due on a condo sale

Source: SC Code 27-31-220, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c031.php. Downloaded with curl,
read 2026-09-06. HISTORY: "1962 Code SECTION 57-515; 1962 (52) 1866; 1967 (55) 449".

- No seller-side duty is stated. The request comes from the purchaser and the duty is the
  council's. Quote: "The council of co-owners shall provide for the issuance and shall issue to
  any purchaser, upon his request, a statement of such amounts due by the seller". The purchaser's
  liability "shall be limited to the amount as set forth in the statement."
- The purchaser (other than at a foreclosure sale) "shall be jointly and severally liable with the
  seller for the amounts owing by the latter under SECTION 27-31-190 up to the time of the
  conveyance".

## Not verified

- A statute sentence saying in words that a lease survives a sale. Chapter 40 assumes it
  (27-40-450, 27-40-410(e), 27-40-420(b), 27-40-210(6)); no section states it.
- The withholding percentage for a 2026 closing as a DOR sentence. The statute says "maximum
  individual tax rate"; the I-290 says "top marginal Individual Income Tax rate for the tax year
  of the sale"; the DOR H. 4216 page gives 5.21% for income from $30,000 for tax year 2026. No DOR
  page found says "withhold 5.21%". For 2025 sales the DOR page says the top rate is 6%.
- A DOR reissue of Revenue Ruling #09-13 after the 2024 rate change; its "(5% or 7%)" is stale.
- A written-notice requirement for showings. 27-40-530(c) says "twenty-four hours notice" only.
- Who must give the tenant the written notice of conveyance under 27-40-450(a), and how a deposit
  is transferred. The statute is silent on both.
- Any SC statute or Commission guidance on moving vacation-rental deposits or advance rent from
  seller to buyer at closing. The Vacation Rental Act only gives the guest a refund right for
  stays starting more than 90 days after recording. The condo page's line that deposits "pass to
  the buyer at closing" is a contract term, not a statute.
- The IRS like-kind tax-tips page (read 2026-09-06, updated 01-May-2026) does not state the 45 or
  180 day periods, and https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/like-kind-exchanges-under-irc-section-1031
  returned 404. Pub 544 and the Form 8824 instructions verify the periods instead.

## What will go stale

- The SC top individual rate, and with it the nonresident withholding percentage. The BEA sets a
  new rate "no later than February 15 of each year" under H. 4216. Re-open
  https://dor.sc.gov/tax/individual-income before any publish; check for a new I-290 revision
  (current is Rev. 7/17/23) and a replacement for RR #09-13.
- IRS Pub 544 and Pub 523 are the 2025 editions; 2026 editions appear in early 2027. Topic 409
  (25-Feb-2026) and Topic 701 (08-Jun-2026) carry review dates; re-open both.
- The Commission's disclosure form is "Effective 6/1/2023"; a new edition would change section
  VII wording and the status checkboxes.
- research/relocating/state-tax-table.md line 199 still says the buyer withholds 7%. That is the
  pre-July-2024 rate and must not be carried to any page.
