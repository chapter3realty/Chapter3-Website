## 1. Does South Carolina require a licensed SC attorney for a residential closing?

**1.1** Practising law in SC without being a member of the SC Bar is a felony.
- Source: SC Code 40-5-310, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c005.php
- Quote: "No person may either practice law or solicit the legal cause of another person or entity in this State unless he is enrolled as a member of the South Carolina Bar"
- read 2026-09-05

**1.2** The SC Supreme Court holds that real estate and mortgage loan closings must be conducted under the supervision of an attorney. This is the court's own restatement of State v. Buyers Service Co., 292 S.C. 426 (1987).
- Source: Doe v. McMaster, SC Supreme Court Op. No. 25508 (refiled 18 Aug 2003), https://www.sccourts.org/opinions/htmlfiles/sc/25508.htm
- Quote: "We held in Buyers Service 'real estate and mortgage loan closings should be conducted only under the supervision of attorneys.'"
- Also: "instructions to the Clerk of Court or Register of Mesne Conveyances as to the manner of recording, if given by a lay person for the benefit of another, must be given under the supervision of an attorney."
- read 2026-09-05

**1.3** The court lists four steps of a residential closing that are the practice of law: title search, document preparation, closing, recording.
- Source: Doe Law Firm v. Richardson, SC Supreme Court Op. No. 26214 (23 Oct 2006), https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/SC/26214.htm
- Quote: "Real estate closings and mortgage loan closings should be conducted only under an attorney's supervision."
- Also: "The title search and preparation of title documents for the lender and subsequent preparation of related documents is the practice of law which must be performed or supervised by an attorney."
- read 2026-09-05

**1.4** The SC Attorney General told lenders in 1997 that Buyers Service remains valid.
- Source: SC Attorney General letter, 19 Dec 1997, https://www.scag.gov/wp-content/uploads/2013/12/97dec19bank.pdf (index page https://www.scag.gov/archives/11727)
- Quote: "That case, in effect, holds that all aspects of real estate and mortgage loan closings must be under the supervision of an attorney."
- read 2026-09-05 (scanned PDF, OCR text)

Not verified: the 1987 opinion itself. sccourts.org does not host 1987 opinions; Justia returned 403 and CourtListener returned an empty page. The holding is quoted above from the court's own 2003 and 2006 opinions instead.

## 2. Can the buyer sign without being in South Carolina?

### 2(a) Remote online notarization (RON) in SC

**2a.1** SC's electronic notary law requires the signer to appear in person. It does not authorise remote (video) notarization.
- Source: SC Code 26-2-50(A)(1), Electronic Notary Public Act, 2021 Act No. 85, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t26c002.php
- Quote: "An electronic notary public shall perform an electronic notarization only if the principal: (1) appears in person before the electronic notary public at the time of notarization"
- read 2026-09-05

**2a.2** "Appear in person" means physical presence.
- Source: SC Code 26-1-5(13), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t26c001.php
- Quote: "'Personal appearance' and 'appear in person before a notary' means an individual and a notary are in the physical presence of one another so that they may freely see and communicate"
- read 2026-09-05

**2a.3** Taking an acknowledgment without the principal appearing in person is a misdemeanor for the notary.
- Source: SC Code 26-1-160(B)(1), same URL as 2a.2
- Quote: "A notary is guilty of a misdemeanor if the notary takes: (1) an acknowledgment or administers an oath or affirmation without the principal appearing in person before the notary"
- read 2026-09-05

**2a.4** The Secretary of State describes SC electronic notarization as in-person only.
- Source: https://sos.sc.gov/services-and-filings/notaries
- Quote: "Electronic notarization, also known as IPEN, requires that the notary public and the signer must be in the presence of one another when the notarization takes place."
- read 2026-09-05

**2a.5** A RON bill (S. 486, "SC Remote Online Notarization Act") passed the Senate in 2019 and died in the House Judiciary Committee.
- Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/sess123_2019-2020/bills/486.htm
- Status lines on page: Senate "Read third time and sent to House" 3/20/2019; House "Referred to Committee on Judiciary" 3/21/2019; no later action.
- read 2026-09-05

Not verified: an exhaustive search of the 2025-2026 session. Web searches restricted to scstatehouse.gov returned only H. 3190 (notary fees). I found no enacted SC RON statute as of 2026-09-05. Treat "SC has no RON" as the current position and re-check each session.

### 2(b) Mail-away closing with out-of-state notarization

**2b.1** A deed or mortgage signed outside SC can be acknowledged before a notary of the state where it is signed and still be recorded in SC. Two witnesses are required.
- Source: SC Code 30-5-30(B) and (B)(5), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t30c005.php
- Quote (B): "the signing must be acknowledged by the grantor, mortgagor, vendor, or lessor in the presence of two witnesses ... If the acknowledgment is taken without the limits of this State, it may be taken before:"
- Quote (B)(5): "a notary public who shall affix to the deed or other instrument his official seal within the state of his appointment, which is a sufficient authentication of his signature, residence, and official character"
- read 2026-09-05

**2b.2** SC recognises notarial acts done in another state by a notary of that state.
- Source: SC Code 26-3-20, Uniform Recognition of Acknowledgments Act, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t26c003.php
- Quote: "Notarial acts may be performed outside this State for use in this State with the same effect as if performed by a notary public of this State by the following persons"
- Quote: "(1) A notary public authorized to perform notarial acts in the place in which the act is performed"
- read 2026-09-05

**2b.3** Horry County Register of Deeds accepts out-of-state instruments if they meet SC form: two witnesses, probate or acknowledgment, notarized, notary's expiry and state shown.
- Source: Horry County ROD, "Prerequisites to Recording Documents" PDF, https://www.horrycountysc.gov/media/404mgbzc/prereqs.pdf (linked from https://www.horrycountysc.gov/departments/register-of-deeds/forms-links/)
- Quote: "Out of state instruments must conform to the recording requirements of this state. 1. Two witnesses 2. Probate or acknowledgement 3. Notarized"
- Quote: "A notary needs to state when their commission expires and state: Notary for the State of ___________."
- Quote: "On all written instruments, with the exception of a mortgage satisfaction, there must be two witnesses and also a probate or acknowledgement statement."
- read 2026-09-05

## 3. Power of attorney at an SC closing

**3.1** An SC power of attorney must be signed, witnessed like a will, and acknowledged or proved the same way as a deed.
- Source: SC Code 62-8-105, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t62c008.php
- Quote: "(2) attested with the same formality and with the same requirements as to witnesses as a will in South Carolina; and (3) acknowledged or proved pursuant to Section 30-5-30."
- read 2026-09-05

**3.2** The statute requires recording only for use after the principal becomes incapacitated. It may be recorded at any time. An out-of-state principal records in a county where the property is.
- Source: SC Code 62-8-109(c), same URL as 3.1
- Quote: "After the principal's incapacity, an agent may exercise the authority granted unto the agent under the power of attorney only if the power of attorney has been recorded in the same manner as a deed"
- Quote: "If the principal resides out of State, the power of attorney may be recorded in any county where property of the principal is located at the time the instrument is recorded."
- Quote: "The power of attorney may be recorded before or after the principal's incapacity."
- read 2026-09-05

**3.3** A POA executed in another state may be recorded in SC even if it does not meet 30-5-30 form.
- Source: SC Code 62-8-109(d), same URL
- Quote: "a valid power of attorney as provided for pursuant to this part, which is executed in another jurisdiction, may be recorded as though it complies with the provisions of Section 30-5-30."
- read 2026-09-05

**3.4** Recording fee for a POA is $25 statewide; Horry County lists it in the deed book.
- Source: SC Code 8-21-310(A)(2)(i), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t08c021.php. Quote: "twenty-five dollars for the following documents: ... (i) a power of attorney"
- Source: Horry County ROD price list, https://www.horrycountysc.gov/media/uevkqtt4/price.pdf. Quote: "POWER OF ATTORNEY DEED #063 8-21-310-(A) $25.00 PER DOCUMENT"
- Source: Horry County ROD instruments list, https://www.horrycountysc.gov/media/ukplb11l/instruments.pdf, lists "POWER OF ATTORNEY" under DEED BOOK.
- read 2026-09-05

Not verified: any statute or Horry County ROD page that literally says a POA must be recorded before it is used to sign a deed or mortgage for a principal who is not incapacitated. The statute only mandates recording for post-incapacity use. Whether the closing attorney or the lender requires the POA to be recorded with the deed is a practice question (see section "Questions only the brokerage can answer").

## 5. Home inspector licensing

**5.1** A home inspector licence from the SC Residential Builders Commission is required by statute.
- Source: SC Code 40-59-520(A), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c059.php
- Quote: "No person may engage in or transact any home inspection business or hold himself out to the public as a home inspector ... unless the person is licensed by the commission."
- Definition, 40-59-500(2): "'Commission' means the South Carolina Residential Builders Commission."
- read 2026-09-05

**5.2** LLR Residential Builders Commission page lists Home Inspector License among its licence types.
- Source: https://llr.sc.gov/res/ and https://llr.sc.gov/res/homeinspector.aspx
- Quote (main page): "The Commission licenses/registers those individuals who are qualified by experience to perform residential building, residential specialty contracting, and home inspecting on residential buildings or structures"

## 6. Property tax: 4 percent legal residence vs 6 percent other

**6.1** Legal residence is assessed at 4 percent.
- Source: SC Code 12-43-220(c)(1), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c043.php
- Quote: "The legal residence and not more than five acres contiguous thereto ... are taxed on an assessment equal to four percent of the fair market value of the property."
- read 2026-09-05

**6.2** The 4 percent ratio requires owner occupancy and an application.
- Source: SC Code 12-43-220(c)(2)(i) and (ii), same URL
- Quote (i): "the owner-occupant must have actually owned and occupied the residence as his legal residence and been domiciled at that address for some period during the applicable tax year."
- Quote (ii): "This item does not apply unless the owner of the property or the owner's agent applies for the four percent assessment ratio before the first penalty date for the payment of taxes"
- read 2026-09-05

**6.3** All other real property is assessed at 6 percent.
- Source: SC Code 12-43-220(e), same URL
- Quote: "All other real property not herein provided for shall be taxed on an assessment equal to six percent of the fair market value of such property."
- read 2026-09-05

**6.4** Horry County states the same two ratios and the application requirement.
- Source: https://www.horrycountysc.gov/departments/assessor/guide-to-assessment/. Quotes: "Legal residence refers to the special 4% assessment for owner occupied homes." Failure to qualify results in "an automatic 6% assessment."
- Source: news item 30 Apr 2025, https://www.horrycountysc.gov/news/articles/applications-for-legal-residence-property-tax-exemption-due-may-31/. Quotes: "South Carolina law provides the 4% assessment ratio on primary residential properties." "The alternative is 6%."
- read 2026-09-05

## 7. Flood insurance waiting period

**7.1** Federal rule: 30-day wait, with a loan-closing exception.
- Source: 44 CFR 61.11, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-44/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-61/section-61.11 (opened with curl; the fetch tool was redirected to a bot check) and the 2024 print edition at https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title44-vol1/xml/CFR-2024-title44-vol1-sec61-11.xml
- Quote (d): "the effective date and time of any new policy, added coverage, or increase in the amount of coverage will be 12:01 a.m. (local time) on the 30th calendar day after the application date"
- Quote (b): "Where the initial purchase of flood insurance is in connection with the making, increasing, extension, or renewal of a loan, the coverage ... shall be effective as of the time of the loan closing"
- Condition in (b): "provided the written request for the coverage is received by the NFIP and the flood insurance policy is applied for and the presentment of payment of premium is made at or prior to the loan closing."
- read 2026-09-05

**7.2** FEMA's NFIP consumer site says the same in plain words.
- Source: https://www.floodsmart.gov/get-insured/buy-a-policy
- Quote: "Your flood insurance coverage will go into effect 30 days after your date of purchase."
- Quote: "There is no wait if you buy flood insurance while making, increasing, extending or renewing a mortgage."
- Also listed: one-day wait for newly designated high-risk zones within 12 months of the map update; one-day wait after flooding caused by wildfire on federal land within 60 days of containment.
- read 2026-09-05

**7.3** FEMA main flood insurance page.
- Source: https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance
- Quote: "Plan ahead as there is typically a 30-day waiting period for an NFIP policy to go into effect"
- read 2026-09-05

Not verified: https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance/work-with-nfip/policy-effective-date and https://www.floodsmart.gov/how-buy-flood-insurance both returned 404. Note that a cash buyer with no loan gets no exception: the 30-day wait applies.

## Questions only the brokerage can answer

1. Which Horry County closing attorneys the brokerage works with, and which of them will run a mail-away closing with out-of-state notarization (2b) or accept a recorded POA (3).
2. Whether the closing attorney or lender requires the POA to be recorded with the deed even when the principal is competent, and who drafts it (3, "Not verified").
3. The brokerage's wire-verification procedure: who calls whom, on which number, and whether wire instructions are ever sent by email (4.6, 4.7).
4. What the video walk-through covers (rooms, mechanicals, attic, crawl space, exterior, street, noise), who records it, whether it is live or recorded, and whether it is kept after closing.
5. Whether the brokerage attends the home inspection for an absent buyer and sends the inspector's report as issued.
6. How a remote buyer's earnest money is delivered and to whom (attorney trust account, brokerage escrow).
7. Which utility serves a specific listing (Santee Cooper, Horry Electric or Duke; GSWSA or a city system; gas or none). Only an address lookup or the seller's bills settle this.
8. Whether the lender used for the file accepts a mail-away or POA closing, and how long before closing they need the signed package.
9. Whether Tim Nash or Devin Day does the remote walk-through, and the SC licence line to show on the page.
10. Which property managers the brokerage refers for an out-of-state owner, if any, and whether the brokerage manages rentals itself.
