# On-site rental program vs self-managing on Airbnb / Vrbo: verified facts

Every fact below was read at the primary source on 2026-09-05 with WebFetch unless the entry
says otherwise. Quotes are verbatim. Re-open every source before reuse (non-negotiable 8).
Facts only; no page copy. Three Vrbo pages could not be opened; they are under "Not verified"
at the end. Nothing here is a conclusion about a named building, HOA, or on-site program.

## 1. Airbnb host fees

Source: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1857, page title "Airbnb service fees". No
"last updated" date on the page. Read 2026-09-05.

- Split fee: most hosts pay 3% of the booking subtotal. Quote: "Most hosts pay a 3% service
  fee, but some pay more. Hosts pay a 4% fee for listings in Brazil and Mexico."
- Host-only fee: most hosts pay 15.5%. Quote: "Most hosts pay 15.5%, remaining hosts
  typically pay 14%-16%, and hosts pay a 16% fee for listings in Brazil and Mexico."
- Who must use host-only. Quote: "This fee structure is mandatory for certain hosts,
  including traditional hospitality listings (e.g., hotels, serviced apartments, etc.),
  hosts who use property management software, and hosts in countries subject to this fee
  structure."
- Guest fee under split fee. Quote: "Guests pay a service fee ranging from 14.1% to 16.5%
  of the booking subtotal".

## 2. Vrbo host fees

Source: https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-is-the-booking-fee-calculated, page title
"About pay-per-booking fees". Read 2026-09-05.

- Pay-per-booking is 5% plus 3%. Quote: "The booking fee includes: 5% commission fee, 3%
  payment processing fee".
- Base of the 5%. Quote: "The 5% commission fee is charged on the rental amount and any
  additional fees you charge the traveler (such as cleaning, pet, and boat fees)."
- Base of the 3%. Quote: "The 3% payment processing fee is charged on the total payment
  amount you receive from your guest, including taxes and refundable damage deposits."
- Software-connected bookings that bypass Vrbo checkout. Quote: "If the source of the
  reservation comes from our sites but wasn't booked through our check-out flow, you'll be
  charged a 10% booking fee."

Annual subscription. Source:
https://help.vrbo.com/articles/Integrated-Property-Managers-Switch-from-Pay-Per-Booking-to-Pay-Per-Subscription
page title "Integrated Property Managers – Switch from Pay Per Booking to Pay Per
Subscription". Read 2026-09-05.

- The subscription plan is closed to new listings. Quote: "Pay-per-subscription (PPS) is no
  longer available as a pricing plan. Only those who already have at least one listing on
  an active PPS plan can convert a listing from pay-per-booking (PPB) to PPS."
- Source: https://help.vrbo.com/articles/How-do-I-pay-for-my-new-subscription, "Pay for
  your listing subscription". Quote: "You cannot purchase a subscription if all of your
  listings are on pay-per-booking (PPB) plans." Read 2026-09-05.
- The subscription dollar figure was NOT verified (see end). A new self-managing owner's
  Vrbo cost is the pay-per-booking 5% + 3%; no subscription is offered to them.

## 3. Platform tax collection in South Carolina

### Airbnb

Source: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2328, page title "Occupancy tax collection and
remittance by Airbnb in South Carolina". Read 2026-09-05. Every line uses the base "of the
listing price including any cleaning fee and guest fee for reservations 89 nights and
shorter" (county and city lines say "cleaning fees and guest fees").

- Quote: "South Carolina State Sales and Use Tax: 5%".
- Quote: "South Carolina State Accommodations Tax: 2%".
- Quote: "Local Taxes collected by the State: 0.5-8%, per Local Tax type".
- Quote: "Horry County - Local Accommodations Tax: 1.5% - 3%".
- Quote: "Myrtle Beach - Local Accommodations Tax: 0.5%" and "Myrtle Beach - Hospitality
  Fee: 1%".
- Quote: "North Myrtle Beach - Local Accommodations Tax: 1.5%".
- Quote: "Surfside Beach - Local Accommodations Tax: 0.5%".
- Owner keeps the rest. Quote: "Hosts located in these areas are responsible for assessing
  all other tax obligations, including state and city jurisdictions."

Caution: Airbnb labels the county charge "Local Accommodations Tax 1.5% - 3%". Horry
County's own page (section 4) calls its charge the Hospitality Fee, 3% outside and 1.5%
inside city limits. Do not reconcile the labels on the page; use the county's name.

### SCDOR: the marketplace remits

Source: https://dor.sc.gov/tax/accommodations, page title "Accommodations | SCDOR". Read
2026-09-05.

- Who pays. Quote: "The person or business who books the accommodations is responsible for
  collecting and paying Accommodations Tax. That may be the property owner or a real estate
  agent, listing service, broker, Online Travel Company (VRBO, Expedia, etc.), or similar
  entity."
- Platform bookings. Quote: "If you use an online travel company for reserving rooms and
  accepting payment for the booking, they are responsible for the tax for the full amount
  of the booking."
- Rate. Quote: "5% Sales Tax, 2% Accommodations Tax" plus local taxes. Quote:
  "Accommodations Tax applies to sleeping accommodations of any kind that are rented to
  guests for less than 90 consecutive days."

Source: SC Revenue Ruling #19-6, served as a PDF at
https://dor.sc.gov/sales-use-tax-marketplace-facilitators-and-third-parties-whose-products-are-sold-marketplace-guidance-and-tax
(opened with WebFetch; text extracted locally from the saved PDF). Effective April 26,
2019. Read 2026-09-05.

- Quote: "A marketplace facilitator is the retailer and is responsible for remitting state
  and local sales and use tax for all products sold via its marketplace".
- Lodging is included. Quote: "A marketplace facilitator can sell or lease any type of
  product subject to sales and use tax, including sales of meals, clothing, lodging,
  vehicles, machinery, and equipment."
- Owner selling only through a marketplace. Quote: "the third party is not a retailer and,
  therefore, is not required to obtain a retail license or remit sales and use tax on such
  sales."
- Owner who also books directly. Quote: "the third party is not responsible for remitting
  the sales and use tax on its products sold through a marketplace operated by a
  marketplace facilitator."

Source: SC Information Letter #19-14, served as a PDF at
https://dor.sc.gov/sales-use-tax-marketplace-facilitator-new-law-effective-april-26-2019
(same method). Dated May 7, 2019. Read 2026-09-05. Quote: "This person is not required to
obtain a retail license since the marketplace facilitator is the retailer required to remit
South Carolina sales and use tax."

Source: SC Code Title 12 Chapter 36, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c036.php. Read
2026-09-05.

- 12-36-71: a marketplace facilitator facilitates a retail sale by, quote: "(a) listing or
  advertising, or allowing the listing or advertising of, the products of another person
  in any marketplace where sales at retail occur; and (b) collecting or processing payments
  from the purchaser".
- 12-36-920(A). Quote: "A sales tax equal to seven percent is imposed on the gross proceeds
  derived from the rental or charges for any rooms, campground spaces, lodgings, or sleeping
  accommodations furnished to transients". (Seven percent is SCDOR's 5% + 2%.)
- 90-day rule. Quote: "The gross proceeds derived from the lease or rental of sleeping
  accommodations supplied to the same person for a period of ninety continuous days are not
  considered proceeds from transients."
- 12-36-920(E). Quote: "The taxes imposed by this section are imposed on every person
  engaged or continuing within this State in the business of furnishing accommodations to
  transients for consideration."
- Six-room exemption. Quote: "where the facilities consist of less than six sleeping rooms,
  contained on the same premises, which is used as the individual's place of abode." Does
  not fit an investment condo.

## 4. When the owner or an on-site program takes the payment

Source: https://dor.sc.gov/tax/accommodations (as above). Read 2026-09-05.

- Direct bookings need a licence. Quote: "If you are directly booking short-term rentals,
  you must have a Retail License to file and pay Accommodations Tax."
- Program bookings. Quote: "If you rent your property exclusively through a property
  management or online travel company that is reserving rooms and accepting payment for
  booking, they are responsible for remitting the tax for the full amount of the booking."
- Small exception. Quote: "A Retail License is not required if you rent a room or space
  for no more than one week each calendar quarter—but you are required to file and pay the
  tax annually."
- Several units. Quote: "If you are a property owner with more than one location that
  provides short term accommodations, you must have a Retail License for each location."

Source: SC Code 12-36-920 (URL above). Rental agents are remitters under the statute.
Quote: "Real estate agents, brokers, corporations, or listing services required to remit
taxes under this section shall notify the department if rental property, previously listed
by them, is dropped from their listings." Read 2026-09-05.

Source: https://www.horrycountysc.gov/departments/treasurer/hospitality-fee/, page title
"Hospitality Fee - Horry County SC.Gov". Read 2026-09-05.

- Outside city limits. Quote: "3% of gross proceeds from Rental of Transient
  Accommodations". Inside city limits the page states "1.5% of gross proceeds" on the same
  base.
- Quote: "All applicable short-term accommodation rentals require collection and remittance
  of the Hospitality Fee."
- Quote: "Long-term accommodation rentals 90 consecutive days at the same location to the
  same patron does not require collection and remittance of the Hospitality Fee."
- Filing. Quote: "The due date is the 20th of the month following the reporting period."

## 5. Condotel financing (sources line only; never name the body in copy)

Source: https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b4-2.1-03/ineligible-projects, section
title "B4-2.1-03, Ineligible Projects (08/05/2026)". Read 2026-09-05. The Guide does not
use the word "condotel"; its heading is "Projects that Operate as Hotels or Motels".

- The rule. Quote: "Fannie Mae will not purchase or securitize mortgage loans that are
  secured by units in certain condo or co-op projects if those projects have
  characteristics that make the project ineligible."
- The category. Quote: "Projects that are managed and operated as a hotel or motel, even
  though the units are individually owned."
- Mandatory pool. Quote: "The HOA or project's legal documents require owners to make
  their unit available for rental pooling (daily or otherwise)."
- Owner-use limits. Quote: "The HOA or project's legal documents restrict owners' ability
  to occupy the unit during any part of the year."
- Profit sharing. Quote: "The HOA or the project's legal documents require unit owners to
  share profits from the rental of units with the HOA, management company, or resort, or
  hotel rental company."
- Voluntary pool also counts. Quote: "is subject to voluntary rental-pooling, revenue,
  profit or commission sharing agreements with the HOA or management company".
- On-site operator. Quote: "is professionally managed by a hotel or resort management
  company that also facilitates short term rentals".
- Hotel services. Quote: "characteristics such as registration services, rentals of units
  on a daily or short-term basis, daily cleaning services, central telephone service,
  central key systems and restrictions on interior decorating".
- Name and marketing. Quote: "has a legal or common name that contains hotel, motel, or
  resort, unless the use of hotel, motel, or resort is a reference to a historical use"
  and "is marketed as a hotel, motel, resort or investment opportunity".
- Caution: one fetch summary reported a phrase about "blackout dates and occupancy limits
  to assure an inventory of units for rent"; a second item-by-item fetch did not return
  it. Re-open the page before quoting that phrase.

## 7. Personal use: the 14-day / 10 percent test

Source: https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p527.pdf (opened with WebFetch, text extracted
locally); the HTML at https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527 carries the same test. Cover:
"For use in preparing 2025 Returns". Read 2026-09-05.

- The test. Quote: "if you use it for personal purposes more than the greater of: 1. 14
  days, or 2. 10% of the total days it is rented to others at a fair rental price."
- Under 15 days. Quote: "Minimal rental use. If you use the dwelling unit as a home and you
  rent it less than 15 days during the year, that period isn't treated as rental activity."
- Same rule, later section. Quote: "its primary function isn't considered to be rental and
  it shouldn't be reported on Schedule E (Form 1040). You aren't required to report the
  rental income and rental expenses from this activity."
- The IRS's own worked example is a resort condo. Quote: "You own a condominium apartment
  in a resort area. You rented it at a fair rental price for a total of 170 days during the
  year." It ends: "That is more than the greater of 14 days or 10% of the 160 days it was
  rented (16 days)." The 10 days the tenant let the family use are subtracted from 170.

## 8. Depreciating furniture and appliances

Source: Publication 527 PDF as above, chapter 2. Read 2026-09-05.

- Class. Quote: "5-year property. This class includes computers and peripheral equipment,
  office machinery (typewriters, calculators, copiers, etc.), automobiles, and light
  trucks. This class also includes appliances, carpeting, and furniture used in a
  residential rental real estate activity."
- Table 2-1 rows (GDS then ADS). Quote: "Appliances, such as: Stoves, Refrigerators 5 years
  9 years"; "Carpets 5 years 9 years"; "Furniture used in rental property 5 years 9 years";
  "Residential rental property (buildings or structures) and structural components such as
  furnaces, waterpipes, venting, etc. 27.5 years 30 years".
- Hotel-type buildings are excluded from the 27.5-year class. Quote: "It doesn't include a
  unit in a hotel, motel, inn, or other establishment where more than half of the units are
  used on a transient basis." The page does not say what class such a unit falls in; it
  refers to Pub. 946. A CPA question, not a page claim. Pub. 946 was opened at
  https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946 but its class list was not readable in the fetch.

## Questions only the brokerage can answer

- What an on-site program owner statement looks like: gross rent, program percentage,
  credit-card fees, housekeeping, linen, supplies, marketing or "program" fees, and whether
  the split is taken before or after taxes and cleaning. Redact; never name the building.
- The range of splits seen on owner statements across programs, and whether any program
  charges a flat monthly fee on top.
- Owner-use terms actually read in program agreements: blackout weeks, notice required to
  hold dates, peak-season limits, minimum inventory the program keeps. The lender rule in
  section 5 treats owner-occupancy limits and mandatory pooling as hotel characteristics,
  so these terms matter twice.
- Whether an owner in a program may also list on Airbnb or Vrbo, and whether the program
  itself lists there through software (which puts it on Airbnb's host-only fee, section 1).
- Who files what when a program takes the money: does the program remit the state 7%, the
  county hospitality fee and the city taxes, and does the owner still hold a Retail
  License (SCDOR says the program remits, section 4).
- Who files the county PT-100 personal-property return on the furnishings (section 6).
- How a rental desk changes the loan in practice: which questionnaire answers Devin and
  BrickWood see fail, whether a non-warrantable or portfolio product was the outcome, and
  how the appraiser treated the unit. Keep it generic; never a named building.
- Whether Airbnb's "Horry County Local Accommodations Tax 1.5% - 3%" line is the county
  hospitality fee under another name.
- What HO-6 and short-term-rental endorsements local carriers require for an oceanfront
  unit, and how wind and flood are placed. AirCover excludes acts of nature (section 10).
