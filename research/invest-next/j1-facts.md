## 1. The program: 22 CFR 62.32 and related sections

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.32, "§ 62.32 Summer work travel." curl, read 2026-09-08.

### What it is

- Quote (b) Purpose: "The purpose of this program is to provide foreign college and university students with
  opportunities to interact with U.S. citizens, experience U.S. culture while sharing their own cultures with
  Americans they meet, travel in the United States, and work in jobs that require minimal training and are
  seasonal or temporary in order to earn funds to help defray a portion of their expenses."
- Who qualifies, (d)(4): sponsors must "Confirm that at the time of application, applicants (including final
  year students) are enrolled full-time and pursuing studies at accredited post-secondary, classroom-based,
  academic institutions physically located outside of the United States and have successfully completed at
  least one semester, or equivalent, of post-secondary academic study."
- State Department summary page, https://j1visa.state.gov/programs/summer-work-travel/, read 2026-09-08:
  participants must be "Post-secondary school students enrolled in and actively pursuing a degree or other
  full-time course of study at an accredited classroom based, post-secondary educational institution outside
  the United States" and "Pre-placed in a job prior to entry unless from a visa waiver country."

### Length, dates, and the 30-day travel period

- Quote (c) Duration of participation: "Summer Work Travel participants are authorized to participate in the
  Exchange Visitor Program for up to four months during the long break between academic years as determined by
  the Department of State. Extensions of program participation are not permitted."
- j1visa.state.gov SWT page, same read: "Program Length: The maximum length of the program is four months; and
  | Returning Home: Students must return to their home country prior to the start date of their university or
  college." Also "Readmittance: Participants can be admitted to the program more than once".
- The dates a participant may work are set per country. Source: "Summer Work Travel 2026 Season Program Dates
  by Country", https://j1visa.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026_SWT-Program-Date-Chart.pdf, "For
  External Use - Updated 01/28/2026", downloaded and read 2026-09-08. Note on the chart: "The program dates
  are windows of opportunity for program participation. Within this timeframe, students are still subject to
  their university's official academic break schedule. Even though the window of opportunity may be longer than
  four months, a participant may only participate a maximum of four months or the length of the period of their
  academic break, whichever is shorter."
  Windows for the countries the news names for Myrtle Beach (section 2), as printed (Opening, Closing,
  Approximate Length): Jamaica "11-May 31-Aug 3.5 months"; Turkey "15-May 1-Oct 4.5 months"; Romania "15-Jun
  30-Sep 3.5 months"; Dominican Republic "15-May 7-Sep 3.5 months"; Kazakhstan "8-May 1-Sep 3 months";
  Poland "25-May 30-Sep 4 months Visa Waiver Country"; Czech Republic "1-May 30-Sep 5 months"; China "1-Jun
  15-Sep 3.5 months"; Ireland "15-May 15-Sep 4 months Visa Waiver Country"; Ecuador "20-Dec 1-Oct 3 months";
  Bulgaria "3-May 1-Oct 5 months"; Mexico "15-May 2-Oct 4.5 months"; Thailand has two windows, "7-Mar 7-Jul
  4 months" and "7-May 7-Sep 4 months".
  Southern-hemisphere windows fall in the Grand Strand's off season: Peru "1-Dec 31-Mar 3 months"; Argentina
  "1-Dec 1-Apr 4 months"; Brazil "15-Nov 15-Mar 4 months"; Chile "1-Dec 5-Apr 4 months Visa Waiver Country";
  South Africa "1-Nov 1-Mar 4 months"; Colombia "1-May 30-Aug 4 months".
- The 30 days after the program. Source: 8 CFR 214.2(j)(1)(ii), https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/8/214.2,
  curl, read 2026-09-08. Quote: "An exchange alien, and J-2 spouse and children, may be admitted for a period
  up to 30 days before the report date or start of the approved program listed on Form DS-2019. The initial
  admission of an exchange visitor, spouse and children may not exceed the period specified on Form DS-2019,
  plus a period of 30 days for the purposes of travel". The regulation describes the 30 days as "for the
  purposes of travel"; no source opened states in words that work is barred in those 30 days (see "Could not
  verify"). Work is tied to the sponsor's vetting, (e)(6): participants "may not begin working at initial,
  replacement, or additional jobs until their sponsors have verified the terms and conditions of such
  employment and fully vetted their host employers".

### The sponsor's role

- Screening, (d): "sponsors are solely responsible for adequately screening and making the final selection",
  including (d)(1) "Conduct and document interviews with potential participants either in-person or by
  video-conference" and (d)(2) on English (quoted in section 8).
- Job vetting, (g)(2): "Sponsors must confirm initial, replacement, and additional jobs placements of all
  Summer Work Travel Program participants before participants may start work by verifying, at a minimum, the
  terms and conditions of such employment and fully vetting their host employers ... Once participants have
  arrived in the United States and identified initial, replacement, or additional jobs, sponsors must vet such
  jobs within 72 hours."
- Employer vetting, (n)(2): sponsors must ensure host employers "are legitimate and reputable businesses by, at
  a minimum: (i) Making direct contact in person or by telephone ... (iii) Obtaining potential host employers'
  Employer Identification Numbers and copies of their current business licenses; and (iv) Verifying the
  potential host employers' Worker's Compensation Insurance Policy". (n)(3)(iii): confirm host employers "have
  not experienced layoffs in the past 120 days and do not have workers on lockout or on strike."
- Monitoring, (j)(1): "Maintain, at a minimum, monthly personal contacts with program participants. Such contact
  may be in-person, by telephone, or via exchanges of electronic mail (including a response from the
  participant) and must be properly documented. Sponsors must promptly and appropriately address issues
  affecting the participants' health, safety, and welfare identified through such contacts".
- No incentives to employers, (g)(1): "Sponsors and third parties acting on their behalf may not pay or
  otherwise provide any incentive to employers to accept program participants for job placements".
- Pay, (i)(1): participants must be paid "at a minimum" the higher of "The applicable Federal, State, or Local
  Minimum Wage (including overtime)" or "Pay and benefits commensurate with those offered to their similarly
  situated U.S. counterparts."
- Placement reports to State, (p)(1): sponsors "Submit placement reports on January 31 and July 31 of each
  year" listing participants' "names, SEVIS Identification Numbers, countries of citizenship or legal
  permanent residence, names of host employers".
- Insurance, 22 CFR 62.14(b), https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.14, read 2026-09-08: "Medical
  benefits of at least $100,000 per accident or illness"; "Deductibles not to exceed $500 per accident or
  illness."

### The host employer's role

- (o) Host employer cooperation: "Sponsors may place participants only with host employers that agree to:
  (1) Make good faith efforts to provide participants the number of hours of paid employment per week as
  identified on their job offers and agreed to when the sponsors vetted the jobs; (2) Pay eligible
  participants for overtime worked in accordance with applicable State or Federal law; (3) Notify sponsors
  promptly when participants arrive at the work sites to begin their programs; when there are any changes or
  deviations in the job placements during the participants' programs; when participants are not meeting the
  requirements of their job placements; or when participants leave their positions ahead of their planned
  departures; (4) Contact sponsors immediately in the event of any emergency involving participants or any
  situations that impact their health, safety, or welfare; and (5) In those instances when the employer
  provides housing or transportation, agree to provide suitable and acceptable accommodations and/or reliable,
  affordable, and convenient transportation."
- Kinds of jobs, (g)(4): only jobs that "(i) Are seasonal or temporary ... and (ii) Provide opportunities for
  regular communication and interaction with U.S. citizens". (h) exclusions include (h)(3) "domestic help
  positions in private homes", (h)(8) "positions requiring work hours that fall predominantly between 10:00
  p.m. and 6:00 a.m.", (h)(16) goods-producing NAICS sectors.

### Every housing rule in 62.32 (quoted)

- (e)(5), pre-departure information: "Information advising participants of their obligations to notify their
  sponsors within ten days after they arrive in the United States and within ten days after they initially
  secure or change residences".
- (e)(7): "For participants with jobs secured prior to departing from selected home countries, documentation
  explaining the terms and conditions of such jobs and providing information about available housing and
  transportation to and from work".
- (e)(9): "Information explaining that sponsors will terminate the programs of participants who fail to comply
  with enumerated program regulations (i.e., reporting their arrivals, reporting changes of residence, not
  starting work at unverified jobs, responding to sponsor monthly outreach/monitoring efforts)."
- (g)(9): "Sponsors must consider the availability of suitable, affordable housing (e.g., that meets local
  codes and ordinances) and reliable, affordable, and convenient transportation to and from work when making
  job placements."
- (g)(9)(i): "If employers do not provide or arrange housing and/or transportation, or if participants decline
  employer-provided housing or transportation, sponsors must actively and immediately assist participants with
  arranging appropriate housing and transportation."
- (g)(9)(ii): "If employers provide housing and/or transportation to and from work, job offers must include
  details of all such arrangements, including the cost to participants; whether such arrangements deduct such
  costs from participants' wages; and the market value of housing and/or transportation in accordance with
  the Fair Labor Standards Act regulations set forth at 29 CFR part 531, if they are considered part of the
  compensation packages."
- (g)(11)(i), visa-waiver nationals arriving without a job: sponsors must "Ensure that such participants
  receive pre-departure information that explains how to seek employment and secure lodging in the United
  States".
- (i)(2)(i): sponsors must show pay standards are met where "The host employers provide housing and/or
  transportation as part of participants' compensation, but the compensation package does not explain that the
  lower hourly wage reflects such benefits".
- (o)(5): quoted above (employer-provided housing must be "suitable and acceptable accommodations").
- What 62.32 does not contain (absence, after reading the whole section): no duty on a sponsor to inspect or
  approve a private landlord's house; no rent cap; no rule on who may own or list housing. The only cost rules
  are disclosure and the FLSA market-value rule for employer-provided housing.

### Housing rules in 62.10 and 62.9 (all sponsors, all categories)

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.10, "§ 62.10 Program administration.", read 2026-09-08.

- (b) pre-arrival: sponsors must give "employment information and terms and conditions of employment
  (including employer name and address, position duration, job duties, number of work hours, wages, other
  compensation and benefits, deductions from wages, including those taken for housing and transportation)"
  and (b)(4) "Housing, including specific information on what housing is provided by the program or otherwise
  available and the expected cost to the exchange visitor"; (b)(6) "Description and amount of other costs that
  the exchange visitor will likely incur (e.g., insurance, living expenses, transportation expenses)".
- (c) orientation must include (c)(2) "Local community resources (e.g., public transportation, medical
  centers, schools, libraries, recreation centers, and banks)", (c)(4) "deductions from wages, including for
  housing and transportation", (c)(8) "Wilberforce Pamphlet on the Rights and Protections for Temporary
  Workers", and (c)(9) "The requirement that an exchange visitor must report to the sponsor or sponsor designee
  within ten calendar days any changes in his or her telephone number, email address, actual and current U.S.
  address (i.e., physical residence), and site of activity".
- (d) Address reporting and SEVIS: sponsors must "(3) Require that exchange visitors report to the sponsor
  within ten calendar days any changes in their telephone numbers, email addresses, actual and current U.S.
  addresses (i.e., physical residence), and site(s) of activity" and "(4) Report in SEVIS within ten business
  days of notification by an exchange visitor any change in the exchange visitor's actual and current U.S.
  address, telephone number, email address, and/or primary site of activity". Practical meaning for a
  landlord: every tenant's address is on file with the sponsor and in SEVIS; a lease is a document the student
  must report within ten days.
- (d) anti-retaliation: "No sponsor or employee of a sponsor may threaten program termination, remove from the
  program, ban from the program, adversely annotate an exchange visitor's SEVIS record, or otherwise retaliate
  against an exchange visitor solely because he/she has filed a complaint".
- 62.9(d)(3), https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.9, read 2026-09-08: recruitment materials must
  explain "the terms and conditions of any employment activities (job duties, number of work hours, wages and
  compensation, and any typical deductions for housing and transportation)" and "must make clear to prospective
  exchange visitors in the exchange categories with a work component that their stipend or wages might not
  cover all of their expenses and that they should bring additional personal funds."

### Termination: 22 CFR 62.40

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.40, "§ 62.40 Termination of program participation.", read
2026-09-08. Quote: "(a) A sponsor shall terminate an exchange visitor's participation in its program when the
exchange visitor: (1) Fails to pursue the activities for which he or she was admitted to the United States;
(2) Is unable to continue, unless otherwise exempted pursuant to these regulations; (3) Violates the Exchange
Visitor Program regulations and/or the sponsor's rules governing the program, if, in the sponsor's opinion,
termination is warranted; (4) Willfully fails to maintain the insurance coverage required under § 62.14 of
these regulations; or (b) An exchange visitor's participation in the Exchange Visitor Program is subject to
termination when he or she engages in unauthorized employment. Upon establishing such violation, the
Department of State shall terminate the exchange visitor's participation in the Exchange Visitor Program."
Losing housing is not a listed ground. Failing to report a change of residence is a sponsor-rule ground under
62.32(e)(9).

### The IPMC occupancy text (Section 404)

The ICC's own page (https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IPMC2021P1/chapter-4-light-ventilation-and-occupancy-limitations)
returned only a "Digital Codes Premium" shell to curl and 403 to WebFetch. The text below is the 2018 IPMC
Section 404 as printed in an ICC-licensed PDF posted by the City of Athens, Alabama ("2018 International
Property Maintenance Code (IPMC)", "Second Version: Apr 2018", "PDF from:
http://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IPMC2018P2/chapter-4-light-ventilation-and-occupancy-limitations"),
https://www.athensalabama.us/DocumentCenter/View/3051/Occupancy-Limitations-2018-IPMC-Section-404-12262024,
downloaded and read 2026-09-08. The same numbers appear in the GSA Property Maintenance Code 2024 shown at
https://up.codes/s/occupancy-limitations (read 2026-09-08). Whether the 2021 or 2024 IPMC changed any of these
numbers could not be checked at the ICC (see "Could not verify"); the 2006 edition (Felton, DE copy) lacked the
per-occupant rule, so the edition matters.

- "404.4.1 Room area. Every living room shall contain not less than 120 square feet (11.2 m2) and every
  bedroom shall contain not less than 70 square feet (6.5 m2) and every bedroom occupied by more than one
  person shall contain not less than 50 square feet (4.6 m2) of floor area for each occupant thereof."
- "404.4.2 Access from bedrooms. Bedrooms shall not constitute the only means of access to other bedrooms or
  habitable spaces and shall not serve as the only means of egress from other habitable spaces. Exception:
  Units that contain fewer than two bedrooms."
- "404.4.3 Water closet accessibility. Every bedroom shall have access to not less than one water closet and
  one lavatory without passing through another bedroom. Every bedroom in a dwelling unit shall have access to
  not less than one water closet and lavatory located in the same story as the bedroom or an adjacent story."
- "404.4.4 Prohibited occupancy. Kitchens and nonhabitable spaces shall not be used for sleeping purposes."
- "404.4.5 Other requirements. Bedrooms shall comply with the applicable provisions of this code including,
  but not limited to, the light, ventilation, room area, ceiling height and room width requirements of this
  chapter; the plumbing facilities and water-heating facilities requirements of Chapter 5; the heating
  facilities and electrical receptacle requirements of Chapter 6; and the smoke detector and emergency escape
  requirements of Chapter 7."
- "404.5 Overcrowding. Dwelling units shall not be occupied by more occupants than permitted by the minimum
  area requirements of Table 404.5." Table 404.5 "MINIMUM AREA REQUIREMENTS", "MINIMUM AREA IN SQUARE FEET",
  columns "1-2 occupants", "3-5 occupants", "6 or more occupants". The Athens PDF's table cells did not
  extract; the up.codes GSA 2024 rendering of the same table, read 2026-09-08: "Living room: 120 sq ft (1-2
  occupants), 120 sq ft (3-5), 150 sq ft (6+)"; "Dining room: No requirement (1-2), 80 sq ft (3-5), 100 sq ft
  (6+)"; "Bedrooms: Must comply with Section 404.4.1 standards". (The 2010 New York State PMC copy at
  Elmsford, NY, read 2026-09-08, prints "Living rooma No requirements 120 150 | Dining rooma No requirements
  80 100"; New York adds a kitchen row the IPMC does not have.)
- "404.5.1 Sleeping area. The minimum occupancy area required by Table 404.5 shall not be included as a
  sleeping area in determining the minimum occupancy area for sleeping purposes. Sleeping areas shall comply
  with Section 404.4."
- "404.5.2 Combined spaces. Combined living room and dining room spaces shall comply with the requirements of
  Table 404.5 if the total area is equal to that required for separate rooms and if the space is located so as
  to function as a combination living room/dining room."
- "404.2 Minimum room widths. A habitable room, other than a kitchen, shall be not less than 7 feet (2134 mm)
  in any plan dimension." "404.3 Minimum ceiling heights. Habitable spaces ... shall have a minimum clear
  ceiling height of 7 feet (2134 mm)."
- "404.6 Efficiency unit ... 4. The maximum number of occupants shall be three."

### The owner's example: 40 people in 2,500 square feet plus 20 in a detached garage at $700 each (computed)

The code counts bedroom floor area, not the gross size of the house. Rules used: 70 sq ft for a one-person
bedroom; 50 sq ft per person for two or more (404.4.1); living and dining minimum areas cannot be sleeping
area (404.5.1); kitchens and nonhabitable spaces cannot be slept in (404.4.4); a detached garage is not a
habitable space unless it was converted under permit (the 2021 Myrtle Beach case below was charged for exactly
that).
- Illustrative 2,500 sq ft four-bedroom plan: one 14 ft x 16 ft bedroom (224 sq ft) holds 4 (224 / 50 =
  4.48, round down); three 12 ft x 12 ft bedrooms (144 sq ft) hold 2 each (144 / 50 = 2.88); total 10
  people. If the three secondary bedrooms are 12 ft x 14 ft (168 sq ft) they hold 3 each: total 13.
- To sleep 40 people lawfully the house would need 40 x 50 = 2,000 sq ft of bedroom floor area, leaving 500
  sq ft for a living room (150 sq ft minimum at 6 or more occupants), a dining room (100 sq ft), the kitchen,
  bathrooms and halls. No ordinary 2,500 sq ft plan has that, and each bedroom would still need its own path
  to a toilet without passing through another bedroom (404.4.3), egress and smoke detectors (404.4.5).
- The detached garage: 0 lawful sleepers unless converted with permits and a certificate of occupancy.
- Revenue: 60 x $700 = $42,000 per month as reported; a lawful 10 to 13 beds at $600 = $6,000 to $7,800 per
  month. (Rent figures in section 9.)

## 5. Removing a J-1 tenant

- The Act applies to the dwelling, not the tenant's status. SC Code 27-40-110,
  https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c040.php, read 2026-09-08: "This chapter applies to, regulates, and
  determines rights, obligations, and remedies under a rental agreement, wherever made, for a dwelling unit
  located within this State." 27-40-210 definition: "'tenant' means a person entitled under a rental agreement
  to occupy a dwelling unit to the exclusion of others". Neither section mentions citizenship or immigration
  status (absence, after reading).
- Exclusion that matters for motel-style J-1 housing, 27-40-120(4): the chapter does not govern "transient
  occupancy in a hotel, motel, or other accommodations subject to the sales tax on accommodations as provided
  by SECTION 12-36-920".
- Grounds, SC Code 27-37-10(A), https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c037.php, read 2026-09-08: "The tenant
  may be ejected upon application of the landlord or his agent when (1) the tenant fails or refuses to pay the
  rent when due or when demanded, (2) the term of tenancy or occupancy has ended, or (3) the terms or
  conditions of the lease have been violated."
- Rent notice, 27-40-710(B): "If rent is unpaid when due and the tenant fails to pay rent within five days from
  the date due ... the landlord may terminate the rental agreement provided the landlord has given the tenant
  written notice"; the bold lease clause "IF YOU DO NOT PAY YOUR RENT ON TIME ..." satisfies the notice. Other
  breaches, 27-40-710(A): written notice, termination "upon a date not less than fourteen days after receipt of
  the notice, if the breach is not remedied in fourteen days." Overcrowding by the tenants (more occupants than
  the lease allows) is a lease breach under (A); note the landlord is the one the city cites for the code
  violation (§ 6-400, § 10-56 above).
- The magistrate process, service, the 24-hour writ and the fee schedule are on /invest/landlord-rules/ (read
  2026-09-08); do not repeat them on the J-1 page. Link to it.
- Short term, 27-40-310(d): "Unless the rental agreement fixes a definite term, the tenancy is week to week in
  case of a roomer who pays weekly rent and in all other cases month to month." Weekly-rent J-1 houses without
  a fixed term are week-to-week: 27-40-770 (WebFetch of the same page, read 2026-09-08): "The landlord or the
  tenant may terminate a week-to-week tenancy by a written notice given to the other at least seven days before
  the termination date specified in the notice." A fixed 3- to 4-month term ends on its date; a holdover
  "remains in possession without the landlord's consent after expiration of the term" and "the landlord may
  bring an action for possession" (27-40-770). Abandonment, 27-40-730: "The unexplained absence of a tenant
  from a dwelling unit for a period of fifteen days after default in the payment of rent must be construed as
  abandonment"; if utilities were shut off by the tenant, "abandonment is considered immediate".
- Deposit, 27-40-410: itemized notice "within thirty days after termination of the tenancy and delivery of
  possession and demand by the tenant, whichever is later"; wrongful withholding costs "three times the amount
  wrongfully withheld and reasonable attorney's fees." The tenant must give "a forwarding address or new
  address" (landlord-rules-facts.md section 1). For a student who leaves the country, that address is abroad.
- Immigration status and eviction (secondary, advocacy source): SC Appleseed, "Know Your Rights as An
  Immigrant Looking for Housing in South Carolina: Frequently Asked Questions" (undated PDF,
  https://www.scjustice.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Immigrant-Housing-FAQ.pdf, downloaded and read
  2026-09-08): "Everyone, regardless of immigration status, has a right to be protected from discrimination by
  landlords, including discrimination based on someone's immigration status." Listed as actions that "could
  be illegal": "A landlord requires a Social Security Number and won't accept an ITIN as a substitute." and "A
  landlord requires proof of citizenship or immigration status." Also: "it is illegal to threaten to report
  someone to Immigrations and Customs Enforcement (ICE) for trying to assert their rights to safe housing."
- What happens to the student's program when housing ends: the sponsor must help, 62.32(g)(9)(i) ("sponsors
  must actively and immediately assist participants with arranging appropriate housing"); the student must
  report the new address within ten days (62.10(d)(3)); losing housing is not a termination ground (62.40);
  the CIEE handbook lists eviction as a reason to call its line: "Call 888-268-6245 if: ... You are evicted
  from housing." (CIEE Work & Travel USA Orientation Handbook PDF,
  https://beacon.ciee.org/forms/CIEE_Work_Travel_USA_Orientation_Handbook.pdf, downloaded and read
  2026-09-08.) Local practice: officials relocated the three students in the 2021 garage case; the city says
  it needs "a place to move those young adults to" (WMBF 2025). No source says a job is lost when housing is
  lost. The reverse rule exists at one sponsor: Greenheart, read 2026-09-08: "Employment must not be contingent
  on the participant living in host organization-provided housing." InterExchange (cooperator guide): where
  housing is in the job offer, "Students cannot leave housing unless their work assignment has ended or
  arrangements have been made with InterExchange."
- The student must leave the country within 30 days of the DS-2019 end date (8 CFR 214.2(j)(1)(ii), section
  1), so a lease cannot lawfully run past that.

## 6. Fair housing

- Federal statute, 42 U.S.C. 3604, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3604, read 2026-09-08. (a): it
  is unlawful "To refuse to sell or rent after the making of a bona fide offer, or to refuse to negotiate for
  the sale or rental of, or otherwise make unavailable or deny, a dwelling to any person because of race,
  color, religion, sex, familial status, or national origin." (b): "To discriminate against any person in the
  terms, conditions, or privileges of sale or rental of a dwelling, or in the provision of services or
  facilities in connection therewith, because of race, color, religion, sex, familial status, or national
  origin." (c): any "notice, statement, or advertisement ... that indicates any preference, limitation, or
  discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin". (d):
  "To represent to any person because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national
  origin that any dwelling is not available for inspection, sale, or rental when such dwelling is in fact so
  available."
- South Carolina statute, SC Code 31-21-40, https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t31c021.php, read 2026-09-08:
  "It is unlawful: (1) to refuse to sell or rent after the making of a bona fide offer, to refuse to negotiate
  for the sale or rental of, or otherwise to make unavailable or deny a dwelling to any person because of race,
  color, religion, sex, familial status, or national origin; (2) to discriminate against any person in the
  terms, conditions, or privileges of sale or rental of a dwelling ... because of race, color, religion, sex,
  familial status, or national origin; (3) to make, print, or publish ... any notice, statement, or
  advertisement ... that indicates any preference, limitation, or discrimination based on race, color,
  religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin".
- HUD on English. Press release HUD No. 16-135, 2016-09-15, https://archives.hud.gov/news/2016/pr16-135.cfm,
  read 2026-09-08: "The Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination on seven protected bases, including national
  origin, which is closely linked to the ability to communicate proficiently in English." Prohibited
  practices listed: "applying a language-related requirement to people of certain races or nationalities;
  posting advertisements that contain blanket statements, such as 'all tenants must speak English;' or
  immediately turning away applicants who are not fluent in English." Gustavo Velasquez: "Having a limited
  ability to speak English should never be a reason to be denied a home." Complaints: "(800) 669-9777".
- HUD Office of General Counsel, "Guidance on Fair Housing Act Protections for Persons with Limited English
  Proficiency", September 15, 2016. HUD's own copy is gone (404 at the old path); read from the copy hosted by
  the National Housing Law Project, https://www.nhlp.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/HUD-Office-of-General-Counsel-Guidance.pdf,
  downloaded and read 2026-09-08. Quotes: "Individuals who are LEP are not a protected class under the Act. The
  Act nonetheless prohibits housing providers from using LEP selectively"; "a blanket refusal to deal with LEP
  persons in the housing context is likely not motivated by genuine communication concerns. Suspect practices
  include advertisements containing blanket statements such as 'all tenants must speak English,' or turning
  away all applicants who are not fluent in English."; "Selective application of a language-related policy,
  or use of LEP as a pretext for unequal treatment of individuals based on race, national origin, or other
  protected characteristics, violates the Act."
- The live hud.gov pages (fair_housing_act_overview, fair_housing_rights_and_obligations,
  limited_english_proficiency_0, /fairhousing, /stat/fheo/...) returned only navigation shells or 403 on
  2026-09-08; no quote could be taken from them.
- What a landlord may ask for, per sponsor guidance (not law): InterExchange housing guide, read 2026-09-08,
  "Documents Landlords May Request: Photo ID; Employer letter with salary information; Proof of sufficient
  funds from home". CIEE handbook: "You will also need to share your SSN with your landlord." (Compare SC
  Appleseed above on refusing an ITIN.) The sponsor's 24/7 number is given to every participant (62.32(e)(4)),
  so a tenant can supply it. No source opened says a landlord may require the DS-2019; the CIEE handbook says
  the DS-2019 is what the student takes to the Social Security office ("DS-2019, passport & J-1 visa, I-94"
  appear in sponsor arrival checklists). Apply one screening standard to every applicant (see
  /invest/landlord-rules/ screening section).
