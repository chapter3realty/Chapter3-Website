## 2. State law on the bidding period

Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t15c039.php, Title 15 Chapter 39. Curl, read 2026-09-06.

- 15-39-680, sale day. Quote: "The regular day of judicial sales is the first Monday in each month
  except when the first Monday in any month is a legal holiday in which case the sale day is on
  the Tuesday next succeeding the holiday."
- 15-39-720, bidding stays open. Quote: "the bidding shall not be closed upon the day of sale but
  shall remain open until the thirtieth day after such sale, exclusive of the day of sale".
- 15-39-720, the lender bids once. Quote: "the mortgagee or his representative shall enter such bid
  as he desires at the time the sale is made, and he and all persons acting in his behalf shall be
  precluded from entering any other bid".
- 15-39-720, the reopening. Quote: "The bidding shall be reopened by the officer making the sale on
  the thirtieth day after the sale, exclusive of the day of the sale, at eleven o'clock in the
  forenoon". Quote: "If the thirtieth day falls on Sunday the bidding shall be closed on the Monday
  immediately following."
- 15-39-740, deposits. The deposit "shall be five per cent of the bid or some lesser percentage
  thereof", and none may be required "prior to the conclusion of the bidding".
- 15-39-760, deficiency waived. Quote: "The provisions of SECTIONS 15-39-720 to 15-39-750 shall
  not apply to any suit brought for foreclosure if the complaint therein states that no personal or
  deficiency judgment is demanded and that any right to such judgment is expressly waived". Quote:
  "the sales officer shall state in the advertisement of sale that no personal or deficiency
  judgment is demanded and that the bidding will not remain open after the sale but that
  compliance with the bid may be made immediately."

Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t29c003.php, Title 29 Chapter 3. Curl, read 2026-09-06.
Sections 29-3-600 to 29-3-780 cover parties, the debt, deficiency and appraisal; none sets a
bidding period. 29-3-660 lets the court order "the payment by the mortgagor of any residue of the
mortgage debt that may remain unsatisfied after a sale of the mortgaged premises".

Source: SC Rules of Civil Procedure, Rule 71,
https://www.sccourts.org/resources/judicial-community/court-rules/civil/rule-71/, page title
"Rule 71 - South Carolina Judicial Branch", heading "RULE 71 FORECLOSURE AND PARTITION". Curl,
read 2026-09-06.

- 71(b). Quote: "Unless the pleadings state that no personal or deficiency judgment is demanded or
  any right to such judgment is expressly waived in writing, the bidding shall not be closed upon
  the day of sale". Quote: "The judgment shall also specify the amount of good faith deposit
  necessary at the time of the sale, and the date that compliance must be made with the bid."
- 71(b). Quote: "The master or other court officer making the sale shall execute a deed to the
  purchaser."

Source: SC Judicial Branch, "FAQ in SC Master-in-Equity Court", 14-page PDF,
https://www.sccourts.org/media/12mcqkhy/faq_eng_masterinequity-court.pdf; metadata created
2024-07-05, "Company: South Carolina Judicial Branch". Curl, pypdf, read 2026-09-06. It says it
"cannot be cited as legal authority". Quote: "Your home will be advertised in your local paper for
three weeks as required by law."

## 3. Right of redemption after the sale

No primary page found that states the post-sale rule in words (see Not verified). What the
primary sources literally say:

- SC Code 29-3-10 (t29c003.php, read 2026-09-06). Quote: "the mortgagor shall be deemed the owner
  of the land and the mortgagee as owner of the money lent or due". Quote: "all releases of the
  equity of redemption shall be binding and effectual in law." 29-3-20 lets a second mortgagee
  "redeem any former mortgage registered", before any sale.
- SC Code 15-39-870 (t15c039.php). Quote: "the proceedings under which such sale is made shall be
  deemed res judicata as to any and all bona fide purchasers for value without notice,
  notwithstanding such sale may not subsequently be confirmed by the court."
- SC Judicial Branch FAQ (section 2). Quote: "you own your home and may stay there until it is
  sold at a foreclosure sale and the Master-in-Equity issues a deed to the successful buyer."
- Absence finding, 2026-09-06: a case-insensitive search for "redeem" and "redemption" in the
  downloaded text of Title 15 Chapter 39 gives 0 hits; in Title 29 Chapter 3, 3 hits, all in
  29-3-10 and 29-3-20 (pre-sale). Control: Title 12 Chapter 51 (tax sales, where a redemption
  period exists) gives 36 hits. An absence in two chapters, not a statement of law.

## 4. Deed recording fee on a master's deed

Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c024.php, Title 12 Chapter 24. Curl, read 2026-09-06.

- 12-24-20(B). Quote: "In the case of a master-in-equity deed" (and deeds from governments and
  qualified retirement plans) "the fee imposed by this chapter is the liability of the grantee, or
  the joint and several liability of the grantees, and not the grantor." 12-24-20(A) makes every
  other deed's fee "the liability of the grantor".
- 12-24-10(A), the rate. Quote: "The fee is one dollar eighty-five cents for each five hundred
  dollars, or fractional part of five hundred dollars, of the realty's value as determined by
  Section 12-24-30."

## 5. What title the master's deed passes

- SC Code 15-39-830 (t15c039.php, read 2026-09-06). Quote: "Upon a judicial sale being made and the
  terms complied with the officer making the sale must execute a conveyance to the purchaser which
  shall be effectual to pass the rights and interests adjudged to be sold."
- SC Code 15-39-880. Liens of parties to the case end "after a public sale of such real property
  at any execution or judicial sale in any action or special proceeding to which the lien creditor
  is duly made a party as provided by law". Liens not in the case survive. Quote: "shall not be
  construed to affect any prior mortgage lien not foreclosed in any such action or special
  proceeding". The section does "not apply to tax execution sales."
- SCRCP 71(b) (section 2). Quote: "notice of any senior liens, taxes, or other rights to which the
  property to be sold is subject".
- Ex parte Johnson, SC Court of Appeals Opinion No. 4165, filed October 16, 2006, refiled December
  18, 2006, Richland County, https://www.sccourts.org/media/opinions/HTMLFiles/COA/4165.htm. Curl,
  read 2026-09-06. The court, quoting Trustees of Wofford College v. Burnett (1946): "real property
  subject to taxes cannot be sold in foreclosure free of the existing tax liens, unless provision
  for payment is made and they are paid." Quote: "by the express terms of the sale, Johnson and all
  other bidders understood that liability for the outstanding tax liens would fall to the
  successful bidder." That master's deed was "subject to assessments, Richland County taxes,
  existing easements, easements and restrictions of record, and other senior encumbrances."
- Horry bidder sheet (section 1): "The Master's Deed is not a Warranty Deed."

## 8. Occupants after foreclosure: Protecting Tenants at Foreclosure Act

Source: 12 U.S.C. 5220, statutory note "Effect of Foreclosure on Preexisting Tenancy", Pub. L.
111-22, div. A, title VII, section 702, May 20, 2009, as amended by Pub. L. 111-203 section
1484(1). https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title12-section5220&num=0&edition=prelim
Curl, read 2026-09-06.

- Quote: "any immediate successor in interest in such property pursuant to the foreclosure shall
  assume such interest subject to". (1) Quote: "the provision, by such successor in interest of a
  notice to vacate to any bona fide tenant at least 90 days before the effective date of such
  notice".
- (2)(A) Quote: "under any bona fide lease entered into before the notice of foreclosure to occupy
  the premises until the end of the remaining term of the lease". Exception. Quote: "a successor
  in interest may terminate a lease effective on the date of sale of the unit to a purchaser who
  will occupy the unit as a primary residence", still "subject to the receipt by the tenant of the
  90 day notice". (2)(B) Quote: "without a lease or with a lease terminable at will under State
  law, subject to the receipt by the tenant of the 90 day notice".
- Quote: "nothing under this section shall affect the requirements for termination of any Federal-
  or State-subsidized tenancy or of any State or local law that provides longer time periods or
  other additional protections for tenants".
- Bona fide, (b). Quote: "(1) the mortgagor or the child, spouse, or parent of the mortgagor under
  the contract is not the tenant; (2) the lease or tenancy was the result of an arms-length
  transaction". Quote: "(3) the lease or tenancy requires the receipt of rent that is not
  substantially less than fair market rent for the property".
- Trigger date, (c). Quote: "the date of a notice of foreclosure shall be deemed to be the date on
  which complete title to a property is transferred to a successor entity or person as a result of
  an order of a court".

Source: Public Law 115-174, May 24, 2018, section 304,
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-115publ174/html/PLAW-115publ174.htm. Curl, read
2026-09-06. Quote: "Sections 701 through 703 of the Protecting Tenants at Foreclosure Act of 2009"
"as were in effect on December 30, 2014, are restored and revived." Quote: "(c) Effective
Date.--Subsections (a) and (b) shall take effect on the date that is 30 days after the date of
enactment of this Act." SC Judicial Branch FAQ (section 2): "As a tenant you may have rights under
federal law that could allow you to stay in your home for a period of time."

## 9. Financing a courthouse purchase

No primary page found that says FHA or conventional loans cannot be used at the sale (see Not
verified). What the primary sources literally say: the Horry terms are certified funds for the 5%
by noon the next day and full compliance within 30 days, deposit forfeited on default (section 1);
neither county document mentions financing or a contingency. SC Code 15-39-710 (t15c039.php, read
2026-09-06) covers sheriff's execution sales, not master's sales: "Every sheriff's sale made by
virtue of the directions of an execution shall be for cash." HUD's REO listings, by contrast,
carry FHA financing codes (section 7).
