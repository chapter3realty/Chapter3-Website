# Hiring a property manager in Horry County / Myrtle Beach, SC — verified facts

Compiled 2026-09-05. Facts only; no page copy. Every fact below gives the claim, the primary URL opened, a verbatim quote under 40 words, and the read date.

Method note. Every URL listed was opened with the fetch tool on 2026-09-05. For statute pages and PDFs the fetch tool returns a summary, so the exact wording was taken from the same URL downloaded directly (curl) and searched as text. Where the fetch tool could not open a source at all, that is stated. Quotes keep the source's own punctuation; ellipses mark omitted words.

Word "manager" below means a person or firm managing rental property for an owner for compensation, unless a quote says otherwise.

## 1. Licensing (SC Code Title 40, Chapter 57; SC LLR Real Estate Commission)

Source A: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php (read 2026-09-05)
Source B: https://www.llr.sc.gov/re/sbreq.aspx (read 2026-09-05)
Source C: https://llr.sc.gov/re/propmanagerincharge.aspx (read 2026-09-05)
Source D: https://llr.sc.gov/re/ (read 2026-09-05)

1.1 Managing rentals for others for compensation requires a licence. Section 40-57-20: "It is unlawful for an individual to act as a real estate broker, real estate associate, or real estate property manager or to advertise or provide services as such without an active, valid license issued by the commission." (Source A)

1.2 Two licence routes exist. A broker licence covers leasing: Section 40-57-30(6)(a) defines a broker as one who "negotiates or attempts to negotiate the listing, sale, purchase, exchange, lease, or other disposition of real estate or the improvements to the real estate". (Source A)

1.3 The separate property manager licence covers rentals only. Section 40-57-30(29): "'Property manager' means a supervised licensee who meets educational requirements and passes the examination for a property manager license, and who will for a fee, salary, commission, other valuable consideration..." "(a) negotiate or attempt to negotiate the rental or leasing of real estate or improvements to the real estate;" (Source A)

1.4 Definition of property manager-in-charge. Section 40-57-30(30): "'Property manager-in-charge' means a property manager who is designated as having the responsibility over the actions of supervised licensees and also the responsibility and control over and liability for real estate trust accounts." (Source A)

1.5 A property manager works under a manager-in-charge or broker-in-charge. Section 40-57-30: "'Supervised licensee' means a licensee affiliated with and under the supervision of a broker-in-charge or property manager-in-charge." (Source A)

1.6 An owner managing their own property needs no licence. Section 40-57-240(1): the chapter does not apply to "the sale, lease, or rental of real estate by an unlicensed owner of real estate who owns any interest in the real estate if the interest being sold, leased, or rented is identical to the owner's legal interest". (Source A)

1.7 Ages. Section 40-57-510(A): "(1) attain the age of twenty-one if applying for a property manager-in-charge; (2) attain the age of eighteen if applying for a license as a property manager;" (Source A)

1.8 Education for the property manager licence. "A prospective licensee must be a high school graduate or hold a certificate of equivalency and must complete a Commission-approved thirty-hour Property Management course in the fundamentals and principles of property management." (Source B)

1.9 No experience needed for the exam. "No licensed experience is required to qualify for the property management examination." (Source B)

1.10 Manager-in-charge needs an extra course. Applicants "complete an additional seven hour Commission-approved PMIC qualifying course". Fees on the page: exam $63, property manager two-year licence $125, manager-in-charge two-year licence $250. (Source B)

1.11 A broker can become a manager-in-charge without the property manager licence. "If you do not hold an active Broker or Broker-in-Charge license with South Carolina, you must first apply for a SC Property Manager license." (Source C)

1.12 The Commission regulates managers. "The Real Estate Commission regulates real estate brokers, associates, and property managers." (Source D)

## 2. Trust accounts (SC Code 40-57-136; definitions in 40-57-30)

Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php (read 2026-09-05)

2.1 Trust funds go in a designated account. Section 40-57-136(A)(1): a broker-in-charge or property manager-in-charge "shall establish and maintain control of and responsibility for an active real estate trust account which must be a demand deposit account designated and titled to include the word 'trust' or the word 'escrow'".

2.2 Purpose. Section 40-57-30 defines a trust account as one "established and maintained by a broker-in-charge or a property manager-in-charge to safeguard funds belonging to parties to a real estate transaction".

2.3 No commingling. Section 40-57-136(B)(3): the manager-in-charge "may not commingle trust funds of the client with his own money".

2.4 Deposit deadline. Section 40-57-136(C)(1)(a)(i): "cash or certified funds must be deposited within forty-eight hours of receipt, excluding Saturday, Sunday, and bank holidays".

2.5 Deposits stay in trust for the whole lease. Section 40-57-136(C)(2): "security deposits, pet deposits, damage deposits, and advance rentals, except earned rental proceeds, and deposited in the trust account must remain in the trust account until the lease or rental transaction expires or is terminated".

2.6 Rent goes to the owner promptly. Same subsection: "Earned rental proceeds must be disbursed to the landlord within a reasonable time after clearance of the deposit by the bank."

2.7 Exception for an owner's own employee. Section 40-57-136(C)(1)(b): "Rent received by a licensee who is directly employed by the owner of rental property may be deposited in an operating or other similar account". "However, an advance rental deposit is a trust fund and must be treated as such."

## 3. Security deposits (SC Code 27-40-410)

Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c040.php (read 2026-09-05)

3.1 No cap. Section 27-40-410 sets no maximum deposit. A text search of the whole chapter found no dollar or months-of-rent limit anywhere. The section says only that "property or money held by the landlord as security must be returned less amounts withheld by the landlord for accrued rent and damages".

3.2 Itemised written notice. Subsection (a): "Any deduction from the security/rental deposit must be itemized by the landlord in a written notice to the tenant together with the amount due, if any".

3.3 Thirty-day deadline. Subsection (a): the notice and balance are due "within thirty days after termination of the tenancy and delivery of possession and demand by the tenant, whichever is later".

3.4 Tenant must give an address. Subsection (a): "The tenant shall provide the landlord in writing with a forwarding address or new address to which the written notice and amount due from the landlord may be sent."

3.5 Penalty. Subsection (b): if the landlord fails to return the deposit with the required notice, "the tenant may recover the property and money in an amount equal to three times the amount wrongfully withheld and reasonable attorney's fees".

3.6 Posting rule for larger landlords. Subsection (c) applies where a landlord "rents more than four adjoining dwelling units on the premises, and (2) imposes different standards for calculating security/rental deposits required of different tenants"; the standards must be posted or given to each applicant.

3.7 Binds the buyer of a rental. Subsection (e): "the holder of the landlord's interest in the premises at the time of the termination of the tenancy is bound by this section."

## 4. Landlord disclosure (SC Code 27-40-420)

Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t27c040.php (read 2026-09-05)

4.1 Written disclosure of owner or agent. Subsection (a): the landlord or anyone signing for the landlord "shall disclose to the tenant in writing at or before the commencement of the tenancy the name and address of an owner of the premises or a person authorized to act on behalf of the owner as agent".

4.2 Must stay current and binds a new manager. Subsection (b): "The information required to be furnished by this section must be kept current and this section extends to and is enforceable against any successor landlord, owner, or manager."

4.3 Consequence for a manager who fails to disclose. Subsection (c): "A person authorized to enter in a rental agreement on behalf of a landlord who fails to comply with subsection (a)... becomes an agent of the landlord for purposes of that rental agreement", including service of process and performing the landlord's obligations from rent collected.

## 5. Written management agreement (SC Code 40-57-135(J) and (K))

Source: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t40c057.php (read 2026-09-05)

5.1 Writing is required. "A real estate brokerage firm shall manage residential and commercial real estate under a written management agreement that shall set forth, at a minimum:" (1) names and signatures of the parties; (2) the property identification; (3) the method of compensation to the licensee; (4) renewal terms; (5) renewal compensation; (6) terms and conditions of tenant rental or lease arrangements.

5.2 Automatic renewal limit. Item (4): an automatic renewal clause is allowed only with a clause that "allows either party to cancel the management agreement for any cause or no cause with thirty days' notice after the original definite expiration date".

5.3 Renewal commissions must be conspicuous. Item (5): "compensation for a future lease renewal by tenants, and if included, the contract must contain a clause in underlined capital letters on the first page providing for such future compensation".

5.4 No tie-in to a future sale listing. Item (6): "a management agreement may not contain a provision binding the property under a future listing agreement if the property is to be sold in the future, in which case a separate listing agreement is required".

5.5 Unlicensed staff limits. Subsection (K): an unlicensed person may not "discuss, negotiate, or explain a contract, listing agreement, buyer agency agreement, lease, agreement, property management agreement, or other real estate document" or "approve applications or leases or settle or arrange the terms and conditions of a lease".

## 8. Accommodations tax when a manager or platform collects the rent

Source A: https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t12c036.php (read 2026-09-05)
Source B: https://dor.sc.gov/sales-use-tax-index/accommodations (read 2026-09-05; same content at https://dor.sc.gov/tax/accommodations)
Source C: SC DOR Sales and Use Tax Manual, Chapter 11 "Accommodations", September 2025, https://dor.sc.gov/sites/dor/files/Documents/Policy%20Manuals/Chapter%2011%20-%20Accommodations.pdf (read 2026-09-05; PDF text extracted after download)
Source D: SC Revenue Ruling #19-6 (effective April 26, 2019), https://dor.sc.gov/sales-use-tax-marketplace-facilitators-and-third-parties-whose-products-are-sold-marketplace-guidance-and-tax (read 2026-09-05; fetch returned the PDF, text extracted after download)

8.1 The tax. Section 12-36-920(A): "A sales tax equal to seven percent is imposed on the gross proceeds derived from the rental or charges for any rooms, campground spaces, lodgings, or sleeping accommodations furnished to transients". (Source A) DOR breaks it down as "5% Sales Tax 2% Accommodations Tax Any local Sales & Use Tax that is collected on behalf of counties by the SCDOR." (Source B)

8.2 Who owes it. Section 12-36-920(E): "The taxes imposed by this section are imposed on every person engaged or continuing within this State in the business of furnishing accommodations to transients for consideration." (Source A)

8.3 DOR: the booker remits. "The person or business who books the accommodations is responsible for collecting and paying Accommodations Tax. That may be the property owner or a real estate agent, listing service, broker, Online Travel Company (VRBO, Expedia, etc.), or similar entity." (Source B)

8.4 DOR manual, same rule: "The person liable for the tax is the person in the business of furnishing the accommodations, whether such person is the owner or a real estate agent, listing service, broker, online travel company, or similar entity handling the accommodations." (Source C, page 2)

8.5 Platforms. "If you use an online travel company for reserving rooms and accepting payment for the booking, they are responsible for the tax for the full amount of the booking." (Source B)

8.6 Owner still owes tax on self-booked stays. "If you don't use property management or an online travel company to book all of your rentals, you are responsible for collecting and paying Accommodations Tax for the rentals that you booked without the service..." (Source B)

8.7 Retail licence. "If you are directly booking short-term rentals, you must have a Retail License to file and pay Accommodations Tax." Owners renting "exclusively through a property management or online travel company" are told: "Therefore, you are not required to have a Retail License with South Carolina." (Source B)

8.8 Manager's service fees are taxable. "any service fees charged by a property management or online travel company are considered part of the cost of the rental and are subject to the tax". (Source B)

8.9 Manager must report dropped listings. Section 12-36-920(C): "Real estate agents, brokers, corporations, or listing services required to remit taxes under this section shall notify the department if rental property, previously listed by them, is dropped from their listings." (Source A)

8.10 Exemptions in the statute. Section 12-36-920(A): no tax "where the facilities consist of less than six sleeping rooms, contained on the same premises, which is used as the individual's place of abode"; no tax on "gross proceeds from rental income wholly excluded from the gross income of the taxpayer pursuant to Internal Revenue Code Section 280A(g)"; stays "supplied to the same person for a period of ninety continuous days are not considered proceeds from transients". (Source A)

8.11 The 15-day rule spelled out. "For this exemption to apply, the taxpayer must rent the dwelling unit for less than 15 days during the taxable year and must use the dwelling unit as a residence... 14 days or more during the taxable year." (Source C, page 2)

8.12 Using an agent voids the small-home exemption. DOR example: "the rental charges paid to the real estate agent by the vacationers are subject to the sales tax on accommodations with the real estate agent liable for the tax". (Source C, page 8) DOR's web page states the exemption requires that "the owner or operator does not use a rental agency or online travel company to rent rooms". (Source B)

8.13 Local add-ons exist but are not DOR's. "local governments may impose a local accommodations tax of up to 3%, and some municipalities may impose a beach preservation fee of up to 1%"; these "are collected by the local government imposing the tax or fee, not the Department of Revenue." (Source C, page 1 footnote 2)

8.14 Marketplace facilitator statute. Section 12-36-71(A)(1): a marketplace facilitator is "any person engaged in the business of facilitating a retail sale of tangible personal property by: (a) listing or advertising, or allowing the listing or advertising of, the products of another person" and "(b) collecting or processing payments from the purchaser, either directly or indirectly through an agreement or arrangement with a third party." Section 12-36-1340 requires a seller to "collect and remit the tax in accordance with this chapter and shall obtain from the department a retail license as provided in this chapter, if the retail seller: ... (5) operates as a marketplace facilitator". (Source A)

8.15 DOR applies it to lodging. "A marketplace facilitator is the retailer and is responsible for remitting state and local sales and use tax for all products sold via its marketplace". "A marketplace facilitator can sell or lease any type of product subject to sales and use tax, including sales of meals, clothing, lodging, vehicles, machinery, and equipment." (Source D)

8.16 Horry County hospitality fee, unincorporated vs city.
Source: https://www.horrycountysc.gov/departments/treasurer/hospitality-fee/ (read 2026-09-05). "Businesses located outside the city limits will calculate the fee at 2.5% of gross proceeds from the Sale of Food/Beverages, 3% of gross proceeds from Rental of Transient Accommodations". "Businesses located inside city limits will calculate the fee at 1.5% of gross proceeds from the Sale of Food/Beverages, Rental of Transient Accommodation and paid Admissions". "The payment of the fee is the liability of the consumer of the services and due to the service provider upon delivery of the service. The service provider remits fees collected to the Horry County Hospitality Fee Department." "If accommodations are arranged by a property manager, then ensure the property manager is collecting and remitting Hospitality Fees." "Long-term accommodation rentals 90 consecutive days at the same location to the same patron does not require collection and remittance of the Hospitality Fee."

8.17 County flow chart for STR owners.
Source: https://www.horrycountysc.gov/media/monjnvh0/sales-tax-chart-short-term-rentals.pdf (read 2026-09-05; PDF text extracted after download). Title: "Sales Taxes/Fees and related filings required when your real property in unincorporated Horry County is used to generate income from short-term rentals". Steps named: get a DOR sales tax number; get a county business licence; a county hospitality/accommodations account "will be setup for you"; file "PT-100" with the county by April 30; file DOR forms ST-388, ST-3T, ST-389. The chart links to the retired sctax.org domain, so it is old.

## 10. Fair housing

Source A: https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/fair-housing-act-overview (read 2026-09-05)
Source B: HUD booklet "Fair Housing: Equal Opportunity for All", https://www.hud.gov/sites/documents/fheo_booklet_eng.pdf (read 2026-09-05; PDF text extracted after download)
Source C: 42 U.S.C. 3604, https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title42-section3604&num=0&edition=prelim (read 2026-09-05)

10.1 Applies to renting. "The Fair Housing Act protects people from discrimination when they are renting or buying a home, getting a mortgage, seeking housing assistance, or engaging in other housing-related activities." (A)

10.2 Protected classes. "The Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination in housing because of: Race Color National Origin Religion Sex Familial Status Disability" (A)

10.3 Tenant selection. "In the Sale and Rental of Housing: No one may take any of the following actions based on race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, or national origin: • Refuse to rent or sell housing"; also "Set different terms, conditions or privileges for sale or rental of a dwelling" and "Falsely deny that housing is available for inspection, sale or rental". (B)

10.4 Advertising, statute text. 3604(c): unlawful "To make, print, or publish, or cause to be made, printed, or published any notice, statement, or advertisement, with respect to the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicates any preference, limitation, or discrimination". 3604(a): unlawful "To refuse to sell or rent after the making of a bona fide offer, or to refuse to negotiate for the sale or rental of, or otherwise make unavailable or deny, a dwelling to any person..." (C)

## Not verified

- National average management fee "8-12%" or "8.49%" and leasing fee "50-100% of first month's rent". Found only on vendor and brokerage blogs, some attributing NARPM. NARPM's 2019 Financial Benchmarks Guide (opened) prints no such average. NARPM's 2017 member survey (opened, https://www.narpm.org/docs/about/17membersurvey.pdf) has no fee question. Buildium's 2015 infographic post (opened) says data came from "nearly 500 property managers" but the numbers are inside an image. Buildium's 2009 post (opened) says fees "are set at 10 percent... (and go down to as low as 6 percent)"; vendor blog, 2009. AppFolio's 2026 Benchmark Report and the Buildium/NARPM 2026 industry report are gated downloads; not opened. Leads only; do not cite.
- Renewal fee norms: no primary source.
- Horry County "$10,000" rule. WPDE (2015 article, opened) described a rule exempting rental income under $10,000 when a management company collects hospitality fees. The current ordinance text (9.8) has no dollar threshold. Treat the $10,000 figure as obsolete.
- "Ordinance 24-18" linked from the county licence page: not opened; may amend Article IV. Check before publishing county licence facts.
- City of Myrtle Beach: whether a management company's licence covers an owner's unit — not stated on any city page opened. Ask the Business License Division (843-918-1200 per page B).
- City STR zoning rules (which districts allow rentals under 90 days): page C covers this but only a summary was returned; not quoted verbatim.
- Local accommodations tax rates for the City and County (the "up to 3%" tax) and any beach preservation fee: not verified from the city or county code.
- Whether the marketplace facilitator statute (tangible personal property) reaches accommodations by its own words: the statute does not say so; DOR RR 19-6 and the DOR FAQ say platforms remit. Cite DOR, not the statute, for this.
- 27-40-410 "no cap": based on my search of the chapter text, not on a DOR or court statement. No source says "there is no cap" in those words.
- SC LLR propmanager.aspx (opened) is a landing page with links only; requirements were taken from sbreq.aspx.
- HUD's old URL (program_offices/...fair_housing_act_overview) returned no body text; the helping-americans URL is the live one.

## Questions only the brokerage can answer

- Does Chapter3 Realty manage property itself? If yes, who is the manager-in-charge or broker-in-charge for the trust account, and the page must say the brokerage has an interest. If no, say so plainly.
- Does Chapter3 refer owners to specific managers, and is any referral fee paid? A referral fee is a licensee activity and should be disclosed on the page.
- What happens at closing when a buyer keeps an existing tenant: how the security deposit and lease are transferred (27-40-410(e) binds the new owner) and who sends the 27-40-420 disclosure to the tenant.
- What Tim Nash has seen go wrong with resort on-site rental programs (owner-use limits, exclusivity, fee stacking, HOA rules that restrict outside managers). Must be written as observable dated facts, never as a conclusion about a named building, HOA or programme (non-negotiable 5).
- Whether the brokerage will review a management agreement for a client, or refer that to an attorney.
