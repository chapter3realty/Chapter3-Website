Rental returns

What return should a Myrtle Beach rental make?
The numbers by area, and when to walk away.

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated September 8, 2026

A Myrtle Beach house rented long-term keeps 1.1 to 2.3 percent of its price a year after costs. The rest of the return has come from the price change.

Have us run the numbers

The short answer

Two numbers. A long-term rental in Horry County keeps 1.1 to 2.3 percent of its price each year after costs. That assumes a manager and a 25 percent allowance for empty months and repairs. Self-managed with a 10 percent allowance, 2.7 to 4.6 percent. A short-term rental nets 1.3 to 5.4 percent of the typical home value, by area. Both are below the 7.3 percent a national research firm reports for single-family rentals. The price change has been the larger part of the return here. It was 6.1 percent a year over ten years and 4.8 percent over five. It fell 1.7 percent over the last three. Below 2 percent with a manager, or with a rent that does not cover the costs, we call it a dealbreaker. The map and the calculator below give the numbers for one area and one house.

What does a Myrtle Beach rental return?

A rental pays you two ways. The first is the rent left after the costs of owning the house. The second is the change in the price when you sell.

The first part is small on the Grand Strand. A typical house rented at the typical rent keeps 1.1 to 2.3 percent of its price each year after costs. That assumes a manager and a 25 percent allowance for empty months and repairs. Investors call that number the cap rate. Self-managed, with a 10 percent allowance, it is 2.7 to 4.6 percent.

The second part has been larger. The typical home value in the metro rose 6.1 percent a year over the last ten years and 4.8 percent a year over the last five. It fell 1.7 percent over the last three.

Short-term rentals sit between the two. The average listing's revenue, after 45 to 65 percent expenses, is 1.3 to 5.4 percent of the typical home value, depending on the area.

When does a number become a dealbreaker?

These are Chapter3's screening rules. They come from the numbers above, not from an industry standard. No published standard says what one rental house should return.

  1. The rent does not cover the costs. Take the rent, subtract the allowance, management, property tax, insurance and dues. If nothing is left, the house loses money before any loan. Pawleys Island at the county's three-bedroom benchmark is the live example: $1,380 a month against a $550,277 house comes up $105 short each year. That house only works as a bet on the price.
  2. The return is under 2 percent with a manager. Below 2 percent, the return depends on the price rising. The metro price fell 1.7 percent over the last three years. At the county benchmark rent, that rule flags North Myrtle Beach, Carolina Forest, Surfside Beach, Garden City, Murrells Inlet and Pawleys Island.
  3. The rent does not cover the loan. Lenders divide the monthly rent by the monthly cost of the loan, taxes, insurance and dues. Below 1.00 the rent does not cover it. Some lenders fund down to 0.75 and charge for it. Most want 1.10 to 1.25 or higher. Divide the rent by 1.25. If the lender's monthly figure is above that, the house fails at most lenders.
  4. A short-term rental that does not beat a lease. Little River is the example above. If the area's listings net no more than a long-term lease, the extra work of nightly guests earns nothing extra.
  5. A projection above 50 percent occupancy in June. AirROI's measured June occupancy is 41.5 to 48.9 percent across the eight areas it covers. A projection above 50 percent in June is above the market's best month.

Common questions

Rental returns FAQ

What is a good cap rate for a rental in Myrtle Beach?

With a manager and a 25 percent allowance for empty months and repairs, the typical house at the typical rent returns 1.1 to 2.3 percent in Horry County. Above 2 percent passes Chapter3's screen. Self-managed houses reach 2.7 to 4.6 percent. A national research firm put single-family cap rates at 7.3 percent in late 2025. Myrtle Beach returns have come from the price change and from short-term rates instead.

What is a good DSCR for a Myrtle Beach rental?

Most lenders want 1.10 to 1.25 or higher. Divide the monthly rent by the monthly cost of the loan, taxes, insurance and dues. At 1.25, a $1,823 rent supports $1,458 a month for all of those together. Some lenders fund down to 0.75 and charge for it.

Which Myrtle Beach area has the best rental returns?

Long-term, Conway and the Myrtle Beach city core, because the houses cost less. Short-term, Surfside Beach, Murrells Inlet and Garden City, where the average listing earned $33,942 to $38,356 from August 2025 to July 2026.

Why are rental returns lower in Myrtle Beach than the national number?

The typical home value rose 80 percent in ten years and rents did not keep up. A rental also pays property tax at the 6 percent ratio, which takes 1.2 to 1.6 points off the return in Horry County. The price change has made up the difference: 6.1 percent a year over ten years.

How much does a short-term rental make in Myrtle Beach?

The average active listing in the city earned $20,946 from August 2025 to July 2026, with 33 percent of nights booked. Surfside Beach averaged $35,482 and Murrells Inlet $38,356. After 45 to 65 percent expenses that is 1.3 to 5.4 percent of the typical home value, by area.

What occupancy should I expect from a Myrtle Beach Airbnb?

AirROI measures 30 to 38 percent of nights booked across the year in the eight areas it covers. June runs 41.5 to 48.9 percent and January 23.5 to 28 percent. A projection above 50 percent in June is above the market's best month.
