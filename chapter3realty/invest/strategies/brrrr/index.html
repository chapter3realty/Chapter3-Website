Strategy

The BRRRR method in Myrtle Beach:
one sum of cash, house after house.

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

BRRRR reuses one sum of cash, rental after rental. In Myrtle Beach it works on a bank-owned or worn house bought below its fixed-up value, where rent is strong.

The strategy

The BRRRR method: one sum of cash, reused house after house

BRRRR means buy, rehab, rent, refinance, repeat: one sum of cash, reused house after house. You buy a worn, outdated house cheap, fix it, rent it, then refinance to pull your cash back out. The two boxes below give you the ten-second read on whether it fits you. BRRRR is the scarcest strategy we work on the Grand Strand, because the right house is rare, and finding it is most of what Chapter3 does for a BRRRR investor.

Why people do it

  • +The same cash buys house after house
  • +Rent covers the new loan
  • +You keep the house and the rent

What can go wrong

  • !The house appraises low and your cash stays stuck
  • !The rehab runs over budget
  • !The rent does not cover the new loan payment

The five steps

How BRRRR works, step by step

  1. 1

    Buy below market. Pay a price that leaves room after repairs.

  2. 2

    Rehab it. Fix what raises the value and the rent.

  3. 3

    Rent it. A signed lease is what the refinance lender wants to see.

  4. 4

    Refinance: pull your cash back out. A new loan on the higher value hands most of your cash back.

  5. 5

    Repeat with the same cash. The money you pulled out buys the next house.

The buy

Buying below market, where the rent is strong

Aim to keep the purchase price plus rehab budget under 70 to 75 percent of the after-repair value, what the house will be worth once it is fixed up. That difference is what covers you if the work costs more than planned.

The house also has to sit where rent is strong. BRRRR pays you back through a refinance, and that loan is judged on the rent the house collects, so a cheap house on a street with weak rent does not work. Needing both in the same house is what makes these deals scarce.

On the Grand Strand, dated single-family homes in Conway, Socastee and Carolina Forest are the usual targets, and the map runs north through Longs and Little River, where prices drop again. Year-round tenants keep the rent side steady.

Sourcing

Where the discounted houses come from

Bank-owned listings are the usual starting point. A bank that has taken a house back sells it for less than a fixed-up house nearby, and that discount is what the strategy runs on. The buyer who can close soonest usually gets the house.

Estate sales and worn rentals coming off long tenancies make up the rest.

We hear about bank-owned and pre-listing houses early, from agents who have worked this market for decades, and our analysts screen every active listing for rent-to-price math at the same time.

Condos

Buying a distressed condo unit

Distressed condo units in stable buildings sell at deeper discounts than houses, because the supply of condos is bigger and many investors will not buy into a building at all. The discount usually comes from the owner, not the building: one mismanaged or foreclosing unit sells cheap while the building itself still passes lender tests.

A building that fails those tests is called non-warrantable. The lenders who will still refinance you there want about 25 percent down, against the 20 percent a warrantable building can take, so another 5 percent of the value stays parked in that unit instead of funding your next purchase. Figures from our affiliated lender, BrickWood Mortgage, a separate company.

Our agents check the building before you offer. The full picture is on the condotel and non-warrantable condo financing guide.

The work

The rehab, the lease, and how we help

Fix what raises the value and the rent, not a wish list. Our agents walk the house with you before you offer and tell you which repairs the appraiser and the tenant will pay for, and which ones you will never get back. We introduce the contractors we use here, so the bids come from people who have done this work on the Grand Strand.

Once the work is done, a signed lease is what the refinance lender wants to see. Inland areas lease fastest. Demand there comes from workers, hospitals, schools and Coastal Carolina University, not tourists. Houses approved for the housing choice voucher program lease fastest of all, and we walk owners through what the housing authority inspects and how to get a unit approved.

One local rehab for scale: a house on Woodlawn Drive took $90,317.80 of work from a local company we recommend. A new roof, replaced rotted studs, new insulation, new cabinets, counters and backsplashes, repaired floors and walls, fresh paint, and a hot tub for short-term guests. The house was worth about $180,000 before the work and appraised at $384,000 after, and BrickWood Mortgage financed the project. Start to finish, financing included, about three months, and the buyer kept it as a short-term rental. His numbers, not a promise.

The refinance

The refinance that returns your cash

The new loan is usually a DSCR cash-out loan: the loan qualifies on the property's rent, not your tax returns. Most lenders cap the cash-out at 75 to 80 percent of the new appraised value. Many also require seasoning, meaning the lender makes you wait a few months before using the new value. See the DSCR loan guide for how the refinance works.

When an appraisal does come in low, and here that is rare, our agents pull their own comparable sales from the Coastal Carolinas MLS and file a reconsideration of value through the lender. In the past, one of those changed the number, and the borrower brought less cash to closing.

The repeat

Doing it again with the same money

If the after-repair value holds, most of your cash is back and the tenant covers the loan. If the house appraises under the number the deal was priced on, your cash stays stuck in it. Pricing that number is the part our agents do with you before you buy, from sold homes nearby rather than from an estimate.

Tim's grandfather owned rental properties in Myrtle Beach, and Tim has his own investments today, so the numbers Chapter3 runs for you are the numbers he runs on his own investments. Run the rent and value numbers through the long-term rental analyzer before you buy. If you would rather sell the finished house than keep it, that is a fix and flip. Read that guide to see whether it suits you better.

Another way in

The new construction version of BRRRR

The scarce ingredient is the discounted house. One way around it is to create the value instead of shopping for it. We work with a local builder whose built houses appraised for more than they cost to build, and a brand new house rents higher than a comparable older one.

The rent side gets solved during the build. We look for tenants moving to the area around the month the house will be finished, so about nine times in ten the house has a renter lined up before the work ends. The cycle is the same as BRRRR, with the discount coming from the build rather than from a distressed seller.

One house took about $260,000 of capital to build, half of it financed, and appraised at $390,000 once it was done. Those are his numbers, not a promise. This path needs a builder relationship or a real discount during construction, so it is not open to every investor. Ask us whether your numbers fit it.

Paying cash

Benefits of buying cash

A cash offer is stronger than a financed one on these houses. Bank-owned and off-market houses are the first two places our agents look, and both usually go to the buyer who can close soonest. Cash has no appraisal to fail and no loan to fall through, which is worth more to those sellers than a slightly higher price.

Cash does not have to stay in the house either. The refinance step is the same one BRRRR already runs: buy with cash, fix it, sign a lease, then take a DSCR cash-out loan and put the money back to work.

You can still buy these houses with a loan, and off-market is where it happens, because there is no competing cash offer to lose to. Our agents talk to owners before a house is listed and find out what the seller needs. Then we build the offer around it: the closing date they want, a shorter inspection window, more earnest money, or a price that pays them for waiting on your loan. A seller who has not listed yet is usually weighing one offer instead of five, and that is the situation where financing wins.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Does the BRRRR method work in Myrtle Beach?

Yes. It works best on dated single-family homes in inland areas like Conway, Socastee, Carolina Forest, and up toward Longs and Little River. Year-round tenant demand there supports the rent side. Distressed condo units sell at deeper discounts, but HOA rules limit what you can renovate and many oceanfront buildings fail refinance lender tests, so check the building against the refinance before you buy.

What is the 70 percent rule in BRRRR?

It is a guideline. Keep the purchase price plus rehab budget under about 70 to 75 percent of the fixed-up value. That margin is what lets you refinance later and pull most of your cash back out.

How soon can I refinance a BRRRR property?

Many lenders require seasoning, meaning they make you wait a few months before lending on the new value. Ask the lender how long its wait is before you buy. The after-repair value is the deciding number. The refinance comes down to what the house appraises for once the work is done. Know the house, its condition, and what similar homes nearby sold for before you buy. That is what protects your cash.

How much cash can I pull out on the refinance?

Cash-out DSCR refinances are typically capped at 75 to 80 percent of the appraised value. Whether you recover all of your cash depends on how accurate your fixed-up value estimate was.

What is the biggest risk with BRRRR?

Guessing the fixed-up value too high. If the house appraises low, the refinance returns less cash and more of your money stays stuck. Model the value and rent before you buy.
