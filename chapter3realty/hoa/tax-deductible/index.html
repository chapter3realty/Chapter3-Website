Are HOA fees tax deductible in South Carolina?
Not on the home you live in. Yes on the nights you rent it out.

  • $0of HOA fees deductible on a home you live in
  • $7,800 a yeardeductible on a condo rented all year at the middle Horry County dues
  • About 11 timesa year of dues against the property tax on a Myrtle Beach condo its owner lives in

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

On a Horry County condo with the middle HOA fees of $650 a month, the nights you rent it decide how much is deductible.

The short answer

HOA fees on a home you live in are not tax deductible. On a condo or house you rent out, the dues are a rental expense, taken off the rent on Schedule E, the IRS form for rental income.

If you rent it out and also stay there, you deduct the rented share. Divide the nights rented at a fair price by those nights plus your personal nights. The same rules on dues apply on the South Carolina return.

Are HOA fees tax deductible?

Only when the home is rented out. The IRS lists homeowners association (HOA) fees, condo fees and common charges with homeowners insurance, utilities and internet service as costs you cannot deduct on a home. On a rental, IRS Publication 527 lets you deduct dues paid for upkeep of the shared parts of a condo building.

A deduction is not money back. It lowers the rental income that is taxed, and what it saves depends on the rest of your return.

How much of the HOA fee can I deduct if I rent and use the condo?

You deduct the rented share: rented nights as a share of rented nights plus personal nights. Nights the condo sat empty count on neither side.

A night rented at a fair price counts as rented, even if you were there that day. A fair price is about what a person not related to you would pay. A rent far below what similar condos nearby charge is not a fair price.

Worked example: fewer personal nights can mean a larger deduction. On the condo below, rented 120 nights, cutting personal nights from 20 to 14 raises the deductible dues. The condo also stops counting as your home.

When the condo counts as your home

The IRS counts a condo as your home if your personal nights are more than 14. If you rent it more than 140 nights at a fair price, the line is 10 percent of those nights instead.

Counting as your home matters when the rent is less than your rental costs. Then the deductions stop at the rent, and you can use the rest on next year's return for the same condo.

If it does not count as your home, the rental can show a loss. Whether that loss lowers the tax on your other income, such as wages, depends on the loss limits on our short-term rental tax page.

This IRS test is not the county's 72-day limit for the property tax rate, which comes below. The 14-day rule page has the other IRS day counts.

What counts as a personal night

Counts as a personal night

  • A night you or a co-owner stays there
  • A night a relative stays, unless they rent it at a fair price as their main home
  • A night anyone pays less than a fair rent
  • A night someone stays in a swap that lets you use another home

Does not count as a personal night

  • A night rented at a fair price, even if you were there that day
  • A day you spend working close to full time on repairs and upkeep, even if family members enjoy the condo that day

Relatives are a spouse, brothers and sisters including half, parents, grandparents and earlier generations, and children, grandchildren and later generations. A day spent improving the condo, not repairing it, is not a repair day.

What do condo HOA fees cost in Horry County?

About $650 a month, or $7,800 a year, for the middle condo for sale in Horry County. In oceanfront buildings the middle is about $925 a month. The middle dues are about $375 a month in Socastee and about $875 at the north end of Myrtle Beach.

Most Horry County condos for sale say their dues include the building's insurance, water and sewer, trash and internet.

On a rental, the insurance, utilities and internet are rental costs too, inside the dues or not.

The deduction needs nights you are allowed to rent. About 7 in 10 condos for sale in Horry County say they allow short-term rentals. When we search for a buyer who plans to rent, we filter out communities that ban renting. Then our agents read the rental rules in the HOA documents.

Do HOA fees count as property tax?

No. HOA fees are not a property tax, because an association, not a government, charges them. The IRS says you cannot deduct them as real estate tax.

A home you live in, once you claim it with the county, is taxed at the 4 percent rate. Rentals, second homes and any home whose owner has not claimed that rate pay 6 percent. An owner who lives in another state pays 6 percent, because the 4 percent rate is only for the owner's domicile.

On a Myrtle Beach condo at the 4 percent rate, a year of dues at the middle for condos for sale in ZIP 29577 is about $9,300. That is about 11 times the 2026 tax bill on a middle-valued condo in the city taxed at that rate, about $830.

On a home you live in, the property tax counts on the federal return if you itemize, meaning you list your deductions instead of taking the standard deduction. The dues never do. On a rental, both are rental expenses.

The county's 72-day limit

A condo you live in can be rented for part of the year and keep the 4 percent rate. It keeps that rate only while you still qualify and rent it no more than 72 days in a calendar year. The assessor may ask for your Schedule E.

The property tax page explains the two rates and works out the bill at either one.

Is an HOA special assessment tax deductible?

On a rental, yes if it pays for upkeep, and slowly if it pays for an improvement. On a home you live in, no. A special assessment is a one-time bill on top of the dues, usually for a large job.

Worked example: your share of a roof assessment on a condo you rent out is $10,000. You deduct about $364 a year in each full year, over 27.5 years from when the new roof is in service. In service means finished and ready to use.

Our page on rental depreciation shows how the first and last years are counted.

On a condo you live in, an assessment that is not just for repairs or upkeep is added to your cost basis, what the home cost you. The basis rarely matters on a main home. If you qualify, the first $250,000 of gain is not taxed, or $500,000 for a married couple filing jointly.

Whether a job is upkeep or an improvement depends on the work. The assessment notice and the board's minutes say what the work is.

Does South Carolina treat HOA fees differently?

No. South Carolina starts from the federal figures, so the same rules on dues, rental days and depreciation apply on the state return. From 2026, South Carolina does not use the federal itemized deductions. That does not touch the dues on a rental, which come off the rent, not off a list of itemized deductions.

About 6 in 10 Horry County condo units have their tax bill mailed to an address outside South Carolina. For an owner who lives in another state, the rent from a Grand Strand condo, less its costs and dues, is South Carolina income.

On the South Carolina return, the dues come off that rent the same way they do on the federal return. How your home state treats the same rent depends on that state's law, not South Carolina's.

Across Horry County, only about 1 in 5 condo units is taxed at the 4 percent rate as the home its owner lives in. Our condo investing page has the counts by town.

Where do HOA fees go on Schedule E, and what records do I keep?

Schedule E has no line for association dues. An expense not listed on lines 5 to 18 goes on line 19, Other.

Line 2 asks for the nights rented at a fair price and the nights of personal use. Keep these four records:

  1. 1The association's statementsThey show what you paid in dues and in any special assessment, and when.
  2. 2A log of nightsWrite down each night rented at a fair price, each personal night and each repair day, as they happen.
  3. 3The assessment notice and the minutesThey say what an assessment paid for, which decides whether it is upkeep or an improvement.
  4. 4The two night countsGive them to whoever prepares your return. Schedule E asks for both.

The HOA documents page lists what each paper shows. What you pay the association at closing is on our page on transfer fees.

Common questions

HOA fees and taxes FAQ

Are HOA fees tax deductible on my primary residence?

No. On a home you live in, HOA and condo fees are a personal cost, like insurance and utilities. They are not a property tax either, because an association, not a government, charges them.

Are HOA fees deductible on a rental property?

Yes. On a condo you rent out, the dues are a rental expense on Schedule E. On a house in an HOA that you rent out, the dues are a cost of renting it, like insurance and repairs.

Are HOA fees deductible on a second home I do not rent?

No. A second home you never rent follows the same rule as a main home. If you rent it for 15 days or more, the rented share of the nights becomes deductible.

Can I deduct HOA fees if I work from home?

Not as an employee: an employee gets no home office deduction. A self-employed owner who qualifies splits whole-home costs by the share of the home used for business. The IRS's examples are insurance, utilities and general repairs. Its home office guide does not say whether association dues count, so this page does not say they do.

Do HOA special assessments lower capital gains when I sell?

On a condo you live in, an assessment for an improvement is added to your cost basis, which lowers the taxable gain. If you qualify, the first $250,000 of gain on a main home is not taxed, or $500,000 for a married couple filing jointly.

Do family stays count as personal use?

Yes, unless the relative rents it at a fair price as their main home. A stay by you, a co-owner or a relative is a personal night, and so is any night rented below a fair price.
