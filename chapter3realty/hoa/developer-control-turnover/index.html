Developer controlled HOAs

Buying into a developer-controlled HOA
South Carolina sets no turnover deadline

By Timmy Fredrick Nash, Broker-in-Charge, 30+ years on the Grand Strand · Reviewed by Devin Day, Operations Officer · Updated July 28, 2026

Turnover is the point where a developer stops controlling a homeowners association and the owners elect their own board. South Carolina has no HOA turnover law. Neither the Homeowners Association Act, Title 27 Chapter 30, nor the Horizontal Property Act, Title 27 Chapter 31, sets a percentage-sold trigger, a deadline, or any duty for a developer to hand control to the owners. Both chapters were read in full at scstatehouse.gov on July 28, 2026. Control passes when the recorded declaration and bylaws say it passes, and the developer wrote those documents before any owner existed. The percentages buyers read about, 90 percent for Fannie Mae and 75 percent for Freddie Mac, are mortgage purchase conditions, not developer obligations.

THE DIRECT ANSWER

South Carolina does not require a developer to hand over the HOA

Turnover is the point where the developer stops controlling the homeowners association and the owners elect their own board. There is no turnover statute in South Carolina. We read the Homeowners Association Act, Title 27 Chapter 30, and the Horizontal Property Act, Title 27 Chapter 31, in full at scstatehouse.gov on July 28, 2026. Neither one sets a percentage of homes sold that triggers turnover. Neither sets a deadline. Neither imposes any duty to hand control to owners at all.

So the recorded declaration and bylaws decide when it happens. The declarant, meaning the party that created the community and recorded its documents, is normally the developer, and it wrote those documents before a single owner existed. The answer to when control passes is in the paperwork, and the paperwork was written by the party the rule affects.

One provision of state law is useful to a buyer. Section 27-30-130(A)(1) says an association's governing documents must be recorded in the clerk of court's office, the Register of Mesne Conveyance, or the register of deeds in the county where the property is located in order to be enforceable. The Register of Mesne Conveyance is simply the county land records office in some South Carolina counties. The practical effect for a buyer is that these documents are public records. You do not need the developer's permission to read them. For the wider state framework, see our page on South Carolina HOA laws.

WHAT THE DOCUMENTS CONTROL

In a condominium, changing the system of administration takes two thirds of the total value

South Carolina Code 27-31-160 lists what a condominium's bylaws must contain. The first required item is the form of administration. That means whether the condominium, which the Act calls a horizontal property regime, is run by an administrator, by a board of administration, or in some other way, together with that body's powers, the manner of removal of its members, and where proper their compensation.

The same section says who may change that system. It provides that the sole owner of the property, or if there is more than one, co-owners representing two thirds of the total value of the property, may at any time modify the system of administration. While the developer still owns every unit it is the sole owner, so it can modify the system by itself. Once units are sold, it takes co-owners representing two thirds of total value. No modification is operative until it is embodied in a recorded instrument, recorded in the same office and in the same manner as the master deed or lease and the original bylaws. A master deed is the recorded document that creates the condominium and defines each unit and the shared areas.

That is the practical limit on what owners can change in a South Carolina condominium. A simple majority of unit owners cannot rewrite how the association is run. The same section also requires the bylaws to state that a majority of at least fifty-one percent is required to adopt decisions, which is a separate and lower bar for ordinary business.

HOW DECLARANT CONTROL WORKS

Two sections of the Nonprofit Corporation Act allow a developer to keep the board

Many South Carolina associations are incorporated as nonprofit corporations. If yours is, the Nonprofit Corporation Act, Title 33 Chapter 31, applies to it. Check the recorded declaration and the Secretary of State's business filings to see whether yours is. Two sections explain how declarant control is lawfully built even though no turnover statute exists.

Section 33-31-610 says all members have the same voting rights unless the articles of incorporation, the document that creates the nonprofit corporation, or the bylaws establish classes of membership with different rights or obligations. That is the statutory basis for a Class A and Class B structure, where the developer's class carries more votes than an owner's.

Section 33-31-804(a) says directors are elected by the members at the annual meeting unless the articles or bylaws provide some other time or method of election, or provide that some of the directors are appointed by some other person. That is the basis for board seats the developer appoints instead of owners electing.

There is one remedy in the same Act worth knowing about. Under 33-31-703(a)(1), if an annual meeting was not held within the earlier of six months after the end of the corporation's fiscal year or fifteen months after the last annual meeting, a member may apply to the court of common pleas where the corporation's principal office sits, and the court may summarily order a meeting to be held. That is general information and not legal advice. Speak to a South Carolina attorney before relying on it.

THE BUDGET BEFORE TURNOVER

The dues figure quoted before turnover can come from a projected budget

Freddie Mac's project review guidance addresses this in its own words. It says the project's budget, or its projected budget if the project has not been turned over to the unit owners, must be consistent with the nature of the project. So a community that has not turned over can be operating on a budget that was estimated in advance rather than built from a full year of actual bills.

We will not tell you what developers on the Grand Strand typically do with early dues, because no primary source measures it. What we can tell you is what to check. Every item below is answerable from documents you can obtain before you go under contract.

Freddie Mac is also raising what it expects a condominium budget to set aside. Its Bulletin 2026-C of March 18, 2026 increases the required reserve allocation for capital expenditures and deferred maintenance from a minimum of 10 percent to a minimum of 15 percent of annual budgeted assessment income, meaning the total the association budgets to collect from owners in dues for the year, for applications received on or after January 4, 2027. That is a lender rule and not a South Carolina rule, but it concerns the same number a buyer should ask about. A reserve study is a report that estimates when shared components will need replacing and what that will cost. Our page on reserve funds and reserve studies explains how to read one.

If reserves are underfunded, the shortfall is billed to owners later. If the reserve account is short when a roof or an elevator fails, the association raises the money from owners. That is what special assessments are, and it is the outcome you are trying to see coming.

  • ✓Budget or projection. Ask whether the dues figure you were quoted comes from a full year of actual operating costs or from an estimate made before the community was occupied.
  • ✓Reserve study and reserve balance. Ask whether a study has been done, when, and what is in the reserve account today.
  • ✓What the declarant owes on unsold lots. Read what the declaration says the developer pays on the homes and lots it still owns, and whether that obligation is written the same way as yours.
  • ✓Who may raise assessments. Read the assessment article for any cap, any limit on annual increases, and who votes on an increase.
  • ✓The notice rule and its exception. Section 27-30-140 requires at least forty-eight hours notice in advance of the meeting at which a decision to raise the annual budget is made. Subsection (2) then says the section does not apply to a homeowners association incorporated under the South Carolina Nonprofit Corporation Act. Check whether the association is incorporated that way before relying on the rule at all.

FINANCING CONSEQUENCE

Developer control alone makes the project a new project to both loan buyers

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are the two companies that buy conventional mortgages from lenders, and their project rules decide where a lender can lend. Both treat developer control as the dividing line.

Fannie Mae's Selling Guide defines an established condo project as one where, among other conditions, at least 90 percent of the units have been conveyed to unit purchasers and control of the HOA has been turned over to the unit owners. Its definition of a new condo project is written in the alternative, and one of the listed conditions is the HOA still being in the developer's control. A finished, nearly sold out building is still a new project to Fannie Mae if the developer still holds the association.

That changes your loan file, though the mechanism itself changed in 2026. Until August 3, 2026, Fannie Mae's Limited Review, the shorter project review, was available only for an attached unit in an established project, so a developer-controlled project had to take the longer Full Review. Fannie Mae retired Limited Review in Lender Letter LL-2026-03, dated March 18, 2026, for all loan applications dated on or after August 3, 2026. Freddie Mac retired its Streamlined Review on the same date in Bulletin 2026-C. From that date every condo project takes a Full Review, or a Waiver of Project Review where one applies. Developer control still changes the file, because the review of a new project expires sooner. A Full Review on a new project must have been completed within 180 days before the note date, which is the day you sign the promissory note at closing. For an established project it is one year. On a long build contract the review can expire and have to be redone before closing. Fannie Mae also requires that at least 50 percent of the units in a new or newly converted project, or in the legal phase being financed, meaning the section of the project the building sits in, have been conveyed or be under contract for sale to principal residence or second home purchasers. Contracts with investors do not count toward that test.

Freddie Mac draws the same line on project type. Its April 2025 fact sheet defines an established project as complete, not subject to further phasing, at least 75 percent sold to others, and with unit owners controlling the association, and a new project as one that is incomplete, subject to further phasing, under 75 percent sold, or still controlled by the developer. Freddie Mac's condominium FAQ confirms it retired the owner occupancy requirement only for established projects, so that test still applies to developer-controlled ones.

Neither the state nor the loan buyers force turnover. Fannie Mae's guide says only that the developer should provide for and promote the unit owners' early participation in the management of the project. Should, not must. None of this stops you buying. It changes which lenders can work and how long the file takes, and when a project cannot pass review, the fallback is a lender that does not follow those rules, which we cover on our page about non-warrantable condos. BEFORE YOU SIGN

Get the recorded documents yourself, and get them before you go under contract

South Carolina's statutory access rights are narrower than they look, and none of them reach a buyer. Section 27-30-130(B) requires rules, regulations and amendments to rules and regulations to be made accessible to a homeowners association member on that member's request, unless they are posted in a conspicuous place in a common area or are available on a website the association maintains. It does not reach the declaration, the bylaws or the budget. Section 27-30-150 applies the Nonprofit Corporation Act's access to documents provisions, sections 33-31-1602 through 33-31-1605, to associations not subject to that Act, and only so homeowners can inspect and copy the annual budget and the membership list. Every one of these rights runs to a member. A person under contract to buy is not yet a member. Your two routes are the county recording office, because recording is a condition of enforceability under 27-30-130(A)(1), and your own purchase contract.

The seller disclosure will not fill the gap on its own. Section 27-50-40(A)(9) requires the owner's disclosure statement to address whether the property is subject to governance of a homeowners association. Section 27-50-40(B) then lets the owner answer that they are making no representations, and section 27-50-30 exempts some transfers from the disclosure requirement altogether.

South Carolina's 2026 HOA report shows most complainants did not have the governing documents before they bought. Our page on which HOA documents to request lists each document, what it reveals, and the state's delivery numbers.

Common questions

Developer Control FAQ

When does a developer have to turn over an HOA in South Carolina?

There is no deadline in South Carolina law. The Homeowners Association Act and the Horizontal Property Act were both read in full on July 28, 2026, and neither sets a turnover date, a percentage of homes sold, or any duty to hand control to owners. The recorded declaration and bylaws decide it. Read the declaration and every recorded amendment, and look for the conditions that end declarant control. If you cannot find such a condition, that is itself an answer, and it is worth asking a South Carolina attorney about before you sign.

What percentage of homes has to be sold before HOA turnover in SC?

None, as a matter of state law. South Carolina sets no percentage-sold trigger for turnover. The percentages people have usually read about come from mortgage rules rather than statute. Fannie Mae treats a condo project as established only when, among other conditions, at least 90 percent of units have been conveyed to unit purchasers and control of the HOA has been turned over to the owners. Freddie Mac's April 2025 fact sheet uses at least 75 percent sold plus owner control. Those are conditions for buying a loan, not obligations on a developer.

Can I get a mortgage on a condo when the developer still controls the HOA?

Usually yes, but the project review takes longer. Fannie Mae's definition of a new condo project includes any project where the HOA is still in the developer's control. Fannie Mae retired the Limited Review process, and Freddie Mac retired the Streamlined Review, for applications dated on or after August 3, 2026, so from that date every condo project takes a Full Review or a Waiver of Project Review. A Full Review on a new project must have been completed within 180 days before the note date, against one year for an established project, so on a long build the review can expire before closing. Ask your lender to start the project review early. It has to be finished before the loan can close.

Will HOA dues go up after the developer turns over the community?

We will not promise a direction, and no primary source measures it for South Carolina. What is verifiable is that the budget before turnover can be an estimate. Freddie Mac's project review guidance refers to the project's budget, or its projected budget if the project has not been turned over to the unit owners. So ask two questions before you sign. Is the current dues figure built from a full year of real operating costs, and has a reserve study been done. An underfunded reserve is the usual reason a special assessment appears later.

How do I get the HOA documents before I buy in a new community?

Use the county recording office and your purchase contract. South Carolina Code 27-30-130(A)(1) says governing documents must be recorded in the clerk of court's office, the Register of Mesne Conveyance, or the register of deeds in the county where the property sits in order to be enforceable, so they are public records. The statutory access right at 27-30-130(B) covers only rules, regulations and amendments to rules and regulations, and it runs to members. Section 27-30-150 adds an inspection right for the annual budget and the membership list, and only for associations not subject to the Nonprofit Corporation Act. Both run to members, and a buyer under contract is not yet a member. Build a document delivery and review deadline into the contract rather than relying on the seller disclosure.

What does declarant control mean in an HOA?

Declarant is the legal name for the party that created the community and recorded its declaration, which is normally the developer. Declarant control is the period when that party still runs the association. In South Carolina it is built from the recorded documents plus, where the association is incorporated, the Nonprofit Corporation Act. Section 33-31-610 allows classes of membership with different voting rights. Section 33-31-804(a) allows the articles of incorporation or the bylaws to provide that some directors are appointed rather than elected by the members. Neither section requires the arrangement to end on any particular date.
