The HOA guide

South Carolina HOA laws:
what the state requires, and what your HOA's papers say.

  • No cap on finesin the HOA Act or the condo law
  • About 700association papers recorded in Horry County in the past 12 months
  • 10 of 416Horry County complaints ended with the owner satisfied

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

The three state laws, the rules they leave to each community's recorded papers, and how Horry County's 416 HOA complaints to the state ended.

The short answer

South Carolina has no one complete law on how an HOA must run. Under the 2018 HOA Act, an HOA's papers must be recorded at the county to be enforced, and money disputes up to $7,500 can go to magistrate court.

Fines, dues limits, rental rules and developer turnover are not in South Carolina HOA laws. Your community's recorded papers have those rules. Consumer Affairs records complaints, but the law bars it from acting as a referee.

What are South Carolina's HOA laws?

South Carolina's HOA laws are three state laws, plus the papers each community records at the county. Consumer Affairs lists the three: the HOA Act, the Nonprofit Corporation Act and the condo law.

Each common question has its answer in one of them, or in your HOA's own papers:

Which South Carolina law applies to my HOA or condo?

The HOA Act applies to every HOA and condo whose owners must pay assessments. A nonprofit HOA also follows the Nonprofit Corporation Act, and a condo also follows the condo law.

  • The HOA ActEvery neighborhood or condo where recorded papers make owners pay assessments, except a timeshare. The HOA Act dates from 2018. It has been changed once, in 2019, when its definition of an HOA was edited where it leaves out timeshares.
  • The Nonprofit Corporation ActAn HOA that filed nonprofit papers with the Secretary of State also follows this law on meetings, notice, the board and records. Whether an HOA did shows in the Secretary of State's business name search.
  • The condo lawThe Horizontal Property Act, from 1962, applies to condos. It says what a condo's founding papers and bylaws must include, how records are kept and how shared costs are split.

The HOA Act itself is short. It deals with five things: recording the papers, notice before a budget increase, the budget and owner list, magistrate court for money disputes, and complaints to Consumer Affairs.

The HOA Act has no rule on late fees, fines, liens or foreclosure.

A property owners association is an HOA under the Act if it meets the same test; our page on HOA vs POA explains the names. An owner cannot opt out, because the recorded papers require each owner to pay. Our HOA guide shows what the fees pay for.

How do South Carolina's HOA laws compare with North Carolina and Florida?

Households moving to Horry County come most often from other South Carolina counties, then North Carolina, New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Florida and Maryland. North Carolina and Florida both limit HOA fines in law:

What HOA rules are unenforceable in South Carolina?

An HOA rule is unenforceable in South Carolina if it was not recorded at the county by January 10 of the year after it was adopted or changed.

  • A rule not recorded in timeA rule takes effect when it is adopted. It stays enforceable only if it is recorded by January 10 of the next year. The HOA must give you a copy on request, unless it is posted in a common area or on its website.
  • Papers never recordedAn HOA's governing papers must be recorded in the county where the property is to be enforceable.
  • An amendment without the voteA South Carolina appeals court threw out an amendment to a subdivision's restrictions because it did not have the vote the restrictions required.
  • A ban on the U.S. flagWhatever the papers say, an owner or tenant may display one portable, removable U.S. flag in a respectful manner, on the property they are entitled to use.
  • An unclear ruleIt can be read against the HOA. In a Hilton Head rental case, the court took the reading that "least restricts the use of the property", and both sides agreed short-term renting was allowed. Our page on HOA rental restrictions tells the story.

Horry County's index of recorded papers is online, and copies come from the Register of Deeds in Conway. Our page on HOA documents before you buy shows which papers to get.

Can I see my HOA's records and go to its meetings?

Yes to the records. A nonprofit HOA must also hold an owners' meeting each year, but owners have no right under state law to attend board meetings.

The records you can see

  • An HOA that is not a nonprofitIts yearly budget and owner list.
  • A nonprofit HOAIts articles, bylaws, minutes of owners' meetings and three years of financial statements. You can inspect and copy them with written notice five business days ahead, paying only for copies.
  • A condoThe board must keep a dated book of every receipt and expense. It is open to all owners at set hours on working days.

The meetings you can go to

  • A yearly owners' meetingA nonprofit HOA must hold one each year. Notice 10 to 60 days ahead is fair under the law, or 30 to 60 days if it is not sent first class or registered.
  • A special meetingOwners with at least 5 percent of the votes can demand one.
  • Board meetingsThe Nonprofit Corporation Act's notice rules for board meetings are about notice to directors. Owners have no right under it to notice of a board meeting, and no right under any of the three laws to attend one. Bylaws can give both.
  • A condo owners' voteDecisions need a majority of at least 51 percent, counted by the units' value percentages. Changing the system of running the building takes owners with two-thirds of the value.

When you buy, the seller must tell you about the HOA. Unless the sale is exempt, the seller fills in the state's disclosure form before the contract is signed. If the home is in an association, that includes a one-page HOA addendum.

Who enforces South Carolina HOA law?

No state agency enforces South Carolina's HOA Act. In its own words, Consumer Affairs "does not administer or enforce" the Act. It records each complaint and sends it to the HOA.

The law bars Consumer Affairs from issuing rules on how an HOA is run, and from acting as a referee in a dispute. Its complaint process is voluntary mediation. It cannot make an HOA or an owner take part, require an outcome or arbitrate.

Where an owner can take a dispute

  • Magistrate courtA money dispute under the HOA Act of $7,500 or less can go to magistrate court.
  • The condo lawIn a condo, the board, or an owner who is harmed, can sue an owner who breaks the bylaws or the recorded restrictions, for damages or a court order.
  • A lawyerThe SC Bar runs a lawyer referral service, at 800-868-2284.

How do I file an HOA complaint in South Carolina?

File an HOA complaint with the SC Department of Consumer Affairs, online or by mail, and send the HOA Supplemental Questionnaire with it.

  1. Raise it with the board firstTalk with the board, and check your HOA's papers for a dispute process. That is Consumer Affairs' first advice. 98 percent of those who filed in 2025 said they had raised the issue with the HOA or its management company first.
  2. File online or by mailUse Consumer Affairs' online complaint portal, or mail the paper form to PO Box 5757, Columbia, SC 29250. The online form times out after 30 minutes.
  3. Send the HOA Supplemental QuestionnaireConsumer Affairs requires it. In the same year, 78 complaints were closed and left out of its report because the owner did not send it. Questions go to hoahelp@scconsumer.gov or (800) 922-1594, toll free in South Carolina.
  4. Consumer Affairs sends it to the HOAThe HOA may answer. The agency cannot make it take part.
  5. The complaint is closedConsumer Affairs closes it and counts it in its yearly report. It says the data helps lawmakers decide if the law needs to change.

Horry County leads the state. In 2025, 140 of the 452 HOA complaints in the state's yearly report, 31 percent, were about associations in Horry County. That is the most of any county.

Is South Carolina changing its HOA laws?

Not yet. None of the five HOA bills below, from the 2025-2026 session, has become law.

Five HOA bills from the 2025-2026 session

  • H. 3447A court hearing before an HOA foreclosure sale. Passed the House 110 to 2; now in the Senate Judiciary Committee.
  • S. 903Would count rules as governing documents, hold them all to the January 10 recording rule, and from 2027 require them to be witnessed and acknowledged to be recorded. Passed the Senate 39 to 0; now in a House committee.
  • S. 366Would void foreclosure clauses in HOA papers. In the Senate Judiciary Committee.
  • H. 3350Would require a dispute process inside every HOA, and let magistrate court hear all matters between an owner and an HOA. In the House.
  • H. 5204Would require a seller packet, and a minimum debt, notice and mediation before foreclosure. In a House committee.

Our page on HOA foreclosure in South Carolina has more on the foreclosure bills.

What Horry County complaints would change

When they file, owners must say what they would change in their HOA's papers and how the papers should be enforced. Of 416 Horry County complaints, 171 named open meetings and notice rules.

On enforcement, 210 of the 416 named state agency oversight, and 183 named a dispute process inside the HOA.

Statewide, in the state's latest report, state agency oversight was also the top choice for enforcing the papers, at 31 percent of all recommendations.

Common questions

South Carolina HOA law FAQ

What is the South Carolina Homeowners Association Act?

The HOA Act is a 2018 state law, SC Code Title 27, Chapter 30. It requires an HOA's papers to be recorded at the county to be enforced, and gives owners the budget and owner list. It lets magistrate court hear money disputes under the Act, and requires Consumer Affairs to record complaints. It has been changed once, in 2019.

Who regulates HOAs in South Carolina?

No state agency enforces South Carolina's HOA Act. Consumer Affairs records complaints and sends each one to the HOA, and the law bars it from acting as a referee. An HOA that is a nonprofit files its papers with the Secretary of State. The rest is in each community's recorded papers.

Can I complain about my HOA to the state?

Yes. File with SC Consumer Affairs online or by mail, with the HOA Supplemental Questionnaire. The agency sends the complaint to the HOA, but its process is voluntary mediation. Of 416 Horry County complaints from 2022 to 2025, 10 ended with the owner satisfied.

Do HOA documents have to be recorded in South Carolina?

Yes. Under the HOA Act, an HOA's governing papers must be recorded at the county's register of deeds to be enforceable. A new rule stays enforceable only if it is recorded by January 10 of the year after it was adopted.

Does a seller have to disclose an HOA in South Carolina?

Yes, unless the sale is exempt. The seller fills in the state's disclosure form before the contract is signed. If the home is in an association, the form includes a one-page HOA addendum.

Can I sue my HOA in South Carolina?

A money dispute under the HOA Act of $7,500 or less can go to magistrate court. For anything larger, or any other dispute, talk to a South Carolina attorney. The SC Bar's lawyer referral service is at 800-868-2284.

Is condo law different from HOA law in South Carolina?

Yes. A condo follows the HOA Act and also the Horizontal Property Act, the state's condo law. The condo law puts a lien on a unit for unpaid assessments and opens the condo's account book to owners. It also requires a condo's founding papers to state any limits on leasing.
