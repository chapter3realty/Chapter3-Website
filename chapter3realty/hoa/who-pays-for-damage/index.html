Damage and liability

Who pays for water damage
in a condo on the Grand Strand

By Timmy Fredrick Nash, Broker-in-Charge, 30+ years on the Grand Strand · Reviewed by Devin Day, Operations Officer · Updated July 29, 2026

In a South Carolina condo, the HOA must insure the building and repair covered damage. Insurance money and reserves get used first. Whatever the repair costs above that becomes a shared cost, split among all owners by their percentage. It is not billed to the one unit the water reached. Where your unit ends, and how far the building policy reaches inside it, comes from the master deed.

THE BASIC RULE

The HOA insures the building and repairs it

South Carolina condo law does two things here. It tells the HOA to insure the building. It then tells the HOA to repair insured parts that get damaged. Both duties sit with the board, not with you.

The money question gets one line. Repair costs above the insurance money and the reserve are a common expense. A common expense is a shared expense. Owners pay their share by percentage.

So a shortfall on a covered loss spreads across the whole building. That cuts against what most owners assume. The bill does not follow the unit the water reached. It follows the ownership shares in the master deed.

Two gaps in the law are real. The word deductible never appears in it. Nothing in it makes one owner pay another owner for damage. We read the whole chapter to confirm both. So water from the unit above raises two questions, not one. Which policy fixes your ceiling and paint is answered here. Whether the owner above owes you money is a question for an attorney.

The law says less about your finishes. It does not say how far the insured building reaches into a unit. Cabinets, floors and paint sit on that line. The master deed and the two policies draw it.

  • ✓The duty to insure names no dollar amount, no deductible limit and no list of covered events.
  • ✓The HOA must repair insured parts that are damaged. It can skip that only if repair would break a state law or a local health rule. It can also skip it if 80 percent of owners vote not to rebuild.
  • ✓Roofs, foundations and main walls are named in the law as shared parts. A pipe inside a main wall may or may not be. The master deed draws that line.
  • ✓A regular HOA that is not a condo is different. That law has no insurance rule and no repair rule at all.

THE DEDUCTIBLE

The deductible decides how much owners pay

A building policy pays a covered loss minus its deductible. Someone absorbs that gap. A deductible is a repair cost above the insurance money. That makes it a shared expense, split by percentage.

The order matters. Insurance money and the reserve go first. Only what is left over becomes a shared cost.

Loan rules confirm this cost reaches owners. Fannie Mae wants your own policy to cover deductible charges the HOA passes down to you.

Both loan buyers cap the deductible they will accept. Fannie Mae allows up to 5 percent of the building coverage amount. If a policy carries more than one deductible, such as a separate wind one, the total for a single event still has to stay at 5 percent. Freddie Mac caps a single event at 5 percent too.

Freddie Mac added a per unit cap this year. For loan applications dated July 1, 2026 or later, a per unit deductible cannot top $50,000. If the building policy has one, your own policy has to be at least that high, or high enough to put the unit back the way it was before the loss. Take the higher of the two. Freddie also caps your own deductible at 5 percent of your limit or $2,500, whichever is more.

Read those as lending rules, not as buyer protection. They do not limit what your HOA can charge you. They still matter. If a building falls outside them, loans get harder for you now and for your buyer later.

One question is worth putting to the board in writing. Both loan buyers let an HOA buy a policy that covers its own deductible. If the deductible is big, ask if the board has looked at that.

WIND AND NAMED STORMS

Wind is where the deductible costs real money

Wind is the loss that turns a deductible into a real bill on this coast. Loan rules expect a building policy to carry a separate wind deductible. Here that setup is normal, not odd.

Named storm is a hard trigger, not a judgment call. It means a storm the National Weather Service or NOAA has given a name or a number. Freddie Mac requires named storms to be part of wind cover.

The state has written down what is happening here. In its report on coastal property insurance, the Department of Insurance named the problem. Condo groups with wooden buildings over fifty years old, close to the ocean, have had a hard time finding cover they can afford.

The same report tracks HOAs leaving the state wind pool. The pool covered 70 condo HOAs and 735 buildings at the start of 2024. By the end of January 2025 it was 20 HOAs and 175 buildings. By August 2025 it was seven HOAs and 23 buildings, and it stayed there through year end. The state says those groups found better wind cover for less money on the open market.

None of that says who pays. It does tell you the wind part of a building's cover was likely repriced recently. Ask when the building policy last renewed, and what the wind deductible did.

BEFORE YOU OFFER

Read these documents before you make an offer

Every question on this page can be answered before you are locked in. The papers already exist. Timing is the problem. In the state's latest HOA report, only 32 percent of people said they got the governing papers before they bought. 53 percent got them after.

The county recording office is a real way around that. Recording is what makes an HOA's papers hold up. So the master deed for a South Carolina community is a public record.

Do not count on asking instead. A buyer under contract has no document right at all. The access rule covers rules and by-laws, for owners who ask. Even that drops away when the HOA posts them in a common area or on its website. A second rule covers the budget and the member list, again for owners, and only for some HOAs. The recording office is often faster.

Expect about three days for an HOA to release papers. That is what we watch them do, not a promise we make. We can promise our own side. The papers go through our analysis tool the moment we have them, and a person reads the output within minutes.

One warning about words. The trade uses short labels for how a building policy is written, and no primary source we opened treats those labels as real categories. Ask a plain question instead. Does the building policy cover the inside of the unit and its upgrades, or not. Take that answer from the policy, not from a label.

Budget for HOA charges at closing too. Most HOAs charge something. A few months of dues up front is common. Add a document fee and a fee to open your account. It varies by HOA, so a good agent asks early. Then it is not a surprise.

This page is general information, not legal advice. If an HOA has refused a repair you believe it owes, talk to a South Carolina attorney.

Common questions

Who Pays for Damage FAQ

Who pays for water damage in a condo in South Carolina?

The HOA has to insure the building and repair insured parts that get damaged. Insurance money and the reserve pay first. Repair costs above that get shared among owners by percentage. The master deed decides where your unit ends and how far the building policy reaches inside it.

Does the HOA pay for water damage from the unit above me?

Two questions are in play. The first is which policy fixes your ceiling and paint. If the HOA had to insure the damaged part, the HOA repairs it, and any cost above insurance and reserve gets shared. The second is whether the owner above owes you money. State condo law does not answer that one. Ask a South Carolina attorney.

Who pays the condo master policy deductible?

The word deductible never appears in the state condo law. We read the whole chapter to check. The law still gets to an answer. Repair costs above the insurance money and the reserve get shared by percentage. A deductible on a covered loss is that kind of cost.

Does the condo HOA have to insure the building in South Carolina?

Yes, for a condo. State law says the council that runs a condo must insure the building, and each owner may still insure their own unit. That duty sets no dollar amount and no deductible limit. A second rule adds the duty to repair insured parts. A regular HOA that is not a condo has no insurance rule at all.

Do I need my own condo policy if the building has a master policy?

Often yes, and your lender may require it. Since July 1, 2026 Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac use one test. You need your own policy if the building policy leaves out any part of the inside of the unit or its upgrades. You also need one if it carries a per unit deductible.
