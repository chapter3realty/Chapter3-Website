Waterfront homes

Waterfront homes in Myrtle Beach:
from the ocean to the lake, what each one costs.

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 14, 2026

From the sun coming up out of the ocean to a boat at your own dock, seven kinds of water, each with its own price.

The short answer

In the past year, a house on the beach sold for about $2.2 million at the middle, and one on the Intracoastal Waterway for about $1.3 million.

A condo in an oceanfront building sold for about $200,000, and every waterfront house for sale under $300,000 is on a lake or pond.

What does a waterfront home cost in Myrtle Beach?

The kind of water makes the biggest difference to the price. A house on the beach sold for about six times the middle house in Horry County. A house on the Waccamaw River sold for less than half of what one on the waterway did.

Lake houses have no bar, because the deeds do not show reliably which houses sit on a lake. On the day we counted, the 429 lake and pond houses for sale asked about $450,000 at the middle.

Which kind of waterfront home fits you?

Waterfront here means seven different purchases. Pick what you want from the water, and go to its entry.

  1. The sun coming up out of the ocean, from your own porch: Oceanfront houses
  2. The same sunrise from a balcony, for far less: A condo in an oceanfront building
  3. A boat at your own dock that can travel north or south behind the coast: The Intracoastal Waterway
  4. Crabbing off your own dock on the tide: The salt marsh and tidal creeks
  5. A boat out back, with a city boat ramp and an inlet to the ocean: The Cherry Grove channels
  6. Cypress, dark river water and a wildlife refuge: The Waccamaw River
  7. A pontoon boat on a lake, for less: A lake or pond

Oceanfront houses: the sun comes up out of the ocean

From central Myrtle Beach south to Garden City, the beach faces the southeast. The sun rises over open sea there on every morning of the year.

Farther north the shore curves. From Windy Hill to Cherry Grove, the sun comes up over the North Myrtle Beach shore on about 100 to 125 mornings around June.

An oceanfront house has no other house between its lot and the beach. More than 400 houses in Horry County stand in the first row like this.

In the past year, at least 20 of them resold, and the middle price was about $2.2 million. Other houses within about two-thirds of a mile, off the water, resold for about $615,000. Want the sand a walk away instead? Our guide to houses near the beach counts them by distance, and our guide to homes with a pool shows where the pools are.

On the day we counted, the houses for sale listed as on the ocean asked about $3.4 million at the middle. About 6 in 10 first-row lots reach FEMA's VE zone, where waves are expected. To check one address, use our guide to checking a flood zone.

A condo in an oceanfront building: the same sunrise for less

A condo in a building on the beach gives you the same sunrise from a balcony, with the sand right below. It costs far less than a house on the beach.

More than 600 condos in oceanfront buildings resold in the past year, for about $200,000 at the middle.

On the day we counted, about 1,100 condos were for sale in oceanfront buildings, and about 2 in 3 asked under $300,000.

Not every unit in an oceanfront building faces the ocean. Of those condos for sale, about 700 list a unit that faces it.

Each building has its own monthly fee and its own rules on renting. We compare the HOA fees of the oceanfront buildings for you, and if you plan to rent, the numbers on an oceanfront condo.

Intracoastal Waterway homes: your boat at your own dock

From a dock on the Intracoastal Waterway here, your boat can head north to Norfolk, Virginia, or south to Florida without going out on the ocean. Our guide to Plantation Point covers one Myrtle Beach neighborhood by the waterway.

The waterway runs behind the Atlantic coast. When you want the ocean, the way out is Little River Inlet, at the North Carolina line.

Our clients from New York bought beachfront in North Myrtle Beach near the waterway, so they could use their boat.

Flooding is lighter here than on the beach, the channels or the river. About 3 in 10 waterway house lots are wholly outside FEMA's high-risk flood zone.

About 985 houses in Horry County have a lot that touches the waterway. In the past year, 55 houses on it sold for about $1.3 million at the middle, about twice the houses nearby, in every stretch.

Marsh front homes: crabs off your own dock

On salt water in South Carolina, you need no fishing license to go crabbing with three or fewer drop nets, fold-up traps or hand lines with bait. People call it chicken necking, and a dock on the marsh is a place to do it.

The marsh fills and drains with the tide twice a day. In Murrells Inlet's creeks, the water at a dock drops about 4 feet from high tide to low.

At low tide, a dock on a small creek may sit on mud. The width of the creek also limits the size of the dock the state allows. We check the depth at the dock at low tide, not at the showing.

In Garden City, about 260 houses are on the salt marsh or a tidal creek. The 13 that sold in the past year went for about $1.24 million at the middle, about twice the other houses on that side of the beach road.

In Murrells Inlet, about 220 houses are on the salt marsh, almost all on the creek side of US 17 Business. Over three years, 21 of them sold for about $930,000 at the middle.

Cherry Grove channel homes: a boat out the back door

In Cherry Grove, in North Myrtle Beach, rows of houses line channels dug through the marsh behind the beach. Many have a dock behind the house.

Cherry Grove has the city's boat ramp on the marsh, with boat access to the marsh and the ocean through Hog Inlet. Inside Cherry Grove, the water moves about 4.5 feet between high and low tide.

The trade-off is flooding. Of about 680 houses on the channels and the marsh edge, all but 3 are in FEMA's high-risk flood zone.

A house on the channels resold for about $715,000 at the middle in the past year. A Cherry Grove house off the channels sold for about $665,000, a group that includes oceanfront houses.

Waccamaw River homes: dark water, cypress and a wildlife refuge

The Waccamaw National Wildlife Refuge, set up in 1997, protects tidal freshwater wetlands and old rice fields along the Waccamaw and Great Pee Dee rivers.

The Fish and Wildlife Service calls them some of the most diverse freshwater wetlands in North America. Visitors paddle a blackwater swamp, walk among cypress trees and look for swallow-tailed kites and more than 200 other bird species.

Even at Conway, the tide still raises and lowers the river about a foot. Nearly every river lot reaches the high-risk flood zone: all but 4 of about 460.

In the past year, 14 houses on the river sold for about $537,000 at the middle, against about $330,000 for houses nearby that are not on the water.

Lakefront homes: a pontoon boat out back

In Plantation Lakes in Carolina Forest, owners take pontoon boats out on a lake with about 15 miles of shoreline. Many homes back onto the lake.

The owners' amenity area has day and overnight docks and a boat ramp. Boats on the lake are limited to 19 feet, with small motors.

On the day we counted, 9 houses on the lake there were for sale, asking about $780,000 to $1.3 million.

Most lake and pond houses for sale ask far less. Every waterfront house for sale under $300,000 was on a lake or pond.

The state's coastal dock permit covers salt water and the marsh, not a lake. On a lake, we read the community's rules on docks and boats before you offer.

Can you build a dock on your waterfront lot?

On salt water, you can if the lot and the creek are wide enough and the state issues a permit. Only 52 of the 506 waterfront houses for sale list a dock. On canals, creeks, the marsh, rivers and the waterway, more than half do.

A dock over the tidal marsh or a saltwater creek needs a permit from the state's coastal permit office, SCDES, before it is built. That office used to be part of DHEC.

The permit covers tidal salt water, the marsh and other land the average high tide covers, and the beach. Docks on lakes and fresh water fall under other rules.

Is the lot wide enough?

On a lot created after May 23, 1993, a private dock needs at least 75 feet of frontage at the marsh edge. On such a lot, under 50 feet means no dock of its own. Two neighbors with at least 50 feet each can share one.

A waterfront subdivision may have a dock master plan, recorded with its map of lots, that shows which lots may get a dock and where. A plan is a guide, not a promise of a permit. To see a lot's width and lines, use our Horry County GIS map guide.

How big can the dock be?

Under the state rule in force now, a dock on a tidal creek is sized by the creek's width, from marsh grass to marsh grass.

Creek widthDock the state usually allows
Under 10 feetNo dock
10 to 19 feetNo dock, except 50 square feet with no boat lift on a lot with over 500 feet of frontage, or where no dock is possible from the other side
20 to 50 feet120 square feet
51 to 150 feet160 square feet
Over 150 feet600 square feet

Walkways, ramps and mooring piles do not count, and neither does a lift 8 by 20 feet or smaller. The table is not for docks on the waterway itself. The state has proposed new widths and sizes, and they need the legislature's approval.

What about the fee, the wait, repairs and selling?

  • The fee. The state charges $150 to apply for a private dock with a walkway 100 feet or shorter, and $250 for a longer one.
  • The wait. Once the file is complete, the state posts a 15-day public notice. By law, the state has 30 days from the complete file to decide.
  • Repairs. Normal repair of a standing dock, kept to its old size, needs only a request and a letter back from the state.
  • Storms. A dock a storm destroys can be rebuilt to its old layout if the work is done within five years.
  • Selling. An active permit can be transferred to the buyer for $25. Both owners sign.
  • Other approvals. The owner may also need the Army Corps of Engineers, the city or county, and the HOA to approve.

If you would rather not ask an HOA about a dock or a boat, 86 waterfront houses for sale listed no HOA fee on the day we counted.

Do you need flood insurance on a waterfront home?

A federally regulated lender requires flood insurance on a waterfront home in FEMA's high-risk zone. How many lots are in that zone depends on the water.

WaterLots that reach the high-risk flood zone
Cherry Grove channelsOver 99%
Waccamaw RiverAbout 99%
Oceanfront housesAbout 98%
Oceanfront houses, in the zone where waves are expected (VE)About 60%
Intracoastal WaterwayAbout 70%

For one-family houses with a FEMA policy in Horry County, the middle yearly cost was:

Where the house isMiddle cost a year
Outside the high-risk zone, a home you live inAbout $550
Zone A or AE, a home you live inAbout $1,070
Zone A or AE, a home you do not live inAbout $1,370
Zone V, where waves are expectedAbout $3,200

The zone of the lot is not always the zone of the house. FEMA and the lender go by where the house stands and by a survey of how high its floor is. We get both for the house you like.

We also look for past flooding at the house, in the records listed in how to find out if a house flooded. Wind and homeowners insurance are in coastal home insurance.

Can you get from your dock to the ocean by boat?

Yes, from the waterway, the channels and the ocean-side creeks. From the waterway between Little River and Socastee, a marked channel about 2 miles long leads to the jetties of Little River Inlet.

The sandbar at the inlet's mouth shifts, and its buoys are moved often. Murrells Inlet opens straight to the ocean between its own jetties, and boaters new to it are advised to enter with local knowledge.

Which bridges does your boat pass under?

Thirteen crossings span the waterway in about 30 waterway miles from the state line to Socastee. Every fixed bridge has a reported clearance of 65 feet, so a boat under 65 feet tall passes each one.

A boat taller than a swing bridge or the railroad bridge when it is closed waits for it to open.

Under the federal rule, the Little River swing bridge opens on signal, except from 7 to 9 a.m. and 4 to 6 p.m. on weekdays that are not federal holidays. Then it opens only on the hour and the half hour.

The Barefoot Landing and Socastee swing bridges have no special schedule in the federal rules, so they open when a boat signals.

The federal channel is designed for at least 12 feet of depth. The federal chart guide still warns of rock ledges along its edges from about Little River to US 501.

How much does the water drop at the dock?

On the ocean side, a lot that floats a boat at high tide may sit on mud at low tide.

Can you keep a boat without a dock?

Yes. A deeded boat slip is another way to keep a boat on the water. Horry County has 322 slips sold as real estate, 223 of them in Little River.

In the past year, slips at Mariner's Pointe there sold for about $16,500 at the middle.

Oceanfront or ocean view: what does waterfront mean in a listing?

Oceanfront means the first row, with no house between the lot and the beach. A house with an ocean view can be across the street or a few rows back.

A listing that names a water is often near it, not on it. Of 117 houses for sale whose listing names the Intracoastal Waterway, only 22 are marked as waterfront.

Every house that names a lake or a canal is marked as waterfront. Before you pay for water, we check the lot line, the deed and the flood map.

Common questions

Waterfront home FAQ

Does the sun rise over the ocean in Myrtle Beach?

Yes. From central Myrtle Beach south to Garden City, the sun rises over open sea every morning of the year. Farther north, from Windy Hill to Cherry Grove, it comes up over the shore on many mornings around June.

What is the cheapest waterfront in Myrtle Beach?

A condo in an oceanfront building, which sold for about $200,000 at the middle. For a house, it is a lake or pond: every waterfront house for sale under $300,000 was on one.

How much more does a waterfront home cost?

On the beach, a house resold for about $2.2 million at the middle, against about $615,000 for houses nearby off the water. On the Intracoastal Waterway, a house sold for about twice the houses nearby. On Garden City's salt marsh, a house sold for about twice the other houses on that side of the beach road.

Can I build a dock on my waterfront lot?

On salt water, yes, if the lot and the creek are wide enough and the state issues a permit. Lots created since 1993 need 75 feet of frontage at the marsh edge.

Can I keep a boat without a dock?

Yes. A deeded boat slip is another way, and most of the slips sold as real estate in Horry County are in Little River.

Do I need flood insurance on a waterfront home?

A federally regulated lender requires it in FEMA's high-risk zone. Nearly every lot on the beach, the Cherry Grove channels and the Waccamaw River reaches that zone. On the Intracoastal Waterway, about 3 in 10 lots are wholly outside it.
