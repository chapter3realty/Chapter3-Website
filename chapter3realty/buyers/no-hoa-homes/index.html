Buying a home

Where can you buy a home with no HOA near Myrtle Beach?
16 areas where most homes have no fee.

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated October 22, 2026

Room for a boat, a truck and a shed, from Little River to Aynor.

The short answer

Almost every house for sale outside a named subdivision is listed with no homeowners association (HOA) fee, and so are about three in four built before 1990. Most are in the areas below, from beach streets and river towns to country land.

1. Downtown Myrtle Beach: the boardwalk on foot

Listed with no HOA fee: nearly 9 in 10 homes for sale

From the older streets downtown, you can walk to the oceanfront boardwalk. From Collins Street it is about a quarter hour on foot. From the day after Labor Day through April, you can ride a bike or walk a dog on it at any hour.

Most of these houses are within half a mile of the beach. All of them are outside the high-risk flood zones, where the federal rule makes a lender require flood insurance.

Plyler Park on the oceanfront has free live music and a fireworks show on Wednesdays in summer. The older houses are on Collins, Chester and Dunbar Streets, and Spivey Park and the New Town Bark Park are on Collins Street too.

A trail with a wooden boardwalk is beside Withers Swash, one of the city's oldest waterways, from Third Avenue South to Broadway and Collins Street.

Lots are modest, about 9,000 square feet in a typical case. Inside the city, a camper may not be kept in the front yard, so it has to fit beside or behind the house.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $435,000.

2. Myrtle Heights: a beach path at the end of each avenue

Listed with no HOA fee: every home for sale

In Myrtle Heights, the plan of the mid-1930s carried the avenues through to the beach, and those ends now give public access to the sand. From every house for sale here, a beach access is less than 5 minutes away on foot.

The same plan set a minimum setback of 75 feet for oceanfront houses. The district is on the National Register of Historic Places, which calls the Myrtle Heights-Oak Park district the largest intact group of historic properties in Myrtle Beach.

The houses, built from about 1925 to 1945, are mostly two-story frame homes, many with a garage or a garage apartment. You will see porches and wood awnings painted solid or striped to look like canvas. A few houses keep old walls of coquina rock or brick.

Back yards along Ocean Boulevard often have native wax myrtles, the plant the city is named for, along with red cedars, palmettos and windswept oaks.

Sea Captain's House, a restaurant in a 1930 beach cottage, is about a 9-minute walk from the middle of Myrtle Heights.

On an oceanfront house, a change to the outside, including its color, needs the city's Community Appearance Board. Ordinary repair does not.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $1.6 million.

3. Pine Lakes' older streets: almost half an acre in the middle of the city

Listed with no HOA fee: every home for sale on the older streets

The older Pine Lakes streets have big lots in the middle of the city: typically almost half an acre, more than twice a downtown lot. There is room for a workshop or a boat beside the house.

The streets are around the golf course, which is less than half a mile from the ocean, on rolling land. The beach is about 0.6 mile on foot, at the Seaside Place street end, one of the city's fully accessible beach paths.

Ocean Forest Memorial Park, with a walking path, is at the Haskell Circle beach access, about half a mile on foot. North Ocean Boulevard has bike lanes.

A resident whose car is registered and taxed in the city gets a free parking decal. With it, you park free at city meters, at beach street ends and at Pelicans baseball games.

The city picks up trash, recycling and yard waste at the curb each week, for about $34 a month.

The Preserve at Pine Lakes, the newer section, has an HOA fee and is not counted here.

You pay for the room: houses here are listed at more than twice the downtown price, for more than twice the land.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $930,000.

4. Windy Hill: free day docks at Barefoot Landing

Listed with no HOA fee: 6 in 10 homes for sale

Windy Hill is 3 minutes by car from Barefoot Landing on the Intracoastal Waterway. Come by boat, and you can use the finger docks free for the day while you shop or eat.

The Alabama Theatre and House of Blues are on the same grounds. Barefoot Landing welcomes leashed pets all year, and many of its shops let them inside.

Most houses for sale here are within a 10-minute walk of a public beach access. Inside North Myrtle Beach, a work truck up to 18,500 pounds may park at home.

On 41st Avenue South, a pocket park with garden plantings, two pergolas and a walking path is on the site of the old water tower. Conway investors developed Windy Hill after World War II, and in 1968 it joined Cherry Grove, Ocean Drive and Crescent Beach to form North Myrtle Beach.

Flood is the cost here. About half of these houses are in a high-risk flood zone, and with a mortgage your lender will almost always require flood insurance.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $745,000.

5. Crescent Beach: half a mile on foot to a beach access

Listed with no HOA fee: nearly 9 in 10 homes for sale

Most houses for sale in Crescent Beach are less than half a mile on foot from a public beach access. From 6th to 27th Avenue South, the beach is one of the city's three surf zones. From mid-May to mid-September, boards are banned from 9 to 4 on the rest of the beach, but you can surf here.

Ingram Dunes Nature Preserve, on Hillside Drive, is the city's last remaining maritime forest. It has native dunes 30 feet tall and an interpretive half-mile nature trail.

Most are older beach houses, on streets such as Hillside Drive, Holly Drive, Perrin Drive and Strand Avenue. Few of them need flood insurance for a mortgage. In Cherry Grove, almost every home does.

In the middle of Crescent Beach, the 18th and Edge pocket park has benches and a fountain. Central Park, 20 acres with lighted tennis courts, pickleball courts and a paved walking path, is about 0.7 mile away.

City beach lots charge for parking from March through October. Residents register their plates each year for free passes.

Lots are small, about 7,600 square feet. Under the city's rule, a trailer or camper goes behind the front of the house or under a carport. That takes a carport, or room beside or behind the house.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $755,000.

6. Ocean Drive: Main Street and the shag

Listed with no HOA fee: more than half the homes for sale

On Main Street in Ocean Drive, you can learn the shag free on Tuesday nights. Fat Harold's Beach Club calls itself the Home of the Shag and teaches the dance.

In the Ocean Drive and Crescent Beach area, a pavilion and amusement park drew crowds who danced the shag.

A Saturday here can start with breakfast at Hoskins Restaurant on Main Street, which opened in 1948.

The North Myrtle Beach Area Historical Museum is free, and its exhibits include beach music artists and shag dancing. McLean Park, a quarter mile from Hoskins on foot, has a two-acre lake and a half-mile paved walking path.

Each summer, the city holds free Music on Main concerts on Thursdays at the Horseshoe. Each December, a nighttime Christmas parade comes up Main Street. Few of the houses for sale here with no fee need flood insurance for a mortgage.

Crowds come with it: on concert nights Main Street closes to cars in the afternoon, and the St. Patrick's festival draws about 40,000 people.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $665,000.

7. Cherry Grove: a boat on the canals

Listed with no HOA fee: more than half the homes for sale

Cherry Grove's canals were created as early as the 1950s. The city has dredged canals there twice since 2016, to give owners boat access to House Creek and the Intracoastal Waterway at high and low tide.

The city's boat ramp at 53rd Avenue North reaches both the marsh and the open ocean through Hog Inlet. It has a two-lane boat launch, a kayak launch, a fishing pier, a floating boat dock and a fish cleaning station.

North Myrtle Beach residents park free at the ramp when they register with the city. It is also the way in to Heritage Shores Nature Preserve, an island in the marsh reached on elevated boardwalks.

Many of the canal houses have their own docks, so the boat stays behind the house. Across Hog Inlet, Waties Island has almost three miles of undeveloped beach.

Each July 4, the city's free fireworks go up at the Cherry Grove Pier. The homes here with an HOA fee are all in named subdivisions.

Two costs come with the canals: flood insurance and a dredging fee. Almost every home here is in a high-risk flood zone. Owners of about 415 properties in the canal district pay the city about $1,700 a year for the dredging, on the county tax bill.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $850,000.

8. Little River: the old fishing village on the waterway

Listed with no HOA fee: every home for sale

The houses on Little River's old waterfront streets are less than a mile from the docks on Mineola Avenue, on the Intracoastal Waterway. Local boatmen began taking visitors fishing for a fee there, probably in the early 1920s. They ran the first organized offshore fishing trips for visitors in South Carolina.

Hurricane Juel's, with a view of the waterway, has been family owned since 1945.

The waterfront holds the Blue Crab Festival each May, on the weekend after Mother's Day; a TV station reported more than 30,000 people there in 2026. ShrimpFest follows each October. On the Saturday after Thanksgiving, a lighted boat parade leaves Little River Inlet and heads down the waterway.

The county's T. Craig Campbell boat landing is on the waterway in town. After the inlet's jetties were finished in 1983, boats could cross the bar at any time of day. The state keeps an artificial fishing reef off the inlet.

Vereen Memorial Historical Gardens, a county park on the waterway, has about 3 miles of trails and boardwalks through its gardens and along the water. Some of the houses for sale are new, on Mineola and Ellis Avenues; others are on McCorsley Avenue and Bayshore Drive.

The beach takes a drive: none of these houses is within a mile of the ocean.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $420,000.

9. Socastee: a county boat ramp 2 minutes away

Listed with no HOA fee: most homes for sale

Rosewood Landing, a county boat ramp on the Intracoastal Waterway, is 2 minutes by car from these streets. Socastee Landing and Park has a second ramp and a new accessible playground. The swing bridge at Socastee, finished in 1935, marked the completion of the Intracoastal Waterway in South Carolina. The waterway's opening celebration was held in Socastee in April 1936.

These streets are outside any town, so the county's rules apply. County zoning names no limit on where you park a boat. Outdoor storage may not be in front of the house, and a small shed can go 5 feet from the line.

Most are 1980s houses in Rosewood Estates and Cimerron Plantation.

Socastee Recreation Park has fields, picnic areas, disc golf, trails and a playground. Market Common and Coastal Grand Mall are each about 10 minutes away, and the beach at Surfside is about 15 minutes by car.

Flood insurance is the extra cost to plan for: about a third of these houses need it if you buy with a mortgage.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $250,000, the lowest on this page.

10. Surfside Beach: two boats at home and golf carts on the streets

Listed with no HOA fee: nearly 9 in 10 homes for sale

In Surfside Beach, the town allows up to two boats or trailers at a home, and only one over 21 feet. A permitted golf cart may be driven in daylight within four miles of home, on streets posted at 35 miles an hour or less.

The town has live oaks well over a century old and 36 public beach accesses. In 2016 Surfside Beach became the first town certified autism-friendly through the Champion Autism Network.

The town holds a farmers market on Tuesdays from spring into December in Memorial Park, and free Sunday afternoon concerts there from late May through August. Each July 4, a golf cart parade goes down Ocean Boulevard.

Each October, the fall festival has more than 100 craft vendors. Fireworks may be set off on the sand from sunset on July 4 and on New Year's Eve, but not within 500 feet of the pier.

The town picks up trash at the curb, and it has more than nine miles of sidewalk.

One cost to know on the ocean side: about 1 in 4 homes there needs flood insurance if you buy with a mortgage.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $940,000.

11. Garden City Beach: a free pier walk and free beach parking

Listed with no HOA fee: most homes for sale

The Pier at Garden City is always free to walk, and every oceanfront beach access in Garden City has free parking all year. On summer nights, live bands play at both ends of the pier.

A pier pass covers fishing, so you need no state license. Its Hugo burger is named for the 1989 hurricane that destroyed the pier.

Most of the houses for sale here are within half a mile of the shore. The county's Stanley Drive landing is a 5-minute drive.

Each July 4, decorated boats start at the point of Garden City Beach and parade through Murrells Inlet.

Budget for flood insurance: with a mortgage, a lender will almost always require it on most of these houses.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $1.4 million.

12. Murrells Inlet's north end: by the creeks and the county boat ramp

Listed with no HOA fee: almost all homes for sale

At the north end of Murrells Inlet, some of the houses for sale are on the creeks of the inlet, and the rest are a few streets back. The county's boat landing on Stanley Drive is a 4-minute drive, and several houses on the market lately have their own floating dock.

People first came to the inlet for oyster roasts and fried shore dinners, and seafood restaurants followed. The MarshWalk, a half-mile wooden boardwalk along the salt marsh with a row of restaurants, is about 6 minutes away by car. The writer Mickey Spillane made Murrells Inlet his home for decades.

The free pier walk and free beach parking at Garden City Beach are a 6-minute drive across the marsh. Brookgreen Gardens, with its sculpture gardens on four former rice plantations, is about 15 minutes away by car.

Murrells Inlet is not a town, so the county's rules apply on most of these streets. The south part is in Georgetown County. There, a vehicle or travel trailer without current plates has to be kept in a closed building.

Flood is the honest cost: more than half of these houses are in a high-risk flood zone.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $820,000.

13. Conway: live oaks and the Waccamaw River

Listed with no HOA fee: nearly 9 in 10 homes for sale

Conway's older streets were designed around its live oaks, on a bluff of the Waccamaw River. The Riverwalk follows the river downtown, and the Waccamaw is a federal National Water Trail.

In town, lots are about 15,000 square feet. Inside the city, a camper or a trailer can go in the side or back yard or under a carport. A shed goes in back, with a permit.

The oldest streets form a National Register district. Its houses range from Greek Revival and Carpenter Gothic through Queen Anne to 1920s bungalows, one of them with a porch around three sides. City Hall is the old Horry County courthouse, finished in 1825 to a design credited to Robert Mills. Its courtroom still holds city court every week.

The Horry County Museum is in the restored 1905 Burroughs School, about a quarter hour on foot from downtown. Riverfest brings music and fireworks to the river on the Saturday before the Fourth of July weekend, and the Celebration of Lights opens each December.

Billy Witherspoon Landing, a county ramp on the Waccamaw, is a 6-minute drive. Conway Medical Center is a 12-minute drive.

The beach is a 25-minute drive.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $285,000.

14. Loris: a small town with its own hospital

Listed with no HOA fee: almost all homes for sale

Loris is a small town with its own hospital: McLeod Loris, with an emergency room, is in town. The older houses were built from the 1940s to the 1970s, on lots of about 0.4 acre. By 1914 Loris was known as the Gate City, because it was the first town on the railroad south of the North Carolina line.

City zoning allows up to two buildings beside the house, a garage or carport included, so there is room for a workshop. A work truck up to 2 tons may park at home.

The Main Street library lends cake pans, state park passes and Wi-Fi hotspots.

On the third Saturday in October, the city holds the Loris Bog-Off downtown, with vendors, a car show and live music. The festival's dish is chicken bog: chicken, rice and sausage.

You drive more from here: the beach is about 35 minutes by car.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $320,000.

15. The country around Loris: new houses on large lots

Listed with no HOA fee: most homes for sale

Around Loris, most houses for sale are new, built in 2024 or later, outside any town limits. Many of the new houses are on lots of about 0.7 acre, with room for a camper, a trailer and a shop under the county's rules.

The Waccamaw River Heritage Preserve protects 28 miles of the Waccamaw River, with riverside camping on its land.

For a work truck, the county lets a business run from home keep three service vehicles on a farm-zoned lot under an acre and a half. McLeod Loris hospital is about 9 minutes away by car.

Outside the towns, a small shed can go 5 feet from the line, and fences can be 8 feet tall. The Solid Waste Authority provides no curbside trash pickup there, so you hire a hauler or use a county drop-off center.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $415,000.

16. Aynor: an acre and a river landing

Listed with no HOA fee: most homes for sale

Around Aynor, lots are typically about an acre, with room beside the house for a boat or a camper. More than half of the houses for sale here are new.

Galivants Ferry Landing, a county ramp on the Little Pee Dee River, is about 6 minutes from the center of town. The Little Pee Dee is popular with canoes, kayaks and small powerboats.

At Galivants Ferry, the old farm houses have full-width front porches on brick piers. Some were built from wood cut on the farm itself, and the district has log tobacco barns. Galivants Ferry has also held a political speaking, the Stump, since 1880.

Each September, almost 25,000 people come to the Aynor Harvest Hoe-Down, a festival the Pilot Club of Aynor started. In the town of Aynor, zoning does not limit a camper or a work truck at home. Most of these houses are outside the town, under county rules.

You will drive more: about 45 minutes to the beach, and about 30 to the nearest hospital, Conway Medical Center.

Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $370,000.

What can you do at home with no HOA?

With no HOA, the limits on parking, rentals and buildings on your lot are the town's, or the county's outside the towns, along with any deed restriction. Most of the homes with no HOA fee around Conway, Loris and Aynor are outside the town limits, under the county's rules.

Before you make an offer, we check the town or county rules for that lot, the deed for restrictions and the county records for an HOA.

In Myrtle Beach, North Myrtle Beach and Surfside Beach, renting out a home needs a business license. In the towns, you can leave a boat in the driveway for a day to load and unload. On county roads, the county bans parking only where it posts a sign.

Example: Teresa wants a home with no HOA and room for her 20-foot boat. She likes a house on a small lot with a Myrtle Beach address.

Her Chapter3 agent finds the house on the county map, inside the city of Myrtle Beach. The city's front-yard rule applies to her boat, and that lot has no other room for it.

The agent shows her houses outside the city limits, under the county's rules. Teresa makes an offer on one with a wide side yard.

  • About 130 from $500,000 to $750,000, a third of them within half a mile of the beach.
  • About 220 at $750,000 and up, about 2 in 3 of them within half a mile of the beach.

What do you give up with no HOA?

No HOA means no shared pool, clubhouse or gate, and no HOA rules for your neighbors' houses either. A home with no HOA can still have deed restrictions on what you build or keep there.

If you want a pool, a clubhouse or a gate, you pay an HOA fee for them. Our guides to the best neighborhoods for what you want nearby and to gated communities around Myrtle Beach show where to look. Common questions

Homes with no HOA FAQ

Can I park a boat at my house in Myrtle Beach?

Yes, on your own lot, within the rule of the town it is in; the table under What can you do at home gives each one.

Which areas near Myrtle Beach almost always have an HOA?

Carolina Forest, the Grande Dunes, the Market Common and Burgess: almost every home for sale there has an HOA fee. Almost all homes for sale in Little River's named subdivisions have an HOA fee too. Longs and the country around Conway look rural, but most homes for sale there have an HOA fee too.

Are there homes with no HOA in Murrells Inlet?

Yes, but only a little more than 1 in 4 homes for sale with a Murrells Inlet address is listed with no HOA fee. At the north end of the inlet, by the creeks and the Stanley Drive landing, almost every house for sale has none.

Are there homes with no HOA in North Myrtle Beach?

Yes. More than half of the homes for sale with a North Myrtle Beach address are listed with no HOA fee. Almost all of them are in Cherry Grove, Crescent Beach, Windy Hill and Ocean Drive.

What is a typical HOA fee in Myrtle Beach?

About $95 a month on a house. A condo fee is much higher because it includes the building's insurance, water and sewer, trash and a pool.

Can you buy a condo with no HOA fee in Myrtle Beach?

Almost never. Of about 2,700 condos for sale around Myrtle Beach, only one was listed with no HOA fee. South Carolina law makes condo owners share the cost of the building.

Does a listing with no HOA fee mean the home has no HOA?

Not always. A blank fee means no fee was listed. We check the deed and the county records for an HOA before you make an offer.

Is a campground home with no HOA fee a no-HOA home?

No. A campground home has no HOA fee, but it sits on leased land, and you pay a yearly lease for the land under it.
