First-time buyers

Your first home in Myrtle Beach
may take less cash than you think.

  • Often no down paymenton VA and USDA loans
  • Seller-paid closing costson FHA and VA
  • $10,000 from SC Housingunder its income limit

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

Your cash to buy in Myrtle Beach is the down payment plus the closing costs, and a first-time buyer can often bring less of each.

The short answer

Your down payment depends on your loan. Your lender gives you the figure when it pre-approves you, after checking your credit and income. Part of your cash is earnest money, the deposit you give with your offer. In an ordinary sale it is credited to you at closing.

A VA loan often needs no down payment. A USDA loan can cover the full price inside USDA's area, mostly around Conway, Loris, Aynor and Longs, for low- and moderate-income buyers. On an FHA loan, a credit score of 580 or more gets FHA's smallest down payment, and the seller can pay your closing costs. SC Housing adds $10,000 for buyers under its income limit.

How much cash do you need to buy a first home in Myrtle Beach?

The closing costs are the fees to make the loan and close the sale. Seven things can lower what you bring.

  • FHA's smallest down payment, at a 580 scoreEvery buyer with a credit score of 580 or more can get FHA's smallest down payment. From 500 to 579 you put more down, and below 500 FHA does not insure the loan. Your lender gives you the dollar figure for your price.
  • No down payment on a VA loanA loan backed by the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) often needs no down payment, as long as the price is not above the appraised value. That is the value an appraiser gives the home for the loan. Our VA loan guide covers who can use one.
  • No down payment on a USDA loanA USDA loan, backed by the U.S. Department of Agriculture, can cover the full price, with no down payment, when the appraiser values the home at the price or more. It is for low- and moderate-income buyers in USDA's area. On our map, about 6 in 10 houses listed at or under $450,000 in Horry County are inside that area. Nearly all have a Conway, Loris, Aynor or Longs address.
  • The seller pays your closing costsOn an FHA loan, insured by the Federal Housing Administration, the seller, a builder or the agents can pay up to 6 percent of the price in all toward your closing costs. On a $270,000 house, the middle one listed now at or under $300,000, that is up to $16,200. It can cover only your actual closing costs, and the seller has to agree to it in your offer.
  • $10,000 from SC HousingSC Housing, the South Carolina State Housing Finance and Development Authority, gives an eligible buyer $10,000 toward the down payment and closing costs. Our guide to SC down payment assistance has who qualifies, when it is repaid and the steps.
  • A gift toward the purchaseOn an FHA loan, the money can be a gift from family, an employer or labor union, a charity or a government homebuyer program. The lender needs a gift letter signed by the giver.
  • Up to $10,000 from an IRAYou can take up to $10,000 out of an IRA, an individual retirement account, for a first home without the 10 percent early-withdrawal penalty. You, and your spouse if you are married, must not have owned a main home in the last two years. The money must be spent within 120 days.

What does a first home cost in Myrtle Beach, and what do you get?

Almost half the homes sold in Horry County in the past year sold for under $300,000. Under $200,000, most were condos. From $250,000, most were houses.

New houses often cost less than resales

In the middle, a new house from a builder sold for about $350,000, against about $375,000 for a resale.

In our experience, most builders here offer incentives to buyers using FHA, VA and conventional loans. A conventional loan is one that no government agency backs. Our new construction guide covers buying from a builder, and our page on the builders shows where each one builds.

Where are the starter houses around Myrtle Beach?

Mostly inland. The most sold around Conway and Longs, then in Socastee, west of Myrtle Beach. In one of Conway's ZIP codes, about 7 in 10 houses and townhouses that sold went for under $300,000. In North Myrtle Beach it was about 1 in 12.

Should your first home in Myrtle Beach be a condo or a house?

A house, if your budget reaches about $250,000 and you can live inland. Below that, most homes that sell are condos, and a condo can be a good first home once we have checked the building.

  • A condo

    A condo is the lowest price into a home of your own here. The loan is the hard part: the building has to be approved for it. HUD, the federal housing department, lists only 9 condo communities in all of Horry County that are approved for FHA loans.

    About 1 in 6 condos that sold under $300,000 were in a high-risk flood zone, where the lender will require flood insurance. About half went to owners who get their tax bill in another state.

  • A house

    A house costs more, and the loan is simpler: there is no building to approve. The middle house listed at or under $300,000 has about twice the floor space of the middle condo, and a yard.

    Only about 3 in 100 houses and townhouses that sold under $300,000 were in a high-risk flood zone. About 1 in 7 went to owners whose tax bill goes to another state.

In our experience, condo buildings with a rental program usually do not meet the rules most conventional loans follow, because so many owners are investors. An FHA loan in a building that is not on HUD's list needs an approval for that one unit.

A condo also has a monthly fee to its homeowners association (HOA), and the chance of a special assessment, a one-time bill for repairs. Our guide to special assessments explains them. Many houses have an HOA too; our guide to homes with no HOA lists places without one.

What we check before you make an offer

  • The building's loan approval. For a condo, we find out whether the building is approved for your loan before you offer.
  • The HOA papers. We read the rules, the budget, the money saved for repairs and the financial statements, so a fee rise or a one-time repair bill is no surprise.
  • The flood zone. We look up every home you offer on, so you know whether the lender will require flood insurance.
  • The insurance quote. If you buy with us, we get you one before you make an offer on a house.

What will your first home cost each year besides the loan?

Property tax is the yearly cost you can work out before you buy. On a $270,000 house you live in, outside the cities, the county tax is about $990 a year once you file for the 4 percent owner rate.

Cost besides the loanWhat to plan for
Property tax on your own home, after you fileAbout $990 a year on a $270,000 house
Property tax if you do not fileAbout $3,260 a year on the same house
Home insurance on a houseAbout $2,000 a year in the middle; half of owners pay $1,300 to $3,000
A condo owner's own insuranceAbout $800 a year; the condo fee may also pay the building's insurance
Electric, one household's summer billAbout $130 a month
Water, the same householdAbout $50 a month

Tax: county tax outside the cities, on the county's value, before special district and stormwater fees. Insurance: what Horry County owners who live in their home told the Census they paid in 2024 for fire, hazard and flood insurance, outside the Loris area. Electric and water: one of our team's own bills, on a 1,400 square foot two-story house; one household, not an average.

The 4 percent rate means your home is taxed on 4 percent of its value. Any home you do not file for is taxed on 6 percent, and pays school operating tax too. Filing saves more than $2,200 a year here, and it is not automatic: the last step below has the deadline. Our property tax guide has every town's rate.

Flood and wind

A lender must require flood insurance when the building is in a high-risk flood zone. A policy bought while the mortgage is made starts with no wait; otherwise there is a 30‑day wait. Before you close in that zone, check what flood insurance costs here.

In Myrtle Beach, homes east of the U.S. 17 Bypass are in the state's coastal wind area, where insurers may leave wind and hail out of a homeowners policy. The insurance figures above include flood and wind policies where owners have them. Your own quote depends on the house. Our coastal insurance guide explains the policies, and our cost to own guide adds up a year.

Do you qualify as a first-time home buyer in South Carolina?

These are the lines that decide whether the help above fits you.

  • Have you owned a home before?SC Housing's main program in Horry County does not ask whether you owned a home in the past. It asks only that you own no other property, or a share of one, on the day the loan closes. Bare land is allowed. The IRA rule above is different: no main home in the last two years.
  • Your credit scoreSC Housing needs at least a 640 credit score. FHA needs 500, and 580 for its smallest down payment.
  • Your income, for SC HousingIn Horry County, SC Housing's household income limit is about $115,000 a year for one or two people and about $134,000 for three or more. The home's price can be up to $450,000.
  • Living in itOn an FHA loan, one buyer moves in within 60 days of closing and plans to stay at least a year.

Is there a federal first-time buyer tax credit? Not yet. The SC Housing programs are the help you can use now.

What are the steps to buy your first home in South Carolina?

  1. Get pre-approved by a lender.The lender checks your credit and income and gives you a price to shop under. When an SC Housing program fits, a Chapter3 agent connects you with an SC Housing approved lender.
  2. Choose a home.We send homes in your budget and run the checks above on each one you like.
  3. Make the offer.You can ask the seller to pay your closing costs in the offer. If a real estate broker holds your earnest money, it stays in the broker's trust account until the sale closes. In an ordinary sale it is credited to you at closing.
  4. Inspect the house.A home inspector looks over the house for you.
  5. The appraisal.The lender's appraiser gives the home a value. Low appraisals are rare here. When one comes in low, our agents gather recent sales of similar homes and ask for the value to be looked at again.
  6. Close with an attorney.Every South Carolina home closing must be supervised by a South Carolina attorney.
  7. Move in, then file for the 4 percent rate.Apply to the county assessor by January 15 after the year you move in.

Common questions

First-time home buyer questions

Is there a first-time home buyer tax credit in South Carolina?

SC Housing no longer offers its tax credit certificate. The tax break an owner gets here is the 4 percent rate on the home you live in, which needs an application to the county assessor.

Is there a homestead exemption for first-time buyers in Horry County?

Not for being a first-time buyer. In South Carolina the homestead exemption is for owners 65 or older who have lived in the state a year, or who are disabled or blind. It exempts the first $50,000 of the home's value.

Is there help if I earn more than SC Housing's limit?

Palmetto Home Advantage, another SC Housing program, has an income limit of $140,000 in every county, counted on the buyers' own income, not the whole household. It is open to first-time and repeat buyers. Its help is forgiven after 10 years, and repaid in full on a sale, refinance or move before then.

What is the FHA loan limit in Horry County?

FHA's 2026 loan limit for a one-unit home in Horry County is about $541,000. That is well above HUD's own estimate of the area's middle sale price, $340,000.
