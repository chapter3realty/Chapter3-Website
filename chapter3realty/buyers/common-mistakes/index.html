By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 2, 2026

The costliest mistake here is paying the 6 percent tax on a home you live in: about $2,930 a year on a $350,000 house. Nine more follow, by cost.

The short answer

Of people who bought a house in Horry County in 2024 and get its tax bill at that house, about 1 in 7 are still taxed at 6 percent. That is the costliest mistake here, and it comes back every year until they apply for 4 percent.

Flood insurance, wind cover and the seller's low tax bill come next.

Are you paying the 6 percent tax on a home you live in?

Yes, until you apply. A home you live in is taxed on 4 percent of its value once you apply and qualify, and it does not pay the part of the tax that runs the schools. Until then, the county bills it at 6 percent, the rate for second homes and rentals.

Newer owners are the most likely to still pay it.

1 in 5bought in 2025
1 in 7bought in 2024
1 in 17bought in 2018 or 2019, not sold since

The share, rounded, of houses owned by a person who gets the tax bill at that same house, still taxed at 6 percent. The newest buyers can still apply for this year.

Why do people moving here miss it?

The county does not apply it for you. It asks for South Carolina papers that a new arrival does not have yet:

  • a South Carolina driver's license or ID at the home's address, for each owner who lives there and a spouse;
  • South Carolina registration for every vehicle you own, at that address;
  • your state and federal tax returns, or the Social Security 1099;
  • if you own another home, its tax bill showing no exemption, or a letter that the exemption was removed.

You also certify that no one in the home claims the 4 percent rate on another home. That means you, a spouse or a child under 18 you support. None of you can claim to be a legal resident of another state.

  • You apply once

    Unless the ownership or who lives there changes.

  • Move in partway through a year

    You get the 4 percent rate for that whole year.

  • Aim for the end of May

    Apply by then, and the bill the county mails later that year comes at 4 percent. If you miss May, you can still apply for that year until its bill is due, usually January 15 of the next year.

We can price the tax at both rates while you choose a house, and give you the county's list of papers at closing. Our property tax calculator works out the bill in any town.

Why is my tax bill so much higher than the seller's?

The seller's bill shows what the seller pays, not what you will pay. A home owned for many years is often taxed on a value far below the county's own value of it. A sale resets that value, and the new value is first taxed the year after the sale.

Example: the seller has lived in a house since about 2010. The county taxes it on much less than its full value. You buy the house as a second home. Your bill is on the full value, at the second-home rate. You pay about five times the seller's bill.

We can work out your bill at your price and your tax rate, not from the seller's bill. Our property tax guide explains how a sale resets the value.

Do I need flood insurance if the house is not in a flood zone?

Federal law does not require flood insurance outside FEMA's high-risk zone. But since 2015, 3 in 10 paid flood claims in Horry County were on buildings outside that zone, and those repairs cost more.

$26,400FEMA's middle repair payment when an insured house outside the high-risk zone flooded
$15,700the same payment for a house inside the zone

One middle payment outside the zone equals about 48 years of the middle flood policy there.

We can get a flood insurance quote on any house you are about to buy, in the zone or out of it. Our flood history page shows how to learn whether a house has flooded before.

Will the listing tell me the house is in a flood zone?

Usually not. Of the 245 houses for sale in Horry County inside FEMA's high-risk flood zone, 196 do not say "flood zone" in their listing's feature list.

In the zone, a loan from a federally regulated lender requires flood insurance, and you must be told in writing.

Flood insurance costs more in the zone, and more again on a house the owner does not live in.

Does a homeowners policy cover wind near the beach?

Not always. In Horry County east of U.S. 17 or Bypass 17, whichever is farther west, and in named coastal areas of other counties, South Carolina lets an insurer leave wind and hail out of a homeowners policy. In Myrtle Beach, that line is the Bypass. Outside those areas, an insurer needs the state insurance director's approval to do it.

If wind is left out, you need a separate wind policy. The state's backup insurer for wind sells one to owners in this coastal area who cannot get it from another insurer. Our coastal insurance guide explains the three policies a house here can need.

Before you offer, we can tell you which side of the line a house is on, and get a homeowners quote that shows whether wind is in it.

How do I know a condo building has saved enough for repairs?

Only from the building's own budget and records. South Carolina does not require a condo to keep repair savings, called reserves, or to study what its repairs will need.

More than half of the condos for sale on the Grand Strand are in buildings 30 years old or older.

Example

An 80-unit building has saved 26 percent of what it should hold by now for its repairs. Association Reserves, a firm that studies condo savings, calls anything under 30 percent weak, with a high chance of a special bill to the owners.

In our example, the bill five years out is about $4,750 a unit, split evenly. It leaves out inflation and interest.

Freddie Mac buys home loans from lenders. For loan applications from January 4, 2027, it needs a building's budget to put at least 15 percent of its dues into savings. A buyer whose loan follows Freddie Mac's rules needs a building that meets it.

Before you offer, we can get the building's budget, its savings figure and its recent board minutes. Our pages on condo reserves, special assessments and condos in a lawsuit explain each one.

Can I rent my Myrtle Beach house by the week?

Inside the City of Myrtle Beach, only where the zoning allows rentals under 90 days. Most of the city's residential neighborhoods are not zoned for them.

  • Zoning districts starting with R

    No rentals under 90 days.

  • The RMV district

    The one R district where rentals under 90 days are allowed.

  • 24 houses

    In the whole city, only 24 houses in the traditional residential zones keep an older right to rent for under 90 days.

A violation is a misdemeanor, with a fine of up to $500, 30 days in jail, or both, each time. Where the zoning allows a short-term rental, the city requires a business license for it.

The city is rewriting its zoning code. The rules also differ in each other town and in the county outside the cities; our short-term rental rules page has each one. Before you make an offer, we can look up the zoning of any address in the city.

Should I skip the inspection to win the house?

No. Under South Carolina law, inspecting the house is the buyer's job, and the agents have no duty to inspect it.

The seller's disclosure form is not an inspection. On most resales of a house with one to four units, the seller must give you the state's form before you sign, unless the contract says otherwise. It tells you only what the seller knows.

The law exempts some sales from the form, such as a new home no one has lived in, a foreclosure, an estate sale and an auction.

Septic tanks are rare at the beach and more common inland. About 1 in 16 houses for sale on the Grand Strand list one, and 79 list well water.

We can line up the inspection once the seller accepts your offer, with a septic and a well check where the house has them.

Will the HOA let me keep my boat, golf cart or rental?

Only if its recorded rules allow it. About 7 in 10 houses for sale on our MLS, which covers the Grand Strand and inland Horry County, have an HOA fee, so most houses here come with a homeowners association (HOA) and its rules.

In South Carolina, those rules bind you only if they are on file with the county. The HOA's papers are recorded at the county's Register of Deeds. A rule the board adds or changes must be recorded by January 10 of the next year to stay in force.

The biggest mistake we see from buyers moving from New York is not checking that a community's rules fit how they live: its rules on boats, golf carts, rentals and pools. The same check matters for any buyer with a boat, a cart or a rental plan.

Many communities here ban renting the house out at all. When we search for you, we leave those out, and we read the HOA papers for any rule that would block your rental plan. We can read them for the boat, the cart and the pool too. If you would rather have no HOA, see our page on homes with no HOA.

Does the agent I called work for me?

Not until you both sign. In South Carolina, no brokerage firm represents you until you and the firm agree to it in writing.

  • Before you both sign

    You are the firm's customer. Under the law, the firm can help the sale along without representing you or the seller.

  • After you both sign an agreement

    The firm is your agent, on the terms the agreement states, including its pay.

Paying an agent does not by itself make the agent yours.

Since 2024, agents who use the MLS, the shared list of homes for sale, must sign a written agreement with you before you tour a home. It must state the agent's pay as an amount or a rate, and bar the agent from taking more from any source. It must also say that fees are fully negotiable and not fixed by law.

An agent on your side also tells you when a house is not a good buy. One buyer spent over a year sending his agent Zillow links. Each time, he was told it was a good buy in an up and coming neighborhood. He came to us after that.

Before we show you a home, we put in writing what we will do for you and what we are paid.

Common questions

Buyer mistakes FAQ

How do I apply for the 4 percent rate in Horry County?

Online, on the county's Online Services page, under Legal Residence Application. If the county asks for a missing paper, you can send it by mail, fax or email, or bring it to the Assessor's Office. The office's phone number is 843-915-5040.

How far back can I get a refund of the 6 percent tax on my home?

If you qualified for the 4 percent rate and paid at 6 percent, you can claim the extra tax back from the county assessor. Under state law, you have two years from the day you paid that bill, and a county council may allow refunds of the county's own share for more past years.

Does a condo's HOA fee show whether it has saved enough for repairs?

No. In our count of condos for sale here, the middle HOA fee is about $640 a month, and about $690 in buildings built in 1986 or earlier. Older buildings do not charge much more, so the fee alone does not show what a building has saved for its age.

Can I change jobs while buying a house in Myrtle Beach?

Wait until after closing if you can. On a loan that follows Fannie Mae's rules, the lender checks again that you still have your job no more than 10 business days before closing, or 15 in some cases. A loan officer at our preferred lender can tell you what your loan will need.
