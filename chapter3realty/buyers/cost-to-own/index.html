Before you buy

The real monthly cost to own a home in Myrtle Beach,
line by line.

Each month, part by part:

  • Tax$110
  • Insurance$170
  • HOA fee$100
  • Upkeep$270

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

A typical $350,000 house in Horry County costs about $650 a month to own besides the loan, $7,850 a year. Each part is below, town by town.

The short answer

A typical house in Horry County, about $350,000, costs about $650 a month to own if you live in it, about $7,850 a year. That does not count the loan payment: paying back the loan and its interest.

In Myrtle Beach and North Myrtle Beach, a typical condo costs more to own each year than a typical house. The condo fee is the reason: condo owners pay it for the building they share. A second home pays about three times the property tax, about $2,900 more a year.

How much does it cost to own a home in Myrtle Beach each year?

Upkeep and insurance are the biggest parts. Your lender usually collects the tax and insurance with each loan payment, but they are counted here, not in the loan. The HOA fee goes to your homeowners association. It and upkeep are usually paid separately.

Not in the total: electricity and water, about $2,280 a year. A home with a loan in the high-risk flood zone also needs a flood policy (see insurance below). The second-home column changes only the tax. Insurance on a second home can cost more.

Inside the City of Myrtle Beach, the same house costs about $7,890 a year as your home. The tax is higher there, about $1,410, and there is no county fee on the tax bill.

What does a house cost to own in each town?

Between about $7,600 and $8,300 a year for a typical house, as your home. North Myrtle Beach has the highest prices, but most of its houses for sale list no HOA fee.

Is a condo cheaper to own than a house in Myrtle Beach?

In Carolina Forest, yes. Its condo fees are the lowest in the table below, and a typical condo there costs about $1,000 a year less to own than a typical house. In Myrtle Beach and North Myrtle Beach, no: their condo fees are the highest.

The condo is cheaper to buy, so its loan is smaller. In Myrtle Beach a typical condo costs less than half as much as a typical house; in North Myrtle Beach, a little more than half.

Why are condo fees so high in Myrtle Beach?

They are highest near the ocean in Myrtle Beach. On the city's north end, a typical condo for sale lists about $875 a month. In Carolina Forest, inland, condos list about half that.

Owners pay the fee for everything they share: running the building, upkeep, and improvements to the grounds, halls, lobby, parking and pool. It may also include the tax and insurance on the shared property. Your own unit's insurance is separate: condo owners pay about $800 a year for it.

In each place below, the condo fee is about three quarters of what a condo owner pays a year to own the unit.

How many homes here pay an HOA fee?

More than half. About 6 in 10 homeowners across most of Horry County pay an HOA or condo fee; in the rest of South Carolina, about 1 in 3. Still, more than 1 in 4 houses for sale here list no HOA fee. Our page on homes with no HOA shows where they are.

How much more does a second home pay in property tax?

About 2.5 to 3.8 times as much as the same house as your home. On a $350,000 house, that is about $2,900 to $3,900 more a year, depending on the town.

On a home you live in, the tax rate is charged on 4 percent of its value, not the whole value: on a $350,000 house, on $14,000. It also pays only part of the school tax. A second home or a rental is charged on 6 percent and pays all of the school tax. Our second home guide covers the rest of owning one.

The 4 percent rate is not automatic: you apply for it. Our property tax guide has the steps, and our buyer mistakes page shows what paying 6 percent by mistake costs.

At 65 you can have part of your home's value exempted from property tax. Our retiree guide covers who qualifies and how much.

What does homeowners insurance cost in Myrtle Beach?

A typical house owner in Horry County pays about $2,000 a year, about 40 percent more than in the rest of South Carolina. That is all their home insurance together, with a wind or flood policy included where they have one. Half pay between about $1,300 and $3,000.

Near the ocean, wind can be a separate policy. On the ocean side of U.S. 17, the highway along the coast, an insurer may leave wind and hail out of a homeowners policy. In Myrtle Beach the line is the U.S. 17 Bypass. The owner then buys that cover separately, for example from the state's backup insurer for wind.

  • On the ocean side

    Most of the City of Myrtle Beach, Surfside Beach and Garden City.

  • West of the law's line

    Carolina Forest, Socastee and Conway are west of it. The state has at times let its wind insurer write policies there too, so we check the address.

  • On both sides

    North Myrtle Beach and Little River. It depends on the street. Murrells Inlet straddles the Horry and Georgetown county line. On the Georgetown side, the law draws its own line near U.S. 17.

The $2,000 includes those wind policies. About 1 in 10 house owners here pay $4,000 or more a year for all their home insurance, and about 1 in 40 pay $6,000 or more. The Census figures do not split by side of the highway. Our coastal insurance guide explains each policy.

If you buy with us, we get you an insurance quote for the address before you make an offer, so wind and flood are in your budget before you commit.

Do I need flood insurance in Myrtle Beach?

You must have it if the home is in the high-risk flood zone and your loan is from a bank or other lender the federal government regulates. Anywhere else it is your choice, but since 2015, 3 in 10 paid flood claims in Horry County were on buildings outside that zone. The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) maps the zone.

About 1 in 15 houses resold in Horry County were in the zone, and about 1 in 4 condos.

A FEMA flood policy on a house, the typical cost a year:

$550your home, outside the high-risk zone
$1,070your home, inside the zone
$1,370a second home, inside the zone

A FEMA flood policy on a condo unit its owner lives in, outside the high-risk zone, costs about $375 a year. Flood is not in the yearly total above; you can add it in the calculator below. Our page on homes that have flooded shows how we learn a house's flood history.

How much should I budget for upkeep?

About $3,270 a year for a house: about $1,470 of routine repairs and about $1,800 put toward the big replacements. A condo owner spends about $690 a year on upkeep; the building's upkeep is paid from the condo fee.

Most years cost far less than the average, and some years cost much more. Half of house owners spend about $520 or less on routine repairs in a year, and about 1 in 6 spend nothing. The average is high because of the big jobs:

  • A new roof$10,400

    About 1 in 9 owners replaced one in two years.

  • Central air conditioning$7,000

    About 1 in 11 owners replaced it in two years.

  • Heating equipment$5,000

    The furnace or heat pump.

  • A water heater$1,000

Budgeting 1 percent of the value a year is a little high: on the typical house it gives $3,500.

What are electric and water bills like in Myrtle Beach?

Electricity costs less here than in the rest of South Carolina, and water is a small bill. These are typical bills for owners:

$150a month for electricity on a house here
$190a month in the rest of South Carolina
$480a year for water and sewer on a house
$90a month for electricity in a condo

One household's bills: on one of our team's own houses, about 1,400 square feet on two floors, summer electricity is about $130 a month and water about $50.

Common questions

Cost to own FAQ

What is PITI, and what is not in it?

PITI stands for principal, interest, taxes and insurance. It is the loan payment plus the property tax and home insurance, which the lender usually collects with it, and mortgage insurance if the loan needs it. An HOA or condo fee is usually paid separately. Upkeep and utilities are not in it.

Will my property tax be the same as the seller's?

Not always. After a sale, the county values the home again, and the new value is first taxed the year after the sale. Do not budget from the seller's tax bill.

Does the cost change if I rent the home out?

Yes. A rental is taxed like a second home, on 6 percent of its value. The second-home section above shows the tax by town.

Is Myrtle Beach expensive to own a home in?

A little more than the rest of South Carolina. Owners of a paid-off house here pay about $50 a month more for tax, insurance, utilities and any HOA fee together.
