Buying a new build on the Grand Strand

New construction in Myrtle Beach:
what the price on the sign leaves out.

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

New construction in Horry County costs about what a nearly new house does. The sign leaves out lot premiums, options, HOA charges, closing costs and the full tax bill.

The short answer

A new house here lists for about the same price per square foot as a house built 2016 to 2023 of similar size nearby. A typical new house for sale is listed at about $370,000. Most are built inland, in large communities near Highway 90, Highway 22 and the Carolina Bays Parkway.

The sign leaves out the full tax bill, closing costs, HOA charges, options you pick and lot premiums, extra charges for a particular lot. For about 4 in 5 of the 2026 builder sales, the county still taxes only the lot. On a typical home you live in outside city limits, the yearly county bill goes from about $290 to about $1,210 once the county values the house.

Is new construction worth it, or should you buy a nearly new house?

The price per square foot, the price divided by the floor space, will not decide it for you. Next to a house built 2016 to 2023 of similar size within a mile, about half of new houses list for less per square foot and half for more. Town by town, the typical prices are close too.

The lot is about the same size too. In Conway, Longs and Myrtle Beach addresses, a new house has about the same size lot as a house built 2016 to 2023 in the same town.

What does a new house give you?

  • No one has lived in it. If the builder has not started it, you also pick the plan and the finishes.
  • A written warranty from the builder. The warranty section below lists what it covers.
  • Built to today's codes. State law has the county or city enforce the building codes: building, electrical, plumbing, gas, energy and fire. East of U.S. 17, state law also has insurers count the building code, the construction and roof tie-downs when they price wind coverage. Roof tie-downs are straps that hold the roof to the walls.
  • Less flood risk, on average. About 1 in 20 new houses for sale is in a high-risk flood zone on the map of the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA). Of all houses for sale, about 8 in 100 are. Our coastal insurance guide covers wind and flood coverage.

Pick by your plans:

  • You want to move in within a monthLook at nearly new resales, houses built 2016 to 2023 and sold by their owners, and at new houses that are finished and never lived in.
  • You want to pick the plan and the finishesChoose a house the builder has not started. Plan on about 6 months of building, more on your own lot.
  • You are buying it to rentThe county taxes a rental or second home on 6 percent of its value, not 4. It also adds the school tax a home you live in does not pay. Its yearly bill is more than three times as high. Our guide to new construction as a rental has the rest.
  • You want to be near the beachNew houses are a small share of what is for sale near the ocean. Three of the ten busiest new communities are about 1 to 2 miles from it.

Who builds new homes here?

Builders sold more than 1 in 4 homes recorded in Horry County in those 12 months. Five builders recorded about 7 in 10 of those sales.

What does a new house cost in Myrtle Beach?

A typical new house for sale in Horry County is listed at about $370,000, with about 1,900 square feet on just under a quarter acre. Half are listed for more, half for less. That is an asking price; the tax example below uses what buyers paid.

Most new houses come with an HOA fee. About 1 in 5 new houses for sale has none. For about 3 in 4, the fee is $100 a month or less, or there is none. Our guide to homes with no HOA lists the areas where most homes have none, and our guide to the most affordable places to live covers the lowest prices.

What does the price of a new house leave out?

Five things: the full property tax, lot premiums, options and upgrades, HOA charges and closing costs. First the tax. A South Carolina tax year looks back to December 31 of the year before. A house finished after that date, or still unsold in the builder's hands, is usually not in that year's bill. Your first bill is often for the lot alone.

When the county does value the house, it values it at about what you paid.

Take a typical 2026 builder sale that the county still taxes as a lot. It sold for $329,000, and the county values the lot at $80,000. A home you live in is taxed on 4 percent of its value once you apply; a second home or rental on 6 percent. Outside city limits, the yearly county tax looks like this:

Kind of homeLot onlyOnce valued
Home you live in, 4 percentAbout $290About $1,210
Second home or rental, 6 percentAbout $960About $3,970

Inside a city, the city's own tax is added, so the bill is higher. Of the ten communities above, Grande Dunes North Village, Waterside and Del Webb North Myrtle Beach are inside North Myrtle Beach and Kingston Oaks is inside Conway. Our property tax guide has each city's rate.

Will the county bill you for the months before?

Not for the months before the house stood on a December 31. The tax year looks back to that date, so the house tax starts with the next year's bill. If a house that was standing is left off the tax roll, the county may bill the missed years. It can go back no more than three years, and never to a year before the house was built.

  • Apply for the 4 percent assessment. Without it, the county taxes your home like a second home, at 6 percent. Apply to the Horry County Assessor by January 15 of the year after you move in, before the first penalty date. It covers the whole year if the house is the home you live in for any part of it. Overpaid tax can be refunded.
  • Plan for a higher monthly payment later. If your lender collects the taxes with your loan, the tax part of the payment may go up once the house is valued. The lender's yearly escrow review finds the difference.

Lot premiums, options and upgrades

The price on the sign is for the base house. Pulte's pages for its communities here say the price may not include lot premiums, upgrades and options. A lot premium is an extra charge for a particular lot. Pulte's annual report names flooring, countertops, fixtures and appliances among its options.

Toll Brothers, which recorded 97 of the builder sales here, gives a national figure in its annual report. Its buyers' options and lot premiums came to about a quarter of the base price in its 2025 fiscal year. Its spec homes have less to add, it says.

Deposits

D.R. Horton, the builder with the most sales here, says in its annual report that its contracts take an earnest money deposit, and more deposits if you pick options or upgrades. If a contract is cancelled, the deposit is kept or refunded under the contract's terms. Our page on the new home builders in Myrtle Beach ranks them by homes sold.

The deposit terms matter before you sign.

HOA charges and closing costs

Closing costs come on top of the price. The federal Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) says the buyer generally pays them.

  • What they include. The appraisal, title insurance, government taxes, and property taxes and homeowners insurance paid ahead.
  • Help from the builder. Meritage, which recorded 334 sales here, says in its annual report that it may pay part of the closing costs for buyers who use its preferred lenders. A seller will usually want a higher price to cover a credit, the CFPB says.
  • HOA charges. A new community's HOA can also charge at closing. Pulte's pages here list community association fees as a possible extra. If you take out a mortgage, any HOA charge paid at closing is listed on your Closing Disclosure. Ask the builder for the HOA's fees before you sign.

Do you need a realtor for new construction?

You can buy without one, but the salesperson in the model home works for the builder. Your own agent works for you. Nationwide, most of D.R. Horton's closings involve an independent real estate broker, its annual report says.

Since 2024, an agent asks you to sign a written agreement before you tour a home together. It states what the agent is paid, and that amount is fully negotiable. A builder, like any seller, can still offer to pay your agent. We confirm in writing what, if anything, you would pay before you tour.

Why does the first visit matter?

Each builder writes its own terms for paying a buyer's agent. Under D.R. Horton's terms on its Florida listings, for example, the builder pays a buyer's agent only if the agent registers you on or before your first visit. You must also not have signed up on its website or talked with its sales agent first.

Can you negotiate with a builder, and should you use the builder's lender?

Yes, you can negotiate. We see most builders here offer incentives to buyers with an FHA, VA or conventional loan. Incentives for investors, whose loans are based on the rent the home brings in, are rare.

Some promotions come with the builder's own lender. Lennar's Kingston Oaks page offers promotions when you finance through Lennar Mortgage, and nationwide, D.R. Horton's mortgage subsidiary financed 81 percent of the homes it closed in its 2025 fiscal year. You never have to use the builder's lender.

The way to judge the offer is full Loan Estimates from more than one lender, side by side. A Loan Estimate is the form that lists a loan's costs. Any lender can run that comparison for you, including our preferred lender.

Spec home or to-be-built: how long does a new house take?

A spec home, one the builder starts before it has a buyer, is often finished and ready now. Of the new houses for sale in the MLS feed, almost half are finished and never lived in. The rest are still being built.

A house the builder has not started takes months. In the South, a builder's house took about 5 and a half months from start to finish in the 2025 figures. A house a contractor builds on your own lot took about 9 months.

What inspections does a new house need?

Three of your own: before the drywall, before closing and before the first year of the warranty ends. Each comes at the last point a problem is easy to see, or still the builder's to fix. The county or city also inspects, for the building code. Your own inspector works for you.

  1. Before the drywall goes up.The inspector sees the framing, the wiring and the pipes while the walls are open. Once the drywall is up, a problem inside a wall is hidden.
  2. Before closing.What is still wrong goes on a written list for the builder, while you can still ask for fixes before you close.
  3. In month 11.Book it before the one-year part of the warranty ends. It finds what to report while the builder still pays for the fix. We see many builders here also send a maintenance person once in the first year to fix defects and touch up paint.

We set up all three, ask the builder for the warranty booklet, and keep a list of what each one finds, so the builder has it in writing.

What can you do when something is wrong with a new house?

Report it to the builder in writing, under the warranty. D.R. Horton gives 1 year on most of the house, 2 years on major mechanical systems and 10 years on major structure. Other builders differ, and the written warranty states the terms.

The warranty says what the builder will fix. The state law says how long you have to sue. Most lawsuits over defects must be filed within 8 years of the house being finished. Inside that, most claims must be filed within three years.

If the builder will not fix it, under South Carolina's defect law you send the builder written notice, and the builder may inspect and offer a repair before you can sue. We keep track of your warranty deadlines after closing.

Common questions

New construction questions

Can I find a new house in Myrtle Beach under $300,000?

Yes. About 1 in 5 new houses for sale in Horry County is listed at $300,000 or less. Of the ten communities where builders sold the most, The Overlook (Indigo Grove), Kingston Oaks and Cypress Ridge had typical sale prices under $300,000.

Are there new townhomes in Myrtle Beach?

Yes. 108 new townhouses are for sale in Horry County, at a typical list price of about $275,000 for about 1,580 square feet.

How does the county know a new house is being built?

From the building permit. South Carolina law has every county require a permit for new construction. The county must send the assessor a copy of each building permit within 10 days, so the assessor knows a house is being built.

Does a home builder in South Carolina need a license?

Yes. A residential builder needs a state license for any job over $5,000, issued by the Residential Builders Commission. A complaint against a builder goes to the Commission in writing and sworn.
