Buyer's guide

Buying in Myrtle Beach:
a buyer's market for condos, not yet for houses.

  • Time to sell, county condos7.5 months
  • Time to sell, county housesabout 4 months
  • Homes with a price cutover half

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

Condos have room to bargain; most houses do not yet. The counts by price and town, what a home costs to own, and the South Carolina steps to buy.

The short answer

Across Horry County, the Myrtle Beach area, it is a buyer's market for condos. The condos for sale would take about 7.5 months to sell at the past year's pace.

Houses would take about 4 months, so sellers still have a slight edge. Houses at $700,000 and up are near the point where buyers gain the edge. Inside the City of Myrtle Beach, condos have about a year of supply.

Is Myrtle Beach a buyer's market right now?

For condos, yes. For most houses, not yet. The test is months of supply: how long it would take to sell every home for sale if sales kept the past year's pace.

Housing researchers count 5 to 7 months as balanced in a local market. Past 7, buyers have room to bargain; under 5, sellers have the edge.

Condos as a whole are past 7 months; two condo price ranges sit just under 7, at the top of balanced. On a condo there is room to ask for a lower price or for repairs. On a house under $700,000 there is less room: those houses would sell out in about half the time.

Sellers already feel it. More than half of the homes for sale have cut their asking price. About 4 in 10 houses and more than half of condos for sale have been listed more than 90 days.

House prices have stopped climbing. A federal index of Horry County house prices rose about 51 percent in four years, then was about flat in the latest year. It tracks houses, not condos.

Sales follow the season. Recorded resales were fewest in January and February, about 430 houses a month, and were about 620 to 670 a month from May to October.

Where in Myrtle Beach do buyers have the most room to negotiate?

Condos in the City of Myrtle Beach have the most supply of any town here. Houses in Surfside Beach, Little River, Carolina Forest, Forestbrook and Red Hill have under 4 months.

Many buyers say "Myrtle Beach" for the whole coast. The map and the table split it into ten towns and communities, from Little River to Garden City and inland to Conway.

How much does a house or condo cost in Myrtle Beach?

The middle resale house or other home in Horry County sold for about $350,000 in the past year, and the middle resale condo for about $215,000. Half sold for more, half for less.

New homes are a big part of the market: builders sold more than 1 in 4 of the homes recorded in the county. The middle home a builder sold, condos aside, went for about $330,000. New houses have the fewest months of supply of any kind of home here, about 3.

What does a home cost each year besides the mortgage?

The middle house, about $350,000, costs about $7,850 a year besides the mortgage if you live in it outside city limits. That is about $650 a month. The biggest parts are upkeep, about $3,270, and insurance, about $2,000.

The middle resale condo, about $215,000, costs about $10,170 a year, and three quarters of that is the condo fee. The fee pays to run and keep up the shared building and grounds, and may pay the building's insurance.

In Myrtle Beach and North Myrtle Beach the middle condo costs more to own each year than the middle house. But it costs less to buy: in Myrtle Beach, less than half as much. Weigh the smaller loan against the larger fee.

What are the steps to buy a house in South Carolina?

Eight steps, from choosing an agent to the deed recorded in Conway. The biggest difference from many states: every South Carolina home closing must be supervised by a South Carolina attorney.

  1. 1 Step 1: Sign a buyer agreement with your agent

    In South Carolina, no firm represents you as your agent until you both sign a written agreement that says so. We sign it with you before your first tour.

    It states what your agent is paid. That fee is negotiable, and a seller may still offer to pay it.

  2. 2 Step 2: Choose a home and make an offer

    Before you offer, we find the home's flood zone and get an insurance quote for the address, if you buy with Chapter3. You can sign the offer by e-signature from anywhere; South Carolina law counts it the same as ink.

  3. 3 Step 3: Inspect the home and read the papers

    In South Carolina, inspecting the home is the buyer's job. We line up the inspection.

    The seller of most resale houses must give you the state's disclosure form before you sign. For a condo, we get the HOA papers and the rental rules.

  4. 4 Step 4: The loan and the appraisal

    Within three business days of your application, your lender must send a Loan Estimate, a form that lists the loan's terms and costs.

    You get a free copy of the appraisal as soon as it is done.

  5. 5 Step 5: The closing attorney

    A South Carolina attorney supervises the title search, the loan papers, the closing and the recording of the deed. With a home loan for personal or family use, the lender must ask which attorney you want and follow your choice.

    We work with several closing attorneys and can recommend them once we are working with you. At least three business days before closing, your lender sends the Closing Disclosure: the loan's final terms and costs.

  6. 6 Step 6: Closing, here or from home

    You can sign at the attorney's office. From another state, you can sign the deed papers and the mortgage before a notary where you live, with two witnesses.

  7. 7 Step 7: The deed is recorded in Conway

    The deed and mortgage are recorded at the Horry County Register of Deeds in Conway. The seller pays the state's deed fee.

    What the buyer pays at closing is listed fee by fee on its own page.

  8. 8 Step 8: Apply for the 4 percent tax rate

    A home you live in and apply for is taxed on 4 percent of its value; a second home or a rental on 6 percent. The 4 percent rate is not automatic: you apply to the county after you buy.

    On a house the county values at $350,000 outside the cities, the current tax is about $1,290 a year at 4 percent and about $4,220 at 6 percent. A sale resets the county's value, so the seller's tax bill is not yours.

Can I buy a home in Myrtle Beach from out of state?

Yes, and many buyers do:

  • 3 in 10homes sold went to a buyer with a tax bill in another state or country
  • Over halfof condos sold went to such a buyer

North Carolina sends the most out-of-state buyers by far, then New York, Pennsylvania, New Jersey and Virginia. In the same building or subdivision, buyers with an out-of-state tax bill paid the same middle price as South Carolina buyers.

More people keep coming. In the latest Census count, the Myrtle Beach metro grew faster than every U.S. metro but one. About 39 more people a day moved into Horry County from the rest of the country than moved out.

What can go wrong when buying in Myrtle Beach?

Six things here surprise buyers from other states. Each has a fix, and we handle most of them before you offer. The ten costliest buyer mistakes are ranked on their own page.

  • Flood zone

    About 1 in 4 condos and 1 in 15 houses resold in Horry County in the past year sat in FEMA's high-risk flood zone. There, most mortgages require flood insurance. For a house you live in there, a federal flood policy cost a middle of about $1,070 a year.

    We find the zone before you offer, and look for past flooding too.

  • Insurance

    The middle owner of a one-family house here pays about $2,000 a year for home insurance, about 40 percent more than in the rest of South Carolina. A condo owner pays about $800 for their own policy.

    We get a quote for the address before you offer, if you buy with us.

  • The second-home tax rate

    If you never apply for the 4 percent rate, your home is taxed at 6 percent, as a second home is: about three times the tax (step 8).

    We work out the tax at both rates on any address you send.

  • A low appraisal

    In our experience, low appraisals are rare here.

    When one comes in low, we send comparable sales with a request to look at the value again. Once, the value changed and the borrower brought less to closing.

  • Nightly rentals

    Inside the City of Myrtle Beach, a rental under 90 days is legal only where the zoning allows it. About 3 in 4 condos are in such zones; about 96 in 100 houses are not. In North Myrtle Beach a short-term rental needs a business license, and for now there is no special zoning or permit process beyond it.

    We read the zoning and the HOA papers before you offer.

  • Condos run like hotels

    Fannie Mae, which backs many ordinary home loans, will not back one in a building run like a hotel. Signs include a front desk, daily cleaning, or a rule that owners join the rental program. Buyers there need a different loan or cash.

    We read the building's rules before you offer, so you know which loans can work there.

Common questions

Buying in Myrtle Beach FAQ

Is Myrtle Beach a buyer's market?

For condos, yes: across Horry County, the condos for sale would take about 7.5 months to sell. For houses under $700,000, not yet.

Will home prices in Myrtle Beach go down?

We do not forecast prices. Horry County house prices were about flat in the latest year, after four years of gains.

Do you need a lawyer to buy a house in South Carolina?

Yes. Every South Carolina home closing must be supervised by a South Carolina attorney. Step 5 says how you choose one.

What is a CL-100?

The termite letter, the Official South Carolina Wood Infestation Report. Only a licensed pest control applicator may issue it for a sale or a loan. It reports visible insect damage, active infestations, and moisture and decay under the house.

Can I rent my Myrtle Beach condo by the night?

Often, if it is inside the City of Myrtle Beach, where most condos sit in zones that allow it. The condo's own rules can still ban it.

How much are property taxes in Myrtle Beach?

Inside the City of Myrtle Beach, on a house the county values at $350,000, about $1,410 a year if you live in it and apply. As a second home, about $5,350.
