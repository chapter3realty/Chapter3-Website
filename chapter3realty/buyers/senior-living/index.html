Assisted living, memory care, nursing homes and continuing care around Myrtle Beach: 2025 costs, the licensed homes town by town, and when buying still makes sense.

The short answer

Assisted living around Myrtle Beach costs about $5,850 a month, about $70,200 a year. The state licenses 20 assisted living homes in Horry County and 3 more in Murrells Inlet and Pawleys Island.

While no one needs daily care, owning costs far less: about $1,380 a year in county tax at 65 on a $400,000 house outside the city limits. Help at home costs about $31 an hour.

How much is assisted living in Myrtle Beach?

  • Against the state and the country. Assisted living here costs more than South Carolina's $5,350 a month, and less than the U.S. figure of $6,200.
  • Against other South Carolina areas. Of the areas the survey prices, Myrtle Beach is third highest, after Columbia and Hilton Head Island.
  • Care at home. A non-medical caregiver, such as a home health aide or a homemaker, costs about $31 an hour. Adult day care costs about $72 a day.

The survey has no separate figure for memory care, and none for independent living. Prices differ from one home to the next, so we get each home's current prices for you.

Assisted living vs. nursing home vs. independent living: what is the difference?

Each choice gives a different amount of care. In South Carolina, assisted living is licensed as a "community residential care facility", and the state's papers use both names.

Assisted living may not admit or keep a person who needs nursing home care or a nurse's daily attention. When the home's care, even with outside home health or hospice care, no longer meets a person's needs, the person must move within 30 days.

South Carolina's health facility licensing law has no license for independent living. A home with room, board and personal help must be licensed as residential care.

Apartments with HUD rent help

HUD pays subsidies to the owners of some apartment buildings to keep rents affordable for people with low incomes, including older adults. They are rental apartments, and none is on the state's list of licensed care homes.

In Horry County, HUD lists four of these buildings for older adults, with 228 apartments; one, with 110, is partly for people with disabilities. Two are in Myrtle Beach, one in Conway and one in Loris. One more is in Pawleys Island, with 48.

HUD's Resource Locator shows each building on a map. Ask the building's office about openings and who may rent there.

Memory care and nursing homes around Myrtle Beach

On the state's facility map, 14 of Horry County's 20 licensed assisted living homes are marked for Alzheimer care, and 13 are marked with an Alzheimer unit.

Horry County has 8 licensed nursing homes with 824 beds. Three are in Conway and three in Myrtle Beach, with one each in Murrells Inlet and Loris. Seven of the eight are certified for both Medicare and Medicaid, and one for Medicare only. Certification is not a promise of an open Medicaid bed.

The county also has 3 licensed adult day care centers, in Myrtle Beach, Conway and Loris, and a 36-bed hospice house in Myrtle Beach. Medicare's Care Compare has a page for each nursing home.

Who pays for long-term care

  • Medicare does not. Medicare and most health insurance, including Medigap, do not pay for long-term care, in a nursing home or at home. Medicaid may, for people who meet the state's rules.
  • South Carolina's Optional State Supplementation (OSS) helps people 65 or older, blind or disabled, whose income and savings are under its limits. It pays toward a licensed assisted living home enrolled in OSS, for a person with an approved OSS slot.
  • OSS is not Medicaid. It is a separate state program, run by the South Carolina Department of Health and Human Services.

Continuing care: two licensed campuses on the Grand Strand

South Carolina licenses 33 continuing care retirement communities. Two are on the Grand Strand: Brightwater in Myrtle Beach and The Lakes at Litchfield in Pawleys Island.

A continuing care retirement community offers, under one contract, an apartment or a cottage and access to a range of long-term care as a resident's needs change. The resident pays an entrance fee, which holds the resident's place.

Under South Carolina law, the person who signs has four protections:

  • The refund. The contract must say what part of the entrance fee is refundable.
  • 30 days to cancel. The resident may cancel within 30 days of signing. The refund is what was paid, less the community's costs and, if the home was ready to move into, a daily rate from the eighth day.
  • A disclosure statement. By the time the contract is signed or any money is paid, whichever comes first, the community must give one.
  • A check of the finances. The state checks a community's finances before it grants a license.

When does buying a home still make sense?

For a couple or a parent who does not need daily care yet, a smaller home here can cost far less each year. A one-level house or a condo with an elevator keeps every room within reach.

Outside the city limits, a $400,000 house you live in costs about $1,380 a year in county tax and stormwater fee at 65. Insurance and any HOA fee are extra. A year of assisted living costs about $70,200. The tax rules at 65 are on our retiring to Myrtle Beach page.

The beach, by wheelchair

Horry County lends beach wheelchairs free on the beaches it runs. Ask at least two days ahead; chairs are limited. In Surfside Beach, special beach wheelchairs are available through the town's police department, and five beach accesses have wheelchair access.

Common questions

Senior living questions

Does Medicare pay for assisted living in South Carolina?

No. Medicare and Medigap do not pay for long-term care, in a nursing home or in the community. Medicaid can, for those who qualify.

Is there help paying for assisted living in South Carolina?

Yes, for some people. A state program called Optional State Supplementation helps pay for a licensed assisted living home enrolled in it, for a person with an approved slot. It is for people 65 or older, blind or disabled, under its income and savings limits. It is not Medicaid.

Do assisted living homes near Myrtle Beach have a hurricane plan?

Yes. Each licensed assisted living home must have a written evacuation plan, made with the county's emergency agency and updated every year. A resident or the resident's responsible party can ask to read it.

Is independent living licensed in South Carolina?

No. South Carolina licenses assisted living and nursing homes, but it has no license for independent living with no care.

What does adult day care cost in Myrtle Beach?

About $72 a day at the middle, or about $1,560 a month at five days a week. Horry County has 3 licensed adult day care centers, in Myrtle Beach, Conway and Loris.

How much is memory care in Myrtle Beach?

There is no published local figure: the cost survey does not price memory care on its own. Prices differ from one home to the next.
