Buying land

Land for sale in Horry County, SC
What an acre costs, and what you can build on it.

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 14, 2026

An acre of land for sale is listed at about $25,000 around Loris and about $150,000 near Myrtle Beach. A lot with no sewer needs a state septic permit first.

The short answer

Land for sale in Horry County is listed at about $25,000 an acre around Loris and $40,000 around Aynor. In Myrtle Beach, Carolina Forest and Socastee it is about $150,000. Those are tracts of an acre or more, and the bigger the tract, the less each acre costs.

Before you offer, three things matter most: the state's septic permit, the zoning and the flood zone. We check all three for you.

What does an acre of land cost in Horry County?

About 760 pieces of land are for sale in Horry County, and about 170 in Georgetown County. In Horry the middle asking price is about $195,000, and the middle size is about 1.7 acres.

Of the areas we counted, the Loris, Green Sea and Nichols ZIP codes have the most land for sale: 266 pieces. Of those, 212 are an acre or more, and 70 are 10 acres or more. In all of Horry County, 17 tracts for sale are 100 acres or more.

These are asking prices, not sale prices. In each area, half the tracts are listed for more than the middle price an acre, and half for less.

Land around Loris and Aynor: river landings, longleaf pine and old farms

The country around Loris and Aynor has the lowest land prices we counted, and a long history as farm land.

Around Loris

By 1903, tobacco markets were thriving in Conway and Loris. By the 1920s, Horry grew more tobacco than any other county in South Carolina.

West of Loris, between Green Sea and Nichols, Cartwheel Bay Heritage Preserve has a 3.5-mile trail through longleaf and loblolly pine. Wild Venus flytraps, pitcher plants and orchids grow along it. It is open dawn to dusk, with no fee.

Around Aynor

Galivants Ferry is west of Aynor, where US 501 crosses the Little Pee Dee River. There is a public boat landing on each side of the bridge. People fish the river for bluegill, redbreast, bass, crappie and catfish. Downriver, below the US 378 bridge, its state scenic stretch has tea-colored water, cypress-tupelo swamps and sandbars.

Since the 1870s, candidates have come to Galivants Ferry to speak at the Stump. These days they speak from the porch of the Holliday family store, and the crowd eats chicken bog and barbecue. What living in these towns is like is in our guide to which town fits you.

Around Conway and Longs

Around Conway an acre is listed at about $51,000, and in Longs at about $73,000. At Longs, the county runs two boat landings on the Waccamaw River: Chris Anderson Landing on SC 9 and Danny Knight Landing at Red Bluff. Our Conway guide covers the city and its edges.

From Chapter3's files. Clients of ours from North Carolina bought land in Longs and built a new house there with a builder.

The more land you buy, the less each acre costs

In Horry County, a lot under half an acre is listed at about $686,000 an acre. A tract of 20 acres or more is listed at about $19,000 an acre.

Do you need a perc test to buy land in South Carolina?

No. South Carolina has not used perc tests since the late 1970s, because a lot can pass in a dry season and fail in a wet one. A state inspector reads the soil instead.

Where no public or community sewer reaches a lot, you need the state's septic permit before you build a house or place a manufactured home. Without it, the county will not issue a building permit. The state's steps:

  • Apply online, for $150. The application needs a copy of the deed, or of the survey map filed for the lot. Our guide to finding a deed in Horry County shows where to get both.
  • The inspector bores the soil. Under the state's traditional method, an inspector visits the lot and takes at least two soil borings where the septic system would go. The soil's color shows how high the water table rises in the wettest season.
  • Wet soil can wait. If the soil is too wet, the visit may be put off until it dries out. Poor soil can limit the number of bedrooms the house may have.
  • The permit is good for five years. If the site passes, the state issues a Permit to Construct.

What the lot needs room for

The drainfield is sized at 120 gallons a day per bedroom. A three-bedroom house needs a drainfield big enough for about 360 gallons a day, plus a spare repair area half its size.

The system must sit at least 75 feet from a private well or a creek, pond or other surface water, and 25 feet from a drainage ditch.

If a lot fails the state's conventional and alternative standards, an engineer and a soil scientist can check whether it can take an engineered system. That can cost thousands of dollars more. Not every lot can take a septic system.

What the listings say

About 1 in 4 Horry land listings are marked "perc or septic approved". We ask the seller for the state's permit itself, not the word "perc". If there is none, we can write your offer so it depends on the lot passing.

No sewer on the lot?

We look for a septic permit on it, ask whether sewer can reach it, and tell you what the soil visit will look for.

Is there public water and sewer, or do you need a well?

Many lots have public water, and fewer have public sewer: about 4 in 10 Horry land listings list public water, and about 1 in 3 list public sewer. Only 41 list a well.

With no public sewer you need a septic system, and with no public water you need a well. Before you offer, we ask the local provider whether each one reaches the lot.

Grand Strand Water and Sewer Authority began east of the Intracoastal Waterway in Horry County. It now serves from the North Carolina line to parts of Georgetown County, and rural areas west of the Waccamaw River. Inside the City of Loris, the city runs water and sewer.

A new drinking-water well needs a state notice with a $70 fee, and a South Carolina certified well driller must drill it.

A listing that says nothing about water or sewer is not proof either way, so we ask the provider.

What can you build on land in Horry County?

Outside the towns, each county zone has its own smallest lot for a home and its own rule on manufactured homes. In the two forest and farm zones, FA and CFA, a house or a manufactured home needs at least half an acre, 90 feet wide.

In Limited Forest Agriculture (LFA), a home needs 1 acre. A lot recorded before the county's zoning ordinance can still be built on if it is no more than 25 percent short of its zone's minimum.

Our count, at each listing's map point: about half of all Horry land listings are in FA or CFA. Among tracts of an acre or more, 6 in 10 are. Another 62 are in SF zones, where no mobile home is allowed.

We look up the zone of any lot you send us. Our guide to the Horry County GIS map shows where the county keeps it.

Deed restrictions

A deed restriction is a rule recorded with the land. It can bar a mobile home even where the zoning allows one. In our count, 74 Horry land listings mention deed restrictions, and 111 are marked as allowing a mobile home. We read the recorded restrictions before you offer. To look up a parcel yourself, use our Horry County property records search.

Can you live in a camper on your land?

Only for part of each month, and only in some places. On land zoned LFA, FA or CFA that touches the Waccamaw River or lies north and west of it, you can keep up to two campers or RVs. The land must have no house or business on it, and you can stay in them up to 15 days a month.

Each camper must be tagged, registered and road-ready, on approved sewer or septic, with permitted power.

Does land in Horry County need road frontage?

Yes, for a new lot. A new lot must have at least 50 feet of frontage on a street. On a cul-de-sac, 25 feet is enough if the lot is 50 feet wide at the building line.

For a lot with no frontage that existed before the zoning ordinance, the county's planning commission decides whether private access is enough to build.

Along some highways, the front of a lot cannot be built on. Outside the towns, no structure may stand within 60 feet of the right of way of certain highways. They include Highways 701, 905 and 917, and two named stretches of Highway 9.

In our count, 77 land listings are marked as on a dirt or gravel road, 49 on a private road and 27 reached by an easement. For those, we find out who keeps up the road and get a copy of the recorded easement.

Is the land in a flood zone?

About 1 in 8 Horry land listings (91 of 763) sit in FEMA's high-risk flood zone at the listing's map point, and 24 more in the moderate zone. A big tract can reach into a flood zone away from that point.

In the unincorporated county, a new home in the high-risk zone must have its lowest floor at least 3 feet above the 100-year flood height. The county asks for more papers before it issues the permit.

Wetlands

If part of a lot may be a wetland, you may need to ask the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers or the state's Bureau of Coastal Management.

Our flood zone check guide shows how to read a flood map. Our guide to finding past flooding shows how to learn whether a place has flooded before.

Big tract near a creek or a swamp?

One map point can miss a flood zone. We look at the flood map across the whole tract, and tell you what a house there needs.

What does it cost to build on your own land in Horry County?

Outside the towns, the state and county fees for a 2,000 square foot house with a septic tank and a well come to about $1,650. Impact fees and the house itself are extra.

Is farm and timber land taxed less in South Carolina?

Yes, when it is in real farm or timber use and the owner applies to the assessor. The land is then taxed on its farm-use value, not its market value.

  • Timber land must be 5 acres or more, with trees grown for sale.
  • Other farm land must be 10 acres or more, or a smaller tract that earned at least $1,000 in gross farm income in three of five years.
  • After a change of use, such as a new house, the land owes rollback taxes. They are the difference between the farm-use tax and the full tax, for the year of the change and up to three years before it.
  • Your own home and up to 5 acres around it are taxed at the 4 percent rate when you live there and apply.

How the county works out a tax bill is on our Horry County property tax page.

Common questions

Buying land: questions

Can you do a perc test before buying land in South Carolina?

South Carolina has not used perc tests since the late 1970s. The state's septic permit takes their place: a $150 application with the deed or the lot's survey map, then a soil visit. We can write your offer so it depends on the lot passing.

Where is the cheapest land for sale near Myrtle Beach?

Around Loris, Green Sea and Nichols, where an acre is listed at about $25,000 on tracts of an acre or more. Around Aynor it is about $40,000.

Can you put a mobile home on land in Horry County?

In the county's FA and CFA zones, yes, on a lot of at least half an acre. In the SF zones, no. Deed restrictions on the land can still bar one.

Is there hunting land for sale in Horry County?

Yes. In our count, 30 Horry land listings are marked for hunting. Tell us the size and the area you want, and we send you the tracts that fit.

What does land cost around Pawleys Island?

In the Pawleys Island area, in Georgetown County, an acre is listed at about $342,000 on tracts of an acre or more. That is the highest of the areas we counted.
