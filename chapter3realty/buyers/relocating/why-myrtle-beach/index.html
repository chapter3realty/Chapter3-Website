Moving to Myrtle Beach

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 14, 2026

Movers come to Horry County for the beach and the water, a short and mild winter, a lower home price and a low property tax.

The short answer

Most households moving to Myrtle Beach and the rest of Horry County come from another state. In most of the out-of-state counties that send the most, a typical home costs more than one here.

They come for the beach and the water, a short winter and, for most out-of-state movers, a much lower property tax bill. In the latest count, about 15,000 households moved in and about 9,100 moved out.

Why are so many people moving to Myrtle Beach?

The City of Myrtle Beach is in Horry County, South Carolina, and the coast here is called the Grand Strand. People move here for the beach and the water, a short winter, lower house prices and low property tax.

  • The beach and the water

    The ocean still averages the low 70s in October. Behind the beach towns are the Intracoastal Waterway, the Waccamaw River and the Murrells Inlet marsh, each with public boat landings.

  • The winter is short and mild

    A January day averages a high of about 56 degrees and a low of about 37. Snow averages about an inch a year, and about two winters in three bring none.

  • A house costs less than in most places movers leave

    A typical home in Horry County is valued at about $320,000. Around Charlotte it is about $415,000, and on Long Island about $730,000 or more. For a side-by-side with Charleston, see our Myrtle Beach or Charleston comparison.

  • The property tax is low

    An owner who lives in the home here typically pays about $944 a year. On Long Island the typical bill is $10,000 or more.

What do people do in Myrtle Beach?

Downtown, the Boardwalk is about 1.3 miles long, beside the ocean, with a raised wooden walk and a paved path along the sand.

Murrells Inlet is known for seafood. Its restaurants grew from the oyster roasts of the inlet's founding families. The MarshWalk there is a half-mile wooden boardwalk on the salt marsh, with restaurants and free live music.

From October through May, anyone with a South Carolina saltwater fishing license may gather oysters and clams on the inlet's public grounds. Parts of the grounds close at times for bacteria.

Inland, Conway's Riverwalk follows the Waccamaw River for about 1.5 miles, with a kayak launch and a public boat landing beside it.

On the Intracoastal Waterway in Carolina Forest, the county's Bike and Run Park has about 7 miles of trails. Huntington Beach State Park, south of Murrells Inlet, has 3 miles of beach and a freshwater lake known for alligators.

Myrtle Beach's first golf course, Pine Lakes, opened in 1927. Anyone who lives or owns a home in Horry County can buy the Myrtle Beach Golf Passport for $72 a year. It gives member rates at the courses that take it.

The off season is quieter, not closed: in our team's experience, only the tourist attractions and water parks close in winter. One mover from Maryland noticed that farm stands and butchers fill the gap the grocery stores leave in organic food.

Is Myrtle Beach still growing?

Yes. Horry County grew about 22 percent from the 2020 census to the latest estimate. The growth is people moving in: in the latest year, about 39 more people a day arrived from other parts of the country than left.

Among the country's metro areas, the Myrtle Beach area, which is all of Horry County, grew the second fastest from mid-2024 to mid-2025. Only Ocala, Florida grew faster.

Is Myrtle Beach getting overcrowded?

Most of the growth is not in the beach towns. The City of Myrtle Beach itself has about 41,000 people. About three in four Horry County residents live outside any city or town.

About four in five of the people added since 2020 settled outside city limits, in places such as Carolina Forest, Socastee, Murrells Inlet and Little River.

Outside city limits is not far from the sand. With no traffic, the beach is about 10 minutes from the middle house in Murrells Inlet, 13 in Socastee, 14 in Little River and 18 in Carolina Forest.

Summer traffic is lighter than most movers expect. In our team's experience, rush hour adds 5 to 10 minutes, and SC 31 or SC 22 avoids the many stop lights on US 17. Our which town page compares the towns.

Where are people moving to Myrtle Beach from?

About six in seven households that move to Horry County come from another state. The South sends the most, then the Northeast.

The single county that sends the most is Georgetown County, South Carolina, just to the south, with about 530 households in the latest count. Outside South Carolina, the most come from Suffolk County on Long Island, then Brunswick, Mecklenburg and Wake counties in North Carolina.

Few go back north. For every household that moved from Horry County to Ocean County, New Jersey, about five came from it.

About six in ten Horry County residents were born in another state, against about one in four Americans. About 3 in 10 homes sold here in the last 12 months went to a buyer whose tax bill goes to another state or country.

What would my house buy in Myrtle Beach?

In most of the out-of-state counties that send the most movers, a typical home is worth more than one here. Sell a typical Suffolk County home and buy a typical one here, and about $410,000 is left before moving costs, sale costs and any loan payoff.

Are there jobs in Myrtle Beach, and what does work pay?

Yes, and the number of jobs grew faster than in the country. Pay is lower, though: the average job here pays about 64 cents for every dollar of the national average.

Most of our buyers who need work find it within a month or two, in fields from child protective services and stores to welding. That is our clients' experience, not a promise.

Many movers bring their work with them. About half of our clients who come from the northern states arrive with a remote job.

Business owners who move here tell us they cannot charge as much for their services as they did up north. Our jobs page has more on work and pay here.

How much are taxes and insurance in Myrtle Beach?

Property tax on a home you live in is low here, a fraction of the bill in most of the out-of-state counties movers come from.

The bills in the chart are what owners pay today. For a house you buy, budget from its price: about $1,470 a year on a $400,000 house you live in outside the city limits. It is about $4,820 if it is a second home or a rental.

Home insurance costs more than in the rest of the state

Owners of a house in Horry County pay about $2,000 a year for home insurance at the middle, against about $1,400 in the rest of South Carolina.

Flood insurance depends on the street. Outside FEMA's high-risk flood zone, federal rules do not require it, and a policy usually costs about $550 a year. Inside it, your lender requires one, usually about $1,070.

Few houses in Carolina Forest, Conway, Myrtle Beach or Little River are in the high-risk zone: 5 percent or fewer. In Garden City, Surfside Beach and around Pawleys Island, about 3 in 10 are. We get you an insurance quote on a house before you make an offer.

If you are retired

  • South Carolina does not tax Social Security.
  • From the year you turn 65, up to $10,000 a year of retirement income is deducted for each person. A second deduction brings the total to $15,000 a person against any income. Military retirement pay is not taxed at all.
  • At 65, after a year living in South Carolina, you pay no property tax on the first $50,000 of your home's value. For 2026 only, an owner who already qualified in 2025 gets $75,000.
  • South Carolina has no estate tax.

If you may need care later, see our guide to senior living around Myrtle Beach.

The tax many movers do not expect

South Carolina taxes vehicles every year. One of our team moved here from a village of about 400 in Ohio, and his first vehicle tax bill was $325.

Each car first titled in another state also pays a one-time $250 state fee when you register it here. Owners on active duty in the military, and their spouses and dependents, do not pay it.

Are people leaving Myrtle Beach, and why?

Some are, but fewer than arrive. In the latest count, about 9,100 households left Horry County and about 15,000 arrived. The top destinations for households that leave Horry County are nearby: Georgetown County, SC, Brunswick County, NC, and Charleston County, SC.

Those who leave reported less income: about $55,000 a household on average, against about $83,000 for those who arrived.

Making friends is harder than movers expect, because many people near the city core are tourists or a seasonal population that leaves. Our advice: buy outside the City of Myrtle Beach. Be a regular at places tourists do not go, such as gyms and clubs.

Summers are hot and humid: about 21 days a year reach 90 degrees. Hurricane season is June through November, and across the Atlantic most activity comes between mid-August and mid-October.

Our hurricane page lists the storms that have hit this coast and what each one did.

Should you move to Myrtle Beach?

The move usually works for people who bring their money or their income with them. It is harder for people who need a local salary like the one they had up north. The rest is on our page on the honest pros and cons of living here year-round.

  • If your home is worth more than a typical one here

    Selling it can pay for a home here with money left over, before costs. The tool above shows the gap for your county.

  • If you live on a pension, Social Security or a remote job

    Lower local pay matters less to you, and the retiree tax breaks in the taxes section apply to you.

  • If you need a local job

    Plan on lower pay than up north, and apply for jobs on your house-hunting visit. One of our clients applied for a child protective services job on a visit and was hired a month later.

  • If you want to be outside all winter

    A day that stays below freezing from morning to night comes about once in three years. In our team's experience, the Boardwalk stays open all winter.

  • If you want big-city night life

    In summer, bands play every night at both ends of the Garden City Pier. Our clients from Virginia describe Virginia Beach as much bigger. One put it this way: nobody here parties like Miami, but people here are enjoying their money.

Common questions

Moving to Myrtle Beach FAQ

Is Myrtle Beach the fastest growing city in America?

No, but it is close. The Myrtle Beach area, which is all of Horry County, grew the second fastest of any metro area from mid-2024 to mid-2025, behind Ocala, Florida. The City of Myrtle Beach itself has about 41,000 people.

Are people leaving Myrtle Beach?

Some are. In the latest count, about 9,100 households left Horry County and about 15,000 arrived. Most who leave go nearby, to Georgetown or Charleston County in South Carolina or Brunswick County, North Carolina.

Where do most people moving to Myrtle Beach come from?

About six in seven come from another state, most from the South and the Northeast. The single largest source is Georgetown County, SC. Outside South Carolina, the most come from Suffolk County on Long Island.

Is it cheaper to live in Myrtle Beach?

Homes and property tax usually cost less than in the out-of-state counties most movers come from. Home insurance costs more than in the rest of South Carolina, and local pay is lower.

What is winter like in Myrtle Beach?

Short and mild. A January day averages a high of about 56 degrees and a low of about 37, and snow averages about an inch a year.

Does South Carolina tax Social Security?

No, South Carolina does not tax Social Security. Military retirement pay is not taxed either.
