Which Grand Strand town fits you?
13 places near Myrtle Beach, compared.

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

From Loris to Pawleys Island: where the sand is closest, where an acre is normal, where builders are selling, and what changes at the city line.

The short answer

Where to live near Myrtle Beach depends on what you want most. Closest to the sand: Surfside Beach, Garden City and North Myrtle Beach. The most land: Aynor and Loris. The newest houses: Carolina Forest and Longs. The shortest drive to everything: Myrtle Beach and Socastee. The lowest prices: Conway, Loris, Longs and Socastee.

Pick the one or two things that matter most to you. The list below keeps only the places that have them.

1. Loris: the most land for the money

Around Loris, a big yard is normal. More than 4 in 10 house lots are an acre or more, and the usual lot is about 0.8 acre. Near the beach, lots are closer to a sixth of an acre.

Loris began in 1887 as a railroad depot, and the tracks still run through the middle of downtown. On the third Saturday in October, downtown holds the Bog-Off, with a chicken bog cooking contest, an antique car show and live performances. West of town, Cartwheel Bay Heritage Preserve has a 3.5-mile trail on old roads through longleaf pine, and wild Venus flytraps, pitcher plants and orchids grow along it. It is open dawn to dusk, with no fee.

More houses for sale here list parking for an RV or a boat than in any other place on this page. Most list no HOA fee. McLeod Health Loris is the nearest hospital for almost every house.

Houses around Loris sold for about $280,000, half for more and half for less. A house here also costs the least of any place on this page to keep each year. Our list of the most affordable places near Myrtle Beach goes street by street.

2. Aynor: an acre is the usual lot

Around Aynor, the usual house has about an acre of land, and more than half of house lots are an acre or more. Farm fields start at the edge of the town's grid of streets.

State law names the third Saturday in September "Aynor Harvest Hoe-Down Festival Weekend". The Pilot Club of Aynor started the festival in 1979.

  • The trade-off is distance. Aynor is the farthest of these places from the beach, and from a hospital.

Since the 1870s, candidates for county, state and national office have come to Galivants Ferry, just west of Aynor on the Little Pee Dee River, to speak at the Stump. These days they speak from the porch of the Holliday family store, and the crowd eats chicken bog and barbecue, with country and gospel music and clogging.

The river itself runs tea-colored past cypress swamps and sandbars, with a public landing on each side of the US 501 bridge, where people fish for bluegill, redbreast, bass and catfish. In October, Southern Palmetto Farms on Garner Road opens a pumpkin patch and a 6-acre corn maze, with wagon rides.

Expect about $350,000 for a house around Aynor, the middle of the past year's sales.

3. Longs: where builders are selling

Longs is where builders are selling at the north end. About half the houses sold here in the past year were new, and about half the houses for sale now are new too.

The Waccamaw River runs past Longs through dark water and cypress, and it is the only river that starts in a Carolina bay. At Chris Anderson Landing on SC 9, a sign marks the Waccamaw River Blue Trail.

From Longs you can paddle the Waccamaw River Heritage Preserve, a state preserve along 28 miles of the river where otters and black bears live, and camp on the riverbank on preserve land. Long Bay Club in Longs is one of two Myrtle Beach area golf courses designed by Jack Nicklaus.

For houses for sale with a Longs address, a new house asks about the same price per square foot as one built three to ten years ago.

A Longs mailing address can be inside North Myrtle Beach. Two of the county's ten busiest new communities, Grande Dunes North and Waterside, have Longs addresses but sit inside the city. This page's Longs is the part outside the city.

Our clients from North Carolina bought land in Longs and had a new house built on it.

In Longs, about $300,000 bought a typical house in the past year. The usual HOA fee is about $100 a month.

4. Little River: old gardens on the waterway, and boat slips

Little River has a county park on the Intracoastal Waterway: Vereen Memorial Historical Gardens, more than 115 acres of forest and marsh with trails and boardwalks. A piece of the original Kings Highway, the colonial road between Boston and Charleston, is still at the front of the park. George Washington rode that road into South Carolina on April 27, 1791, and stopped to eat at the Cochran house in Little River.

Each October, Little River ShrimpFest fills the old waterfront with more than 200 vendors and two music stages; 2026 was its 20th year. Just off the coast is Waties Island, one of the last undeveloped barrier islands in South Carolina. Loggerhead turtles nest on its beach, and a few months later the hatchlings come out of the sand together and run for the water.

Boats are the other draw. About 2 in 3 of the county's boat slips sold with their own deed and tax bill are here. Glen Dornoch Waterway Golf Links, in Little River, is set among live oaks, with views over the marsh and the Intracoastal Waterway.

Little River is not a town, so the county makes the rules. There is no city tax, and trash goes to a private hauler or a county drop-off center.

Half the houses here sold for more than $380,000, and half for less. Nearly every sale was a resale, not a new house.

5. Conway: a river city with its own services

Conway was laid out in 1732, as Kingston. Its older streets of houses under live oaks run down to the Waccamaw River. Locals call those oaks the city's "oldest citizens": from the city's founding, crews built roads around the trees instead of cutting them down, and the town's unofficial slogan is "The trees have the right of way."

The Riverwalk is a path along the river with benches and a kayak launch. Riverfront Park beside it has a public boat landing with floating docks. Conway is a city, so it picks up trash at the curb.

Conway has held Riverfest since 1980, on the Saturday before the Fourth of July weekend. In 2026 it brought live music and vendors to Elm Street and the marina, free, and ended with fireworks over the river. Every October, Conway turns into Halloween, SC, a month of decorations and events across the city. Santee Cooper transferred nearly 800 acres, including Lake Busbee, to Conway in 2025, and the city plans new trails, an inland beach and a revitalized Riverwalk there.

  • The city tax is high: about $654 a year on each $100,000 of value, against about $368 outside the towns.
  • The river reached major flood stage 6 times from 2015 through 2025. The first streets to flood are low ones near Crabtree Swamp, such as Long Avenue and Sherwood Drive. About 95 in 100 homes in the city are outside both FEMA's high-risk zone and the land Florence's water reached.

At about $271,000, a typical house in Conway sold for the least of any place here.

6. North Myrtle Beach: most houses for sale near the sand

About 3 in 4 houses for sale in North Myrtle Beach are within a mile of the beach. Ocean Drive has Main Street, McLean Park with its lake, and the Ingram Dunes Nature Preserve, which the city calls its last maritime forest.

From June through September, the city holds free Music on Main concerts every Thursday evening at the Horseshoe on Main Street. At Fat Harold's Beach Club, a DJ plays beach music every night from 7, and couples take shag lessons on Monday nights. Three times a year, for the Society of Stranders, thousands of shag dancers fill the clubs on Main Street.

The state Senate calls North Myrtle Beach the home of the shag, South Carolina's state dance. At Cherry Grove, at the north end, about 680 houses sit on dug channels and the marsh edge. The city's boat ramp there reaches the ocean through Hog Inlet.

From the city's boat ramp on 53rd Avenue North, you can paddle a kayak about a mile and a half through Hog Inlet, past pelicans and ospreys, to the empty beach on Waties Island. There you can walk the shore looking for shells and fossil shark teeth.

Our clients from New York bought beachfront here near the waterway, so they could finally use their boat.

On the county's tax rolls, about half the houses here are someone's main home. The rest are taxed as second homes or rentals.

Count on about $565,000 for a typical house. About half the houses for sale list no HOA fee.

7. Carolina Forest: the newest homes, almost no flood zone

Half the homes in Carolina Forest were built in 2007 or later, the newest of any place here. Under 1 percent of its houses are in FEMA's high-risk flood zone.

Just north of Carolina Forest, the state's Lewis Ocean Bay Heritage Preserve holds the largest group of Carolina bays in South Carolina, and it is open to the public. Wild Venus flytraps grow there and bloom in early June, and Horry is the only county in South Carolina where they still grow naturally. The preserve also has black bears, bald eagles, red-cockaded woodpeckers and pitcher plants.

It is a large planned area of neighborhoods outside the city limits, with a Myrtle Beach address. McLeod Health cut the ribbon on a new hospital here in August 2026. Off River Oaks Drive, the county's Bike and Run Park, which riders call "The Hulk", has 7 miles of mountain bike and running trails on about 70 acres.

Nearly every house for sale in the Carolina Forest ZIP code lists an HOA fee, about $125 a month in a typical listing. Almost none of the houses for sale here are listed as open to nightly rentals.

The middle sale here in the past year was about $440,000.

8. Myrtle Beach: the shortest drive to everything

From a typical house in Myrtle Beach, the beach, the airport and Coastal Grand Mall are each about 15 minutes or less by car. Pine Lakes Country Club, which opened in 1927, was the city's first golf course.

The Market Common is a town center with shops, restaurants and a movie theater, and apartments above the stores. Grand Park and Valor Park are next to the shops, with a lake and a path around it, sports fields and a playground. Our Market Common guide has each neighborhood's homes, prices and HOA fees.

Myrtle Beach State Park, the first state park in South Carolina to open, in 1936, keeps a mile of undeveloped beach with a forest of live oaks and magnolias behind the dunes.

On summer Wednesdays, people bring lawn chairs to Plyler Park on Ocean Boulevard for free live music from 6 to 8, then stay for fireworks at 9. After the summer season, from September to May 1, you can walk a leashed dog or ride a bike on the beach and the boardwalk at any hour.

  • A city resident's parking decal costs nothing, and it covers parking at the city's meters and pay stations.
  • Nearly every house in the city is outside the zones that allow rentals under 90 days.

Half the houses in the city sold for more than $535,000, and half for less.

9. Socastee: a historic village on the waterway

Socastee began in the late 1800s as a lumber and turpentine camp. Its old swing bridge, built in the mid-1930s, carries Dick Pond Road over the Intracoastal Waterway. The bridge, two houses, a store and a pecan grove make up a historic district on the National Register.

The bridge still turns on its pier to let tall boats pass, and the Netflix show Outer Banks booked it on four different days to film a scene. Each April, Socastee holds its Heritage Festival at the Sarvis House on Peachtree Road, next to the bridge, with a classic car show, live music on two stages and food trucks.

South of Socastee, Freewoods Farm is a 40-acre working farm museum run with mules, not tractors, and each November its cane is pressed into syrup in a mule-powered mill.

A house here costs about what it does in Longs, but Socastee is much closer to the beach and the airport. The beach, the airport and Coastal Grand Mall are each about 15 minutes or less from a typical house.

  • About 1 in 9 houses here are in FEMA's high-risk flood zone, more than twice the share in Conway or Myrtle Beach. We check the zone for each house.

That is about $300,000 for a typical house in Socastee.

10. Surfside Beach: the sand at the end of the street

In Surfside Beach, a typical house is about 3 minutes by car from the sand, the closest of any place here. Every home in the town is less than a mile from Ocean Boulevard, the beach road.

The town's new concrete pier opened in 2024. Walking on it is free, and it has two restaurants, an ice cream shop and a bait shop.

On summer Monday nights, you carry a beach chair down to the sand by the pier and watch fire dancers spin flaming knives, in a free show the town puts on. On one December night, the town runs free trolleys around Surfside Beach to see the Christmas lights, with hot cocoa while you wait in Memorial Park.

The town runs a farmers market in Memorial Park every Tuesday from April to mid-December, 10 a.m. to 3 p.m., with local produce, eggs and honey. From mid-September to mid-May, you can surf anywhere on the town's beach from sunup to sundown, except within 300 feet of the pier.

Fewer than half the houses here are taxed as someone's main home. Along the ocean, only about 1 in 8 homes is. On many inland streets, about 7 in 10 are.

  • About 3 in 10 houses here are inside FEMA's high-risk flood zone, so we check each one.

At about $705,000, a typical house here sold for the most of any place on this page.

11. Garden City: between the ocean and the marsh

Garden City is a strip of beach houses between the ocean and the salt marsh. Its pier is 668 feet long, and in summer bands play every night at both ends, weather permitting. A pier pass covers fishing, with no state fishing license needed on the pier.

From November through February, the county lets golf carts drive on its beach here, with a state permit and a licensed driver.

From a dock on the marsh, you can tie a chicken neck to a string and pull up blue crabs for dinner, and with three or fewer drop nets, fold-up traps or hand lines you need no license.

Every Fourth of July afternoon, decorated golf carts drive the "wet" parade while people along the route soak them with water guns. At the inlet end of Waccamaw Drive, people go up to the rooftop deck of Gulfstream Cafe to watch the sun set over the water. Parking is free all year at every oceanfront beach access Horry County runs in Garden City.

Houses in Garden City sold for about $406,000 at the middle, about $300,000 less than in Surfside Beach, the next place north.

12. Murrells Inlet: the creek and the closest hospital

The Murrells Inlet on this page is the part in Georgetown County, south of the county line. Houses on the creek side of Business 17 have docks over the marsh.

The MarshWalk is a half-mile wooden boardwalk along the marsh, with restaurants and free live music. A hospital is about 6 minutes from a typical house, the closest of any place here.

The inlet's name for good cooking is older than the MarshWalk: in the 1800s, summer people came by river steamboat, and many of the steamboat cooks stayed. From the day after Thanksgiving to New Year's Eve, every restaurant along the MarshWalk is outlined in lights that play in time to Christmas music, on the hour and the half hour from 5 to 11 p.m.

Since 1983, decorated boats have paraded on July 4 from the south end of Garden City Beach up the creek in front of the MarshWalk. On the creek side stands the Hermitage, where the inlet's well-known ghost story is set. The story goes that Alice Flagg is still seen at the front door in a white dress, looking for the engagement ring her brother threw into the marsh.

  • Sales tax here is 7 percent, against 8 percent in Horry County.

The middle sale here was about $610,000. A house here also costs the most of any place on this page to keep each year.

13. Pawleys Island and Litchfield: an island town where houses rarely sell

The Town of Pawleys Island is the island itself, a town of its own since 1985. The mainland around it with a Pawleys Island address is county land, and so is Litchfield.

The town says people who come back year after year feel "their blood pressure goes down when they cross the causeway." In 2012, the island house called Liberty Lodge marked 100 years in one family. Islanders tell of the Gray Man, a figure in a long gray coat said to walk the beach before a big hurricane to warn people to leave.

A sea turtle group formed for Pawleys in 1990 walks the beach at dawn every day from May until the last nest hatches. At the town's Fourth of July parade, prizes go to floats, golf carts, decorated houses and docks.

Near Litchfield, the road into Huntington Beach State Park crosses a causeway between the marsh and a freshwater lake, and more than 300 kinds of birds have been seen in the park.

The island has about 480 houses, and they rarely sell: only 10 changed hands at a market price in the past year. Every island house but one is in FEMA's high-risk flood zone. Across the whole area, about a third of houses are, the highest share on this page.

Across the island, the mainland and Litchfield, the middle sale was about $650,000, and nearly all of those sales were off the island. On the island itself, the middle sale over the last three years was about $2.45 million.

Two things keep the gap small:

  • A home you live in is taxed on 4 percent of its value, not 6 percent, and most of the school tax comes off its bill.
  • North Myrtle Beach has a low city tax rate, and about half its houses for sale have no HOA fee.

Two things can widen it, and both matter most near the beach:

  • Insurance. The cards use about $2,000, the middle that Horry County owners pay for a house; half pay between about $1,300 and $3,000. East of US 17, or of Bypass 17 where it runs farther west, an insurer may leave wind and hail out. You can buy that cover separately.
  • Flood insurance. Among Horry County owners who carry a FEMA flood policy on the house they live in, the middle policy costs about $1,070 a year in the A zones. Those are FEMA's high-risk zones where big storm waves are not expected. Outside the high-risk zones it is about $550.

Near the beach, both can apply. A typical Surfside Beach house in the high-risk flood zone, with a $3,000 insurance bill, costs about $10,400 a year to keep instead of $8,300. If you buy with us, we get you an insurance quote for the address before you make an offer.

What changes when a house is inside city limits?

The tax rate, the trash pickup, the sales tax and the rules for rentals and golf carts. Four of the 13 places are a city or town in full. Myrtle Beach, North Myrtle Beach and Conway are cities, and Surfside Beach is a town. The City of Loris, the Town of Aynor and the Town of Pawleys Island sit inside places that are mostly county land.

At 65, after a year living in South Carolina, the first $50,000 of your home's value is not taxed. Outside the towns in Horry County, that saves about $180 a year; in Conway, about $330.

Trash: Myrtle Beach, North Myrtle Beach and Conway pick it up at the curb, and Loris, Aynor and Surfside Beach offer service too. Renting by the night, place by place:

  • Myrtle Beach: most neighborhoods of houses are not zoned for rentals under 90 days.
  • North Myrtle Beach: no special zoning or permit, beyond a yearly business license and the lodging taxes.
  • Surfside Beach: stays under 30 days only in the zoning districts along the ocean.
  • Conway: the code allows them in three downtown districts.
  • Outside the towns in Horry County: zoning puts no limit on how long a home is rented.
  • Outside the towns in Georgetown County: a tourist home only in the resort residential district, as a conditional use.

Outside the towns, the county's Solid Waste Authority does not collect trash at the curb. Only county residents who live outside the towns may use its 25 drop-off centers. Under state law, a golf cart needs a $5 permit decal and liability insurance, and may use only roads posted 35 mph or less.

Common questions

Questions about the towns

Is it better to live in Myrtle Beach or North Myrtle Beach?

A typical house costs about the same in both: $535,000 in Myrtle Beach and $565,000 in North Myrtle Beach. In North Myrtle Beach it is closer to the beach, but more often in the high-risk flood zone. Myrtle Beach is much closer to the airport, and most of its neighborhoods of houses are not zoned for nightly rentals.

Does a Myrtle Beach address mean the house is in the city?

Not always. Carolina Forest houses, and many in Socastee, have Myrtle Beach mailing addresses but are outside the city limits. They pay no city tax and get no city trash pickup. We check the city line for any address you send us.

Where near Myrtle Beach are most houses someone's home all year?

In most of these places, about 3 in 4 houses or more are the owner's main home, taxed at the 4 percent rate. In North Myrtle Beach about half are, and in Surfside Beach fewer than half. The rest are taxed at the 6 percent rate for second homes and rentals.

Which town near Myrtle Beach has the least crime?

Chapter3 does not rate any town on crime or describe who lives anywhere. Fair housing law bars agents from steering buyers toward or away from an area. We give you the record of the house itself: its flood zone, its tax and the rules for its address.
