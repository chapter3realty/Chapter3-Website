Relocation FAQ

Do I need to visit Myrtle Beach before buying?

Not necessarily before making an offer, but we strongly recommend one in-person visit before closing. We do live video walkthroughs of neighborhoods and properties before you travel so the trip is efficient. Most of our out-of-state buyers find their home on a single 2-day visit.

How does a remote closing work in South Carolina?

SC law allows remote closings. You sign documents through a notary in your home state and submit them electronically to the closing attorney in South Carolina. We coordinate between the closing attorney, your lender, and you, so no step gets missed.

Which Myrtle Beach neighborhoods are best for relocators?

Carolina Forest is the most popular landing spot for families relocating to the Grand Strand. It has the best schools in Horry County, approximately 30 sub-neighborhoods across a range of price points, and an active resale market. Market Common is the top pick for professionals who want walkability. Murrells Inlet and Pawleys Island attract those looking for a quieter lifestyle away from the tourist corridor.

How is Myrtle Beach's cost of living compared to the Northeast?

Overall cost of living in Myrtle Beach runs approximately 7% below the national average. Housing is significantly lower than markets in NC, NY, NJ, and PA. There is no state income tax on Social Security in SC, and property taxes are among the lowest in the region. The main cost surprises are coastal insurance and HOA fees on condos, which buyers from inland markets do not expect.

What should I know about schools in Horry County?

School quality varies significantly by location in Horry County. Carolina Forest has the strongest school performance, including River Oaks Elementary and Carolina Forest High. Conway, Socastee, and Saint James districts are also solid options. Myrtle Beach city schools trend lower in performance ratings. If schools are a priority, filter your search to the Carolina Forest and North Myrtle Beach areas.

Mistakes Relocators Make

Buying in the wrong neighborhood for their lifestyle

The Grand Strand is not one market. A family relocating from the suburbs of Charlotte who buys an oceanfront condo in Myrtle Beach city often finds the area too touristy and lacking the suburban feel they expected. Spend time in multiple neighborhoods on your visit before narrowing down. We build a neighborhood tour into every relocation plan.

Underestimating coastal insurance costs

Buyers from inland markets are accustomed to one homeowners insurance policy. In coastal SC you need three: homeowners, wind/hail, and flood. Together these can add $400 to $700 per month to your housing cost. Get insurance quotes during your due diligence period, not after.

Not getting pre-approved before shopping

Out-of-state buyers sometimes start touring before completing their pre-approval, assuming they can transfer a pre-approval letter from their current state. SC lenders underwrite coastal properties differently, particularly for condos, flood-zone properties, and investment purchases. Get your pre-approval with a SC-licensed lender before you tour. Our preferred lender, BrickWood Mortgage, is SC-licensed and can pre-approve you directly.

Relying on Zillow neighborhood data instead of local knowledge

Zillow's neighborhood ratings do not reflect the block-by-block differences in the Grand Strand market. A zip code that looks average in the statistics may contain a top-rated school district, a golf-cart-friendly community, or a high-performing STR corridor. Ask your agent for a block-by-block breakdown before drawing conclusions from online ratings.

The real monthly number

Lower prices, but build the real monthly number first

Almost every relocator sees Grand Strand prices and feels relief. Homes here usually cost less than the Northeast and less than many big metros. That part is true. The mistake is stopping at the sticker price. On the coast, three things move the monthly cost that you may not have paid up north.

  • Coastal insurance. Near the water you often need more than one policy, and it can add hundreds of dollars a month. We flag this in the mistakes list above and get you real quotes early, not after closing. The buyer mistakes that are not relocation-specific have their own page: common Myrtle Beach buyer mistakes.
  • Flood zones. FEMA, the federal agency that maps flood risk, marks some areas as more likely to flood. If your home is in one, a lender will require flood insurance. Two homes a block apart can be rated differently, so we check the map before you commit.
  • HOA or condo regime fees. Many Grand Strand condos and planned communities charge a monthly fee that covers shared insurance and upkeep. On some buildings it is small; on others it is a big part of the payment. Always ask for the current figure.

None of this means the coast is expensive. It usually is not. It means the honest number to compare against your old home is the all-in monthly cost, not the price tag. We build that number with you before you commit. For the city-by-city comparison see the cost of living calculator, and for a town-by-town look at prices, our neighborhood guides.

You do not know the area yet

How to pick a Grand Strand town before you know the area

When you are moving from far away, every Grand Strand town sounds the same at first. They are not the same. Before you tour, it helps to answer a few honest questions about how you want to live. The town most relocating families tour first has its own guide: Carolina Forest.

  • Beachfront or a few minutes inland? Right on the water costs more and can mean more tourists and traffic in summer. A short drive inland often gets you more house for the money and a quieter street.
  • Busy and tourist-heavy, or year-round and quiet? Some areas are lively all summer and slow in winter. Others feel like a normal neighborhood every month of the year.
  • How far is your daily drive? If you or your partner will commute, check the drive to where the jobs are: Conway, the Market Common district in Myrtle Beach, and the main hospitals such as Grand Strand Medical Center and Conway Medical Center. Conway is the county seat, home to local government and Coastal Carolina University.
  • Schools or a 55-plus community? School quality varies a lot by area, which we cover in the FAQ above, and some communities are age-restricted.

We do not expect you to know this yet. That is our job. We match your answers to the right towns, then set up a tour so you feel the difference in person. For the full profile of each town, use our neighborhood guides.

Buying from far away

How out-of-state buyers close remotely, step by step

You do not need to be in South Carolina to get a home under contract. Most of our out-of-state buyers do the early steps from home and make one short trip. This is the usual order.

  1. Get pre-approved with a South Carolina lender first. Pre-approval is a lender's written estimate of what you can borrow. Coastal condos and flood-zone homes are reviewed differently here, so a letter from your old state may not hold up. Our preferred lender can do this remotely.
  2. Tell us how you want to live, and we build a shortlist. We match your budget and must-haves to the right towns and send only homes worth your time.
  3. We tour for you on live video. We walk the home and the street on a video call, point the camera where you ask, and tell you what a photo will not, like road noise, the neighbors, and the real condition.
  4. Make your offer from home. You sign the offer digitally. You can be under contract before you ever move.
  5. Inspect during your due diligence period. That is the window after your offer when you can inspect the home and still back out. We line up the inspections and get your insurance and flood quotes so the monthly number is accurate.
  6. Close remotely. In South Carolina an attorney handles the closing. You sign the final papers in front of a local notary, a person authorized to witness signatures, and send them in, or sign on a planned trip. Then the home is yours.

One in-person visit before closing is still smart, and we build one efficient trip into the plan. Many buyers find their home on a single two-day visit.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy a home while I’m still relocating from out of state?

Yes, most of our relocating buyers start from hundreds of miles away. We handle video walkthroughs, local due diligence, and the paperwork remotely so you can make a confident offer without flying down for every showing.

Can I get a mortgage if I’m changing jobs to move here?

Often yes, but a new job or an income gap can complicate qualifying. Because our lending partner, BrickWood Mortgage (NMLS #189497), works with us daily, the loan timing and the contract timing can move together, and we’ve negotiated contract extensions with sellers to buy clients time to get their income in place.

Where do most people relocating to Myrtle Beach come from?

North Carolina is the single biggest state we see, since it sits right next door. After that, the largest groups come from the Northeast, mainly New York, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania, plus Ohio. Most are moving for the coast, the lower taxes and cost of living, and a slower pace.

Are property taxes lower in South Carolina?

For a primary residence, usually yes. Owner-occupied homes in South Carolina are assessed at 4 percent and exempt from school operating taxes, which keeps effective rates among the lowest in the country.

Can I buy in Myrtle Beach before I move?

Yes. Remote purchases are routine on the Grand Strand, with video tours, digital signing, and a mail-away or remote closing coordinated through a South Carolina attorney.
