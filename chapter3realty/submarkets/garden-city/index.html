Overview

Garden City Beach sits on a narrow peninsula between the Atlantic on one side and the salt marsh and Murrells Inlet creeks on the other. That geography defines it. The oceanfront blocks are a dense, popular short-term rental market anchored by the Garden City Pier, while the inlet side offers creek-front and marsh-view homes with boat access and a more residential feel.

It is a seasonal, vacation-driven market with a smaller year-round population than the incorporated towns. For investors, that means the short-term rental engine is the main event, and the inlet side adds a distinct waterfront niche that appeals to boaters and fishing-focused buyers.

Property types and pricing

On the ocean side, expect raised beach houses and condos built for weekly rentals, many sleeping large groups. On the inlet and creek side, you find single-family homes with docks and marsh views. The two sides draw different renters and different price points, and the best returns usually come from understanding which side a property sits on and pricing the demand accordingly.

Because Garden City is largely unincorporated, county rules govern much of it, which historically has meant a relatively permissive environment for vacation rentals compared with some incorporated towns. That can be an advantage, but it still requires confirming the rules for the specific parcel.

Where investors are looking

The peninsula's two sides define where investors look. Oceanfront blocks near the Garden City Pier hold the group-friendly beach houses that drive peak-season revenue. The creek and inlet side, backing onto the Murrells Inlet waterways, offers dock-access homes at a lower entry than oceanfront. Garden City Point at the southern tip is the most exposed and most sought-after stretch for premium vacation houses.

The rental market

Short-term rental dominates the oceanfront, and group-friendly beach houses near the pier are the strongest performers in peak season. The creek and inlet homes attract a boating and fishing renter and a buyer who wants water access without oceanfront pricing. Seasonality is pronounced, so winter carrying costs and a realistic annual occupancy assumption are essential to the underwrite.

How the returns work here

The return profile is heavily seasonal, so the underwrite lives or dies on the annual occupancy assumption. Peak-summer weeks on a group-friendly oceanfront house produce most of the year's revenue, while winter carries cost with little income. Build the model on a conservative full-year occupancy, subtract the real expense stack of a large vacation home, and judge the deal on net operating income. Inlet-side homes trade some peak ceiling for a steadier, lower-cost profile.

Financing notes

Large vacation houses and condos finance differently. Houses usually qualify more conventionally; condos hinge on project warrantability and reserves. Investor and second-home loans require larger down payments, and DSCR financing on projected rents is common for the bigger short-term-rental houses. Given the flood exposure on the peninsula, lenders will also want the insurance picture settled early.

Local rules and costs that move the numbers

Three line items move a Garden City deal. South Carolina's 6 percent assessment ratio on non-owner-occupied property raises the tax bill above what a primary residence would pay, and Chapter3 will confirm the parcel's current figure. Short-term rentals carry state and local accommodations taxes and county licensing. And because the peninsula is exposed to water on both sides, flood and wind insurance are real, recurring costs that have to sit in the model from the start, not get discovered at closing.

What a first deal looks like here

A first Garden City purchase usually comes down to which side of the peninsula fits your plan. An oceanfront or near-pier house aimed at summer group rentals carries the highest ceiling and the heaviest seasonality, so the model has to survive a conservative annual occupancy. An inlet or creek-side home with dock access trades some peak revenue for a steadier, lower-cost profile. Either way, settle the flood-insurance picture early and confirm the county rules for the exact parcel before you commit.

What to watch before you buy

  • Heavy seasonality. Revenue concentrates in summer. Model the full year and a conservative occupancy, not just July and August.
  • Flood and wind exposure. The peninsula is exposed on both sides. Insurance is a real line item; get quotes before you offer.
  • County versus town rules. Rules differ across the unincorporated and incorporated lines nearby. Confirm jurisdiction for the parcel.

Where Garden City sits, and why being unincorporated matters

Garden City Beach is an unincorporated community about 10 to 12 miles (a 20 to 25 minute drive) south of Myrtle Beach on U.S. 17, sitting between Surfside Beach to the north and Murrells Inlet to the south. It straddles two counties, mostly Horry with a southern tip in Georgetown. Because it is unincorporated, there is no city government imposing its own short-term-rental ban, so it follows the more permissive county framework, which is exactly why it behaves differently from the City of Myrtle Beach for investors.

The Garden City market right now

Garden City is a genuine oceanfront and vacation-rental market. Oceanfront and ocean-view condos along North and South Waccamaw Drive commonly trade from the low $200,000s to about $400,000 (buildings like Waters Edge, Royal Garden, and Sea Watch), while raised beach houses with four to seven bedrooms and oceanfront single-family homes run from the $600,000s past $1 million. For context on the segment, Horry County oceanfront and resort condos were reported around a $238,825 median in early 2026 with days on market near 162, meaning buyers have room to negotiate.

Short-term rental rules in Garden City

Short-term rentals are generally allowed in Garden City because it is unincorporated, so there is no municipal STR ban of the kind the City of Myrtle Beach enforces; properties follow Horry County (or Georgetown County, for the southern tip) rules plus any HOA restrictions. That makes Garden City one of the more STR-friendly oceanfront options on the Strand. You still collect and remit accommodations tax, and an HOA or condo regime can restrict rentals, and Chapter3 will verify the specific building or community rules before you count on nightly income.

What it costs to own here: the 4 percent versus 6 percent tax rule

South Carolina assesses an owner-occupied primary residence at a 4 percent ratio with the owner-occupant school credit, and a second home or investment at 6 percent with no credit, so the effective property tax on the same home can be close to double when it is not your primary residence. See the South Carolina Department of Revenue for the assessment-ratio rules.

Flood and insurance

Garden City is a narrow strip between the ocean and the inlet, so flood exposure is real and location-specific. Oceanfront and inlet-front lots sit in FEMA flood zones that require flood insurance on a federally backed loan, and wind coverage is a meaningful line item. Raised construction and newer builds generally price better on insurance. Pull the FEMA flood map for the exact address.

Lifestyle and amenities

The heart of Garden City is the Garden City Pier, a 650-plus-foot fishing pier with a tackle shop, arcade, cafe, and the "End of the Pier" bar with live music in season. The Garden City Inlet nature preserve offers crabbing and kayaking, the Village Surf Shoppe is a local institution, and the Murrells Inlet MarshWalk restaurants are minutes south. It is a golf-cart-friendly, laid-back beach community, and Myrtle Beach International Airport is only about 10 miles away.

Schools serving Garden City

Public schools on the Horry County side generally fall within Horry County Schools, including Seaside Elementary, St. James Middle, and St. James High; the small Georgetown County portion is served by the Georgetown County School District. Assignment is by address, and Chapter3 will confirm the exact schools on the district boundary map.

What it is like to live in Garden City

Garden City is a narrow, golf-cart-friendly strip between the ocean and the inlet, more laid-back than a resort town. The Garden City Pier anchors the community with its tackle shop, arcade, and end-of-pier bar, the inlet nature preserve offers crabbing and kayaking, and the Murrells Inlet MarshWalk restaurants are minutes south. Myrtle Beach International Airport is only about 10 miles away.

For a live-in buyer, the tradeoff is proximity to the sand against the reality of a busy summer season and real coastal flood exposure. Oceanfront and inlet-front lots sit in FEMA flood zones, and Chapter3 will check the flood map and insurance for the exact address before you commit.

Buying in Garden City to live, not just to rent

Garden City is best known as an oceanfront rental market, but it also suits second-home owners and full-time residents who want to be steps from the sand without big-city density. Because it is unincorporated, it does not have a town STR ban, so if you want a quiet street you will lean on the HOA rules rather than zoning. A primary residence is taxed at 4% with the owner-occupant credit; a second home at 6% without it.

Read the HOA rules before you fall in love with a house

In a market with this many associations, the covenants matter as much as the house. Before you make an offer, read the HOA bylaws and rules, because they can restrict short-term and long-term rentals, pets, parking, golf carts, fences, and exterior changes. The classic trap: you have spent years training your dog to walk off leash, then buy into a community with a strict leash rule and lose the thing you valued most. Read the documents before you make an offer, not after.

Ask us and we will get you the governing documents for any Garden City community, from our own files if we already have them or by requesting them for you if we do not. It is also why a Chapter3 agent asks a lot of questions about how you actually live: we would rather spend the time up front than match you to a home with a rule or an HOA that blocks what you enjoy or turns out to be a poor investment.

Who Garden City suits

Garden City is built for the oceanfront short-term-rental investor and the vacation-home buyer. Because it is unincorporated and STR-friendly, the nightly-rental math that does not work inside the City of Myrtle Beach often works here. It also suits second-home owners who want to be steps from the sand without big-city density. If you specifically want an easy STR path on the ocean, this is one of the strongest fits on the Strand.

Frequently asked questions

Can you short-term rental in Garden City Beach?

Generally yes. Garden City is unincorporated, so there is no city STR ban; properties follow Horry County (or Georgetown County) rules plus any HOA or condo-regime restrictions. You still collect and remit accommodations tax.

Is Garden City in Horry or Georgetown County?

Mostly Horry County, with a southern tip in Georgetown County near the inlet. The county can affect taxes and school assignment, and Chapter3 will confirm this by address.

What kind of property is Garden City known for?

Oceanfront and ocean-view condos along Waccamaw Drive and raised beach houses, with a strong vacation-rental market anchored by the Garden City Pier.

What are property taxes on a second home in Garden City?

South Carolina assesses primary homes at 4 percent with a school-tax credit and second homes or investments at 6 percent with no credit, so the effective tax on the same home can be close to double.

Which schools serve Garden City?

Horry-side properties are generally served by Seaside Elementary, St. James Middle, and St. James High in Horry County Schools; the Georgetown-side tip is served by the Georgetown County School District. Confirm by address.

Is Garden City good for vacation rentals?
