Overview

Murrells Inlet is the Seafood Capital of South Carolina, and its identity is built around the water rather than the beach. The MarshWalk, a half-mile boardwalk of waterfront restaurants and live music, anchors a community of creek-front homes, golf communities, and established single-family neighborhoods. There is no wide public beach in the inlet itself; beach access comes via neighboring Garden City, which shapes how the rental market behaves.

That distinction makes Murrells Inlet more of a primary-residence and long-term-rental market than a pure vacation-rental corridor. Investors here are often buying for steady cash flow and appreciation rather than peak-summer nightly rates, and the buyer pool leans toward primary residents and second-home owners drawn to the golf and the waterfront lifestyle.

Property types and pricing

The inventory leans single-family: golf-community homes around Wachesaw Plantation and TPC Myrtle Beach, creek-front and marsh-view properties with docks, and conventional neighborhoods set back from the water. Condos exist but are less central than in the beach towns. Waterfront and golf-community homes command premiums; inland single-family offers more approachable entry for a long-term-rental strategy.

Dock access and deep-water frontage are the features that carry the most value here, drawing boaters and a specific lifestyle buyer that inland inventory cannot reach.

Where investors are looking

Investor and primary-residence demand clusters around the golf and waterfront communities. Wachesaw Plantation and the TPC Myrtle Beach area anchor the premium golf-community market. Prince Creek is the large master-planned choice with a range of price points. Creek-front homes near the MarshWalk carry the highest waterfront premiums, while Blackmoor and the inland subdivisions offer more approachable single-family entry for a long-term-rental strategy.

The rental market

Long-term rental is the steadier play in Murrells Inlet, supported by the year-round population and proximity to jobs across the southern Strand. Short-term rental exists, particularly for waterfront and golf properties marketed to visitors who want the inlet experience, but it is more limited and more rules-dependent than the beachfront markets. The result is a market that rewards buy-and-hold investors who value stability over seasonal upside.

How the returns work here

Long-term-rental math governs most of Murrells Inlet. Start with achievable monthly rent, subtract the 35 to 45 percent that taxes, insurance, maintenance, HOA dues, and management typically take, and judge the deal on cash flow and cash-on-cash return. Golf-community homes carry higher dues that compress yield, so factor them honestly. The appeal here is predictability: year-round demand means fewer vacancy surprises than a seasonal beach asset.

Financing notes

Because the strategy here leans long-term, financing is comparatively clean. Conventional investment loans and DSCR loans that qualify on rent both fit single-family rentals well. Golf-community homes are standard collateral, though lenders will note the HOA dues in the debt-to-income or coverage math. Primary-residence buyers, common in this market, get the best terms of all.

Local rules and costs that move the numbers

The tax treatment matters here because so many buyers are choosing between primary residence and investment. South Carolina assesses a primary residence at 4 percent and non-owner-occupied property at 6 percent, a meaningful gap that changes the yield on a rental. Golf-community homes add HOA dues on top. If a property fronts the creek or marsh, factor flood insurance as well. Confirm the current millage, ratio, and dues for the specific home before you treat any quoted return as real.

What a first deal looks like here

A typical first deal here is a single-family home held as a long-term rental, often in an established neighborhood rather than a golf community where dues compress yield. Start with an achievable monthly rent, subtract a realistic expense load, and confirm the cash-on-cash return works on conservative numbers. Because demand is year-round rather than seasonal, a Murrells Inlet first deal is usually about steady, unglamorous cash flow, which for a first investment is a feature rather than a flaw.

What to watch before you buy

  • Beach access reality. The inlet has no wide public beach. Price short-term-rental expectations accordingly; this is not Garden City.
  • HOA and golf-community fees. Premium communities carry dues that affect net yield. Factor them in.
  • Dock permitting and condition. Waterfront value hinges on the dock. Confirm permits and structural condition before you pay the premium.

Where Murrells Inlet is, and why the county line matters

Murrells Inlet sits about 13 miles, a 20 to 25 minute drive, south of Myrtle Beach along U.S. 17, and it is unincorporated, which means the county governs it directly rather than a town council. The community straddles two counties: the northern portion falls in Horry County and the southern portion in Georgetown County. That one fact drives three things buyers care about here, because short-term-rental rules, school assignment, and county millage all change depending on which side of the line an address sits on.

It is a car-dependent area. Roughly nine in ten households own at least one vehicle and public transit is limited, so plan on driving to the beach, the airport, and daily errands. Myrtle Beach International Airport is about 20 to 25 minutes north.

The Murrells Inlet market right now

Murrells Inlet is a buyer's market as of mid-2026, with homes taking well longer than the national norm to sell. Public trackers put the median sale price in a wide band, from the mid $330,000s to about $445,000 depending on the source, month, and whether condos are included, with median days on market reported between roughly 90 and 175. Redfin reported a median near $360,000 in early 2026. Those aggregator figures disagree because each uses a different geography and date range, so treat them as a range, not a single truth.

For an accurate, current figure we pull directly from the Coastal Carolinas MLS for the exact area and property type you are considering, which is what actually reflects closings. The strongest window to list here is generally February through July, when buyer demand peaks.

What it costs to own here: the 4 percent versus 6 percent tax rule

Your South Carolina property-tax bill depends on how you use the home, not just its value. A primary, owner-occupied residence is assessed at a 4 percent ratio and qualifies for the owner-occupant school-operating credit. A second home or investment property is assessed at 6 percent and does not get that credit, so the effective tax on the same house can run close to double when it is not your primary residence. See the South Carolina Department of Revenue for the assessment-ratio rules. Millage also differs between the Horry and Georgetown County portions of Murrells Inlet, one more reason the county line matters.

Short-term rental rules in Murrells Inlet

Short-term rentals are allowed in Murrells Inlet, but the rules depend on the county and the HOA, not on the community name alone. On the Georgetown County side, the county treats a rental of under 30 days as a residential use and does not require a special short-term-rental permit in most zones, though owners still need a county business license and must collect and remit accommodations tax. See Georgetown County zoning for specifics. On the Horry County side, the property follows Horry County's unincorporated rules, which are more restrictive in some residential zones. In both counties an HOA or planned-unit development can restrict or ban short-term rentals regardless of what the county allows, and enforcement is generally complaint-driven. Chapter 3 will verify the zoning for the exact address and pull the HOA governing documents before you assume a property can be rented nightly.

Flood zones and insurance

Flood risk is a real underwriting factor here because the community is built around a saltwater estuary and tidal creeks. Homes inside a FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area generally require flood insurance when financed with a federally backed loan, and First Street data reported by Redfin flags a meaningful share of Murrells Inlet properties as having severe flood risk over a 30-year horizon. Pull the FEMA flood map, and on waterfront or low-lying lots an elevation certificate, for the specific address, since the zone and the premium can change block to block.

Lifestyle and amenities

The center of gravity in Murrells Inlet is the water, not a wide beach. The half-mile MarshWalk boardwalk anchors the dining and live-music scene along the estuary, and two working marinas, Crazy Sister Marina and Marlin Quay Marina, put boating, fishing charters, and the inlet within reach. Just south, Brookgreen Gardens, a National Historic Landmark with the country's largest collection of American figurative sculpture and a Lowcountry zoo, and Huntington Beach State Park give the area a nature-and-culture draw most of the Grand Strand lacks. Golf is deep, with courses including TPC Myrtle Beach, Wachesaw Plantation, and Blackmoor nearby. Everyday needs are covered by Publix, The Fresh Market, and Lowes Foods, and Tidelands Waccamaw Community Hospital serves the area. Wide public beach access is a short drive north in Garden City.

Schools serving Murrells Inlet

School assignment in Murrells Inlet depends on the county side and the exact address. Properties on the Horry County side are generally served by the St. James attendance area within Horry County Schools, including St. James Elementary, St. James Intermediate, St. James Middle, and St. James High on Highway 707, with Seaside Elementary serving parts of the area. Properties on the Georgetown County side are generally served by the Waccamaw schools within the Georgetown County School District. Because the two districts meet inside Murrells Inlet, Chapter 3 will confirm the exact assignment for any address against the district attendance-boundary map.

What is coming

Murrells Inlet already connects to the rest of the Grand Strand through the Carolina Bays Parkway, S.C. 31, which currently terminates at S.C. 707 at the edge of the community. The larger S.C. 31 extension that appears in regional news runs northward toward the North Carolina line, a future Interstate 74 corridor funded on the South Carolina side through Horry County's RIDE III program with S.C. construction targeted around 2029 per SCDOT statements reported locally, so it matters more to the North Strand than to the inlet directly.

What it is like to live in Murrells Inlet

For a primary or second home, Murrells Inlet is built around the water rather than a wide beach. Daily life centers on the half-mile MarshWalk, two working marinas, and the estuary, with Brookgreen Gardens and Huntington Beach State Park just south for nature and culture most of the Strand lacks. It is quieter and more residential than the Myrtle Beach core, and wide public beach access is a short drive north in Garden City.

Everyday needs are covered by Publix, The Fresh Market, and Lowes Foods, and Tidelands Waccamaw Community Hospital serves the area. As a live-in buyer, the numbers that shape daily life here are the flood elevation and insurance cost for the exact lot, the regime or HOA dues on a condo, and the community rules, more than nightly-rate projections.

Buying in Murrells Inlet to live, not just to rent

Much of what is written about Murrells Inlet is aimed at investors, but many buyers here want a full-time residence or a second home on the water. If you are buying to live, weigh boat and dock access, the walk or drive to the MarshWalk, and how seasonal your specific street gets. The primary-versus-second-home choice also changes your tax bill: a primary residence is assessed at 4% with the owner-occupant credit, a second home at 6% without it, so budget for the higher effective rate if this will not be your full-time home.

Read the HOA rules before you fall in love with a house

In a market with this many associations, the covenants matter as much as the house. Before you make an offer, read the HOA bylaws and rules, because they can restrict short-term and long-term rentals, pets, parking, golf carts, fences, and exterior changes. The classic trap: you have spent years training your dog to walk off leash, then buy into a community with a strict leash rule and lose the thing you valued most. Read the documents before you make an offer, not after.

Ask us and we will get you the governing documents for any Murrells Inlet community, from our own files if we already have them or by requesting them for you if we do not. It is also why a Chapter 3 agent asks a lot of questions about how you actually live: we would rather spend the time up front than match you to a home with a rule or an HOA that blocks what you enjoy or turns out to be a poor investment.

Who Murrells Inlet suits

Murrells Inlet fits buyers who want a waterfront and golf lifestyle over beachfront nightlife, and it rewards a specific kind of investor. Because wide beach access is a drive away in Garden City, the numbers here favor long-term and mid-term rentals and steady appreciation more than pure peak-summer nightly rates, the opposite of an oceanfront-condo play. Primary-residence buyers, second-home owners, and buyers targeting a specific gated or golf community also do well here. If your plan depends on high-season nightly rental income, an oceanfront submarket like Garden City or Myrtle Beach usually pencils better, and we will tell you that rather than sell you the wrong street.
