Real estate investing in Surfside Beach, SC

Surfside Beach markets itself as The Family Beach, and the branding is accurate. It is a small, incorporated town with a quieter, lower-key feel than Myrtle Beach, a mix of beach cottages, condos, and single-family homes, and a reputation for families who want the coast without the crowds and neon. That positioning supports both a healthy short-term rental market and a real base of year-round residents.

Because Surfside has a genuine local population, it is one of the more balanced submarkets on the Strand. An investor can run a beach cottage as a vacation rental, hold a single-family home as a long-term rental for local tenants, or pursue the snowbird hybrid that fills winter months with monthly renters. That optionality lowers risk relative to the pure-tourism corridors.

Surfside Beach property types and pricing

Inventory ranges from older raised beach cottages near the ocean to newer single-family homes and townhomes set back from the water, plus a stock of mid-rise condos. The closer to the beach, the more the property leans short-term; the further back, the more it behaves like a conventional residential rental. Entry pricing is generally more approachable than the prime oceanfront corridors, which is part of the appeal for first-time investors.

The town's manageable scale is a feature. It is walkable, the pier is a focal point, and renters value the calmer atmosphere, which translates into loyal repeat bookings for well-kept properties.

Where investors are looking in Surfside Beach

The closer to the pier and the ocean, the stronger the short-term-rental case, and the original beach cottage blocks are where vacation demand concentrates. Set back from the water, conventional subdivisions and the Deerfield golf community support long-term rentals to local tenants. Ocean Lakes, the large leasehold beachfront community nearby, is a distinct niche with its own ownership structure that investors should understand before buying in.

The Surfside Beach rental market

Short-term rental works well near the beach, and the family positioning supports strong summer occupancy and repeat guests. Away from the immediate beach, long-term rental to local tenants is a viable and lower-volatility path. The town administers its own business licensing and accommodations requirements, and as a municipality it can set rules that differ from the unincorporated county around it, and Chapter3 will confirm the rules for the specific street.

How rental returns work in Surfside Beach

Because Surfside supports both strategies, the return math splits two ways. Near the beach, model it like a vacation rental: gross revenue less cleaning, management, and seasonal vacancy. Set back from the water, model it like a long-term hold: monthly rent less the 35 to 45 percent that taxes, insurance, maintenance, and management typically absorb. The more approachable entry pricing here means cash-on-cash returns can pencil even on conservative rent assumptions.

Financing an investment property in Surfside Beach

Conventional single-family homes here finance straightforwardly, while condos depend on the project's warrantability and HOA health. For the long-term-rental side, DSCR loans that qualify on rent are a clean fit. Confirm condo-project approval early if you are buying in a building, since a non-warrantable project narrows your lender options considerably.

Surfside Beach rules and costs that move the numbers

A few local costs shape the math. Non-owner-occupied property is assessed at South Carolina's 6 percent ratio rather than the 4 percent primary-residence ratio, and Chapter3 will verify the tax line for the address. If you run the property as a short-term rental, the town requires licensing and the usual state and local accommodations taxes apply. Near-beach cottages also need a genuine flood-insurance quote. None of these are deal-killers, but they separate a pro forma that holds up from one that looks good only until the first tax bill.

What a first Surfside Beach deal looks like

A sensible first Surfside deal often means choosing strategy by location. Near the beach, a cottage or condo run as a vacation rental; set back from the water, a single-family home held for a local long-term tenant. Either way, Chapter3 will confirm the town's licensing rules, verify condo-project warrantability if you are buying in a building, and run conservative rent or occupancy numbers with you. The more approachable entry pricing means a first deal here can cash-flow without heroic assumptions, which is exactly what a first investment should do.

What to watch before you buy in Surfside Beach

  • Older cottage condition. Many beach cottages are older raised structures. Budget for roof, HVAC, and elevation-related maintenance.
  • Town rules. Surfside is incorporated and sets its own short-term-rental and licensing rules. Chapter3 will verify them for the exact address.
  • Flood insurance. Near-beach properties need a real flood quote in the underwriting, not an estimate.

Where Surfside Beach sits, and how it is governed

Surfside Beach is a small incorporated Horry County town just south of Myrtle Beach that brands itself "The Family Beach." It runs on golf carts as much as cars (South Carolina allows registered carts on secondary roads in daylight within a few miles of home), and because it is its own town it sets its own short-term-rental zoning, which is stricter than unincorporated areas nearby.

The Surfside Beach market right now

Surfside is a two-tier market, so read the numbers carefully. A blended median can look high (Redfin reported around $530,000 in early 2026) because it mixes small inland condos with oceanfront homes, while detached single-family homes carry a much higher median (into the $800,000s) and the typical home-value index sits closer to the $347,000 to $360,000 range. Price per square foot averages around $322 town-wide but climbs toward $540 in ocean-adjacent corridors, and days on market have run near 108. Always separate the oceanfront tier from the inland tier.

Short-term rental rules in Surfside Beach

Surfside Beach permits short-term rentals (under 30 days) only in specific zoning districts: the R3 high-density residential zone closer to the ocean and the C3 commercial zone. In the inland R1 low-density zones, transient rentals are restricted or prohibited. Operators need a town short-term-rental permit and must pass a safety inspection, and long-term rentals (a year or longer) are allowed in all residential zones. Verifying R3 versus R1 for a specific address before you write an offer is the single most expensive mistake buyers avoid here. Confirm current rules with the Town of Surfside Beach. Every jurisdiction's licenses and taxes: short-term rental rules by city.

What it costs to own in Surfside Beach: the 4 percent versus 6 percent property tax rule

South Carolina assesses an owner-occupied primary residence at a 4 percent ratio with the owner-occupant school credit, and a second home or investment at 6 percent with no credit, so the effective property tax on the same home can be close to double when it is not your primary residence. See the South Carolina Department of Revenue for the assessment-ratio rules. Full county math, with a calculator: Horry County property taxes.

Flood zones and insurance in Surfside Beach

Surfside enforces elevation requirements and participates in the FEMA Community Rating System, which can lower flood-insurance costs for compliant properties. Oceanfront and low-lying lots still carry meaningful flood and wind exposure. Review the FEMA flood map and, on ocean-adjacent lots, the elevation certificate before you buy, and on condos ask for the reserve study, since regime (HOA) fees have risen with coastal insurance costs. What the three coastal policies cost: coastal home insurance costs.

Surfside Beach lifestyle and amenities

Surfside is deliberately low-key: a walkable downtown, the Surfside Pier, golf-cart access throughout, and a quieter, residential feel than Myrtle Beach. South Strand Medical Center is close by, and the Murrells Inlet MarshWalk dining scene is a short drive south, so residents get restaurants and nightlife without hosting the crowds themselves.

Schools serving Surfside Beach

Public schools fall within Horry County Schools, with the area generally served by Lakewood Elementary, St. James Intermediate, and St. James High. Assignment is by address, and Chapter3 will confirm the exact schools on the district boundary map.

What it is like to live in Surfside Beach

Surfside Beach is deliberately low-key: a small incorporated town that runs on golf carts, with a walkable downtown, the Surfside Pier, and a quieter, more residential feel than Myrtle Beach. South Carolina allows registered golf carts on secondary roads in daylight within a few miles of home, and residents get the Murrells Inlet MarshWalk dining scene a short drive south without hosting the crowds themselves.

For a live-in buyer, the inland R1 zones (where short-term rentals are restricted anyway) are quieter and more year-round, while the R3 zone near the ocean is busier in season. South Strand Medical Center is close, and the key checks are the flood zone and elevation for the exact lot and, on a condo, the regime fees and reserve study.

Buying in Surfside Beach to live, not just to rent

Surfside brands itself "The Family Beach," and many buyers want a primary residence or a quiet second home rather than a rental. If that is you, an inland R1 property fits, and the short-term-rental restriction there is a plus for a residential street. A primary residence is taxed at 4% with the owner-occupant credit; a second home at 6% without it.

Read the Surfside Beach HOA rules before you commit to a house

In a market with this many associations, the covenants matter as much as the house. Before you make an offer, read the HOA bylaws and rules, because they can restrict short-term and long-term rentals, pets, parking, golf carts, fences, and exterior changes. The classic trap: you have spent years training your dog to walk off leash, then buy into a community with a strict leash rule and lose the thing you valued most. Read the documents before you make an offer, not after.

Who Surfside Beach suits

Surfside fits STR investors who buy specifically in the R3 or C3 zones near the ocean, and primary or second-home buyers who want a quiet, walkable, golf-cart beach town and are buying inland where nightly rentals are restricted anyway. The two goals map to two different parts of town, so the zone you buy in matters more than the town you pick.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can you Airbnb a property in Surfside Beach?

Only in the right zone. Surfside permits short-term rentals (under 30 days) in the R3 high-density and C3 commercial districts and restricts or prohibits them in inland R1 low-density zones. A town STR permit and safety inspection are required.

Is Surfside Beach a golf-cart town?

Yes. Golf carts are a primary local mode of transport, and South Carolina allows registered carts on secondary roads during daylight within a few miles of home.

Why do Surfside Beach median prices look so high?

Because the blended median mixes small inland condos with oceanfront homes. Detached single-family medians run much higher than the overall home-value index, so compare within a tier.

What are property taxes on a second home in Surfside Beach?

South Carolina assesses primary homes at 4 percent with a school-tax credit and second homes or investments at 6 percent with no credit, so the effective tax on the same home can be close to double.

Which schools serve Surfside Beach?

Public schools fall within Horry County Schools, generally Lakewood Elementary, St. James Intermediate, and St. James High. Chapter3 will confirm the exact assignment on the district boundary map.

Why is Surfside Beach called the family beach?

It has a quieter, more residential feel than central Myrtle Beach, with steady seasonal demand and a mix of homes and condominiums.

What does Surfside Beach offer investors?

A calmer setting close to the main attractions, with both single-family and condominium options.
