Grand Strand town guides

Grand Strand towns: nine places near Myrtle Beach, and how they differ.
The water, the winter, and what a home costs in each.

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

Among the Grand Strand towns, Pawleys Island counted 130 residents in 2020, and Conway became a town 40 years before Myrtle Beach.

The short answer

Seven of the nine Grand Strand places in our guides are on or by the coast, from Little River to Pawleys Island. Carolina Forest and Conway are inland.

Most of the vacation homes are in the seven places on the coast. Inland, in Carolina Forest and Conway, fewer than 1 in 10 homes were kept for vacations in 2020. Five of the nine are cities or towns, and the county runs the other four.

What towns are on the Grand Strand?

On the coast, north to south, the Grand Strand places in our guides are Little River, North Myrtle Beach, Myrtle Beach, Surfside Beach, Garden City, Murrells Inlet and Pawleys Island. Inland are Carolina Forest and Conway.

How long is the Grand Strand?

From the North Carolina line to the south end of Pawleys Island, the ocean shore is about 48 miles long. To the mouth of Winyah Bay, it is about 63. The City of Myrtle Beach calls the Grand Strand a 60-mile stretch of the coast.

The two big cities have the longest beaches: about 10 miles inside Myrtle Beach, and a little over 8 inside North Myrtle Beach.

Pawleys Island has about 3 1/2 miles of ocean beach, and Surfside Beach about 2. More than a third of the shore is outside every town: unincorporated land, Myrtle Beach State Park and Huntington Beach State Park.

Which Grand Strand towns are quiet in winter?

In the 2020 Census, Pawleys Island and North Myrtle Beach had the most homes kept for vacations: about 7 in 10 and about 5 in 10.

A street of vacation homes is full in summer and quiet from fall to spring. A street of homes people live in looks much the same in January as in July. If the vacation home would be yours, our second home guide covers the tax, the insurance and renting it out.

What is each Grand Strand town like?

Each place below leads with the thing people love there, then what a home costs. Where a city or town has its own rule on renting by the night, the place says so.

Little River: on the waterway, by the inlet

From a dock on the Intracoastal Waterway at Little River, a boat can head north to Norfolk or south to Florida without going out on the ocean.

For the ocean, boats go out through Little River Inlet, at the North Carolina line, down a marked channel about 2 miles long. The tide here rises and falls about 4 feet.

Resold houses in Little River usually sell for about $357,000.

North Myrtle Beach: the shag on Main Street

The shag, South Carolina's state dance, is still danced on Main Street. At Fat Harold's Beach Club, the home of the shag, DJs play every night from 7.

The city is four old beach towns in one. In 1968, Cherry Grove, Ocean Drive, Crescent Beach and Windy Hill voted to join.

A resold house usually sells for about $565,000. A nightly-rental owner needs a yearly city business license, with no special zoning or permit beyond it.

Myrtle Beach: the Boardwalk, and the airport in town

Downtown, the Boardwalk is about 1.3 miles long, beside the ocean. The airport is inside the city, with nonstop flights to more than 50 destinations.

Myrtle Beach became a town in 1938. It became a city in 1957, when its permanent population first topped 5,000.

A resold house in the city usually sells for about $540,000. Nightly rentals are allowed only in some of the city's zones, and most neighborhoods of houses are not zoned for them.

Surfside Beach: a free pier, and a town of under 2 square miles

The town's concrete pier reaches 814 feet into the ocean, and walking on it is free.

The beach was Roach's Beach, then Floral Beach, before it took the name Surfside Beach in 1952. Much of the land was once the Ark Plantation, and the town was incorporated in 1964.

Nightly rentals are allowed only in the two zones by the ocean, where a house sold for about $1 million at the middle. On the inland streets, houses sold for about $485,000 to $540,000.

Garden City: line dances on the pier

At the far end of the Garden City Pier, people learn line dances out over the ocean.

Behind the beach, about 260 houses between Waccamaw Drive and Business 17 sit on the salt marsh or a tidal creek.

About a third of a mile south of the pier, the line between Horry and Georgetown counties crosses the beach. The pier is on the Horry side.

On the Horry part, the middle sale for a condo on the ocean side by the pier was about $320,000.

Murrells Inlet: restaurants and music on the salt marsh

The MarshWalk is a wooden boardwalk along the salt marsh, lined with restaurants, and its live music is free to hear.

The inlet opens straight to the ocean between jetties. Outside Garden City, about 220 houses sit within about 100 feet of the inlet's salt marsh, almost all on the creek side of US 17 Business.

The nearest ocean beaches are Huntington Beach State Park and Garden City Beach, each about 9 minutes from the MarshWalk by car.

On the Horry side, the middle sale for a house was about $450,000.

Pawleys Island: old cypress houses by the sea

The old island houses were built mostly of cypress, hewn on the mainland. Each timber was chiseled with Roman numerals so the house could be put together on the island.

The island had about 4 homes for every resident in 2020.

On the mainland, west of US 17, a house usually sells for about $580,000. On the island, the middle sale for a house over three years was about $2.45 million, and nightly rentals are allowed.

Carolina Forest: trails on the waterway

The county's Bike and Run Park, on the Intracoastal Waterway, has about 7 miles of mountain-bike and hiking trails on 70 acres.

More people lived in Carolina Forest than in North Myrtle Beach in 2020. It has no city or town government, so there is no city tax.

In Carolina Forest, a resold house usually goes for about $440,000.

Conway: a city on the Waccamaw River

Riverfront Park on the Waccamaw has a playground built like a steamboat, the F.G. Burroughs, beside a riverwalk of about 1.5 miles.

Conway became a town in 1898, the first of the five cities and towns here to do so. It is the one city of the nine away from the ocean.

A resold house or townhouse in the city usually sells for about $265,000. For a nightly rental, we check the city's zoning for each address.

Common questions

Grand Strand towns FAQ

Why is it called the Grand Strand?

The name dates from 1949. It was the title of a newspaper column by Claude Dunnagan, and the name stuck for the whole region.

How many people live in the Grand Strand towns?

In the 2020 Census, Myrtle Beach was the largest of our nine places, with 35,682 residents, and Pawleys Island the smallest, with 130. Conway and Carolina Forest, inland, came next after Myrtle Beach.

Is Atlantic Beach part of North Myrtle Beach?

No. Atlantic Beach is a separate town, and it was not part of the 1968 merger that formed North Myrtle Beach.

Why does the 13-town comparison show different prices?

Both come from county deed records, but each counts a different group of homes. In Conway, this page counts houses and townhouses in the city: about $265,000. The comparison counts houses in its own outline: about $271,000.
