Little River, SC

Little River, SC: the boating end of the Grand Strand.
About two of every three deeded boat slips in the county are here.

  • Deeded slipsAbout 220
  • Beach11 min drive
  • Typical house$357,000

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 2, 2026

An unincorporated waterway community at the North Carolina line, with deeded boat slips, public ramps and people who live here all year.

The short answer

Little River is the year-round waterway community at the north end of the Grand Strand, at the North Carolina line. If the boat comes first, look at the old village, where all three marinas with deeded slips are. For golf and dry ground, look north-west toward the Waccamaw River; for the hospital and the highest-priced neighborhoods, look west.

A resold house here usually sells for about $357,000. Little River is not a town, so Horry County makes the rules and there is no city tax.

What is it like to live in Little River, SC?

Little River sits on the Intracoastal Waterway, the boat channel behind the Grand Strand's beaches. The old village meets the water at the end of Mineola Avenue, with docks, boats and restaurants on the water, about a minute from US 17.

Each May, on the weekend after Mother's Day, the Blue Crab Festival fills that waterfront with seafood from local restaurants, live music and crafters. It has been held for more than 40 years, and a water taxi brings people in from the water.

The beach at Cherry Grove Pier in North Myrtle Beach, Calabash in North Carolina and McLeod Seacoast hospital are all within about 11 minutes by car, as the map shows. The airport is the long drive, about 41 minutes.

A swing bridge built in 1935 still carries Sea Mountain Highway over the waterway. It turns on a center pier to let boats through, and it is one of the oldest swing bridges on SCDOT's highways. Boaters signal it to open, and at weekday rush hours it opens only on the hour and half hour.

Vereen Memorial Historical Gardens, a county park on the waterway, has about 3 miles of trails and boardwalks through forest and marsh. A piece of the colonial Kings Highway, the road from Boston to Charleston, still runs at the front of the park.

Little River is a place people live all year. When the Census last counted, about one in five of its homes was kept for seasonal use. In North Myrtle Beach, which borders it, it was nearly half.

Is Little River, SC a town?

No. Little River has no town government and no town limits, so Horry County makes its rules and collects its tax. A home here pays the county's property tax and no city tax on top.

One corner is different. A small part of the Little River ZIP code along the waterway is inside the city of North Myrtle Beach. Its homes, about 250 and mostly condos, have a Little River address and pay North Myrtle Beach's city tax. We check which side of the line a house is on.

Living on the water in Little River: boat slips, ramps and waterfront homes

Most marinas rent their slips. At Little River's three marinas you can buy one: a deeded slip is its own piece of real estate, with its own deed and its own county tax bill. The 12 slip sales recorded here in the past year were each for a slip alone, at $10,000 to $95,000, without a condo.

The slips come in sizes. The table gives the length of most slips at each marina, from the county's parcel map. Coquina Harbour's are about 30 to 45 feet. The county's parcel records do not give the depth of the water, so we check it at low tide for any slip you like.

The county taxes a slip like a second home, on its own value for the slip. It values most Mariner's Pointe slips at $38,000 or more, above nearly every price paid there in the past year. On the county's full $38,000 value, a slip pays about $460 a year in property tax; a slip that has not sold in years may be taxed on a lower, capped value. The marina's association charges a fee on top, and its recorded papers say who may own a slip. We read both before you make an offer.

Where to launch a boat

Horry County runs two public boat landings in the Little River ZIP. T. Craig Campbell Landing is on the Intracoastal Waterway at the US 17 bridge, about 7 minutes from the old village, with two ramps, a dock and a paved lot. Johnny Causey Landing faces it across the waterway, just inside North Myrtle Beach, with two more ramps and a larger lot.

On the Waccamaw River, Worthams Ferry Landing has one ramp and an unpaved lot. Chris Anderson Landing, on the river near Longs, has two ramps.

Homes on the water

Few houses here sit on the water: fewer than 2 in every 100 have a lot on the Intracoastal Waterway, a canal off it, or the Waccamaw River. Those few cost far more. Only 5 resold in the past year, and the middle one sold for about $1.3 million.

Most homes right by the water are condos, and about half of them are in three communities: Carolina Yacht Landing, Mariner's Pointe and Lightkeepers Village. A condo by the water usually sold for about $263,000 in the past year, and one away from it for about $180,000.

A typical resold house and a Mariner's Pointe slip come to about $375,000 together. If you would rather keep the boat on a trailer at home, our guide to homes with no HOA covers where that is allowed.

Which part of Little River should you look at?

Little River's homes fall into five parts, and each gives you something the others do not. The numbers on the map match the entries below.

The old village and the harbors: all three marinas within a mile

This is the part to choose if the boat comes first. The waterfront at the end of Mineola Avenue, its restaurants and all three marinas with deeded slips are within about a mile of the village center. At Mariner's Pointe, the condo buildings ring the slip basin.

Golf is here too. Hidden Lakes Village borders the River Hills golf course, and Eastport's homes line a course beside Coquina Harbour. Cypress Village, with houses and townhouses, is the largest subdivision in this part.

Homes in Cypress Village resold for about $385,000 at the middle in the past year, and in Hidden Lakes Village for about $318,000. Flood risk is highest at the water's edge: about half the condos at Mariner's Pointe and most homes at Eastport are in FEMA's high-risk zone.

North and east, toward Calabash: Heather Glen and Vereen Gardens

Heather Glen is the biggest neighborhood in Little River, with more than 1,100 houses north-east of the old village, and the busiest: more of its houses resold in the past year than in any other. Heather Lakes is east of it.

This is the side nearest Calabash, North Carolina. Vereen Memorial Historical Gardens is in this part too, between US 17 and the waterway, with its trails along the marsh.

Houses in Heather Glen and Heather Lakes usually resold for about $375,000. Windjammer Village, the third large neighborhood here, has some homes in the high-risk flood zone.

Get the homes for sale in east Little River

North-west, toward the Waccamaw: golf, dry ground and a river ramp

River Hills is built along the River Hills golf course. Lafayette Park has more than 400 houses on lots of about a quarter of an acre.

Worthams Ferry Landing, the county's ramp on the Waccamaw River, is north of this part, for a boat you keep on a trailer.

Houses resold for about $385,000 at the middle in River Hills and about $348,000 in Lafayette Park. The marinas are back toward the old village, a drive rather than a walk.

Get the homes for sale in north-west Little River

West, around the hospital: the landing and condos on the water

McLeod Seacoast hospital is in this part, about 6 minutes from the old village. T. Craig Campbell Landing, the county's ramp at the US 17 bridge, is at its south edge.

Carolina Yacht Landing, a condo community on the waterway west of that landing, has about half its units within about 50 feet of the water. Bay Tree, near the hospital, has condos and townhouses.

Among Little River subdivisions with at least five resales, the two highest prices are here: Sawgrass Village at Bridgewater, at about $585,000, and Waterside Village, at about $560,000. Condos at Carolina Yacht Landing resold for about $252,000.

Get the homes for sale in west Little River

Far west, at SC 9: a shorter drive to Myrtle Beach

This part suits a buyer who drives south to Myrtle Beach often. SC 31, the Carolina Bays Parkway, starts at its edge. From its subdivisions, Coastal Grand Mall in Myrtle Beach is about 30 to 35 minutes by car and the airport about 35 to 40, a few minutes sooner than from the old village.

It is the part farthest from the water: the old village's waterfront is about 12 to 15 minutes back east by car, and McLeod Seacoast hospital about 9. Its three largest subdivisions, Hidden Brooke, Palm Lakes Plantation and Waterfall, are neighborhoods of houses.

In the past year, houses resold for about $340,000 at the middle in Palm Lakes Plantation, $390,000 in Hidden Brooke and $555,000 in Waterfall.

Does Little River flood?

Most of it does not. About 96 in 100 Little River homes are outside FEMA's high-risk flood zone. Homes right by the water are the exception: close to half of them are inside it.

Hurricane Hazel came ashore near Little River, at the North Carolina and South Carolina line, in October 1954 as a Category 4 hurricane. It hit at the year's highest tide, and the storm surge reached as high as 18 feet at Calabash.

Few houses for sale at our last count were in the high-risk zone. We check the flood map, the elevation and the seller's flood disclosure for every house before you make an offer. Our page on how to find out if a house flooded shows every record we use.

Do you need flood insurance in Little River?

A lender must require flood insurance when the building is in FEMA's high-risk zone, and can ask for it anywhere.

In the Little River ZIP, a flood policy on a house outside the zone usually costs about $480 a year, and inside it about $1,250. A new policy starts 30 days after you apply and pay, unless you buy it with your home loan.

What does it cost to own a home in Little River each year?

A typical resold house you live in costs about $3,000 to $4,300 a year in property tax, a flood policy and homeowners insurance, before wind and hail cover.

What do homes cost in Little River, SC?

A resold house in Little River usually sold for about $357,000 in the past year. Half sold for between about $304,000 and $451,000. New houses from builders sold for less in the middle.

What kind of homes are here

About two in three homes in the Little River ZIP are houses, and more than a quarter are condos.

Room to spread out is uncommon. Fewer than one in twenty houses sits on an acre or more, and the typical house lot is about a fifth of an acre.

Homes for sale now

About 44 of the houses for sale were new construction, and 22 asked under $300,000.

What do homes rent for in Little River?

HUD's Small Area Fair Market Rent for a three-bedroom in the Little River ZIP is $2,100 a month, with utilities. That is above North Myrtle Beach's ZIP and Myrtle Beach's, and close to Carolina Forest's.

Rents people pay here are higher than the county's: the Census survey's middle rent in Little River is about $1,530 a month, against about $1,260 countywide, apartments included.

A worked example

Take a typical resold house. At HUD's three-bedroom figure of $2,100 a month, a year comes to about $25,000, about 7 percent of the price. As a rental, the house pays about $4,400 a year in property tax, about 17 percent of that rent.

Insurance, upkeep, management and empty months come out of the rent too. Our rental analyzer works them out for any address.

Short-term rentals

Little River has no city ordinance, so county rules apply. A home in Little River outside North Myrtle Beach that is rented short term needs:

  • a Horry County business license;
  • a county hospitality and accommodations account;
  • a South Carolina sales and accommodations tax account;
  • a yearly county personal property return for its furnishings, due April 30.

An HOA's rules can forbid short stays, and we read them first. See the short-term rental rules by town and what a nightly rental earns by town.

Is the Carolina Bays Parkway being extended through Little River?

The extension is planned but not built. The Carolina Bays Parkway, SC 31, would be extended from SC 9 north-east, passing north and northwest of Little River's old village. It would cross the state line to US 17 near Shallotte, North Carolina. It would be a multi-lane freeway, partly on new ground.

South Carolina's part is in SCDOT's current program. North Carolina has not funded land buying or building for its part. The whole project is estimated at $797 million.

The two states have named a preferred route. The final choice comes after they review the comments from the public hearings. If you like a house in the north-west part, we show you where it sits against the preferred route's map.

What are the pros and cons of living in Little River?

The good points are the water, the price and a year-round town. The hard ones are the few houses on the water, flooding at the water's edge, and the drive to the airport.

Pros

  • Deeded boat slips you can own, and public ramps on the waterway and the Waccamaw River.
  • No city tax, golf in the neighborhoods, and the beach about 11 minutes away.
  • A year-round community, with the waterfront, the Blue Crab Festival, the swing bridge and Vereen Gardens.

Cons

  • Few houses are on the water, and those cost far more. We pair a house with a deeded slip instead.
  • Homes right at the water's edge have the most flood risk. We check the flood map and the elevation first.
  • The airport is about 41 minutes away by car.

Common questions

Little River, SC FAQ

Is Little River, SC a city, and what county is it in?

Little River is not a city. It is an unincorporated community in Horry County, South Carolina, at the North Carolina line, and the county runs it.

What is there to do in Little River, SC?

Most of Little River's life is on the water: the docks and restaurants on the Mineola Avenue waterfront, and public ramps on the waterway and the Waccamaw River. On land there is golf in the neighborhoods, the trails at Vereen Memorial Historical Gardens and the Blue Crab Festival each May.

How far is Little River from Myrtle Beach?

About 36 minutes by car to Coastal Grand Mall in Myrtle Beach, without traffic. Barefoot Landing, in North Myrtle Beach, is about 18 minutes, and the airport about 41.

Does Little River, SC flood?

Most of it does not: about 96 in 100 homes are outside FEMA's high-risk flood zone. The risk is at the water's edge, along the Intracoastal Waterway.

What is the property tax in Little River, SC?

A home you live in pays only the county's property tax, about $1,400 a year on a typical resold house, with no city tax on top. A second home or rental is taxed at a higher rate.

How much is a boat slip in Little River?

Deeded slips at Mariner's Pointe, most about 30 to 35 feet long, sold for $10,000 to $38,000 in the past year, each on its own deed, without a condo. Bigger slips at Dock Holidays, across the line in North Myrtle Beach, sold for $65,000 to $115,000.

Is Little River, SC safe?

We do not rate any place as safe or unsafe. Reported crime is published by police agency, and we pull the figures for the area around any house you ask about.

Which schools serve Little River, SC?

Horry County Schools assigns schools by address, in nine attendance areas, and none is named Little River. We look up the assigned schools for any house you like.
