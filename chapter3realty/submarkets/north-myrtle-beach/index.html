North Myrtle Beach, SC

North Myrtle Beach real estate: nine miles of beach, eight different markets.
A dock behind the house in Cherry Grove, shag nights on Main Street, golf across the waterway.

  • BeachAbout 9 miles
  • Channel housesAbout 680

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

Its own city at the north end of the Grand Strand, made from four beach towns in 1968, with its own rental rules and city tax.

The short answer

North Myrtle Beach has about nine miles of beach and eight parts, each suited to a different buyer. For a boat behind the house, look in Cherry Grove. For Main Street and a maritime forest, look in Ocean Drive. For a house near the ocean for less, look in Crescent Beach or Windy Hill. For newer houses and little flood zone, look across the waterway or west of US 17.

A typical resold house sold for about $565,000 in the past year, from about $410,000 in Crescent Beach to $725,000 in Tilghman Beach. Nightly rentals need a yearly city business license, with no special rental zoning or permit.

Which part of North Myrtle Beach should you look in?

The city has eight parts a buyer can tell apart, and each gives you something the others do not. Five run along the ocean; the other three lie inland. Under each one are the homes for sale there now.

On the ocean, from north to south

  • Cherry GroveA dock behind the house, and a pier at sunrise
  • Tilghman BeachHouses by the ocean, most of them the owner's main home
  • Ocean DriveMain Street and its shag clubs
  • Crescent BeachA house near the ocean for less, with little flood zone
  • Atlantic BeachA separate town, not part of the city
  • Windy HillBeach houses at some of the lower prices by the ocean

Inland

  • Little River NeckWoods and trail rides north of the Cherry Grove marsh

Cherry Grove: a dock behind the house, and a pier at sunrise

Cherry Grove is the part for a boat at the back door. Channels were dug into its marsh in the 1970s, and about 680 houses now back onto them or the marsh edge. Many have a dock behind the house, and boats reach the ocean through Hog Inlet.

From the city's boat ramp on 53rd Avenue North, you can paddle a kayak about a mile and a half through Hog Inlet, past pelicans and ospreys, to the empty beach on Waties Island. People walk the shore there looking for shells and fossil shark teeth, including megalodon teeth.

Walk 985 feet out on Cherry Grove Pier to the two-story deck at the end, where people watch the sun rise over the ocean and look for dolphins, then eat at the Driftwood restaurant on the pier. In 1964 a fisherman landed a 1,780-pound tiger shark from this pier, and the pier still lists it as a world record.

On the Fourth of July, the city puts on a free fireworks show over the ocean at the pier. People carry beach chairs onto the sand to watch it, after checking the tide. Heritage Shores Nature Preserve has walking paths on an island in the marsh, and the Russell Burgess Coastal Preserve has a boardwalk.

A house on the channels resold for about $715,000 at the middle (38 sales), and elsewhere in Cherry Grove about $665,000. The trade-off is flood insurance: almost every house here is in FEMA's high-risk flood zone, channel houses included. Only about 1 in 4 houses is the owner's main home.

Tilghman Beach: houses by the ocean, most of them main homes

Tilghman Beach suits a buyer who wants a house by the ocean among owners who make it their main home. About 2 in 3 houses here are, the most of any beach part.

From about May to mid-August, loggerhead turtles about three feet long come up this sand at night and lay about 120 eggs each. Volunteers from the Sea Turtle Patrol watch over and protect the nests, and 45 to 65 days later the hatchlings dig out at night and head for the moonlit water.

Tilghman Beach grew from one large tract that Charles T. Tilghman Jr. developed in 1948. Its houses sell for the most of any part. In Tilghman Estates, its largest neighborhood, the middle of 19 house resales was about $1.05 million. Seabrook Plantation resold for about $750,000, the middle of 9 sales.

Most of its homes in the flood zone are condos, not houses.

Ocean Drive: Main Street and its shag clubs

Ocean Drive is the part with Main Street, which runs back from the ocean. McLean Park, built around a small lake, is about a 5-minute walk from it.

The shag is South Carolina's state dance. In the old beach towns of Ocean Drive and Crescent Beach, a pavilion and amusement park drew crowds to dance it.

It is still danced on Main Street, all year:

  • Every night. At Fat Harold's Beach Club, a DJ plays beach music from 7. Couples take shag lessons on Monday nights, and Tuesday night is for learning the state dance.
  • January, April and September. Thousands of shag dancers fill the clubs on Main Street for the Society of Stranders: Midwinter Break, Spring Safari and Fall Migration.
  • March. People set up beach chairs and golf carts along Main Street by 8:30 a.m. for a St. Patrick's Day parade of more than 100 entries, then stay for a free festival until 4.
  • November. The Carolina Beach Music Awards weekend brings beach music bands to the stages of the shag clubs.

The Ingram Dunes Nature Preserve, which the city calls its last maritime forest, has dunes 30 feet tall and a half-mile trail. Houses in the Ingram Beach neighborhood resold for about $1.35 million at the middle (12 sales).

Prices here spread wide: the middle half of house resales ran from about $385,000 to $980,000. Most of its homes in the flood zone are oceanfront condos; about 1 in 10 houses is in it.

Crescent Beach: a house near the ocean for less

Crescent Beach suits a buyer who wants a house near the ocean at a lower price. Its houses resold for the least of the beach parts, and only about 1 house in 100 is in the flood zone, the fewest of the beach parts.

The loggerheads nest on this beach too, and the Sea Turtle Patrol's volunteers watch over the nests until the hatchlings head for the water.

Houses on the original Crescent Beach lots resold for more. The county records those lots under two names, and their middle prices were about $665,000 and $718,000.

The 18th and Edge pocket park is its own small green space. Crescent Beach ends at Atlantic Beach, a separate town that did not join the city.

Windy Hill: beach houses south of Atlantic Beach

Windy Hill suits a buyer who wants a beach house at one of the lower prices by the ocean. Local history says it got its name in 1791, when George Washington rode this stretch of beach on a windy day and his hat kept blowing off.

Investors from Conway built it up after World War II. Houses on the original Windy Hill Beach lots resold for about $439,000 at the middle (10 sales). Most of its houses are outside the flood zone; most of its condos are in it.

Yow Park and the 41st Avenue South pocket park are its neighborhood parks.

Little River Neck: woods and trail rides north of the marsh

Little River Neck suits a buyer who wants a house among owners who live there. It lies north of the Cherry Grove marsh, along Little River Neck Road.

Inlet Point Plantation Stables is two miles down a one-lane dirt road through the woods. From there, an hour-long trail ride passes deer, wild turkeys and an alligator sunning by a lake, follows the Intracoastal Waterway across from Calabash, and ends at the marsh behind Waties Island.

The Open Space Institute has bought more than 600 acres on and around Waties Island and the mainland at Little River Neck. The state plans to make parts of it a new state park and parts a Heritage Trust preserve, open to the public.

Tidewater Plantation is its largest neighborhood, and about 8 in 10 of its houses are the owner's main home. Builders are still selling new houses here: 53 in the past year. The Neck also holds most of the city's manufactured homes, about 660 of 800.

West of US 17: newer houses a few minutes from the beach

West of US 17 suits a buyer who wants a newer house close to the beach, with little flood zone. From a typical house here, the drive to Ocean Boulevard is about 4 minutes.

Every Monday night in summer, Barefoot Landing, on the waterway at US 17, has a fireworks show at 10 p.m.

Builders sold 94 new houses here in the past year, and the city's 20-acre Central Park is in this part. Chestnut Greens is the largest neighborhood, and its houses resold for about $515,000 at the middle.

Sunset Harbour resold for about $760,000 (9 sales), but 68 of its homes are in the high-risk flood zone. Condos here cost the least in the city, about $175,000 at the middle.

Across the waterway: golf, newer houses and almost no flood zone

Across the Intracoastal Waterway suits a buyer who wants golf, a newer house and almost no flood zone: 2 homes of about 5,200 are in it. More of the city's new houses sold here than anywhere else.

Barefoot Resort's four golf courses were designed by Greg Norman, Davis Love III, Tom Fazio and Pete Dye. Seven holes of the Norman course run along the waterway, and the resort has a deep-water marina with a bar and grill on the water.

Barefoot Landing, the shops and restaurants on the waterway at US 17, is about 11 minutes by car from Main Street. From a typical house here, the drive to Ocean Boulevard is about 11 minutes.

Houses in Grande Dunes North resold for about $374,000 at the middle, and in Waterside for about $385,000. We pull the HOA papers and fees for any house here.

What is North Myrtle Beach like to live in?

North Myrtle Beach has about nine miles of ocean beach, running south-west from Hog Inlet. Cherry Grove, Ocean Drive, Crescent Beach and Windy Hill voted to become one city in 1968.

From the 1970s, condo and hotel towers began replacing houses on the oceanfront. Today condos outnumber houses about 3 to 2.

About 18,800 people lived in the city at the 2020 Census, about 37 percent more than in 2010. Close to half its homes were kept for seasonal or vacation use. Our Little River guide compares that share with the towns nearby.

How is North Myrtle Beach different from Myrtle Beach?

North Myrtle Beach is not part of Myrtle Beach. It is a separate city in Horry County, with its own city tax and its own rules for nightly rentals. The Myrtle Beach oceanfront, at the SkyWheel, is about 30 minutes south by car without traffic.

The map shows the drives from Main Street to the pier, McLeod Seacoast hospital and Barefoot Landing. The airport is about 35 minutes away and Grand Strand Medical Center about 20. Summer traffic on US 17 makes those drives longer.

What do homes cost in North Myrtle Beach?

A typical resold house in North Myrtle Beach sold for about $565,000 in the past year; half sold between about $420,000 and $740,000. A condo sold for about $310,000, and a new house from a builder for about $440,000.

Condos sell for about the same in every part along the ocean, about $310,000 to $360,000. Houses differ by part far more. More than half the homes sold here in the past year went to a buyer whose tax bill goes to another state.

Does North Myrtle Beach flood?

About 1 in 3 homes in North Myrtle Beach is in FEMA's high-risk flood zone, more of them condos than houses. The part you choose decides most of it.

The high-risk zone has two kinds here. The VE zone is the oceanfront, where storm waves add to the flood; the A zones are the rest of it.

Do you need flood insurance in North Myrtle Beach?

A federally regulated lender must require flood insurance when the building is in FEMA's high-risk zone, and any lender can ask for it elsewhere. In ZIP code 29582, which covers most of the city, a federal flood policy on a house usually cost:

Can you rent out a home by the night in North Myrtle Beach?

Yes, with a city business license. Every short-term rental in the city needs one, renewed each year, and must collect lodging taxes. The city has no special zoning or rental permit beyond the license.

An HOA or the deed can still bar rentals, and we read the HOA's rules and the deed before you offer. A rental owner here has five things to get right:

  1. The license. A city business license, renewed every year.
  2. The parking number. When you apply or renew, you report the parking spaces on the property's own land. That number is the most cars guests can park there. It goes in every ad and rental agreement and is posted inside. Where an HOA or the deed limits parking, the owner certifies the number matches.
  3. The lodging taxes. Guests pay 12 percent on a stay under 90 days, split between the state, the city and the county.
  4. The trash days. The city collects a rental's trash on its regular day plus Friday, Saturday and Sunday. Recycling goes out only on the regular day.
  5. The quiet hours. They run from 11 p.m. to 7 a.m., and fireworks are banned everywhere in the city, with a $310 ticket for a first offense.
Example. On a $2,000 week, a guest pays about $240 in lodging taxes, which the owner collects and pays to the state, the city and the county.

Is North Myrtle Beach changing its rental rules?

The city has held workshops and public sessions on possible new short-term rental rules. Its rental page lists no new rental ordinance adopted, and the license rule is the one in force. Earlier tries, a moratorium on large rental houses and a draft ordinance, failed.

What are the pros and cons of living in North Myrtle Beach?

The good points are the beach, the water, the golf and the choice of parts. The hard ones are the flood zone at the north end, the condo fees, and how few houses by the ocean are main homes.

Pros

  • About nine miles of beach, Main Street at Ocean Drive, and the marsh parks and boat ramp at Cherry Grove.
  • Houses on the Cherry Grove channels, with water behind the house.
  • Four golf courses at Barefoot Resort, across the waterway.
  • Almost no flood zone across the waterway, and about 7 in 100 homes west of US 17.

Cons

  • Cherry Grove is almost all in the high-risk flood zone, and its flood policies cost more. We check the flood zone, the elevation and the flood history before you offer.
  • Most houses by the ocean are second homes or rentals, and fewer neighbors live there all year. Inland, about 3 in 4 houses are the owner's main home.
  • Condo fees are high: the middle condo for sale lists about $700 a month. We read the condo's budget and rules before you offer.
  • Summer traffic on US 17 slows every drive. We time the drives from any house you like.

Common questions

North Myrtle Beach FAQ

Is North Myrtle Beach part of Myrtle Beach?

No. North Myrtle Beach is its own city in Horry County, South Carolina. Four beach towns joined to form it in 1968, and it has its own city tax and rental rules. The Myrtle Beach oceanfront is about 30 minutes south by car.

What are the sections of North Myrtle Beach?

The four old beach towns are Cherry Grove, Ocean Drive, Crescent Beach and Windy Hill. Tilghman Beach, Little River Neck, the mainland west of US 17 and the neighborhoods across the waterway make eight parts.

Can you drive a golf cart in North Myrtle Beach?

Yes, from sunrise to sunset, on city streets posted 35 mph or less, with a state permit sticker from the DMV and a licensed driver. A cart may cross Ocean Boulevard, 27th Avenue South, US 17 and Sea Mountain Highway, but not drive along them.

Are dogs allowed on the beach in North Myrtle Beach?

Yes, on a leash. From May 15 to Labor Day they must be off the beach from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. In that season the beach takes umbrellas only, no tents or canopies.

What does it cost to own a home in North Myrtle Beach each year?

As your home, before any mortgage, about $8,400 for a typical house and about $11,000 for a typical condo. That covers tax, insurance, a flood policy outside the zone, the condo fee and upkeep.

What is the property tax in North Myrtle Beach?

On a house the county values at $565,000, about $2,500 a year as your home and about $7,500 as a second home or rental.

Is North Myrtle Beach safe?

We do not rate any place as safe or unsafe. We pull the reported crime figures for the area around any house you ask about.

Which schools serve North Myrtle Beach?

Horry County Schools assigns schools by address. North Myrtle Beach is one of its nine attendance areas, with North Myrtle Beach High School, North Myrtle Beach Middle School and Ocean Drive Elementary. We look up the assigned schools for any house you like.
