Community guide · verified July 2026

Carolina Forest real estate
and community guide.

By Devin Day, Operations Officer & licensed MLO · Reviewed by Timmy Fredrick Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Published July 20, 2026

The Myrtle Beach area's biggest master-planned community: 26,000+ residents, eleven major subdivisions, the county's flagship school zone, no city tax, and no wind-pool insurance surcharge. Here is the whole picture, subdivision by subdivision, with sources.

The direct answer

What Carolina Forest is, and why relocating buyers choose it

Carolina Forest is unincorporated Horry County between US 501 and International Drive, master-planned by International Paper in the late 1990s on part of its old 30,000-acre timber tract. It is not a city and pays no city taxes; the mailing address says Myrtle Beach, ZIP 29579, and the beach is a 10-to-15-minute drive. The 2020 Census counted 23,342 residents; the latest Census estimate puts it near 26,263, and 14.5 percent of residents moved in within the past year, roughly ten points above the state's migration rate.

The draw is a combination, not a single feature: the county's flagship school zone, twenty years of continuous new construction, single-family prices well below beach product, no municipal millage, and, because it sits west of the Intracoastal Waterway, standard homeowner insurance that still includes wind. Most of our relocating clients tour it first, and many stop looking after.

The market

Carolina Forest home prices in 2026

The two best reads, each with its own lens: Redfin's Carolina Forest page showed a median sale price of $418,000, up 7.7 percent year over year, with homes going under contract in about 105 days as of mid-2026. SC Realtors data for ZIP 29579 showed a $464,710 single-family median in December 2025, up from $426,500 a year earlier, on 129 days of market time. Condos along the corridor, the area's entry tier, ran a median near $215,500, up about 17 percent.

Context makes those numbers make sense: the Horry County median sits near $329,000 and the City of Myrtle Beach near $266,000, but both are dragged down by condo-heavy stock. Carolina Forest trades at a premium to the county because it is almost entirely single-family product in amenity communities, and local press has described it as a seller's market even while beach condos favor buyers. Trailing twelve months, roughly 1,330 homes changed hands here. Current numbers for any specific community: ask, and see the monthly market report for the region-wide picture.

Schools

Carolina Forest schools and attendance zones

The attendance area belongs to Horry County Schools: Carolina Forest High School, Ten Oaks and Ocean Bay middle schools, and five elementary schools. Carolina Forest High enrolls about 3,181 students, the fifth-largest public high school in South Carolina, ranked 33rd in the state by U.S. News, with 80 percent math and 91 percent reading proficiency against state averages of 43 and 52.

The growth showed up in the classrooms first: by 2024, Carolina Forest Elementary ran at 136 percent of capacity, Ocean Bay at 144, and River Oaks at 166. The district's answer opened in August 2025: Ten Oaks Elementary ($62.3 million) and Pine Island Elementary ($65.7 million), 1,200 seats each, with 1,920 students reassigned to fill them. Which school serves which subdivision is set by the district's attendance maps, and lines just moved, so we check the current district maps on every showing rather than trusting a listing's claim.

Districtwide, Horry County Schools posted 38 of 57 schools rated Excellent or Good on the 2025 state report cards, with none Unsatisfactory, and an 89.6 percent graduation rate.

Costs and risk

Carolina Forest property taxes, flood zones, and wind insurance

Taxes. Carolina Forest's 2025 levy totals about 201.0 mills: county base 52.1, schools 119.1, fire district 21.7, solid waste 8.1, plus an $89 stormwater fee and a $50 per-vehicle road fee. There is no city millage; Myrtle Beach city residents pay 83.4 additional mills for comparable houses. Owner-occupants at the 4 percent rate are also exempt from the 109.1 school-operating mills, which is most of the bill. Exact numbers for any price: our Horry County property tax calculator.

Flood. Much of the area maps outside FEMA high-risk zones, with Zone AE pockets near ponds and wetlands, so we pull the parcel-level map, in effect since December 16, 2021, on every home. A pond view and a flood requirement are sometimes the same lot; our flood history guide covers how we check what maps do not show.

Wind. The state wind pool's Horry County territory runs east of the Intracoastal Waterway's west bank and east of US 17 further south. Carolina Forest sits entirely west of the waterway, outside that territory, so wind coverage stays inside a standard homeowner policy instead of a separate wind-pool policy. Against a beach address, that is a real annual saving; the full comparison is in our coastal insurance guide.

Renting

Carolina Forest rental rules: long-term leases, not short-term

Horry County has no ordinance against short-term rentals in unincorporated areas, but in Carolina Forest the HOA is the binding rulebook, and the HOAs mostly say no: Avalon's recorded rules prohibit any rental under three months, and communities like Waterbridge are reported to require one-year minimum leases. Buy here for long-term tenants, not nightly guests; the county-by-county legal map is in our short-term rental rules guide.

As a long-term rental play, the corridor works: three-bedroom houses list roughly $1,650 to $2,500 a month against an area three-bedroom average near $1,941, tenant demand follows the school zones, and entry condos in the $200,000s offer the highest yields. Run any address through the rental analyzer before you offer.

Growth

Carolina Forest growth: roads, schools, and new development

The two long-running road complaints are resolved: the $54.7 million Carolina Forest Boulevard widening is complete, four lanes with a multi-use path, and the Postal Way extension is open. The RIDE 4 penny tax that took effect May 1, 2025 funds the next round, including an SC 31 interchange at Augusta Plantation Drive. County planners have also approved a 1,154-unit mixed-use village concept for the area, apartments over retail in the style of Market Common; its construction timing is not yet set.

The area's defining 2025 event was the Covington Drive Fire: 2,059 acres over 95 days, the longest-burning wildfire in South Carolina history, which forced evacuations in eight neighborhoods and destroyed zero homes. It reshaped how this market thinks about defensible space and insurance, and it is part of every honest conversation we have about buying along the preserve edges.

Common questions

Carolina Forest FAQ

Is Carolina Forest part of Myrtle Beach?

No. It is unincorporated Horry County with a Myrtle Beach mailing address and ZIP 29579, sitting between Myrtle Beach and Conway. That distinction is worth money: homeowners pay no city property tax, while City of Myrtle Beach residents pay an additional 83.4 municipal mills.

What schools serve Carolina Forest?

Horry County Schools' Carolina Forest attendance area: Carolina Forest High School, Ten Oaks and Ocean Bay middle schools, and five elementary schools, including Ten Oaks Elementary and Pine Island Elementary, which both opened in August 2025 with 1,200 seats each. Carolina Forest High enrolls about 3,181 students, the fifth-largest high school in South Carolina, ranked 33rd in the state by U.S. News.

How far is Carolina Forest from the beach?

About 10 to 15 minutes by car to Grand Strand beaches using US 501, SC 31, or International Drive, depending on the neighborhood and the season. It is the trade the area is built on: newer construction and bigger lots than the beach, a short drive from it.

Is Carolina Forest in a flood zone?

Much of it maps outside FEMA's high-risk zones, but pockets of Zone AE exist near ponds and wetlands, so the parcel matters more than the neighborhood. Check any address against the county flood maps in effect since December 16, 2021. Carolina Forest also sits west of the Intracoastal Waterway, outside South Carolina's wind pool territory, so wind coverage stays inside a standard homeowner policy.

Can you Airbnb a house in Carolina Forest?

Usually not. Horry County does not prohibit short-term rentals in unincorporated areas, but most Carolina Forest HOAs do: Avalon's recorded rules prohibit any rental under three months, and several communities require one-year minimum leases. Treat it as a long-term rental market and verify the covenants of the specific community before buying for rental income.

What do homes cost in Carolina Forest?

Recent single-family medians run about $418,000 per Redfin's mid-2026 snapshot and $464,710 per SC Realtors data for ZIP 29579 in December 2025, against a roughly $329,000 Horry County median. Condos in the corridor had a median near $215,500. Entry points run from the $200,000s in older condo sections to over $1 million on the Intracoastal.
