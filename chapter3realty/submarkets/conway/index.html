Conway, SC

Conway, SC: the Grand Strand's inland value market.
A river town about 25 minutes from the beach.

  • Beach25 min drive
  • Airport27 min drive
  • UniversityIn the city
  • Hospital10 min drive

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

In Conway you get Main Street and the Riverwalk downtown, a university and a hospital inside the city, and the biggest lots at its north edge.

The short answer

Conway is a river town with an old downtown on the Waccamaw River, a university and a hospital inside the city, and the beach about 25 minutes away. A house here resells for about $60,000 less than across Horry County.

Most homes with a Conway mailing address are outside the city limits. Only a few places are in the flood maps, near the river, Crabtree Swamp and Kingston Lake. The two maps are FEMA's high-risk flood zone and the county's map of where Hurricane Florence's floodwater reached in 2018.

What is it like to live in Conway, SC?

Conway is a small city on the Waccamaw River, said WAH-kuh-maw. Much of downtown was built in the early 1900s, after a fire, and its streets have old live oaks.

The Riverwalk is a path of about 1.5 miles along the river, with benches and a kayak launch. It starts about 3 minutes by car from the center of town. At Riverfront Park beside it are a public boat landing with floating docks, a playground built like an old river steamboat, and rocking chairs under a gazebo.

Conway was laid out in 1732, as Kingston, and later named Conwayborough for General Robert Conway of the state militia.

City Hall on Main Street was finished in 1825 as the county courthouse. Its design is credited to Robert Mills, who designed the Washington Monument.

Coastal Carolina University is inside the city, about 10 minutes from downtown. It enrolled more than 11,000 students in fall 2024, a record for the school. Horry-Georgetown Technical College has its Conway campus on US 501, and Conway Medical Center is about 10 minutes from downtown.

The city is growing fast. By Census estimates it grew about 22 percent since 2020, the seventh fastest among South Carolina cities and towns of 10,000 people or more.

The beach is about 25 minutes away by car, and the airport about 27.

Is a Conway address inside the city of Conway?

Usually not. Only a little more than 1 in 4 homes with a Conway mailing address is inside the city limits. The rest are in the county, outside any city.

The same is true of homes for sale. At our last count, only about 1 in 4 houses for sale with a Conway address was inside the city.

What the city line changes

Inside the city, the property tax is about $670 a year more on a typical house you live in. The City of Conway picks up garbage, recycling and yard waste at the curb once a week for homes in the city, and the city has its own police and fire departments.

Outside the city there is no city pickup. Homes in the county, outside any city, are the only ones allowed to use the county's 25 recycling and convenience centers, where owners bring their own trash.

Which part of Conway should you look at?

The city's homes fall into five parts. Each has something the others do not. The numbers on the map match the entries below. Each price is what a house or townhouse there usually resold for in the past year, from county deeds.

1Downtown: a walk to Main Street and the river

Downtown is the old town on the river. Main Street, City Hall, the Riverwalk and Riverfront Park are all here. Kingston Park on Main Street has shaded tables and a fountain, and the Garden Walk off Third Avenue is a walkway with music and lights in the evening.

Downtown here means the streets within about a mile of the center of town, on house lots of about a third of an acre. Snowhill, Mayfair, Buckwood, Highland Park and Collinswood are the largest subdivisions. Few homes here come up for sale. Those that did resold for about $275,000.

Get the homes for sale in Downtown Conway

2Near the university, off US 501: the university and the hospital

Along US 501 toward the beach, the city reaches past Coastal Carolina University. Conway Medical Center, on Singleton Ridge Road, and the Conway campus of Horry-Georgetown Technical College are in this part too.

Wild Wing, Coastal Point West, Kiskadee Parke and Aviary Village are the largest subdivisions, with about 1,430 homes in all, on lots of about four-tenths of an acre. Homes here resold for about $360,000. Only the north edge was higher.

Get the homes for sale in Conway near the university

3West and south: the most homes, three city parks and the lowest prices

West and south of downtown, toward US 378 and US 701 South, is the largest part of the city. The Billy Gardner Recreation Complex on Mill Pond Road holds the Conway Recreation Center, with an indoor pool and walking track, plus ball fields and a playground built for children of all abilities. Smith Jones Park has an outdoor pool, and Collins Park has tennis and pickleball courts.

Rosehaven, New Castle, Oak Glenn, Woodland Lakes and Jasmine Woods are the largest subdivisions, on lots of about a quarter of an acre. Homes here resold for about $245,000, the lowest of the five parts.

Get the homes for sale in West Conway

4Around Crabtree Swamp: north and northwest of downtown, with a park on Sherwood Drive

North and northwest of downtown, around Crabtree Swamp, are about 2,390 homes, most of them 2 to 4 miles out. Sherwood Park on Sherwood Drive has a playground and a sand volleyball court, and across the street a nine-hole disc golf course inside a walking track.

Midtown Village, Rivertown Row, Elmhurst, Woodcreek, Kingston Bay, Sherwood and Country Club Forest are the largest subdivisions. The lots are the smallest in the city, about a fifth of an acre, and homes resold for about $260,000.

Get the homes for sale in Conway near Crabtree Swamp

5The north edge: the biggest lots in the city

At the city's north edge, 4 to 5 miles from downtown, a typical lot is about three-quarters of an acre, the largest in the city. Ridgewood West, Country Manor and Maple Hill are the largest subdivisions, with about 560 homes in all.

Few homes resold here, and the middle price was about $400,000, the highest of the five parts.

Get the homes for sale in North Conway

Does Conway, SC flood?

A few parts do. About 95 in 100 homes in the city are outside both FEMA's high-risk zone and the land Florence's water reached in 2018. The rest are near the river, Crabtree Swamp and Kingston Lake.

By part of town, none of the homes near the university are in either flood map. Downtown and at the north edge, about 3 in 100 homes are in one. West and south, it is about 5 in 100, and around Crabtree Swamp about 1 in 10.

The worst flood on record came after Hurricane Florence in 2018. The river gauge at the Conway Marina read about 21 feet, its highest ever. That is the height of the river on the gauge, not the depth of water on a street. Major flooding starts at 14 feet.

Why the flood comes late and stays

The flood here comes down the river, days after the storm. Florence came ashore in North Carolina, and the river at Conway was highest 12 days later.

During Florence, the county closed 58 streets in and at the edge of the city for flooding. Long Avenue, Third Avenue, Church Street and Marina Drive stayed closed about four weeks.

How often it happens

Of the 14 floods on record that reached major flood stage at Conway, 6 came in the last 11 years.

For Florence, flood insurance paid 109 claims in the city, and the middle claim was about $134,000. After Hurricane Matthew, the city used FEMA grants to buy 86 buildings. Land bought this way must stay open space.

Do you need flood insurance in Conway, and what does it cost?

Federal law makes a lender require it only when the house is in FEMA's high-risk zone. A lender can still ask for it anywhere.

Outside the high-risk zone, a flood policy on a house usually costs about $560 to $680 a year, depending on the ZIP code.

A new federal flood policy takes effect 30 days after the application and payment are complete, unless it is bought when a home loan is made. Buy it with the house, not when a storm is coming.

South Carolina taxes a home you live in on 4 percent of its value, and a second home or rental on 6 percent. You must apply for the lower rate. Inside the city, that makes a rental's bill about 2.5 times a home's. Our property tax page shows how to apply.

What do homes cost in Conway, SC?

A house or townhouse that resold inside the city in the past year usually went for about $265,000. Half sold for between about $220,000 and $340,000.

At that price, a house in the city is usually three bedrooms and about 1,400 square feet. That is what we found among the houses for sale inside the city near that price at our last count, about 3 in 4 of them with three bedrooms. Some were new, built in the last two years.

Builders sold about 120 new homes in the city in the same year, for about $295,000 in the middle. Few resales were condos, and those went for about $150,000.

Conway is not the cheapest town in the county. Homes in Loris and Aynor, two smaller inland towns, resold for less.

At our last count, about 670 houses were for sale with a Conway address, more than half of them new construction, and about 60 on an acre or more. Our no-HOA homes guide and most affordable places include Conway.

What do homes rent for in Conway?

HUD's fair market rent for a three-bedroom in the Conway ZIP codes is $1,630 a month. HUD counts rent and utilities together, so the rent alone is less. The figure sits a little under the middle of the market.

What are the pros and cons of living in Conway?

The good points are the price, the downtown and the university. The hard ones are flooding in a few places, the city tax and the drive to the beach.

Pros

  • Homes cost less than in the county as a whole, and new homes and land are for sale.
  • A downtown on the river, with Main Street, the Riverwalk and Riverfront Park.
  • A university and the hospital inside the city, and city trash pickup each week.

Cons

  • A few parts flood, and the river stays high for weeks.
  • The city's property tax is higher than just outside it.
  • The beach is a drive of about 25 minutes, not a walk.

Common questions

Conway, SC FAQ

Is Conway, SC a good place to live?

For a buyer who wants a river town with a downtown and a university, at prices under the county's middle, yes. The trade is the drive: the beach is about 25 minutes away by car.

How far is Conway from Myrtle Beach?

About 25 minutes by car from downtown Conway to the beach in Myrtle Beach, without traffic, and about 27 minutes to Myrtle Beach International Airport.

Does Conway, SC flood?

In a few places. About 95 in 100 homes in the city are outside both FEMA's high-risk zone and the land Florence's water reached; most of the rest are near the Waccamaw River, Crabtree Swamp and Kingston Lake.

What is the property tax rate in Conway, SC?

Inside the city, a home you live in pays about $654 a year for every $100,000 of the county's value for it. Just outside the city, it pays about $368, plus an $89 county stormwater fee. A second home or rental pays about 2.5 times as much inside the city.

How many people live in Conway, SC?

About 30,600 in 2025, by the Census Bureau's estimate, up from 24,849 at the 2020 Census.

Is Conway, SC safe?

We do not rate any place as safe or unsafe. Inside the city limits, the City of Conway has its own police department and fire department.

Which schools serve Conway, SC?

Horry County Schools assigns schools by address, and one of its attendance areas is named Conway. We look up the assigned schools for any house you like.
