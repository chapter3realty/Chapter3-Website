Pawleys Island, SC

Pawleys Island real estate:
the upscale south end of the Strand.

  • Island housesAbout 480
  • Beach parkingFree
  • Town property taxNone

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

Pawleys Island is the south end of the Grand Strand: old beach houses on an island, with most homes that share its name on the mainland.

The short answer

Pawleys Island is a quiet beach town of about 480 houses on a barrier island, a narrow strip of land just off the coast. The ocean is in front, and a tidal creek and salt marsh are behind. The middle of the island is a historic district of old wooden summer houses, and beach parking is free.

Island houses rarely come up for sale: 10 sold at a market price in the past year, and over three years one sold for about $2.45 million at the middle. Most homes with a Pawleys Island address are on the mainland, where a house sold for about $580,000 to $675,000. Every island house but one is in a high-risk flood zone.

Is Pawleys Island an island, and what are its four parts like?

Yes. Pawleys Island is a barrier island, less than 4 miles long and mostly one house wide, with salt marsh between it and the mainland. Two short causeways, the North Causeway and the South Causeway, cross the marsh.

The Town of Pawleys Island is the island itself. The mainland around it has no town government; it is run by Georgetown County. Much of it shares the island's name and its ZIP code, 29585, so a Pawleys Island address can be a house on the mainland. This page covers four parts, from the beach inland.

Pawleys Island, the townThe beach in front, the creek behind

The island is one long strip of houses between the ocean and the creek. About 220 houses face the beach. The rest look onto the marsh and the creek, or stand on the streets between. In the middle of the island are the old wooden summer houses of the historic district.

The water is close on both sides. People fish in the surf and crab from the two marsh bridges, and the island has three boat ramps. Beach parking is free: the south-end lot is the largest free beach lot in Georgetown County, and 8 other public accesses add about 141 spaces. Dogs are allowed on the beach all year, on a leash on summer days.

The pace is slow. The speed limit is 25 mph, and golf carts are allowed on island roads. There are no lifeguards and no fireworks. The town still calls itself by its old nickname, "arrogantly shabby".

Over three years an island house sold for about $2.45 million at the middle, more on the ocean and less on the creek side. At our last count, only 2 island houses were for sale, each asking about $3 million or more.

LitchfieldThe beach north of the island, and more houses for sale

North of the island, Litchfield runs along the beach on the ocean side of the highway. It has about 1,200 houses, and more of them are lived in all year: about 4 in 10 are their owner's main home, twice the island's share.

  • A bike pathThe Waccamaw Neck Bikeway runs along US 17 through Litchfield and north to Murrells Inlet.
  • Huntington Beach State ParkFarther north: 3 miles of beach, and Atalaya, the old winter home of Archer and Anna Hyatt Huntington.
  • Brookgreen GardensOpposite the park. The Huntingtons set it up as a nature preserve to show her sculpture and the work of other sculptors.
  • A landmarkAtalaya and Brookgreen Gardens together are a National Historic Landmark.

More houses sell here: 50 in the past year, at about $1.25 million at the middle. Its oceanfront houses sell for more than the island's: about $3.6 million at the middle over three years. Almost all of Litchfield's ocean side is in a high-risk flood zone too.

The mainland behind the islandShops, groceries and golf near the causeways

Between US 17 and the marsh, south of Litchfield, are about 1,200 houses, and most are their owner's main home. This is where the island shops, a few minutes by car from Town Hall.

  • The Hammock ShopsOn the highway near the North Causeway: more than 20 shops and two restaurants, Local and BisQit.

The Pawleys Island hammockThe Hammock Shops take their name from the Original Hammock Shop, which has made them since 1938. They say a riverboat captain who carried rice between Georgetown and Waverly Mills made the first one in 1889, to sleep cooler on summer nights. It was woven of cotton rope, with no thick knots where he lay. The shops still sell handmade hammocks.

West of US 17Most of the homes, most of the golf, the lowest prices

West of US 17 are most of the homes with a Pawleys Island address: about 4,800 houses, and most are their owner's main home. Most of the area's golf courses are here. We count nine on the map, among them Caledonia, True Blue, Heritage and Litchfield Country Club.

What is it like to live on Pawleys Island all year?

Winter on the island is quiet. On Census Day, in April, most of the island's homes were seasonal or vacation homes. The town's lodging tax shows when guests come: in 2024, almost 6 in 10 of its dollars came in from July to September, and about 2 in 100 from January to March.

Winter days are mild.

  • About 57°January's normal high
  • About 37°January's normal low

Those are NOAA's normals at its weather station at Brookgreen Gardens, about 6 miles north. From October through April, dogs may run on the beach off the leash, if the owner carries one and the dog comes when called. Winter is also outside hurricane season, which runs from June 1 to November 30.

That makes winter the easiest time for work on a house: rentals are fewest and the storm season is over.

Behind the island and west of the highway, about 3 in 4 houses or more are their owner's main home. On the island about 1 in 5 is.

A South Carolina beach

The tax bills for most island houses go to South Carolina addresses: about a quarter to Pawleys Island itself, and many to Columbia, Greenville and Charleston. About 3 in 10 go out of state.

Where to eat

The town says island night life is dining out, long talks on screened porches, a mainland shop and a walk on the beach. On the map we count 20 restaurants from Litchfield south past the causeways, and 19 of them are within about 650 feet of US 17. Among them are the Pawleys Island Tavern, Frank's and Frank's Outback, and Local at the Hammock Shops.

How often does a house on Pawleys Island come up for sale?

Rarely. In the past five years at least 44 of the island's 480 houses were bought at a market price, about 1 in 11. In Litchfield and west of the highway, houses were bought about twice as often.

Island houses tend to stay in families. On most of them, the last recorded transfer was not a sale. The deed shows under $1,000: the kind of deed used to pass a house to heirs or a trust, or to move it into a company.

The town says many houses in its historic district belong to the third, fourth or fifth generation of one family. About 1 in 10 island houses has not changed hands at all since before 2000. The island is also built out: in seven years, only about a dozen new buildings went up.

What it means for you: the house you want can come up once in years.

Why do the old Pawleys Island houses look the way they do?

The old Pawleys Island houses were built as summer houses for rice planters' families, who came from May to November to escape the malaria of the rice fields. Enslaved people carried the households to the island each May, and enslaved workers built the older of the two causeways under a rice planter who became governor.

Cypress was the building material used most often. It was hewn on the mainland, and each timber was cut with Roman numerals so the house could be put together on the island. Most of the old houses stand on high brick foundations, which keep them above ordinary storm tides and let air move under the floor.

The middle of the island is on the National Register of Historic Places as the Pawleys Island Historic District.

The town's historic rules

In 2026 the whole island became a local historic district. A house built in 1954 or earlier can be put on the town's list of historic houses. The owner can nominate it, and so can a member of the town council or of the town's board, so a house can be nominated without its owner asking. The council decides.

A listed house needs the board's approval for changes to its outside and to be torn down, and the board can delay a demolition for up to 180 days. The listing stays with the house when it sells. No building permit is issued while a nomination is pending. A house not on the list has none of these rules. We check whether a house you like is listed or nominated.

What does a Pawleys Island house cost to own each year?

The biggest yearly costs are property tax and insurance. Both depend most on whether the house is your main home.

Property tax

As a second home or rental, a house pays about 3 times the property tax it pays as the home you live in. South Carolina taxes the home you live in on 4 percent of its value and any other house on 6 percent, and the tax rate on a second home is also about twice as high.

The tax a year, from Georgetown County's own estimate. $580,000 is the middle sale west of US 17, and $2,450,000 the island's. A second home here includes a rental.

The estimate includes the fees on the bill. The fees are $44 a year on the island. West of the highway they are $96, for storm water and household fees together. The county taxes its own appraised value, which can differ from the price. The Town of Pawleys Island levies no property tax of its own, so an island house pays the same rates as the mainland around it.

Flood insurance

A federal flood policy on an island house that is not its owner's main home cost more than 4 times what one on a mainland house in the same ZIP code cost. As a main home, the gap is much smaller. The town's flood work earns island owners a 25 percent discount on these policies, and the prices below already include it.

Most island policies cover the building for $250,000, the most the federal program pays on a home. A house worth more needs private flood coverage on top. Wind and homeowners insurance come on top of that. Their prices depend on the house's height, age and roof, so we get quotes on the house before you offer.

Can you rent out a house on Pawleys Island, and what do rentals take in?

Yes. Short-term rentals are allowed on Pawleys Island. Guests pay about 11 percent in lodging taxes on stays under 90 days. The town's share is filed with the town each month.

The town's lodging tax receipts show what island rentals take in. In 2024, guests paid about $27 million to stay on the island. Spread across the roughly 380 island houses that are not an owner's main home, that is about $70,000 a house for the year, before the rental company's fee and other costs.

  • About $27 millionRent guests paid on the island in a year
  • About $70,000A year, on average, per house that is not a main home

That is an average. Some houses are never rented, and some of the rent is paid on the island's condos and other rooms.

On the mainland, a homeowners association can limit rentals in its own papers, and we read them for you. The rules town by town are on our short-term rental rules page. For a yearly tenant instead, see our long-term rental guide. If you are selling a rental elsewhere to buy here, a 1031 exchange can put off the tax on the sale.

Does Pawleys Island flood, and how often do hurricanes come?

Every island house but one is in one of FEMA's high-risk flood zones. A lender making a mortgage on any of them will require flood insurance. The island's houses are split about evenly between two zones:

  • About half: water and wavesRising water and storm waves are both expected here. FEMA calls it the VE zone.
  • The other half: waterWater rises in a big flood, but big waves are not expected. FEMA calls it the AE zone.

The south end is the most exposed. South of the South Causeway, more than 9 in 10 houses are in the VE zone. North of the North Causeway, fewer than 1 in 10 are.

FEMA's flood height for island houses is 12 to 16 feet. It is measured from a fixed survey level, not from the ground, so it is not the depth of water at a house. The town requires the floor of a new house to sit 3 feet above that.

On ordinary days, the town's flood problem is water on the roads at the highest tides of the year, the king tides. The town has adopted a drainage plan for the whole island to deal with it.

Hurricanes

In the past 75 years, 44 storms have passed within 50 miles of the middle of the island, and 7 of them were hurricanes at their closest point. Ian passed about 5 miles away, and Matthew and Charley came closer still.

Hurricane Hazel, in 1954, washed away most of the houses built on the south end after World War II. The older houses came through it, as they had come through many storms before.

Can you rebuild an oceanfront house on Pawleys Island?

Yes, within limits that matter most at the south end. The state draws two lines along the beach. The baseline follows the top of the front dune. The setback line runs 20 feet behind it, on the land side.

On about 100 of the island's roughly 220 oceanfront houses, part of the house stands on the ocean side of the setback line. Most of them are at the south end. On Litchfield's oceanfront it is more than half of the houses.

For a house past the setback line, state law allows:

  • RepairA house damaged by a storm, but not destroyed, can be repaired.
  • RebuildA house a storm destroys can be rebuilt no larger past the setback line, no wider along the beach and no farther toward the ocean than before. It moves back from the ocean where possible, and never past the baseline.
  • New houseA new house built partly past the setback line can have at most 5,000 square feet of heated space.
  • The town's own lineThe town draws its own line too, the Shore Protection Line, and it is usually stricter than the state's.

Before you offer on an oceanfront house, we get the survey and see where all three lines fall on the lot.

What the town lets you build

  • HeightUp to 35 feet above the town's flood height for the lot, which is 3 feet above FEMA's.
  • SizeHeated floor area between 2,000 and 4,000 square feet, and no more than 40 percent of the lot.
  • PavingRoofs and paved areas together no more than 40 percent of the lot. Driveways are gravel, shell or crushed stone, never paved.
  • ApprovalA new house needs the town's Architectural Review Board. Taking down a protected tree needs a town permit.

The beach in front

The town rebuilt its beach with about 1.1 million cubic yards of new sand. The plan paid for it from three sources: the town's beach fund, which is its 3 percent lodging tax on rentals, a state grant and a bank loan.

The south tip will change. Under a settlement with the Prince George community across the inlet, a new inlet is to be cut about 800 feet south of the south-end parking lot and kept inside a set zone. The town gets a right to sand for future beach work.

Common questions

Pawleys Island FAQ

What county is Pawleys Island in?

Georgetown County, both the island and the mainland that shares its name. Its property taxes and its public schools are Georgetown County's.

Is Pawleys Island part of Myrtle Beach?

No. The island is its own town, and the mainland around it is run by Georgetown County. Broadway at the Beach, in Myrtle Beach, is about 26 miles north by road.

What is the ZIP code for Pawleys Island?

29585. It covers the island and most of the mainland around it, from the beach to well west of US 17.

How many people live on Pawleys Island?

The Census counted 130 people living in the town in 2020. Most island houses are second homes or rentals, so far more people stay in the summer.

Is Pawleys Island expensive?

On the island, yes: over three years a house sold for about $2.45 million at the middle. On the mainland, a house sold for about $580,000 to $675,000 at the middle in the past year.

Can you drive a golf cart on Pawleys Island?

Yes, on island roads. The cart needs a state registration and a town sticker, and the address on its registration must be within 2 miles of the town limits. The driver must be 16 or older with a license. No driving at night or in the rain.
