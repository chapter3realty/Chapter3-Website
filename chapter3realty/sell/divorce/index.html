Selling a house during a divorce in South Carolina
The three choices: sell and split, one buys the other out, or one keeps it for a time.

  • Before it is finalyou can sell during the case, when the two of you agree
  • Off the loanonly by a sale, a new loan or the lender's release
  • A spouse who will not signcan be replaced by the clerk of court, on a judge's order

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 14, 2026

If the two owners cannot agree on the house during a divorce, South Carolina's Family Court decides, and it can order the house sold.

The short answer

Yes. You can sell a house during a divorce in South Carolina, before or after the divorce is final, when the two of you agree. If you do not, the Family Court decides, and it can order the house sold.

A sale pays off the loan, so neither of you owes it after. If one of you keeps the house, the other still owes the old loan until it is paid off or the lender releases them.

Who gets the house in a divorce in South Carolina?

The Family Court divides the house as part of the marital property. Lawyers call this equitable distribution, a fair split that is not always half.

In South Carolina, a house bought during the marriage is usually marital property, even if only one spouse's name is on the deed. The exceptions include a house bought with one spouse's inherited or premarital money, or one a prenuptial agreement leaves out.

Usually divided

  • A house either of you bought during the marriage
  • Anything else either of you bought during the marriage and still owns on the day the case is filed
  • A rise in the value of one spouse's house that came from the other spouse's efforts

Not divided

  • What one spouse inherited, or was given by someone other than the other spouse
  • What one spouse owned before the marriage, and what was bought with either of those
  • What a written agreement, such as a prenuptial agreement, leaves out
  • What is bought after a temporary court order or a signed property agreement

A house one spouse owned before the marriage is not marital property under the law. Any rise in its value that came from the other spouse's efforts can be divided.

Is it split 50/50?

No. South Carolina law does not split marital property in half by rule. The judge weighs 15 factors, as much or as little as the judge finds right.

The factors include how long the marriage lasted and each spouse's income. They include what each spouse put in, counting work in the home, any debts on the property and the tax effect of the split. One factor is whether the parent with custody of the children gets the family home, or the right to live in it for a reasonable time.

Once the court divides the property, that part of the order is final. It can be changed only on appeal.

Sell, buy out your spouse, or keep the house: what does each leave you?

The two of you can choose any of the three by agreement. If you cannot agree, the judge chooses.

  • Choice 1Sell and splitThe house is sold, the loan is paid off at closing, and the two of you divide what is left. The sale pays the deed fee and agent fees. Neither of you owes the old loan after.
  • Choice 2One buys the other outOne of you keeps the house and pays the other for their share. The deed between you pays no deed fee, and there is no agent fee. The money can come from a new loan.
  • Choice 3One keeps it for a timeOne of you stays in the house for a time, and it is sold or bought out later. Both of you still owe the loan, unless the lender releases one of you.

When a buyout will not work

If the one who wants to keep the house cannot get a new loan for the buyout, the buyout cannot be paid that way. Then the choices left are a sale, keeping it for a time, or the judge's decision.

If one of you keeps the house for a time, the money question waits until it is sold or bought out. A written agreement or order that lets one spouse live there also matters for the tax rule later.

How does a buyout work, and how does the other spouse get off the mortgage?

The spouse who leaves gets off the loan in one of three ways. The house is sold, a new loan pays off the old one, or the lender releases them. A divorce order that gives the house to one spouse does not do it, and neither does a quitclaim deed.

The money for a buyout can come from that new loan, which some people call a refinance.

  1. Agree on the valueUse an appraisal, or a real estate agent's written price estimate. If you do not agree, the judge decides the value from the evidence and may appoint an expert, such as an appraiser.
  2. Write the terms downThe agreement says who keeps the house, what the other is paid and by when.
  3. The new loanThe spouse who keeps the house must qualify for a new loan. It has to pay off the old loan and pay the other spouse. On the example above, that is at least $275,000.
  4. The deedA deed from one spouse to the other as part of the divorce is exempt from the deed fee. The exception is a spouse who gets it and is neither a US citizen nor a US resident. A sale of the same house pays $1,295. To find a house's deed, use our Horry County deed search guide.
  5. The old loan is paid offThe new loan pays off the old one. That ends the debt of the spouse who left.

If the old loan stays

The lender cannot call the whole loan due because of the divorce, if the spouse on the loan lived there and the one who keeps it lives there. This does not cover a rental or a second home. The spouse who left still owes that loan unless the lender releases them.

Can my spouse sell the house without me, or refuse to sell?

If both of your names are on the deed, your spouse cannot sell the whole house without you. A sale of the whole house needs both of you to sign the deed, or a judge's order.

If your spouse refuses to sell, the judge can order the sale and order your spouse to sign the deed. If your spouse still will not, the judge can have the county's clerk of court sign it, and that signature counts the same.

If only your spouse's name is on the deed

Then your claim to the house does not bind a buyer or lender until a notice of the divorce case is filed. Either spouse can file it with the clerk of court in the county where the house is. Lawyers call the notice a lis pendens.

After it is filed, a buyer or lender who takes an interest later is bound by the case. It does not affect a buyer or lender whose rights came first. A family law attorney can tell you whether to file one.

If the deed says joint tenants with rights of survivorship

Some deeds name just the two spouses as "joint tenants with rights of survivorship". Then, until the final divorce order is filed, if one of you dies, the other owns the whole house. The final order ends that, unless it says otherwise.

Listing the house while you disagree

An agent may not market a house without a written listing agreement with the owner. Every offer reaches the seller in writing. Changes made during negotiations are in writing, initialed and dated by both sides.

If you cannot agree on a price or an offer

Most contested issues in a divorce case, the house included, must go to mediation, unless an exception applies. A mediator is a neutral person who helps the two sides talk. The mediator does not decide, and any agreement is voluntary.

If the two of you still do not agree, the judge decides the terms.

Capital gains tax in a divorce: should you sell before or after it is final?

For most Horry County houses, selling before or after the divorce is final does not change the tax, if each of you meets the 2-year tests. The whole gain on a typical house bought in 1998 or later is under $250,000, one person's limit. For one bought in the mid-1990s it is close to the limit.

The chart shows the gain on the whole house. We checked what a house that sells for about $350,000 today would have cost in each year, using the federal house price index for the Myrtle Beach area.

When a couple sells a house they own together, each reports their own share of the gain. Each single owner has their own $250,000 limit for their share.

Timing matters when the whole gain is near or over $250,000 and one of you will own the house alone, for example after a buyout. While you are still married, a joint return can use a $500,000 limit, if both of you meet the tests on our capital gains page.

Before or after the divorce is final

If you have no final decree of divorce or separate maintenance by the last day of your tax year, you count as married for that whole year. For most people that day is December 31, and the rule applies even if you live apart. Separate maintenance is a court order for support while a couple lives apart.

If one of you has moved out

A spouse who moved out can still count the house as their home for the tax rule. That holds if a divorce decree, a written separation agreement or a support order lets the other spouse live there, and the other spouse does. Without one of those papers, the time after you move out does not count as time you lived there.

A buyout and tax

Generally, moving the house, or your share of it, to your spouse as part of the divorce is not a sale for tax purposes. There is no gain or loss to report then. This does not hold if the spouse who gets it is neither a US citizen nor a US resident.

The spouse who keeps the house takes on the gain from the original purchase. When they sell it later, that whole gain counts.

If you sell before you meet the 2-year tests, a divorce is one of the listed reasons that can still allow a smaller, partial exclusion. Ask a tax professional before the terms of your agreement are set.

What are the steps to sell a house in a divorce in South Carolina?

Seven steps, from living apart to the money from the sale. The order of steps 5 and 6 depends on when the two of you agree.

  1. 1Before the caseLiving apartA no-fault divorce is one where neither spouse has to prove the other did wrong. In South Carolina it needs one year of living apart, without living together as a couple.
  2. 2The caseThe case is filed in Family CourtIn Horry County, it is filed with the Clerk of Court. The clerk has offices in Conway and Myrtle Beach.
  3. 3If neededA notice of the case on the houseIt matters most when the house is in one spouse's name.
  4. 4Before a judge decidesMediationMost issues you disagree on, the house included, go to a mediator first.
  5. 5The decisionAgreement or orderA signed agreement or the judge's order says whether the house is sold, bought out or kept.
  6. 6Before or after the divorceThe sale or the deedOn most grounds a final divorce cannot be granted until three months after the case is filed. On the one-year-apart ground, it can be granted once the other spouse has answered the case or missed the deadline to answer.
  7. 7ClosingThe moneyThe lawyer who holds the money from a sale, such as the closing attorney, must keep any part the two of you dispute separate. It stays separate until you agree, a court decides, or a narrow exception in the rule applies. The part you agree on is paid out promptly.

Common questions

Selling a house in a divorce: FAQ

Is South Carolina a 50/50 divorce state?

No. The law has no equal-split rule, so the judge's split of the house can be more or less than half.

Who pays the mortgage during the divorce?

Both of you owe the lender while both names are on the loan, so a missed payment is missed for both of you. While the case is open, the Family Court can order temporary support. A support order can make one spouse pay debts for the other, such as the mortgage.

What if we owe more than the house is worth?

Then the money from a sale would not pay off the loan. The lender may agree to a short sale, a sale for less than what is owed. If the lender forgives the rest, get that in writing.

Is a house I inherited marital property in South Carolina?

Usually not. What one spouse inherited is not marital property under the law. Whether it became marital, for example after it was put in both names, is a question for a family law attorney.
