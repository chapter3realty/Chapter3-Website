Selling

Sell your Grand Strand home from another state.
You do not have to come back.

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

Your Horry County home can sell while you stay in your own state. You sign the deed before a notary there, and the closing here goes ahead without you.

The short answer

None of the six steps needs you in South Carolina. We price and show the home, and you sign the contract online. A South Carolina attorney supervises the closing, where the home changes hands.

The buyer holds back part of the price for state income tax, unless it was your main home and your gain, or profit, is tax-free under federal law. For 2026, it is 5.21 percent of your gain if you sign the state's Form I‑295, or of the price less your selling costs if you do not.

About 3 in 10 buyers here are from another state, and we found no sign that they pay more than local buyers.

Can you sell a Grand Strand home without coming back?

Yes. About 1 in 4 homes in Horry County has its tax bill mailed outside South Carolina. Each step below says where you are for it.

  1. We price the home and show it to buyers.Our agent prices it to what sold near it and shows it to buyers and their agents.
    Where you are: at home
  2. You sign the offer and the contract from home.You sign them by e-signature on your phone or computer. In South Carolina that counts the same as ink.
    Where you are: at home
  3. The buyer's inspector and appraiser visit the home.The inspector checks the home's condition, and the appraiser judges its value. They work for the buyer. Someone with a key lets them in, and you choose that person. See who holds the key. Low appraisals are rare here. When one comes in low, one of our agents sends recent sales nearby with a request to reconsider the value.
    Where you are: at home
  4. A South Carolina attorney supervises the closing.The deed is the paper that passes the home to the buyer. Only a lawyer may prepare one for someone else, so a lawyer here draws up yours. The contract can name the attorney and who pays. See who picks the attorney.
    Where you are: at home
  5. You sign the deed where you live.The closing attorney's office sends you the deed. You sign it before a notary near you and send it back. See how the papers travel.
    Where you are: a notary near you
  6. The deed is recorded in Horry County.Recorded means filed in the county's public records. The recording date starts the 90-day count for any guest stays you have booked. The guest bookings section below gives each date.
    Where you are: at home

How the deed gets to you and back

The closing attorney's office sends you the deed and the other closing papers. You sign them before a notary where you live and send them back to that office.

The South Carolina Bar's ethics committee calls this a closing by mail. No law requires you to attend the closing. For the attorney's own client, the attorney must review every paper and answer questions by phone, fax or email.

Send the signed papers back with a carrier that tracks the package. The office then records the deed in Horry County.

Who must be there when you sign the deed

Can you use an online notary from your state?

Do not plan on it. South Carolina's own notary law defines appearing before a notary as being in each other's physical presence. The state has no video notarization of its own, so sign in person.

Who picks and pays the closing attorney

The sales contract can name the closing attorney and say who pays the attorney's fees. You and the buyer agree on both. When the buyer has a home loan, the lender must ask the buyer which attorney to use and follow that choice.

That attorney works for the buyer. An attorney who works for one side should not advise the other side, except to get a lawyer. For advice of your own, hire your own South Carolina attorney. We work with plenty of attorneys here and can recommend some.

Who holds the key

You decide. Someone must let in the buyer's inspector and appraiser and the agents who show the home. Name that person in your listing agreement, the contract you sign with us to sell the home. South Carolina law requires our agent to carry out the terms of that agreement.

If you would rather someone sign for you

You can name a person you trust to sign for you, with a power of attorney, a signed paper that lets that person act for you. One made under South Carolina law is witnessed and notarized, and one made under your own state's law can also be valid. Recording it in Horry County costs $25.

What does our agent do once your home is listed?

Once you sign the listing agreement, South Carolina law lists what a seller's agent must do. Our agent must:

  • carry out the terms of the listing agreement;
  • seek a sale at your price and terms, or at a price and terms you accept;
  • bring you every written offer and counteroffer promptly, even after you are under contract;
  • tell you every material problem with the sale that the agent knows about;
  • tell you to get expert advice on anything beyond an agent's expertise;
  • account promptly for any money or property received for you;
  • keep private what you tell us that could hurt your sale, except in the cases the law allows, such as your written consent.

Each step above says what we add to that, from pricing to the appraisal. None of it needs you in South Carolina.

What does South Carolina hold back at closing when you live out of state?

When your permanent home is in another state, the buyer must hold back part of the price for South Carolina income tax, unless the sale is exempt. A main home whose gain is inside the federal exclusion is exempt. The buyer sends it to the South Carolina Department of Revenue. If the closing attorney's office holds it back, that office must send it.

For a sale closed in 2026, the rate is 5.21 percent. It is the state's top income tax rate for the year of the sale, so a sale that closes in a later year may use a different rate. We can tell you the rate for the year your sale will close.

Your gain is what you get from the sale less what the home cost you.

The amount held back is never more than the cash you would get at closing.

Does the main-home exclusion cover you?

Only if it was your main home. The federal main-home exclusion leaves up to $250,000 of gain out of your income, or $500,000 for a married couple filing jointly. You must have owned and lived there 2 of the last 5 years, with no exclusion used on another home in the 2 years before the sale. On a joint return, both of you must have lived there. A rental or a second home does not get it.

Form I‑295: your sworn statement

Form I‑295 is a sworn statement on a South Carolina tax form. On it you state your gain, or that the home was your main home. You sign it before a notary, so you can sign it at the same visit as the deed. You give it to the buyer and keep a copy with your tax records.

Do you get back what was held back?

You file a South Carolina return for the year of the sale, and the amount held back counts toward the tax you owe there. If more was held back than you owe, you can ask the state for the difference as a refund. To run your own numbers, use the calculator in our capital gains and withholding guide.

A Canadian owner who is not a U.S. citizen or resident also faces a separate federal hold-back under FIRPTA, generally 15 percent of the price. Our page for Canadian owners covers it. Selling a house you inherited from far away? Our inherited house guide covers that sale.

Keep your money safe

Confirm the account your money goes to with the closing attorney's office by phone, on a number you agreed on in advance. Never follow wire or bank instructions in an email.

What must you tell a buyer about a home you rarely see?

Tell the buyer what you know. You do not have to report what you do not know. You give the buyer South Carolina's written disclosure statement, the same as an owner who lives next door. On any item you do not know, you can mark that you make no representation, which means you are not saying either way.

What you do know, you must tell. An owner who knowingly gives false, incomplete or misleading information is liable to the buyer for the damage. For a condo, the statement covers your unit, not the parts of the building everyone shares. As your listing agent, we must tell you in writing what this law asks of you.

Sales that need no statement

  • a sale by the person settling an estate, or by a trustee for a trust;
  • a sale to a spouse, a child or another direct relative;
  • the first sale of a home no one has lived in;
  • a sale where both sides agree in writing to skip it.

What happens to your guest bookings when you sell?

If you rent the home to guests, South Carolina law says what happens to each booking, and when the buyer and your rental company must hear about it. Your disclosure statement must also say if a lease or a contract with a rental company will still be in place at closing.

  1. Before the contract is signedThe buyer gets, in writing, every future date the home is booked.
  2. Within 14 days of the contractThe rental company gets the buyer's name and address in writing.
  3. Within 14 days after the saleThe rental company gets the buyer's name, address and the date the deed was recorded.
  4. Up to 90 days after recordingGuest stays that begin in this time go ahead. The buyer must honor them.
  5. Stays that begin laterThey cannot be enforced, and the guest is due a refund within 45 days after the recording.

Selling a home with a long-term tenant in it? Our rental property guide covers the lease.

Do out-of-state buyers pay more for Grand Strand homes?

Buyers from other states paid what their neighbors paid. In the same building or subdivision, they and buyers from South Carolina paid the same typical price. About half of each group paid more than that price, and half paid less.

Some sellers expect a buyer from a pricier state to pay more. We price your home to what sold near it, the same as for a local buyer.

Condos draw the most buyers from other states: more than half, against about 1 in 4 buyers of other homes. Near the ocean the share is higher. In the city of North Myrtle Beach it was more than half; inland in Conway, about 1 in 10. These buyers close here all year, so list your home when it is ready.

Common questions

Selling from out of state: questions

Is anything held back if I sell at a loss?

Not if you sign Form I‑295. The hold-back is figured on your gain, and a home sold for less than it cost you has no gain. Without the form, it is figured on the price less your selling costs.

Does the notary have to be from South Carolina?

No. A notary in the state where you live can notarize the deed, and the act counts the same as a South Carolina notary's. The notary writes in their state and the date their commission ends.

Do I pay unpaid HOA dues at closing?

Usually, yes. Unpaid dues are put as a lien on the home and taken out of the seller's money at closing. Our agents check for them before closing, so they do not surprise you.

Is fall a bad time to sell to out-of-state buyers?

No. More buyers from other states closed in October than in any other month. Even in January, the slowest month, about 1 in 4 buyers came from another state.
