Selling your house by owner in South Carolina
What it saves on a Myrtle Beach-area home, and the three things the law requires.

The short answer

Yes. You can sell your house by owner in Myrtle Beach, or anywhere in South Carolina, without a real estate agent or a license. A South Carolina attorney must supervise the closing, and you give the buyer the state's disclosure form before you both sign the contract.

In Horry County, the middle-priced home resold in the year to September 2026, not counting condos, sold for about $350,000. On that home, each 1 percent of fees you do not pay is $3,500.

Can you sell your own house in South Carolina without a real estate agent?

Yes. South Carolina's license law does not apply to an owner selling their own home, so you need no real estate license. This is called selling "for sale by owner", or FSBO.

Three duties apply to most owners who sell, with or without an agent:

Do you need a lawyer to sell a house by owner in South Carolina?

Yes, for the closing, the meeting where the buyer pays and you sign the house over. Every home closing in South Carolina must be supervised by a South Carolina attorney.

Preparing a deed for someone else is the practice of law. Anyone who prepares your deed for you, the paper that transfers the house, must be a lawyer.

Who picks the closing attorney?

  • The buyer has a home loanThe buyer choosesIf the loan is for a home the buyer will live in, the buyer picks the closing attorney, and the lender must follow that choice.
  • Every saleThe contract can name oneThe contract can name the closing attorney and say who pays the attorney's fees. You and the buyer agree on it.
  • You want adviceHire your ownAn attorney who represents only the buyer does not advise the seller. Advice on your side of the deal comes from your own attorney.

Who writes the purchase contract?

A 2007 opinion of the state Attorney General's office looked at agents who fill in the blanks of a printed sale contract an attorney wrote. If the buyer has an agent, the offer may come to you on that kind of form.

The office said that does not appear to be the practice of law, if no legal advice is given or asked for. The state Supreme Court has the final word. Preparing a legal paper for someone else is the practice of law when it involves legal advice. Advice on the contract's terms comes from a lawyer. A South Carolina attorney can write the contract for you, or read the buyer's before you sign.

How you get paid safely

Closing money often moves by wire transfer, and scammers send fake wiring instructions by email. Never follow wiring instructions in an email. Confirm them by phone with the attorney's office, at a number you agreed on in advance.

What paperwork do you need to sell by owner in South Carolina?

The main paper is the state's Residential Property Condition Disclosure Statement, a form where you tell the buyer what you know about the home. State law requires it for a home with one to four units, unless the sale is one of the kinds listed below that need no form. The form is free. Our guide to the South Carolina seller disclosure form goes through all 28 questions.

The form has nine parts. It asks 28 numbered questions, each answered Yes, No or No Representation, plus lines where you describe things such as the roof's age, pests and leases.

A home in a homeowners association (HOA), a property owners association or a condo also needs the form's addendum. It asks 11 more questions and lists the dues and the association's contact. Our HOA documents page lists the association papers a buyer will ask for.

Why the flood questions matter here

FEMA has paid 8,815 flood insurance claims in Horry County since 1978. Nearly 1 in 4 were on buildings rated outside the high-risk flood zone. That is why questions 23 to 27 matter here, even away from the water. Our flood history page shows what a buyer can find out about a home's flood past.

Four rules about the form

  • "No Representation"Not a way to skip a questionIt means you do not know enough to answer Yes or No. It does not protect you if you know of the problem.
  • After you hand it overCorrect it if anything changesIf the roof starts to leak before closing, you must correct the form or repair the roof.
  • If you leave something outYou can owe the buyerA seller who knowingly leaves a problem off the form, or answers falsely, can owe the buyer their actual losses and court costs. A court may add the winner's attorney fees.
  • Selling as isThe form still appliesYou can sell "as is", which means without repairs. An as-is sale still needs the form, unless it is one of the sales listed below.

Not giving the form does not cancel the contract or stop the closing.

What you do not have to tell a buyer

You do not have to disclose that someone died in the home. You also do not have to disclose an illness of someone who lived there if it is highly unlikely to spread by living in the home. The same goes for the sex offender registry. If a buyer asks you directly, you may not lie.

Sales that need no form

South Carolina lists 15 kinds of sale that need no form. Among them:

  • A sale only to your spouse, or only to your children, grandchildren, parents or grandparents. If your child's spouse is also a buyer, the sale needs the form.
  • A sale only between co-owners.
  • A sale by an executor or trustee settling an estate or a trust.
  • The first sale of a home no one has lived in.
  • A sale at public auction.
  • Any sale where buyer and seller both agree in writing to skip the form.
Homes built before 1978A seller of most homes built before 1978 must also give the buyer the federal lead pamphlet. You also say what you know about lead paint and hand over any lead reports. The contract must include a lead warning statement. The buyer gets 10 days to test for lead, unless both agree in writing to a different period or the buyer waives it. Keep the signed form for three years. Four cases are not covered. One is a unit with no bedroom. Another is housing for older people or people with disabilities where no child under six lives. The others are a home a certified inspector found free of lead paint, and a foreclosure sale.

Do houses sold by owner sell for less?

Nationally, yes, but many of those sales were to someone the seller knew. NAR, the National Association of Realtors, surveys people who bought a home to live in and also sold one. In its 2025 survey, 5 percent sold by owner, the lowest share in NAR's records.

More than half of by-owner sellers already knew the buyer. In resort and recreation areas, 9 percent sold by owner.

County deeds point the same way for sales between people who share a last name. We read the county's deed index for a year and compared those sales with other sales between two people.

A shared last name is not proof of family, and relatives with different last names are missed. Even so, those 73 sales recorded far lower prices.

Selling to a stranger

Selling to a stranger is a different job. The price has to come from the sales most like your home, and enough buyers have to see the house.

Asked the hardest part, 17 percent of by-owner sellers named getting the price right, more than any other task.

The MLS is the database real estate brokers share to list homes for sale, so an owner gets on it through a broker. In NAR's survey, 7 percent of sellers who used an agent said the agent put the home on the MLS and did little else.

On the MLS, your house competes with every other home for sale:

Houses for sale in Horry County now

  • About 3,700houses listed for sale on the MLS, the database brokers share
  • 56 percentof them have already cut their asking price. Buyers compare yours with all of them.

Broad marketing generally exposes a home to the greatest number of qualified buyers. Keeping a home off the open market may reduce buyer competition and may hurt the price, terms or time to sell.

Do you have to pay the buyer's agent?

No rule requires it. Since August 2024, an agent who uses the MLS signs a written agreement with a buyer before touring a home. Offers to pay the buyer's agent are no longer made on the MLS.

You can still offer to pay the buyer's agent off the MLS, or offer the buyer a credit toward their costs. Agents' fees are negotiable, and no law names an amount.

  3. 3ShowingsReach buyers and show the houseUse the ways in the chart above. Keep valuables and medicines out of sight during showings.
  4. 4Before you signCheck that the buyer can payAsk for a loan approval letter from a lender, or proof of the cash, before you sign anything.

Do you pay tax on the sale?

Often not. Under federal tax law, you can leave part or all of your gain out of your income.

  • How muchUp to $250,000 of gainOr $500,000 of gain for a married couple filing jointly.
  • Who qualifiesYou lived there 2 of 5 yearsYou owned and lived in the home at least 2 of the last 5 years. You did not use this exclusion on another home in the 2 years before.
  • You live in another stateThe buyer holds back state taxFor a 2026 sale it is 5.21 percent. It is figured on your gain if you sign the state's gain affidavit, Form I-295, or on the whole price if you do not. A main home whose gain fits inside the federal exclusion has none.
  • Getting it backYour South Carolina returnFile a South Carolina return for the year of the sale. The amount held back counts toward the tax you owe there. You can ask for a refund of any amount held back beyond the tax.

Common questions

Selling by owner FAQ

Who pays the deed stamps when you sell by owner in South Carolina?

The seller pays them, whether or not an agent is involved: $1,295 on a $350,000 sale.

Can I sell my house to my son or daughter without the disclosure form?

Yes, if the sale is only to your children, grandchildren, parents or grandparents. If your child's spouse is also a buyer, the sale needs the form.

Do I have to tell a buyer that someone died in the house?

No. South Carolina does not require it. If a buyer asks you directly, you may not lie about it.

Can I sell my house as is in South Carolina?

Yes. An as-is sale still needs the state's disclosure form, unless it is one of the sales that need no form, such as some family sales.

Can a buyer use their own agent to buy a for-sale-by-owner home?

Yes. The buyer's written agreement with their agent says how that agent is paid. You can offer to pay that agent, or offer the buyer a credit toward their costs.
