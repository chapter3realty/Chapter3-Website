Selling a home

A cash offer for your Myrtle Beach house, or list it?
House flippers here paid about 68 percent of the price they resold for.

  • About 68%what flippers here paid, of the price they resold for
  • At least 1 in 3home resales here had no home loan recorded

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 8, 2026

What cash buyers in Horry County paid against what they resold for, when cash is the better choice, and how to check the buyer and the contract.

The short answer

List it, unless the house needs heavy repairs, cannot get a loan or insurance, or must be sold by a fixed date. Then a cash offer can be the better choice.

Companies that bought Horry County houses from their owners and resold them within a year paid about 68 cents for each dollar of the resale price. That is the middle sale: half paid more, half paid less. On a house worth $260,000 fixed up, that is an offer of about $178,000.

A cash sale skips the wait for a loan. In September 2026, the middle listed home in the Myrtle Beach area took 53 days to get a signed contract, then about five weeks to close.

Who makes cash offers on houses in Myrtle Beach?

Three kinds of buyer make cash offers on houses here:

  • Online instant-offer buyersA company that makes an offer online from the address, also called an iBuyer.
  • Local investorsPeople and companies who buy a house to fix up and resell, or to rent out.
  • "We buy houses" buyersBuyers who send letters, call, or put up roadside signs offering cash for houses. We check them the same way as any other buyer.

Many more buyers here pay without a home loan. We matched the county's deed and mortgage records for a full year of home resales. At least 1 in 3 had no new home loan recorded in the buyer's name, and about 3 in 4 of those buyers were people, not companies. A house listed for sale reaches those buyers too.

How much less is a cash offer, and how do cash buyers set the price?

In the middle sale, a company paid about a third less than it later resold the house for.

The chart shows the middle prices. Companies paid $170,000 for houses they bought from the owners, and resold them for $260,000, about four months later.

A company that buys to resell has to pay for repairs, months of holding the house, two closings and the costs of selling. It pays for all of them, and its profit, out of the difference between the two prices. Offers vary from house to house.

Cash offer or listing: which leaves you more money?

Listing leaves you more when repairs, fees and time cost less than the cash buyer's discount. Take a house that would sell for $260,000 once fixed up, the middle resale price in our count. A cash offer at the middle share companies paid would be about $178,000.

When is a cash offer the better choice?

A cash offer is the better choice when a buyer with a loan cannot buy the house, or when you need a sure closing date.

A cash sale has no lender, so there is no loan approval or lender's appraisal to wait for. A South Carolina attorney still supervises the title search, the closing and the recording of the deed. Our page on the best time to sell shows how the wait for a contract changes by month.

Can you sell a house as is in South Carolina?

Yes. "As is" means you sell without making repairs. The state's disclosure form is still required, and a cash sale is not exempt for being cash. Our page on the seller disclosure form goes through it question by question.

Sellers already list houses as cash only

A seller can list the house as it is and ask for cash buyers only. The local listing feed had these:

A cash offer made before the house is listed is one buyer's offer. A listing puts the house in front of every buyer at once, with a loan or without. The state Real Estate Commission warns that keeping a home off the open market can mean less competition and a lower price, worse terms or a longer sale.

One sale to a cash buyer

One of our agents sold a rented house this way. The owner was moving to New York to be with his sister, and the lease still had eight months left. The next investor would make only about half his monthly cash flow, the rent left after costs, because of higher taxes and a bigger loan.

The agent marketed the house to our list of investors who accept a modest cash flow on a home rising in value. A cash buyer closed within 13 days of the day it was listed. That was his sale and his figures, not a promise. Our rental property page tells the rest.

How do you check a cash buyer and the contract?

Before you sign a cash buyer's contract, our agents check six things:

  1. Proof of the moneyA recent bank statement or a letter from the buyer's bank that shows the cash. We ask for it before you sign.
  2. The depositThe buyer's good-faith deposit is called earnest money. A third party, such as an attorney, holds it until closing. If the buyer walks away for a reason the contract does not allow, the seller may keep it. Across the US, no law requires a deposit, and deposits typically run 1 to 10 percent of the price.
  3. The inspection periodIf the contract makes the sale depend on an inspection, the buyer can back out of the sale. A buyer who walks away because a condition of the contract was not met gets the deposit back. A long inspection period with a small deposit means the buyer can back out late at little cost while the house is under contract.
  4. Any right to hand the contract onThe words "and/or assigns" after the buyer's name let the buyer pass its place in your contract to someone else. That is not wholesaling by itself. It becomes wholesaling when the buyer also markets your house before owning it.
  5. Who the buyer isWe look the company up by name in the Secretary of State's business records, where every South Carolina LLC and corporation is filed.
  6. The closingEvery South Carolina home closing, cash or loan, must be supervised by a South Carolina attorney.

Wire fraud. Never follow wiring instructions in an email. Confirm them in person or by phone with the attorney's office, at a number you agreed on in advance.

Is wholesaling legal in South Carolina?

No, as the state Real Estate Commission reads the law. Since May 2024, South Carolina's license law has defined wholesaling. It means having a contract to buy a home from its owner, then marketing it to a different buyer before owning it.

  • Marketing takes a licenseMarketing a home someone else owns, for pay, takes a real estate license. In its advisory opinion, the Commission reads that as a ban on wholesaling.
  • What counts as marketingThe MLS, social media, email groups and word of mouth. Naming the address, a photo or the neighborhood counts too.
  • BrokeragesA licensed brokerage such as Chapter3 may not take part in wholesaling or help anyone do it.

The Commission's opinion is guidance, not a court ruling. If a buyer markets your house before closing, a South Carolina attorney can tell you what it means for your contract.

Common questions

Cash offer FAQ

Does Chapter3 buy houses for cash?

No. Chapter3 Realty is a licensed real estate brokerage. We do not buy houses ourselves; we list and sell them for their owners.

Do you pay closing costs in a cash sale?

Yes, at least the state and county deed fee. The seller owes it by law in a cash sale or a listed one: $1.85 for each $500 of the price, about $659 on $178,000. A contract can shift who pays it. Our net proceeds calculator adds the other costs.

Can a cash buyer skip the seller disclosure form?

Only if buyer and seller both agree in writing not to complete it. Neither side has to agree. Without that, the state form is required in a cash sale too.

Are "we buy houses" companies legitimate?

We judge no company by name. We check any cash buyer and its contract the same way, in the six steps on this page, before you sign.
