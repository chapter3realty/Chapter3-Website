Myrtle Beach real estate market report: September 2026.
Houses cost about what they did in 2024. Condos cost less, and more of them went under contract.

By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge · Updated November 12, 2026

The Myrtle Beach market in September: the median house sold for $370,000 and the median condo for $230,125.

The short answer

In September 2026, the median house sold in Horry and Georgetown Counties went for $370,000, and the median condo for $230,125.

House prices in this market have been about level for three years. Condos cost about 6 percent less than in 2024, and buyers had more condo offers accepted this year than in either of the last two.

What is the median home price in Myrtle Beach?

From January to September, the median house sold for $362,000 and the median condo for $232,000, close to the September figures. The median is the middle sale: half of the homes sold for more, and half for less.

Why do other websites show a different median?

These numbers are sales through the CCAR MLS, the listing service of the local REALTOR association for Horry and Georgetown Counties. A home sold without a listing on the MLS is not in them.

We checked Horry County's deed records, which hold those sales too. For the 12 months to September, the middle resale price there was about $215,000 for condos and about $350,000 for the other homes, mostly houses.

Areas differ too. CCAR's areas follow ZIP codes or MLS areas, not city limits. Its "Myrtle Beach" area is the two ZIP codes 29572 and 29577.

For what homes sold for in your area, from the deeds, see our home value page.

Are Myrtle Beach home prices dropping?

Myrtle Beach house prices are not dropping. The median condo price has fallen two years in a row.

A median depends on which homes sold that month, so it changes more from one month to the next. The January to September figure is steadier. Our condo guide compares how condo prices have done next to houses, over more years.

Condo buyers did not stay away. From January to September this year, sellers accepted 4,105 offers on condos, more than in the same months of either of the last two years.

Many condo sellers have already come down. In Horry County, more than half of the condos for sale on November 3 had cut their asking price, our buying guide found.

How long do homes take to sell in Myrtle Beach?

Houses took about 4 months from listing to an accepted offer from January to September, and condos a little longer. The closing comes after the accepted offer, on the date the contract names.

How many homes are for sale in Myrtle Beach?

Fewer houses were for sale at the end of September than a year before, and the same number of condos. Condos for sale would last almost twice as long as houses.

That is months of supply: how long the homes for sale would last at the past year's pace of accepted offers, if no new homes were listed.

Over the past 12 months, the median house price stayed between about $350,000 and $371,000. The median condo stayed between about $220,000 and $249,000.

Common questions

Market report: questions

What is the median home price in Myrtle Beach?

Half of the houses sold in Horry and Georgetown Counties in September went for more than $370,000, and half for less. For condos, the middle sale was $230,125.

Are Myrtle Beach home prices dropping?

Not for houses: the median from January to September was about the same in 2024, 2025 and 2026. The condo median was lower in each of the last two years than the year before.

How long does it take to sell a home in Myrtle Beach?

Houses took 122 days on average from listing to an accepted offer this year, and condos 131. The closing comes after that.

How many homes are for sale in Myrtle Beach?

About 7,100 houses and condos in Horry and Georgetown Counties at the end of September. At the past year's pace of accepted offers, the condos would last almost twice as long as the houses.

Why do websites give different median prices?

Websites count different sales and different areas. These numbers are MLS sales in two counties. County deed records also hold sales never listed, and a site's "Myrtle Beach" can be a city, a ZIP code or a whole county.
